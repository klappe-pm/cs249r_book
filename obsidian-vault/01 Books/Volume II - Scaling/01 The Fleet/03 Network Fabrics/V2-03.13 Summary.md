# V2-03.13 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/03 Network Fabrics/V2-03 Network Fabrics|V2-03 Network Fabrics]]

^sec-network-fabrics-summary

The five-level model of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/03 Network Fabrics/V2-03.03 How ML Networking Inverts Data-Center Assumptions#^fig-network-five-level-model|figure 3]] bridges physical reach, transport primitives, switch topologies, congestion dynamics, and cluster integration into a single system contract. A fabric must provide both enough capacity for the workload’s traffic and predictable completion at its synchronization boundaries. InfiniBand and RoCE distribute this responsibility differently across links, endpoints, and operational policy; the production cases show why neither port rate nor protocol name determines delivered performance.

Three numerical anchors show how to apply the model. One GPU’s NVLink bandwidth exceeds its own external port’s per-direction bandwidth by 9×; the modeled NDR path has equal startup and transmission costs near 75 KB; and the 4:1 oversubscribed cut increases the fixed-payload transfer bound by 4×. Each result depends on its stated configuration and traffic assumptions. In the Fleet Law, transfer time contributes to ${T_{\text{comm}}}({N})$ and residual synchronization waiting to ${T_{\text{sync}}}({N})$; consistent timeline accounting prevents counting one delay in both. The communication and coordination axes of C³ meet where those dependencies determine useful compute progress.

The same model guides design and diagnosis. Message sizes determine whether startup latency or bandwidth dominates; communication patterns determine which paths must carry that traffic. During operation, application throughput, transport counters, and physical telemetry distinguish inadequate capacity from contention or a degraded component. These measurements also test whether a proposed optical or composable architecture removes the limiting constraint or merely moves it.

**Key Takeaways: The fabric decides useful compute**

- **Synchronous training inverts data-center traffic**: A bulk-synchronous step releases a few large, correlated flows that must complete before the next step can start, so the fabric is judged by collective completion time and its p99 tail rather than by average per-flow throughput. Static ECMP hashing turns flow collisions into persistent hot spots.
- **Link speed is not fabric speed**: For traffic that crosses a fabric cut, available capacity bounds transfer time (principle [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/V2 Fleet Principles#^pri-bisection-bandwidth|6]]). Workload placement and collective schedules determine how much data must cross, so advertised port speed alone cannot predict useful training throughput.
- **Latency and bandwidth limit different messages**: The ${\alpha}$-${\beta}$ model of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/03 Network Fabrics/V2-03.05 Level 2 Transport and the Performance Model#^sec-network-fabrics-performance-model|section 3.4.4]] separates startup cost from per-byte transfer cost, with ${n^*} = {\alpha}\cdot {\beta}$ marking the regime change. Message-size distributions, not vendor peak numbers, determine whether to optimize software latency or hardware bandwidth.
- **Reliable delivery moves the risk**: RDMA transports need reliable completion and bounded recovery, not one universal lossless-fabric mechanism. InfiniBand normally suppresses loss with link-level credits; many RoCE deployments use PFC and ECN, while PFC-free designs place more recovery responsibility at the endpoints. Each choice trades hardware flexibility against tail latency and operational complexity.
- **Topology must match traffic**: Fat-trees buy flexible bisection bandwidth, rail-optimized designs give same-rank AllReduce traffic its own isolated switch network, and dragonfly or torus designs trade cabling and locality differently. The right fabric depends on whether the workload stresses AllReduce, AllToAll, or multi-tenant sharing, and optical circuit switching lets a pod revisit that choice per job at millisecond reconfiguration cost.
- **Losslessness and load create their own failures**: PFC prevents drops by pausing senders, which can spread one slow drainer’s congestion to victim flows or freeze a lossless class in a cyclic buffer dependency. Under synchronized load, end-to-end congestion control is a latency requirement, adaptive routing repairs hash collisions without adding capacity, and incast is spread in time, space, and priority rather than eliminated.
- **Telemetry saves GPU-hours**: PFC counters, link error rates, bandwidth baselines, and application throughput must be correlated across layers. Without that observability, a degraded transceiver or PFC storm silently converts a high-end fleet into a queue of waiting accelerators.

^callout-takeaways--3-13-0fbb97ff

At fleet scale, the interconnect is part of the computer. Its value lies in how much useful computation it allows the participating accelerators to complete together. A fabric decision therefore has to be judged against the workload’s communication pattern and synchronization schedule, because additional capacity helps only where those dependencies expose a communication limit.

**What’s Next: From wires to data pipelines**

A fabric that completes every collective on time still leaves two flows of bytes unaccounted for. Each step needs its next input batch in place before the accelerators ask for it, and the fleet’s training state must outlive the next hardware failure, which at this scale means writing multi-terabyte checkpoints on a schedule. Those checkpoint bursts are the traffic class this chapter isolated from the synchronization path, and for text pretraining they can outweigh the training corpus itself. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/04 Data Storage/V2-04 Data Storage#^sec-data-storage|Data Storage]]** treats storage as the fleet’s fuel line, sizing the tiers and pipelines that deliver inputs ahead of demand and persist model state without stalling the step.
