# V2-02.02 The Infrastructure Walls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02 Compute Infrastructure|V2-02 Compute Infrastructure]]

^sec-compute-infrastructure-infrastructure-walls-4f2c

The scaling laws of **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.04 AI Scaling Laws#^sec-vol2-intro-ai-scaling-laws-a043|AI Scaling Laws]]** are why demand outruns any single device. Loss falls predictably as parameters, training tokens, and compute grow together, so each step up in capability multiplies both the memory a model occupies and the arithmetic its training consumes; a GPT-3-class model already needs several accelerators merely to store its FP16 weights. A model that exceeds one accelerator’s memory capacity forces a hardware decision before training begins. Adding accelerators supplies capacity, but also creates transfers between devices, increases power demand, and couples progress to more components that can fail. The familiar single-node compute and memory limits therefore become inputs to fleet design.

Four recurring physical constraints appear at escalating physical scales, from an eight-GPU node to a 25K-accelerator fleet ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.02 The Infrastructure Walls#^fig-three-walls-grid|figure 1]]). Memory bandwidth, rack power, pod communication, and fleet failure exposure provide the examples; the workload and configuration determine which constraint binds at each scale.

At the scale moment, a model outgrows one machine and meets the memory, energy, and network walls, and growing the fleet opens its reliability gap. The four constraints here are those same limits, seen from the hardware that imposes them, so this chapter names each by the physical resource that runs out. The energy wall becomes the power wall, the limit on power delivery and heat removal. The network wall becomes the communication wall, the limit on link bandwidth. The reliability gap becomes the reliability wall, the same exposure measured as a failure interval. The memory wall keeps its name.

↩︎ The memory, network, and energy walls of the scale moment are established in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.02 The Scale Moment#^sec-vol2-introduction-scale-moment|The Scale Moment]]**.

The 175B model anchors this progression. It is the chapter’s running instance of Archetype A (GPT-4/Llama-3), a GPT-3-class dense transformer trained with mixed precision and the Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam) optimizer, whose reference training cluster is 1,024 H100 GPUs in 128 eight-GPU nodes. Accelerator selection establishes the compute rate, memory capacity, and power envelope that a fleet can buy. The node must connect enough of those accelerators to hold and execute the workload; the rack must power and cool them; the pod must sustain communication and survive component failures. Each level’s limit becomes an input to the next, because node power sets what the rack must cool and rack density shapes the pod’s layout. This progression is the hardware contribution to C³, the compute, communication, and coordination taxonomy of **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.06 The C³ Taxonomy and Foundations of Scale#^sec-vol2-introduction-c-cube|The C^3 Taxonomy and Foundations of Scale]]**. Useful fleet compute depends on the communication and coordination costs created at each physical boundary.

↩︎ The three fleet workload archetypes are established in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.07 The Fleet Stack and the Lighthouse Archetypes#^sec-vol2-introduction-archetypes|Three systems archetypes]]**.

↪ The running model’s parameters and reference cluster are developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume II - Scaling/V2 System Assumptions#^sec-appdx-fleet-reference-workload|Reference Workload]]**.

The fourth wall follows the naming convention of **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.02 The Scale Moment#^sec-vol2-introduction-scale-moment|The Scale Moment]]**, which separates two failure figures. A failed GPU is replaced rather than repaired, so the per-device figure is a mean time to failure (MTTF). Nodes and fleets keep running through repeated failures and replacements, so their figure is a mean time between failures (MTBF). The reliability calculation therefore starts from a per-GPU MTTF and ends at the MTBF of a node or fleet.

![[90 Assets/Volume II/02 Compute Infrastructure/Infra Walls 0bdd0b6c40.svg]]

Figure 1: **The Four Infrastructure Walls**: Read left to right, the examples illustrate four constraints at increasing physical scales; the workload and configuration determine which one binds. The node compares 3.35 TB/s of HBM3 with about 2,000 TFLOP/s of FP8 arithmetic; the four-node DGX H100 rack draws 33.5 kW; the pod compares per-direction NVLink bandwidth with one InfiniBand port, a ninefold budget gap; and the fleet assumes independent GPU failures, reducing a 50,000-hour per-GPU mean time to failure (MTTF) to about 2 hours between failures at 25K GPUs.

^fig-three-walls-grid

The chapter climbs the same scale ladder, so the four walls arrive in the order the figure reads, though not one wall per section. The memory wall begins at the accelerator and is developed in [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.04 The Memory Wall#^sec-compute-memory-wall|section 2.3]]. The communication wall first appears inside the node, where [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.06 The Node#^sec-compute-bandwidth-hierarchy|section 2.5.1]] measures the cliff between local and inter-node links, and returns at pod scale in [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.08 The Pod#^sec-compute-pod|section 2.7]]. The power wall binds at the rack ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.07 The Rack#^sec-compute-power-wall|section 2.6.1]]), and the reliability wall accumulates from node health ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.06 The Node#^sec-compute-infrastructure-node-health-reliability-56a6|section 2.5.5]]) through facility controls to the fleet-scale failure interval of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02.08 The Pod#^sec-compute-fleet|section 2.7.1]].
