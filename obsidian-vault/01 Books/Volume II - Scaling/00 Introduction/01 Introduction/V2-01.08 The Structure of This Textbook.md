# V2-01.08 The Structure of This Textbook

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01 Introduction|V2-01 Introduction]]

^sec-vol2-introduction-structure

Meeting those three fleet challenges requires climbing the fleet stack in dependency order, because choices fixed in the physical substrate constrain every algorithm, runtime, and governance control built above it. The chapter sequence is therefore a dependency map rather than a topic inventory. Silicon, cooling, and network limits constrain training partitioning; partitioned algorithms create fault-tolerance and orchestration obligations; and those mechanisms support production operation. Requirements also flow in the other direction: security, governance, and service targets constrain which lower-layer designs are acceptable. Part I builds the physical cluster substrate, Part II coordinates distributed execution and fault recovery, Part III runs the fleet in production, from efficiency and serving through edge deployment and operations, and Part IV enforces governance, security, and sustainability invariants ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.08 The Structure of This Textbook#^fig-vol2-roadmap|figure 16]]).

![[90 Assets/Volume II/01 Introduction/Roadmap dfe87bf183.svg]]

Figure 16: **The Chapter Roadmap**: The chapters in reading order, top to bottom. The introduction frames the book; the four parts then climb the fleet stack of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.03 From Single Node to Fleet#^fig-fleet-stack|figure 4]] from physical infrastructure through distribution and production to governance, as the small stack beside each part shows by lighting that part’s band; the conclusion gathers the principles of all four parts.

^fig-vol2-roadmap

The infrastructure arc begins with a limit no algorithm can ignore, because no single server has enough memory, power, cooling, or I/O to train the largest models. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/02 Compute Infrastructure/V2-02 Compute Infrastructure#^sec-compute-infrastructure|Compute Infrastructure]]** therefore starts with high-density silicon and facility physics, **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/03 Network Fabrics/V2-03 Network Fabrics#^sec-network-fabrics|Network Fabrics]]** turns isolated machines into a cluster-scale gradient bus, and **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/04 Data Storage/V2-04 Data Storage#^sec-data-storage|Data Storage]]** completes the substrate by keeping accelerators fed without letting storage become the hidden bottleneck.

The distribution arc begins once the physical fleet exists and coordination costs become unavoidable. Splitting math across machines creates two new problems: replicas must exchange state fast enough to behave like one optimizer, and failures become routine rather than exceptional. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05 Collective Communication#^sec-collective-communication|Collective Communication]]** develops the communication primitives that bind independent nodes into a coherent computer and prices each exchange. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/06 Distributed Training/V2-06 Distributed Training#^sec-distributed-training-systems|Distributed Training]]** uses them to build partitioning strategies for models with hundreds of billions to trillions of parameters. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07 Fault Tolerance#^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability|Fault Tolerance]]** treats recovery speed as more important than individual-node uptime. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/08 Fleet Orchestration/V2-08 Fleet Orchestration#^sec-fleet-orchestration|Fleet Orchestration]]** manages the multi-tenant cluster where those jobs must be placed, isolated, and rescheduled.

The production arc puts the built fleet to work on real jobs and live traffic. Once a fleet runs in production, the binding question is no longer only time-to-train; it is whether utilization, latency, and lifetime serving cost stay inside the operating envelope. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/09 Fleet Efficiency/V2-09 Fleet Efficiency#^sec-performance-engineering|Fleet Efficiency]]** measures how much of the fleet’s capacity becomes useful work, in a training step and a serving replica alike, and charges each loss to scheduling, runtime, or the program. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/10 Serving at Scale/V2-10 Serving at Scale#^sec-inference-scale|Serving at Scale]]** turns each replica’s measured capacity into admission, batching, and routing policy for millions of users. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/11 Edge Intelligence/V2-11 Edge Intelligence#^sec-edge-intelligence|Edge Intelligence]]** moves learning onto phone-class devices whose memory, battery, and thermal budgets decide what adaptation they can afford. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/12 ML Operations at Scale/V2-12 ML Operations at Scale#^sec-ops-scale|ML Operations at Scale]]** develops the shared controls (registries, release gates, monitoring, and cost accounting) that govern hundreds of models as one fleet.

The governance arc treats responsibility as an engineering layer rather than an afterword. At global scale, technical bugs become societal hazards: adversaries can poison data or extract weights, open-world inputs can break brittle models, energy use becomes a fleet constraint, and governance failures can turn technical capability into social harm. **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/13 Security & Privacy/V2-13 Security & Privacy#^sec-security-privacy|Security & Privacy]]**, **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14 Robust AI#^sec-robust-ai|Robust AI]]**, **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/15 Sustainable AI/V2-15 Sustainable AI#^sec-sustainable-ai|Sustainable AI]]**, and **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/16 Responsible AI/V2-16 Responsible AI#^sec-responsible-ai|Responsible AI]]** develop those obligations as system properties that must be designed, measured, and operated.

    1.  Part I: The Responsible Fleet -\> Part II: The Fleet in Production -\> Part III: Distributed ML -\> Part IV: The Fleet; starting with high-level societal policies and deriving physical silicon top-down.
    2.  Part I: The Fleet in Production -\> Part II: The Fleet -\> Part III: Distributed ML -\> Part IV: The Responsible Fleet; prioritizing user-facing inference before hardware infrastructure.
    3.  Part I: Distributed ML -\> Part II: The Fleet -\> Part III: The Responsible Fleet -\> Part IV: The Fleet in Production; grouping software training algorithms before facility physics.
    4.  Part I: The Fleet -\> Part II: Distributed ML -\> Part III: The Fleet in Production -\> Part IV: The Responsible Fleet; following the fleet stack bottom-up from physical silicon and network fabrics to distribution logic, operational serving, and societal governance.
