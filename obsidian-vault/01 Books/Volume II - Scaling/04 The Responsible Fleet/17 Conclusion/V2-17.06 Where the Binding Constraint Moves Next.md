# V2-17.06 Where the Binding Constraint Moves Next

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/17 Conclusion/V2-17 Conclusion|V2-17 Conclusion]]

^sec-conclusion-path-forward-caa2

When a reasoning model spends seconds of test-time compute on a single user prompt or an optical interconnect replaces copper across a switching tier, that test of transfer applies directly. The binding constraint shifts again. The same six principles govern workloads and substrates that are only now emerging. Test-time compute, introduced among the scaling regimes in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.04 AI Scaling Laws#^sec-vol2-intro-resourceconstrained-scaling-regimes-062d|Resource-constrained scaling regimes]]**, already moves part of the scaling curve from the training cluster into the serving path, where **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/10 Serving at Scale/V2-10.07 Batching Strategies at Scale#^sec-inference-logic-wall|Test-time compute and the per-request budget]]** prices it as a per-request compute budget. Beneath the fleet, new interconnect and packaging substrates change the rates that every Fleet Law term inherits. Each shift relocates cost without exempting it from the Fleet Law.

Gains from scheduling, placement, and overlap depend on a physical fabric, so software and substrate choices must be evaluated together. Once software has removed avoidable coordination, the remaining frontier is the substrate that moves tokens, activations, and power through the fleet. For Archetype A, the pressure on that substrate comes from specific collective patterns rather than from scale in general. Tensor parallelism issues frequent collectives across partitioned tensors, and in mixture-of-experts (MoE) variants of such models, routing issues an AllToAll dispatch at each expert layer. Across thousands of accelerators, link energy accumulates with the volume of that collective traffic, which makes lower-energy interconnects a plausible fabric-efficiency lever.

Candidate substrates attack different factors of that physical account: energy per bit, path length, bandwidth density, or the distance between memory and compute. Optical I/O and co-packaged optics lower the energy required to move each bit across the fabric, while 3D integration extends the vertical stacking of HBM and advanced packaging to shorten interconnect paths. Neuromorphic event-driven substrates instead organize computation around sparse events such as spikes and keep state beside the compute units that use it. Automated architecture search and AI-for-systems optimization offer ways to explore candidate designs, but each proposal still needs a workload-specific comparison of useful work, data movement, power, and implementation cost.

↩︎ Neuromorphic and other non-von Neumann directions are established in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/15 Sustainable AI/V2-15.11 Policy, Regulation, and the Path Forward#^sec-sustainable-ai-future-challenges-58e2|Future research directions]]**.

A lower energy per bit reaches the fleet only in proportion to the links’ share of fleet power, the affected-share arithmetic of Amdahl’s law applied to energy. At fixed traffic, halving the energy per bit saves at most half of the links’ share of fleet power, not half of fleet power. A lower link-energy measurement alone therefore does not establish a better fleet. The optical dividend of **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/03 Network Fabrics/V2-03.11 Network Technologies That Move the Ceiling#^sec-network-fabrics-optical|Optical interconnects]]** sizes the optics power saving for one switch and for a cluster of switches, the starting point for that share calculation. Even a substrate that wins the energy comparison still needs fault tolerance and responsible governance before it improves the fleet.

Whatever the substrate, the discipline stays the same. An engineer chooses the unit of useful work, accounts for its full resource cost, and states which comparisons the evidence supports. Narrowing that boundary to a single component, layer, or benchmark in isolation produces the recurring failure modes of fleet design.

    4.  It replaces pretraining, so the training cluster can shrink by the same amount the serving fleet grows.
