# V2-14.12 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14 Robust AI|V2-14 Robust AI]]

^sec-robust-ai-summary-a274

Robust AI makes prediction quality an explicit engineering obligation alongside uptime and latency. Environmental shifts, adversarial manipulation, poisoning, and system faults can leave a service apparently healthy while its outputs become unreliable. The chapter’s diagnostic framework reads release timing, fixed-input replay, and infrastructure telemetry before accepting a drift or attack hypothesis, then connects each failure class to its own evidence and response. Fleet recovery mechanisms must therefore be paired with behavioral acceptance tests.

Inside the training step, robustness is paid for in the terms of the Fleet Law (**[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.06 The C³ Taxonomy and Foundations of Scale#^eq-fleet-law|equation 1.3]]**). Multi-step PGD adds gradient work in ${T_{\text{compute}}}$; at a fixed worker count ${N}$, the wall-clock increase depends on implementation, reuse, and the other terms in the step. Whether it also reduces clean accuracy, and by how much, depends on the model, data, perturbation set, and training recipe. Robust aggregation for distributed or federated contributors replaces a reducible mean with a median, trimmed mean, or Krum selection, which moves cost into ${T_{\text{comm}}}({N})$ because the individual updates must be gathered rather than summed.

Outside the training step the same C$^3$ axes set the price. Randomized smoothing and self-consistency sampling spend compute on every request they cover. Invariant checks and golden-input canaries spend compute and communication to catch faults that health probes miss. Poisoning sanitization spends compute and storage bandwidth on extra passes over the training corpus. Drift monitoring spends communication, shipping reference statistics and serving telemetry between machines. Release gates, abstention thresholds, and escalation ownership spend coordination, because validated model versions must be adopted consistently across the fleet.

Conformal routing touches all three axes: computing prediction sets adds bounded compute, sending requests with large sets to a stronger model adds communication, and routing abstentions to reviewers adds coordination. If prediction sets grow and trigger more deferrals, human-review or fallback capacity can become the bottleneck. Distribution shift can also invalidate coverage without enlarging the sets, so set size is a load signal rather than a complete drift detector. A coverage claim is operationally useful only when the deployment also provisions the path that consumes uncertain cases. Robustness engineering measures these workload-specific costs against the reduction in silent risk where failure consequences are highest.

**Key Takeaways: Silent failure is the real threat**

- **Silence is the failure mode**: Robustness exists because drift, adversarial perturbations, poisoning, and numerical faults often preserve uptime and latency while corrupting predictions. Production systems need monitors that surface competence loss before user complaints or downstream business metrics reveal it.
- **Diagnose before defending**: A software fault can look like covariate shift, a poisoned sample like a rare outlier, and an adversarial input like natural variation. Read the release timing, fixed-input replay, and infrastructure telemetry before spending a defense budget on the hypothesis the surface symptom suggests, then combine ingress validation, training defenses, uncertainty signals, and output verification.
- **Robustness is bought explicitly**: Strong adversarial training (principle [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/V2 Responsible Fleet Principles#^pri-robustness-compute-penalty|24]]) can reduce clean accuracy and multiply training compute, while certified defenses and uncertainty sampling add serving cost. Price each effect against a like-for-like baseline before deciding how much resilience the failure consequence justifies, and claim robustness only for the evaluated attacker access and perturbation budget.
- **Poisoning defense follows the data path**: Poisoned records can leave clean aggregate accuracy intact, so no single detector suffices. Layer source controls, sanitization, robust objectives, and post-training behavioral checks, and keep lineage so every admission decision can be traced and reversed.
- **Drift alerts begin an investigation**: Calibrated distance measures identify changes that need review but can stay silent under concept drift that leaves the input distribution unchanged, so outcome evidence must separate a change in label frequencies from a change in the input-to-label relationship. The shift type and root-cause analysis then determine whether the response should be continued monitoring, data repair, recalibration, retraining, or fallback.
- **Per-request contracts have operating conditions**: Raising a selective-prediction threshold lowers risk only if the score ranks requests by error risk, and conformal prediction provides finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability, not per-input or per-subgroup certainty. Version and refresh the calibration evidence, and provision fallback capacity for rejected requests and large prediction sets.
- **Generative reliability is semantic**: Large language model hallucinations are confidently fluent failures rather than simple label mistakes. Entropy and self-consistency are routing signals, not factuality guarantees; grounding checks, external verification, and escalation policies must bound what the system is allowed to assert.

^callout-takeaways--14-12-dd997b65

No finite evaluation establishes robustness to every shock. A deployable claim must bind behavioral evidence to the evaluated artifact and operating conditions, then provision the detection and fallback paths without violating service-level objectives (SLOs). The release record in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/12 ML Operations at Scale/V2-12.06 CI CD for ML at Scale#^sec-ops-scale-end-to-end-evaluation-contract|The end-to-end evaluation contract]]** preserves that link so a changed model or workload cannot silently inherit obsolete evidence. Governance cites the resulting record as evidence in an assurance case, a versioned argument that a model is fit for a stated use. Governance uses that case to assign acceptable residual risk and decision authority; a robustness score alone cannot make those decisions.

↪ The assurance lifecycle that turns such evidence into launch and stop decisions is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/16 Responsible AI/V2-16.03 The Governance and Assurance Lifecycle#^sec-responsible-ai-governance-assurance-lifecycle|The Governance and Assurance Lifecycle]]**.

**What’s Next: From resilience to sustainability**

Even when a fleet is secure and robust, it can still be bounded by the electrical power its accelerators demand. Every defense in this chapter is paid for in compute. Adversarial training multiplies gradient work, randomized smoothing and self-consistency sampling repeat inference, and replay harnesses and redundant checks run beside the serving path. That compute draws electricity and rejects heat, and a facility’s power and cooling capacity bounds how much of it the fleet can run. The next chapter treats those limits as design constraints, beginning with the energy ceiling on fleet capacity and extending to operational and embodied carbon and carbon-aware operation.
