# V2-14.03 Real-World Robustness Failures

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14 Robust AI|V2-14 Robust AI]]

^sec-robust-ai-realworld-robustness-failures-c119

A shared storage outage stops a service outright, whereas a perception or classification error leaves it running with an unsafe result. Separating those two outcomes ensures that a successful request or an operational processor is never mistaken for evidence of a valid prediction. While deliberate input-level attacks are best documented through the controlled field studies of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.06 Adversarial Attacks#^sec-robust-ai-inputlevel-attacks-model-robustness-d6ea|section 14.5]], operational failures in the field expose the other pillars across three deployment tiers. Cloud fleets reveal silent hardware corruption during training and harmful errors on population slices, subsets of inputs such as a demographic group or an operating condition, hidden inside aggregate accuracy. Edge vehicles show perception failing outside its validated operating envelope with no time for a remote fallback. Embedded controllers show the hazard a learned component inherits when a false sensor signal drives an irreversible physical action. Even in a healthy cloud service, a model can fail silently on a critical population slice while its aggregate benchmark metrics look strong.

**War Story 14.1: The label that exposed the test gap (2015)**

**Context**: In June 2015, Google Photos used automatic image tagging to organize user photos ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-kasperkevic2015googlephotos|Kasperkevic 2015]]).

**Mechanism**: A deep convolutional image classifier placed Jacky Alciné’s photos of himself and a Black friend under the album tag “Gorillas,” a severe slice-level failure mode. The failure demonstrates why aggregate top-1/top-5 accuracy over a full test set cannot establish acceptable behavior for high-risk population slices.

**Impact**: The high-visibility misclassification caused public outrage and exposed a major gap in multi-class vision model safety validation.

**Fix**: Google disabled the “gorilla” label, blocking the symptom without demonstrating that the classifier itself had been corrected.

**Systems lesson**: Production ML robustness cannot be defined by aggregate test set accuracy. High-risk classification tasks require slice-level evaluation and a serving response to harmful errors. Automated toxicity guards and validated fallbacks are candidate controls whose coverage must be tested against the specific failure.

### Cloud Infrastructure Failures

^sec-robust-ai-cloud-infrastructure-failures-1c8c

Cloud services rest on a hidden assumption that a shared dependency remains both available and correct. The 2017 AWS S3 outage shows the familiar half of that assumption, in which a shared storage dependency failed and otherwise healthy services stopped.[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.15 Footnotes#^fn2|²]] ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-aws2017s3outage|Amazon Web Services 2017]]) An outage at least announces itself. The robustness problem is the other half, when the dependency stays up but returns plausible, corrupted data, so the service keeps answering while incorrect results propagate.

Silent data corruption (SDC), in which hardware returns a wrong value without raising any error, is that case. The decompression incident analyzed in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.06 Check-And-Verify Defenses Against Silent Data Corruption#^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability-physics-silent-corruption-5d5d|The physics of silent corruption]]** demonstrates the failure signature. A defective CPU returned zero during a size computation for a valid file, causing the decompression pipeline to omit files from its output even though system and kernel logs showed no fault ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-dixit2021silent|Dixit et al. 2021]]). The same section’s training illustration (**[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.06 Check-And-Verify Defenses Against Silent Data Corruption#^fig-sdc-example|Silent Data Corruption Propagation]]**) traces how unhandled corruption can enter gradients, weights, and later checkpoints. In distributed ML training, a corrupted gradient or activation can perturb optimizer state, spread to every replica through gradient synchronization, and persist into later checkpoints. Its blast radius therefore grows with the synchronization and checkpointing design, and recovery may require rollback to the last clean checkpoint ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-dixit2021silent|Dixit et al. 2021]]; [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-ma2025understanding|Ma et al. 2025]]). In both settings, checking that execution continues is insufficient; evaluation must test the validity of the resulting data or model behavior.

↩︎ Silent data corruption during training and its fleet-scale evidence are established in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.06 Check-And-Verify Defenses Against Silent Data Corruption#^sec-ft-sdc-verification-fleet-scale|Verification at fleet scale]]**.

### Edge and Embedded Failures

^sec-robust-ai-edge-device-vulnerabilities-ddfe

A cloud training job that detects silent corruption can roll back to a clean checkpoint, whereas an edge system acting in the physical world cannot undo a control decision once issued. In May 2016, a Tesla Model S operating on Autopilot failed to identify a tractor-trailer crossing its path and struck it at highway speed with neither warning nor braking ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-ntsb2017tesla|National Transportation Safety Board 2017]]). The system had not been validated for that scene, and nothing in its own behavior signaled that it had left its validated envelope. Edge deployments must respond within local compute, power, and connectivity limits, and in a vehicle, a perception or planning failure can require intervention before a remote service or human operator can respond. Vehicle safety therefore depends on the conditions the system can handle locally and the fallback available when those conditions no longer hold.

↩︎ The 2016 crash, read as an edge model whose fallback was a supervising driver, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/03 The Fleet in Production/11 Edge Intelligence/V2-11.12 Engineering Challenges and Mitigations#^sec-edge-intelligence-production-deployment-risk-assessment-db49|Compliance, security, and recovery risks in production]]**.

Even when sensors detect an obstacle, unstable predictions across frames can defeat downstream tracking and human fallback. In March 2018 in Tempe, Arizona, an Uber self-driving test vehicle struck and killed a pedestrian ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-ntsb2019uber|National Transportation Safety Board 2019]]). The system detected her 5.6 seconds before impact, but its classification of her was unstable. Repeated reclassification among “vehicle,” “other,” and “bicycle” caused path prediction to discard tracking history. The final investigation placed recognition of the imminent collision at 1.2 seconds before impact. The system then suppressed action for one second without alerting the operator; only afterward did it issue an alert and plan gradual slowdown. The operator did not intervene in time. The case connects perception, tracking, response timing, and human fallback, and it shows that detecting an object did not establish that the system would act safely on that detection.

An inspection drone surveying power lines may likewise lose reliable localization when lighting changes or repetitive textures confuse visual odometry. An alternate inference or control path provides **fallback redundancy** when the primary path cannot support the required decision, but its value depends on independent failure coverage and response time. A local sensor-based controller or validated landing mode may be more useful than a remote model that cannot answer before the control deadline. Edge AI must provision that response within the device’s resource limits; a data center’s additional replicas would not by themselves correct the same perception error.

Embedded systems can operate through critical intervals in which external intervention is too slow. Remote maintenance may remain possible between those intervals, but an autonomous landing or control maneuver needs a local response before its deadline. Validation must therefore cover both the component’s failure modes and the system behavior that follows, including any ML model in the decision loop. NASA’s Mars Polar Lander was lost in 1999 when, according to its review board, a spurious touchdown indication likely shut down the engines before landing ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-nasa2000mpl|NASA Mars Program Independent Assessment Team 2000]]). For a learned component in such a control loop, validation must connect plausible input and sensor failures to the actions they can trigger, because exhaustive testing of every possible input is not a feasible safety argument.

Commercial aviation shows the same inherited hazard. A 2015 FAA airworthiness directive followed Boeing’s discovery that a 787 powered continuously for 248 days could lose all AC power if all four generator control units entered failsafe mode at once,[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.15 Footnotes#^fn3|³]] so uptime itself became the risk factor ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.14 Source References#^ref-faa2015boeing|Federal Aviation Administration 2015]]). An endurance test shorter than that interval would not have exposed the defect. A learned component inherits the same hazard whenever deployment reaches a condition its evaluation never covered, such as accumulated state, a rare season, or an unusual sensor reading. Safety-critical systems[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/04 The Responsible Fleet/14 Robust AI/V2-14.15 Footnotes#^fn4|⁴]] impose stringent reliability requirements precisely because such latent hazards surface only in the field. Across these settings, robustness depends on connecting the failure signal to a bounded response, and the fleet framework must distinguish the source of that signal before choosing a defense.

1.  During the February 2017 AWS S3 outage, dependent services such as EC2 launches, EBS snapshot-dependent volumes, and Lambda experienced elevated error rates. Which design assumption does this incident most directly invalidate for robust ML systems?

    1.  That deep neural networks are too computationally intensive for cloud object stores and must be compressed.
    2.  That adversarial inputs are the dominant cause of availability loss in production inference services.
    3.  That regional cloud storage availability can be treated as an absolute invariant rather than a probabilistic dependency in the serving pipeline.
    4.  That distributed training clusters must enforce Byzantine-tolerant gradient aggregation to prevent worker dropouts.

2.  The chapter describes a defective CPU that returned zero in a file-size calculation, causing a decompression pipeline to omit files without raising a fault. Explain how an analogous undetected error can persist and propagate through distributed ML training, and why its blast radius depends on synchronization and checkpointing.

3.  In the March 2018 Uber ATG pedestrian fatality in Tempe, the vehicle perception stack detected the pedestrian 5.6 seconds prior to impact but did not recognize the imminent collision until 1.2 seconds before impact. Which perception and tracking mechanism described in the chapter contributed to that delay?

    1.  Object classification instability, where the model toggled between ‘vehicle’, ‘other’, and ‘bicycle’, resetting the trajectory prediction history on each reclassification.
    2.  Complete radar transceiver hardware failure that left the vehicle with zero sensor measurements.
    3.  A sudden geographic covariate shift that rendered the obstacle detection model unable to process Arizona road layouts.
    4.  A cloud network disconnection that prevented the on-vehicle computer from receiving remote classification confirmations.

4.  True or False: During a safety-critical embedded maneuver such as autonomous landing, a sensor or software fault can cause irreversible harm before remote intervention, so validation must cover both the fault and the local response within the maneuver’s deadline.

5.  Order the stages of a silent data corruption cascade in a distributed ML system from initial hardware fault to final production failure: (1) Contaminated parameters persist through optimizer updates and are saved into a checkpoint artifact, (2) An undetected bit flip occurs in the ALU or memory during forward-backward computation on a single worker node, (3) The deployed model serves degraded or erratic predictions while standard latency and uptime health checks stay green, (4) Corrupted gradients are broadcast and aggregated across all workers during AllReduce synchronization.
