*Why can machine learning systems fail silently even when conventional health signals remain normal?*

A model service can maintain stable latency and uptime while its predictions become unreliable. Changes in operating conditions, manipulated inputs, or corrupted computation may leave ordinary health signals untouched, allowing errors to affect decisions long before business metrics expose them. Conventional software can also fail silently; machine learning adds statistical failures that a successful request cannot reveal. At fleet scale, replicating a model across healthy machines does not remove a weakness shared by every copy. Traffic also spans different regions, devices, and user populations, so acceptable average performance can conceal a serious failure in one group. The decision to continue answering therefore requires evidence about the conditions under which the predictions remain useful, not merely confirmation that the service is available. Producing that evidence consumes resources, and responding to it creates further obligations. Additional checks can delay answers; rejecting uncertain requests can overwhelm reviewers or a backup service. A replacement model must demonstrate acceptable behavior before the fleet adopts it, while waiting too long can prolong exposure to errors. The engineering challenge is to connect evidence to a bounded response without exhausting the resources needed to serve useful predictions. Protection must cover the model and the surrounding data path, because either can invalidate the result. In C³ terms, verification spends compute and communication, while coordination determines when the evidence should trigger investigation, fallback, or a halt.

**Learning Objectives**

- Classify a silent failure by robustness pillar and diagnose its source using input replay, deployment history, and system telemetry
- Distinguish covariate shift, label shift, and concept drift, and judge when drift evidence justifies a model update
- Apply a risk-coverage curve to choose a selective-prediction threshold and size its fallback load, and contrast it with calibration and with conformal coverage under exchangeability
- Evaluate adversarial attacks by attacker access, and select defenses such as adversarial training, certification, and input detection
- Select layered poisoning defenses that combine source controls, anomaly detection, robust training, and post-training checks
- Evaluate generative-model reliability against a named evidence source, treating entropy, self-consistency, and output guardrails as priced routing signals
- Evaluate robustness designs against clean accuracy, compute, latency, and fallback-capacity budgets
