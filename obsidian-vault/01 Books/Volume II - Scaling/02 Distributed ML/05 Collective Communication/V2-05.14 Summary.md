# V2-05.14 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05 Collective Communication|V2-05 Collective Communication]]

^sec-communication-collective-operations-summary

Every mechanism in this chapter works on the communication term ${T_{\text{comm}}}({N})$ of the Fleet Law (**[[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V2-01.06 The C³ Taxonomy and Foundations of Scale#^eq-fleet-law|equation 1.3]]**), either shrinking it through better schedules, placement, and smaller payloads or enlarging ${T_{\text{overlap}}}$ so that less of it is exposed. Each acts on the Communication axis of C³ so that added compute does not sit waiting. The mechanisms compose into one ordered judgment that starts with the collective contract. The primitive whose output the next computation consumes fixes which ranks exchange data and what each holds afterward, and no faster schedule can recover bytes spent delivering a broader result than the model needs. Once the contract is fixed, the ${\alpha}$-${\beta}$ model, the critical size ${n^*} = {\alpha}\cdot {\beta}$, and the ring-tree crossover ${M_{\text{crossover}}}$ price each candidate schedule round by round on its per-step chunk rather than on the whole tensor.

A schedule priced on a flat network realizes that cost only when hierarchy, rail alignment, and rank order map the logical exchange onto the physical links. Compression and overlap then determine how much of the remaining transfer reaches the critical path. LogP separates the network transit time that overlap can hide from the processor overhead it cannot, and every candidate is judged by time to the same quality target rather than isolated collective speed. A model calibrated against the same collective, process group, and topology closes the loop by separating a schedule’s inherent traffic from library or placement loss.

**Key Takeaways: Every byte has a travel cost**

- **Contract first, then price per round**: Choose the narrowest primitive whose output the next computation consumes, then price its schedule round by round. Each round pays the startup cost on a chunk, so the critical size ${n^*} = {\alpha}\cdot {\beta}$ (principle [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/V2 Fleet Principles#^pri-alpha-beta-model|5]]) classifies the chunk rather than the tensor. Collective duration also depends on round count and traffic volume; fit parameters with a benchmark that matches the workload’s collective and topology.
- **Algorithm choice is scale-dependent**: Ring AllReduce is bandwidth-optimal but pays $\mathcal{O}({N})$ latency; the simple full-message Tree model uses fewer rounds ($\mathcal{O}(\log {N})$) but more bandwidth work. Use the log-aware crossover estimate ${M_{\text{crossover}}} \approx {N}{\alpha}{\beta}/ \log_2 {N}$ to reason about the switch point.
- **Hierarchy exposes parallel network capacity**: Local reductions over fast NVLink leave smaller shards for the slow InfiniBand tier. The effective bandwidth gain requires independent port capacity for concurrent shard exchanges; splitting a payload cannot multiply the capacity of a shared uplink.
- **AllToAll schedule selection follows the active bound**: Direct exchange preserves simplicity for balanced coarse chunks, hierarchy amortizes startup across scarce inter-node links, and variable-size exchange removes padding under skew. Injection bandwidth, bisection capacity, oversubscription, message granularity, and the hottest receiver determine which schedule wins.
- **Error feedback controls lossy compression error**: Sparsification can discard 99 percent of each update while accumulating the residual locally and adding it before the next compression step. Under the analyzed assumptions, this bounds accumulated error and restores convergence guarantees even though individual updates can remain biased, but compression pays only if it shortens time to the same quality target.
- **Overlap is limited by remaining independent work**: Gradient hooks and bucket fusion let communication run during later backward computations, but the final transfer and any communication backlog remain exposed. Evaluate the resulting step time, not model depth alone (principle [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/V2 Distributed ML Principles#^pri-distributed-step-time|10]]).
- **Placement determines usable bandwidth**: Logical collective schedules share physical links according to rank placement and routing (principle [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/01 The Fleet/V2 Fleet Principles#^pri-bisection-bandwidth|6]]). A matched benchmark must record that placement; changing the mapping can change the bottleneck even when the algorithm and hardware stay fixed.

^callout-takeaways--5-14-37279bc0

If parallelism decides what must be communicated, collective communication measures what that communication costs. Every collective, whatever its contract, is priced first by the same two terms, a startup latency paid once per round and a transfer time paid per byte. The useful optimization is the one that shortens the training dependency path while preserving the required result.

**What’s Next: From traffic to partitioning**

The fleet now has its traffic patterns and a price for each of them: a startup latency per round, a transfer time per byte, and a placement that decides which links carry the load. Which exchanges a training step actually issues, how large they are, and how often they run all follow from how the model and its data are divided among accelerators. The next chapter makes that division explicit. It partitions training across data, tensor, pipeline, and expert dimensions and uses the costs derived here to judge when each partition repays the communication it creates.
