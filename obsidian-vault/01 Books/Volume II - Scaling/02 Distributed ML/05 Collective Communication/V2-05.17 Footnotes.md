# V2-05.17 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05 Collective Communication|V2-05 Collective Communication]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **AllReduce**: The name comes from MPI and describes the result, not the schedule: a Reduce (combining every rank’s data at one root) followed by a Broadcast (returning the combined value to all ranks). A direct gather to the root concentrates raw input traffic there, but a tree can aggregate contributions along the way. The algorithms in [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.07 AllReduce Algorithms and Topologies#^sec-communication-collective-operations-collective-operations-ring-allreduce-ffce|section 5.6.3]] distribute the reduction without a root.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Ring AllReduce**: The algorithm dates to the HPC collective-communication literature; Patarasuk and Yuan analyzed bandwidth-optimal AllReduce algorithms for workstation clusters, Baidu’s Andrew Gibiansky popularized ring AllReduce for deep learning in February 2017, and Horovod plus NVIDIA NCCL helped make it a common distributed-training primitive ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.16 Source References#^ref-patarasuk2009|Patarasuk and Yuan 2009]]; [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.16 Source References#^ref-gibiansky2017baidu|Gibiansky 2017]]; [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.16 Source References#^ref-sergeev2018horovod|Sergeev and Balso 2018]]; [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.16 Source References#^ref-jeaugey2017nccl|Jeaugey 2017]]).

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **The speed-of-light constraint**: Light travels through optical fiber at approximately 200,000 km/s, or 200 meters per microsecond. For a rack-to-rack fiber path of 100 meters, propagation alone contributes approximately 500 ns in one direction. Faster switches can reduce other latency components, but reducing this propagation delay requires a shorter path or a different propagation medium.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Zero-copy communication**: GPUDirect RDMA lets the NIC read gradients directly from accelerator memory, so a 140 GB gradient never passes through host DRAM, whereas a staged path writes and then rereads it there (280 GB of host-memory traffic per send).

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **DLRM (Deep Learning Recommendation Model)**: Meta’s 2019 architecture for click-through rate prediction uses sharded embedding tables and exchanges the resulting sparse lookups across workers, an AllToAll whose limiting term [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.09 AllToAll Exchanges at Scale#^sec-communication-alltoall|section 5.8]] develops.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **SHARP (Scalable Hierarchical Aggregation and Reduction Protocol)**: In-network computing on Quantum InfiniBand switches that performs reduction operations (such as sum, min, and max) as packets traverse an aggregation tree ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/05 Collective Communication/V2-05.16 Source References#^ref-graham2020|Graham et al. 2020]]). The same work reports substantially higher reduction bandwidth than host-based algorithms and describes switch-resource limits on outstanding aggregation groups and trees, which can create contention when many collectives compete for the same in-network resources.

^fn6
