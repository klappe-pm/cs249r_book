# V2-07.16 Case Studies

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/Volume 2 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07 Fault Tolerance|V2-07 Fault Tolerance]]

^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability-reliability-case-studies-927f

Training a 175-billion-parameter language model across 992 GPUs for two months, keeping a 4,096-chip supercomputer slice routable despite daily hardware faults, and serving low-latency recommendations across dozens of microservices each stress a different layer of the fault-tolerance stack. Public operational reports from Meta, Google, and Netflix show how production fleets combine failure detection, state preservation, topology reconfiguration, and chaos testing at scale. Their reported numbers are operational magnitudes that expose the design pressure on each system rather than universal hardware constants.

### Large-Scale LLM Training at Meta

^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability-reliability-largescale-llm-training-fault-tolerance-f4aa

Over two months of training the Open Pre-trained Transformer (OPT-175B) on 992 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, Meta’s engineering team reported at least 35 manual restarts and estimated 70 or more automatic restarts triggered by hardware failures including ECC memory errors, NCCL and InfiniBand network faults, and lost GPUs ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-zhang2022opt|Zhang et al. 2022]]). Alongside these restarts, more than 100 hosts were cycled ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-zhang2022opt|Zhang et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-opt2022logbook|Meta AI Research 2022]]). These counts establish repeated interruption, but cycling hosts and restarting jobs are different events; their totals do not directly measure one common MTBF. Numerical instability created a separate recovery problem. The recovery design had to preserve progress while distinguishing failed execution resources from suspect optimization state.

During the OPT run, the team monitored progress through train-log freshness checks. The public logbook records a 15-minute modified-file threshold, later relaxed to one hour, as part of restart monitoring ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-opt2022logbook|Meta AI Research 2022]]). For a hang that raised no error, this threshold contributed to ${T_{\text{detect}}}$; polling and response delays could extend the wait before restart. The logbook illustrates the trade-off of [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.10 Failure Detection and Recovery#^eq-timeout-calculation|equation 11]] in operational form, since relaxing the threshold reduced sensitivity to delayed logs while allowing longer idle windows.

Recovery procedures also had to account for loss spikes associated with numerical instability. Unlike a fail-stop event, this symptom made recent model state suspect without identifying a failed device. The team combined rollback to an earlier checkpoint with a lower learning rate to recover from divergence ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-opt2022logbook|Meta AI Research 2022]]). The paper separately reports approximately 147 TFLOP/s per A100 (about 47 percent of dense FP16 peak) ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-zhang2022opt|Zhang et al. 2022]]); that execution-rate metric alone does not establish end-to-end useful throughput after interruptions and replay. The enduring lesson is the distinction between recovery paths. A failed device calls for replacement and restoration of valid state, while numerical divergence can require an older checkpoint and a changed optimization policy. Retaining checkpoint history makes that second path possible.

### Google TPU Pod Resilience

^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability-reliability-google-tpu-pod-resilience

Google’s TPUv4 supercomputer combines configurable topology with recovery software. A pod contains 4,096 chips organized as 64 cubes of 64 chips, each arranged in a $4{\times}4{\times}4$ 3D mesh ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-zu2024tpuv4|Zu et al. 2024]]). Losing one chip removes $1/4096$th of pod capacity, but can interrupt an entire job that requires that participant. The recovery unit therefore exceeds the failed device.

War story [7.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol2/07_fault_tolerance/07_fault_tolerance.html#ws-fault-tolerance-google-tpuv4-routing) traced the recovery mechanism, in which the OCS fabric assembles healthy cubes into job-sized slices, reallocating spare cubes after machine outages and routing around unavailable links for supported OCS outage patterns. The published study adds the software that runs that loop, components including `libtpunet` and `healthd` for topology setup and health monitoring. It also gives the operating statistics that show how often the loop runs. System availability was 99.98 percent, with hardware outages affecting approximately 1 percent of training jobs ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-zu2024tpuv4|Zu et al. 2024]]). Daily failure rates were 0.08 percent of TPU machines, 0.005 percent of ICI cables, and 0.04 percent of OCS units. Small daily fractions still add up across thousands of components, so the control plane absorbs a steady stream of reconfiguration events rather than an occasional emergency. The architectural lesson is to match each failure boundary to a feasible replacement or routing mechanism.

### Netflix Chaos Engineering for Serving Dependencies

^sec-fault-tolerance-reliability-reliability-netflix-chaos-engineering

While training resilience focuses on long-running batch jobs, Netflix’s published work on Chaos Engineering ([[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.20 Source References#^ref-basiri2016|Basiri et al. 2016]]) provides the serving-side contrast, and it is the production origin of the injection discipline that [[01 Books/Volume II - Scaling/02 Distributed ML/07 Fault Tolerance/V2-07.15 Fault Injection Tools and Frameworks#^sec-ft-fault-injection-tools-frameworks|section 7.14]] applied to training. The premise is simple but operationally demanding. Because production systems have many interacting dependencies, teams should validate steady-state behavior by deliberately injecting failures instead of waiting for real incidents to discover whether fallbacks work.

For ML serving, the same discipline applies to feature stores, model-serving dependencies, caches, and fallback paths. A model endpoint can be healthy while a feature service is slow, stale, or unavailable; if the system lacks a tested fallback, a local dependency problem can become user-visible latency or degraded recommendations. Chaos tests make those assumptions executable by injecting latency, terminating dependencies, or forcing fallback paths in controlled conditions.

The recovery procedure for these injected faults centers on fallback hierarchies. If the primary deep learning model fails or times out, the serving system can switch to a lighter model, cached response, or simple popularity-based default. The systems lesson is that resilience in ML serving is not a static property but a continuous practice of active verification. A fallback path that is never exercised is documentation, not fault tolerance.

Across these production case studies, three architectural patterns recur. First, detection speed belongs in the recovery budget. Meta’s log-freshness threshold and Google’s health monitors determine when recovery can begin, while Netflix’s chaos experiments test detection and fallback for selected dependency failures. Checkpoint age and replication lag separately govern recoverable state. Second, the atomic unit of failure shapes the architecture. Google combines cube replacement with fault-tolerant routing, the OPT run restarted from durable checkpoints and kept their history for numerical rollback, and Netflix validates service-dependency fallbacks. Third, fault tolerance is a spectrum rather than a switch. Exact state recovery trades normal-operation overhead against lost work and recovery time; graceful degradation additionally trades service capability against continued availability. Those distinct trade-offs help identify the misconceptions that follow.

**Self-Check: Question**

1.  Why does Google’s TPUv4 recovery design combine OCS-based replacement of unhealthy cubes with fault-tolerant routing for supported link-outage patterns?

    1.  Because TPU chips have zero local memory and cannot execute software routing algorithms.
    2.  Because optical circuit switches are cheaper than standard copper Ethernet cables for intra-rack links.
    3.  A machine outage can remove a required job participant, requiring a healthy replacement cube; a link outage can instead require a supported alternate communication path. The response must preserve the job’s communication topology.
    4.  Because OCS switches completely eliminate the need for checkpointing during multi-month training jobs.

2.  During the training of Meta’s OPT-175B model on 992 A100 GPUs, describe how the team addressed both physical hardware failures and mathematical loss spikes.

3.  What fundamental principle of ML serving resilience is demonstrated by Netflix’s chaos engineering practice?

    1.  Resilience is an active practice of continuous verification: intentionally injecting latency, terminating feature caches, and simulating dependency outages under controlled conditions to empirically verify that fallback hierarchies function before real outages occur.
    2.  Fault tolerance code should only be executed during unplanned production disasters to minimize server load.
    3.  Machine learning models should never rely on external feature stores or databases.
    4.  Chaos testing is only applicable to CPU-based web servers and cannot be used in GPU inference clusters.

4.  Google’s TPUv4 study reports daily failure rates of 0.08 percent of TPU machines, 0.005 percent of ICI cables, and 0.04 percent of OCS units. Explain why such small daily fractions still require an automated reconfiguration control plane rather than manual repair.
