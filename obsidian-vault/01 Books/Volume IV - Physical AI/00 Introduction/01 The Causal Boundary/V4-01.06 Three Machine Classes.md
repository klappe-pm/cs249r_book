# V4-01.06 Three Machine Classes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01 The Causal Boundary|V4-01 The Causal Boundary]]

^sec-boundary-three-classes

As the warehouse mobile manipulator travels down an aisle, its primary engineering challenge is kinetic: preserving enough clearance to stop safely before an obstacle. When the same robot presses against a door latch, the governing constraint shifts to contact mechanics, regulating interaction forces to avoid damaging the mechanism or the workpiece. When holding a suspended payload stationary, the machine performs zero mechanical work, yet its actuators must sustain continuous stall torque without exhausting thermal headroom or browning out the shared electrical supply. Because each phase of the task spends a different physical margin, the book names three machine classes by the margin each one spends ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.06 Three Machine Classes#^fig-01-three-archetypes|figure 5]]).

The first class, **mass-dominated mobility** (Class 1), spends stopping clearance. A mobile base, delivery vehicle, or aerial drone carries significant momentum and cannot halt instantaneously. Forward velocity, control and sensing latency, and available braking deceleration or reverse thrust jointly dictate the spatial clearance required to arrest or redirect its motion.

The second class, **contact-dominated manipulation** (Class 2), spends force and deformation margin. When an articulated arm presses against a stiff fixture, minute position displacements can generate destructive contact forces over fractions of a millimeter. Physical contact is both the means of task success (seating a part or opening a latch requires deliberate force) and the immediate hazard. The engineering task is to supply the wrenches (the combined forces and torques) needed to complete the assembly while guaranteeing that contact forces never exceed the mechanical yield limits of the payload or the manipulator.

The third class, **energy- and thermal-dominated operation** (Class 3), spends heat and electrical supply margins. Supporting a heavy load or resisting gravity demands sustained motor current, driving continuous Joule heating in actuator windings even when joints remain stationary. Concurrent peak current draws from drive inverters, compute accelerators, and sensor payloads can induce voltage sag across a shared DC bus. The system must sustain these thermal and electrical loads across the entire operational profile while reserving sufficient energy and thermal headroom to execute a safe protective abort.

^fig-01-three-archetypes

These three classes identify dominant physical constraints rather than mutually exclusive hardware categories. A practical robot transitions across all three regimes throughout a single task. The running machine integrates a wheeled base, an articulated arm, navigation lidar, and wrist cameras. An end-to-end learned policy can coordinate simultaneous base trajectories and arm proposals ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-fu2024mobile|Fu et al. 2024]]), but the integrated machine must enforce the safety envelopes of every regime concurrently. A fixed-base arm still faces thermal and bus-voltage limits during prolonged holding, just as a wheeled base encounters contact constraints when docking against a workstation.

**Systems Perspective 1.1: The humanoid crucible**

A humanoid robot exposes the tight coupling across all three regimes. Catching a shifted load perturbs dynamic balance, requiring an immediate recovery step that demands peak joint torque. That torque surge draws heavy current from the shared DC bus and injects rapid thermal energy into the joint windings. If the power distribution network sags or thermal protection limits current, the recovery step fails and the machine falls. Shielding the onboard computing stack from reset does not by itself supply the transient mechanical energy needed to stabilize the body. The part-ending humanoid examples throughout this book test how each systems principle survives these tightly coupled physical demands.

↪ The multi-contact balance mechanics a humanoid’s recovery step depends on are developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-centroidal|Centroidal Dynamics and Floating-Base Control]]**.

Identifying whether a task spends stopping clearance, contact force, or thermal headroom tells the engineer which physical budget binds, regardless of how the controller is implemented. Whether the machine also requires the proposal-and-permission architecture of physical AI, rather than a classical feedback loop or an offline tool, depends on a separate test of how its decisions are generated and authorized.
