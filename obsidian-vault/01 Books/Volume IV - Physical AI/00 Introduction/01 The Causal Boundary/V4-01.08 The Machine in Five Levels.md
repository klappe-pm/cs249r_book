# V4-01.08 The Machine in Five Levels

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01 The Causal Boundary|V4-01 The Causal Boundary]]

^sec-boundary-machine-model

When the running machine’s chunk policy stalls mid-aisle, something other than the policy must still be able to stop the base. When the policy emits a chunk that would carry the arm through a coworker’s hand, something other than the policy must refuse it. An embodied machine therefore needs an architecture that prevents learned models from writing directly to the actuators they could damage, while still letting those models guide physical behavior.

The Body establishes what motion, force, and mechanical power the hardware can physically sustain. The Brain generates learned proposals intended to operate within those capabilities. Mediating between them, the Nervous System coordinates communication and control, preserving both the temporal margin and the hardware authority required to intercept and refuse an inadmissible command. Adding the interface where commands become current and the environment the machine acts on gives a five-level model that runs from learned computation down to the physical world:

1.  **The Brain (Level 1):** The learned models that interpret sensory observations and propose candidate goals, trajectories, or torques. Generating a proposed trajectory or sequence of setpoints confers no intrinsic authority to execute it.
2.  **The Nervous System (Level 2):** The deterministic communication buses, real-time schedulers, and control loops that bridge proposals to physical execution. Its permission path evaluates, filters, or rejects proposals against fresh sensory evidence and invariant physical limits, and it executes a fallback shown feasible from the current state under the stated sensing, plant, and response-time assumptions when the proposer falters.
3.  **The Body (Level 3):** The physical plant, including motor drives, transmissions, electromagnetic windings, structural linkages, and energy storage, that delivers mechanical action.
4.  **The Boundary (Level 4):** The hard physical and electrical interface where digital registers translate into actuator voltages or currents, and where physical phenomena register across sensor transducers as raw measurements.
5.  **The World (Level 5):** The external physical environment (surfaces, payloads, obstacles, ambient lighting, and human collaborators), which evolves continuously under the laws of physics regardless of whether the machine measures it.

The list orders the levels by abstraction, from learned computation down to physics, not along the path a command travels. A command leaves the Nervous System, crosses the Boundary at a register write, and only then drives current through the Body’s windings. The Boundary is listed after the Body because it gathers the transducers at both edges of the machine, the register writes that energize the windings and the sensor captures that turn light, force, and motion into numbers. Those are the places where the machine exchanges energy and information with the World. The actuation half of this level is the causal boundary defined earlier in the chapter. The proposal boundary, defined below, is not a level at all. It is the line between the Brain and the Nervous System that separates proposing from permitting.

A concrete physical AI implementation often maps this hierarchy across distinct computational tiers. Learned proposers run on high-throughput accelerator hosts (such as an application processor paired with a GPU or neural processing unit). The permission path executes on an independent real-time microcontroller or dedicated real-time operating system (RTOS) core. The current loops that regulate motor-phase current run directly on dedicated motor drives. This architectural isolation is rooted in strict timing guarantees and fault-containment boundaries rather than specific silicon families or nominal operating system labels.

↪ The loaded test of the two-processor split that realizes this isolation is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.08 Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation#^sec-placement-testing-separation|Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation]]**.

Governance does not constitute a sixth level in this real-time control hierarchy. Instead, it serves as the operational and lifecycle envelope enclosing all five. Governance determines which entity holds valid command authority, what verification evidence licenses the machine to operate, and when that license lapses and must be renewed. Governance transitions unfold across the human and operational timescales of safety interventions, policy rollouts, and maintenance releases, rather than the millisecond rhythms of real-time control loops.

↪ The authority state machine that hands each actuator channel to exactly one owner during a transfer is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^sec-intervention-authority-transitions|Authority Transitions]]**.

**Definition 1.3: Proposal boundary**

***Proposal boundary*** is the line between a learned model, which may only propose an action, and the **permission path**, an independent mechanism with bounded latency that the model cannot delay or corrupt and that alone may permit the action to reach an actuator. The permission path validates every proposal against fresh sensory evidence, the stopping envelope (the state space from which the machine can safely decelerate to a halt prior to obstacle collision), and a fallback trajectory shown feasible from the current state. On the running machine, the algorithmic admission checks remain an unrated performance layer (one not certified to a functional-safety rating) above separate certified protective functions. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-certified-functions|Certified safety functions beneath the permission path]]** explains that distinction. If any condition is violated, the permission path rejects the proposal within the established response bound.

1.  **Significance**: Prevents learned model outputs from directly manipulating actuators. This architectural protection remains dependable only as long as the permission path’s independent sensing, dynamic models, and fallback maneuvers remain physically valid.
2.  **Distinction**: Even a deep reinforcement learning policy trained to emit joint torques possesses zero raw actuator-write authority; its output remains a mere proposal until explicitly admitted by the permission path.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Treating the permission path as a cosmetic post-processing filter or output clamp, rather than an autonomous supervisory mechanism capable of seizing control when the learned proposer hangs, violates deadlines, emits pathological values, or falls silent.

The proposal boundary determines who holds the authority to approve an action, whereas the causal boundary marks the physical interface where an admitted command alters the state of the plant. The Nervous System bridges this divide, verifying proposals against hard constraints before energizing the drives.

Readers steeped in classical control theory will recognize this design as a supervisory architecture. Specifically, the proposal boundary implements runtime assurance. In classic Simplex architectures, a complex, high-performance controller (here, the learned policy) is paired with a certified baseline controller, coordinated by decision logic that determines which subsystem drives the plant ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-seto1998simplex|Seto et al. 1998]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-sha2001using|Sha 2001]]). The lineage table in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.11 The Four Bedrock Laws#^sec-boundary-bedrock-laws|section 1.10]] ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.11 The Four Bedrock Laws#^tbl-01-law-lineage|table 3]]) maps this book’s terms to their established names once the four laws are stated.

↪ Runtime assurance for physical AI, which pairs the learned proposer with a resident protective controller and an output selector, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.02 The Per-Tick Gatekeeper#^sec-enforcement-deterministic-gatekeeper|The Per-Tick Gatekeeper]]**.

Hardware fault isolation alone does not guarantee safety, because architectural independence also constrains the permission path’s sensor inputs. The learned detection path used in the stopping budget prices response delay, but its lease cannot protect against a perception error that produces timely, unsafe proposals. If an enforcement monitor validates a proposed trajectory using the very same learned perception pipeline that synthesized it, a single perception error will corrupt both components simultaneously. The sensing pipelines that grant execution authority must therefore maintain distinct failure modes from the learned models they supervise.

Across the five stacked levels in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.08 The Machine in Five Levels#^fig-01-locator|figure 7]], characteristic operating rates span four orders of magnitude. The continuous physical World sits at the bottom, followed by the event-timed Boundary, the 10–25 kHz current loops of the mechanical Body, and the 1 kHz permission loop of the Nervous System. At the top, the hierarchical Brain operates from 40 Hz setpoints to 2 Hz intent models. The prominent gap between Brain and Nervous System marks the foundational proposal boundary: components above this line may only formulate proposals, while only deterministic subsystems below it possess the authority to command physical actuators. The operating frequencies charted across the levels reflect the reference architecture analyzed throughout this book, rather than immutable physical constants for every robotic platform. Subsequent chapters derive these timing budgets directly from task dynamics and mechanical plant characteristics. What remains fundamental across all physical AI systems is the proposal boundary that severs learned computation from unchecked execution authority.

Deploying capable physical AI requires balancing expressive policy proposals with deterministic execution guarantees. This separation of concerns does not demand rejecting every imperfect proposal out of hand; real-time intervention mechanisms can project a trajectory onto safe constraints or smoothly blend corrections, preserving task progress while respecting physical invariants. Systems evaluation must therefore disentangle what the learned proposer achieves in isolation, how effectively the integrated machine executes under routine supervisory filtering, and whether the fallback mechanisms themselves introduce secondary hazards.

↪ The minimal-intervention optimization filters that project a proposal onto its constraints are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-minimal-intervention|The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters]]**.

Distributing responsibilities across these five levels gives the rest of the book its structure. It also gives a way to diagnose real failures by asking which level failed, rather than reducing every physical malfunction to a deficiency in neural network weights.
