# V4-01.17 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01 The Causal Boundary|V4-01 The Causal Boundary]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Moravec’s Paradox**: Moravec ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-moravec1988mind|1988]]) observed that computers could be made to perform at an adult level on intelligence tests or at checkers far more easily than they could be given a one-year-old’s perception and mobility, and he traced the gap to evolutionary age, since sensorimotor skill was refined over a far longer history than abstract reasoning. For a physical machine the hard part is therefore the part that touches the causal boundary, perceiving a changing world and acting on it before a deadline, which is where learning and this book both concentrate.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Recovery Data (ALVINN)**: The neural network that steered Carnegie Mellon’s Navlab autonomous van directly from camera imagery confronted this challenge in early trials: because human test drivers kept the van centered in the driving lane, training logs contained no examples of recovering from the road shoulder, causing the system to veer off whenever it drifted off center. To synthesize recovery demonstrations without hazardous trials, Pomerleau ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-pomerleau1991efficient|1991]]) transformed recorded road images using synthetic lateral shifts and calculated corresponding steering corrections back toward the lane center. While synthetic shifts work for simple geometric offsets, a physical policy must still be trained over the states its own actions visit.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **System Identification**: The control-engineering practice of fitting low-order dynamic models to measured input-output time series. When this fitting establishes bounded parameter uncertainty under stated modeling assumptions, control engineers can use those bounds to prove stability guarantees before deploying the controller. Modern high-dimensional neural networks lack tractable closed-form uncertainty bounds and therefore require empirical verification across explicitly specified operating domains.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Reading the Tempe Record**: Times in this section count backward from impact at ${T}$, as the event table of NTSB/HAR-19/03 does, and speeds are those the ADS itself recorded ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ntsb2019uber|National Transportation Safety Board 2019]]). The report also cautions that communication delay leaves unclear exactly when the planned slowdown began. Reading each time as an interval before impact, rather than as a clock time, is what lets it be converted into distance traveled at the recorded speed, the conversion this section applies.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Tracking-History Loss**: The NTSB record states only that a changed classification caused the predictor to omit the object’s earlier locations; it does not describe a Kalman-filter reset or a zero-velocity initialization, so neither should be read into it ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ntsb2019uber|National Transportation Safety Board 2019]]). The consequence follows from the omission alone. Each relabeling shortened the evidence window from which motion is estimated, so an object crossing the lane began again, after each change, as one with no recorded history of crossing.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Jerk Limit**: Jerk, met in the stopping notebook as the rate of change of deceleration, is measured in m/s³. A jerk limit makes a brake ramp its deceleration up instead of stepping it, which spares occupants, loads, and transmissions a shock but costs stopping distance, because full deceleration arrives later. The warehouse machine’s card carries a jerk bound for the same reason, and the machine’s finished stopping budget pays for the ramp.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Safety-Critical Standards**: Three standards divide the ground. ISO 26262 addresses hazards caused by faults in a vehicle’s electrical and electronic systems ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-iso26262|International Organization for Standardization 2018]]); ISO 21448, safety of the intended functionality (SOTIF), addresses hazards that arise when a function works as designed but performs insufficiently, as perception can ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-iso21448|International Organization for Standardization 2022]]); and UL 4600 sets out the safety case for autonomous products with no human driver to fall back on ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ul4600|UL Standards & Engagement 2023]]). Tempe falls on the SOTIF side, since the recorded failures were in classification and path prediction rather than in a faulted component, and none of the three prescribes a processor architecture. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.04 Safety Cases and Claims#^sec-release-standards-map|The standards map for the running machine]]** maps the standards that govern the running machine.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Minimal-Risk Condition**: In automated driving, the stable, usually stopped, state a system brings a vehicle to when a trip cannot or should not continue. Reaching it can itself require motion, as a pullover to the curb does, and in the Cruise case that motion was the harm. A fallback trajectory therefore needs the same permission check as any other proposal, judged against fresh evidence about the space it will move through.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Operational Design Domain (ODD)**: The automated-driving term for the stated environmental and operating conditions (roadway types, speed ranges, weather, lighting) under which a system is designed to work, and the starting scope of a UL 4600 safety case ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ul4600|UL Standards & Engagement 2023]]). This book applies the concept to the payloads, surface friction, operating speeds, and disturbances covered by the machine’s empirical testing. Operating outside that tested domain has no evidence behind it, which is why **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry|Choosing a tractable coverage table]]** organizes the operating domain as an explicit scenario table against which collected training data are audited.

^fn9
