*Why can a better prediction still produce a worse physical action?*

A learned model can recognize a task correctly and still act too late to stop a moving machine or prevent excessive contact force. Once a command produces motion or heat, replacing the prediction cannot undo the work already delivered. The engineer must therefore connect model quality to the body’s limits, the time available to respond, and the evidence supporting the next action. An independent permission path must be able to refuse a proposal and carry out a feasible fallback even when the model stalls. At the causal boundary, where commands become physical work, irreversibility makes these responsibilities part of the system’s architecture.

**Learning Objectives**

- Trace how machine learning systems evolved from disembodied prediction through digital agency to physical embodiment, and explain why that progression gives data, compute, evaluation, and deployment physical stakes
- Explain why an action that crosses the causal boundary requires reasoning about consequences that software rollback cannot undo
- Calculate how response delay consumes a moving machine’s stopping clearance
- Apply the scope test to distinguish physical AI from advisory learning and classical control
- Distinguish learned proposals from permission to execute them in the five-level machine
- Diagnose how perception, response timing, and missing redundancy interacted in the Tempe collision
- Relate the four laws to their established names in the field and to the book’s four parts
