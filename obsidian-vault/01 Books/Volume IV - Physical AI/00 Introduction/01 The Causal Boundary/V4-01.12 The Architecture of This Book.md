# V4-01.12 The Architecture of This Book

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01 The Causal Boundary|V4-01 The Causal Boundary]]

^sec-boundary-book-architecture

Moving a learned proposal, such as the running machine’s trajectory to open a warehouse cage door, from an abstract task requirement to safe, justified physical operation requires a disciplined four-stage progression. The engineer must first bound what the hardware plant can physically achieve and characterize what the learned proposer has demonstrated under rigorous testing. The engineer must then build an isolated runtime monitor to intercept live proposals and establish the operational conditions under which continued execution remains justified. Each stage corresponds to one of the four laws. The system of records illustrated in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.12 The Architecture of This Book#^fig-01-spine|figure 9]] formalizes this progression, capturing operating assumptions and empirical evidence so that downstream runtime decisions can inspect and rely on them. Conceptually, these records trace two converging pipelines. One strand establishes offline physical limits and verified competence; the second processes online sensor observations and real-time candidate actions. Both pipelines converge at the permission decision, where the system admits or rejects a proposed trajectory. In **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18.02 From a Better Prediction to Useful Service#^sec-frontier-architectural-synthesis|From a Better Prediction to Useful Service]]**, this architecture synthesizes both strands to resolve the book’s central challenge of engineering dependable, high-performance physical systems out of unverified learned models and unforgiving mechanical hardware.

A record is a machine-checkable artifact that downstream safety and runtime decisions can verify and trust. The initial stopping calculation (notebook [1.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/01_boundary/01_boundary.html#nbk-boundary-stopping-budget)) already sketches the first record in the chain: a limit record, with the six elements the reader guide introduces (nominal value, operating conditions, uncertainty, margin, runtime detector, and provenance). Its nominal value is the derived stopping distance under stated operating conditions of loaded payload mass, dry concrete, a stationary obstacle, and constant approach speed. Because those inputs are illustrative scalars rather than calibrated distributions, however, its uncertainty remains unquantified. The apparent buffer between the obstacle and the machine is therefore only an arithmetic difference between provisional figures rather than a verified safety margin. The calculation also lacks a runtime detector (no sensor yet monitors whether floor traction drops or cargo shifts outside the assumed envelope, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.08 Measuring a Machine’s Own Limits#^sec-body-limit-record-schema|The six elements of a limit record]]**), and its provenance rests on baseline assumptions rather than calibrated hardware logs. An ungrounded calculation becomes operational engineering evidence only when all six fields are populated and backed by empirical data. Every numbered box across the four part bands in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.12 The Architecture of This Book#^fig-01-spine|figure 9]] establishes a machine-checkable record of this rigor for a distinct architectural decision.

What the plant can do and what the proposer has shown it can do run as one strand, what the proposer proposes now as another, and the two meet at the permission decision.

Part I, The Machine Anatomy, translates physical irreversibility into rigid architectural limits. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02 The Physical Body#^sec-body|The Physical Body]]** profiles the mechanical plant to quantify its physical budgets (measurement freshness, stopping distance, deliverable torque, winding heat, and supply voltage). **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03 The Cognitive Brain#^sec-brain|The Cognitive Brain]]** measures the throughput and inference latency of the learned proposer to determine what it can deliver before deadlines expire. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System#^sec-nervous|The Nervous System]]** links compute to the actuators across deterministic buses, structuring a permission path whose timing guarantees derive from the plant’s stopping requirements. Together, the limit record and handoff record make every physical and temporal assumption explicit. Part II, Teaching the Machine, confronts the reality that physical actions continuously alter future sensor observations. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data#^sec-data|Physical Data]]**, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training#^sec-training|Policy Training]]**, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation#^sec-evaluation|Closed-Loop Evaluation]]** build the evidentiary chain required to collect demonstrations, train control policies, and bound policy performance to a declared operational design domain. Across this pipeline, the provenance record, policy manifest, and evaluation record document both the empirical competence demonstrated by the model and the operational boundaries where its statistical guarantees break down.

Part III, Running the Machine, enforces at runtime the separation between proposing an action and permitting it to execute. From **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08 Sensor Perception#^sec-perception|Sensor Perception]]** through **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement#^sec-placement|Workload Placement]]**, the chapters trace candidate commands through the online runtime. They qualify sensor measurements in observation contracts and maintain state estimates in a belief record. They bound a goal with an intent lease (a time-limited grant of a target, never of a path, and distinct from the chunk lease that bounds one motion segment). They check candidate trajectories against real-time physical constraints before admission and dispatch, then test whether the hardware keeps the permission path independent. Part IV, Governing the Machine, enforces the principle that permission must be earned. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention#^sec-intervention|Supervisory Intervention]]**, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification#^sec-verification|Adversarial Verification]]**, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release#^sec-release|Deployment Release]]** govern how authority transfers between the machine and a human operator, how fault injection tests whether protective responses hold, and how deployment decisions are assembled into release manifests. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17 Responsible Physical AI#^sec-responsibility|Responsible Physical AI]]** then names who answers for each decision behind an admitted action. It also tests whether each human role the design relies on is one a person can perform, and it counts the people who bear the machine’s risk and appear in its records. Finally, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18 Engineering Physical AI#^sec-frontier|Engineering Physical AI]]** brings the framework to bear on an unfamiliar machine, synthesizing what an engineering team can construct and formally justify while identifying the residual risks that require further mitigation. Across these parts, the warehouse mobile manipulator introduced here provides a single concrete machine on which to ground each architectural decision. Its records render design assumptions fully inspectable, though an engineer must remember that assembling a record documents an explicit claim rather than guaranteeing its physical truth. The system record register in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-record-register|The record register]]** catalogs all sixteen records, noting their producing chapters and the specific invariants they bind, while the machine card in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-running-machine|section 1.4.2]] specifies the foundational physical parameters of the robot itself. Together, this four-stage route forms a repeatable discipline for making learned behavior dependable in physical space, guarding against the common misconceptions that arise when digital assumptions are carried across the causal boundary.
