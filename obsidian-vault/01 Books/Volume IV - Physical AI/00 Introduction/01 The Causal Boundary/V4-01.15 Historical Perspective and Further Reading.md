# V4-01.15 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01 The Causal Boundary|V4-01 The Causal Boundary]]

^sec-boundary-history

In physical artificial intelligence, moving learned models directly into the actuation loop creates severe timing and safety hazards. The engineering principles that resolve these challenges originate in embedded systems, real-time control, and safety engineering. General-purpose computing treats execution time as a mere performance optimization, assuming that correctness depends solely on logical input-output relationships. Cyber-physical systems theory overturned this assumption, holding that physical dynamics and computational processes must be modeled jointly because a command dispatched at the wrong time is physically incorrect ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-lee2008cyber|Lee 2008]]). This insight forms the foundation of the causal boundary. Similarly, separating command proposal from execution permission stems from runtime assurance architectures. The Simplex architecture demonstrated that an industrial control system could safely run an unverified, high-performance controller if paired with a certified baseline controller and deterministic switching logic capable of taking back the plant before safety boundaries were violated ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-seto1998simplex|Seto et al. 1998]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-sha2001using|Sha 2001]]). Modern safety filters in embodied machine learning generalize this design, validating neural network proposals against mathematical certificates of safe states (control barrier functions or reachability envelopes, which **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement#^sec-enforcement|Safety Enforcement]]** develops) before granting actuator permission ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-hsu2024safetyfilter|Hsu et al. 2024]]).

Many foundational safety principles in physical systems emerged from forensic investigations of catastrophic failures. The Therac-25 radiation therapy disasters demonstrated the fatal peril of replacing mechanical interlocks with software routines ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-leveson1993investigating|Leveson and Turner 1993]]). When race conditions bypassed software checks, the machine administered lethal radiation overdoses, proving that software reliability does not imply system safety. Systems-theoretic safety subsequently recast accidents not as isolated component breakdowns, but as failures of the overall control structure to enforce safety constraints on the interactions among components, feedback loops, and human operators ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-leveson2011engineering|Leveson 2011]]). This shift led directly to the assurance case, a structured argument demonstrating that concrete evidence satisfies defined safety claims within explicit operating environments ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-kelly1998arguing|Kelly 1998]]). The UL 4600 standard adopted this structure as its central framework for autonomous products ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ul4600|UL Standards & Engagement 2023]]), abandoning arbitrary road-testing mileage in favor of verifiable, claim-based arguments. Public accident records, particularly the National Transportation Safety Board investigation of the 2018 Tempe autonomous vehicle collision ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ntsb2019uber|National Transportation Safety Board 2019]]), remain the field’s most complete documentation of how safety arguments unravel when classification changes, a response delay chosen in software, and reliance on a human fallback interact in the field.

- **Cyber-physical systems** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-lee2008cyber|Lee 2008]]). Lee articulates why classical computing abstractions fail when computation interacts with physical processes. General-purpose computer architectures deliberately abstract away time, treating execution speed as a performance consideration rather than a correctness specification. Lee demonstrates that for physical interactions, the timing of an actuation command is inseparable from its physical meaning, providing the conceptual foundation for treating the causal boundary as an explicit systems interface.
- **Runtime assurance** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-sha2001using|Sha 2001]]). Sha’s paper on the Simplex architecture is the place to see how the switching logic decides when to take the plant back from the unverified controller, and why that logic must be simpler than what it supervises. It is the pattern behind this book’s separation of proposal from permission.
- **Therac-25** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-leveson1993investigating|Leveson and Turner 1993]]). Leveson and Turner’s forensic analysis traces the specific race conditions and the development and reporting practices that let the overdoses recur, detail the summary above omits. It remains the essential case study in why protective functions need justification independent of the software they supervise.
- **Systems-theoretic safety** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-leveson2011engineering|Leveson 2011]]). Leveson introduces the STAMP accident model, reframing safety as a continuous control problem rather than a collection of component failure probabilities. Accidents occur when the control structure fails to enforce safety constraints across component interactions, sensor delays, and feedback loops. This text provides the theoretical basis for enforcing safety invariants as dynamic runtime constraints rather than static design assumptions.
- **The Tempe investigation** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.16 Source References#^ref-ntsb2019uber|National Transportation Safety Board 2019]]). The National Transportation Safety Board report on the fatal 2018 Tempe autonomous vehicle crash offers the most thorough empirical record of an embodied AI safety failure. The report details how classification changes that discarded tracking history, a one-second action suppression meant to limit responses to false alarms, the separate deactivation of the vehicle’s factory emergency braking over possible radar interference, and reliance on an operator whose attention nothing monitored combined in the collision. The event timeline and sensor telemetry provide vital reading for anyone building real-time perception-action loops.

**What’s Next: From the causal boundary to physical limits**

A permission decision needs a physical limit against which to judge the proposed action. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02 The Physical Body#^sec-body|The Physical Body]]** begins Part I with the five budgets the permission path must check before it commits torque (measurement freshness, stopping distance, deliverable torque, winding heat, and supply voltage) and asks which binds first for a given maneuver, under what stress, and on what evidence. Each budget ends as a limit record, the six-element form this chapter sketched from its stopping calculation. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03 The Cognitive Brain#^sec-brain|The Cognitive Brain]]** then examines the learned proposer’s capabilities and timing, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System#^sec-nervous|The Nervous System]]** uses both sets of constraints to design the handoff and independent permission path. The progression starts with what the machine can sustain and ends with how computation may safely request it.
