# V4-14.11 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention|V4-14 Supervisory Intervention]]

^sec-intervention-summary

Supervisory intervention transfers the right to propose, never the right to admit. A capability matrix grants each authenticated role named operations on named actuator channels, each with a preemption tier and a clearance-derived deadline, while the enforcer checks every command from every source against the same physical envelope. Enforcement changes a proposal and leaves the proposer in place; intervention changes the proposer and leaves the check in place, and the machine needs both.

A takeover is a delay inserted before braking on a moving machine, so it is priced in the stopping budget rather than in the user interface. At the 1.3 m/s aisle speed the subtotal before dispatch-to-admission travel leaves 131.0 mm, a waiting window of 100.8 ms, and only 37.5 ms once the 63.3 ms dispatch-to-admission allocation of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.07 Silicon Isolation Decisions#^sec-placement-dispatch-admission|The board-link budget and the dispatch-to-admission interval]]** is charged. Sourced human responses of 605 ms in the loop and 4.5 s out of it cap the request speed at 0.797 m/s and 0.189 m/s once it is charged. No brake, however strong, admits the aisle speed, because the waiting term rather than the braking term spends the clearance. The design therefore slows to 0.75 m/s before an anticipated in-loop request, crawls at 0.15 m/s or stops first before an out-of-loop one, and answers a newly discovered hazard with its own local stop. None of these speeds qualifies until loaded measurements confirm the allocation and the braking premises.

Authority is bounded and moved by mechanisms that never wait on the network. Execution leases on the safety microcontroller’s clock, the autonomous chunk lease and the 100 ms manual lease, revoke a silent proposer and select the fallback that matches the machine’s state. A four-phase handshake commits ownership on one control tick for an explicit channel mask, the enforcer remains the only writer to the actuators, and every timer that expires leads to a defined state. Authentication keeps forged commands out of admission, but only a rate limiter ahead of the verifier keeps forged traffic from delaying a genuine veto, and hardwired protective inputs bypass both.

Changing the proposer does not change the physics of the command. A fixed-endpoint ${C}^2$ smoothstep bounds the command rate between latched endpoints, but it spends clearance. The base window is 528 ms at every speed because the jerk bound belongs to the loaded base, and at the 0.75 m/s request speed no braking blend fits the clearance that remains. Within the stated one-dimensional stopping model and boundary conditions, no smoothed braking under the same bounds beats the resident profile, so the base receives the resident stop while the arm blends over 38 ms.

The authority record turns every handover into evidence. It binds the sealed static manifest to per-tick evidence tuples that record who held each channel and what was proposed, admitted, and dispatched alongside independent measurements of the response, chains them against tampering, and declares every gap. It draws its fallback rungs from the enforcement record and its declared timing bounds from the placement record, whose loaded measurements are still to come. The same ownership timestamps serve two readers. An investigator can reconstruct an incident from them, provided independent current, encoder, and contact measurements confirm what the commands did, and a learner can apply this chapter’s conservative four-interval direct-imitation mask, under which only validated, stabilized human recovery supplies post-takeover demonstration targets. Other learning objectives use the same provenance with different eligibility rules ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^tbl-14-intervention-learning|table 8]]). **Key Takeaways: Supervisory intervention and authority bounds**

- **State-dependent fallback and monotonic leases:** Bind each proposer to an explicit, mode-specific lease and stopping reserve. Lease expiry invokes the validated local fallback response appropriate for the current physical state, along with the sensing, drive energy, braking, and holding premises required to execute it safely. For manual teleoperation, the reference design chooses a 100 ms lease, bounded from below by heartbeat loss and jitter and from above by the clearance its extra travel over the chunk lease consumes after the takeover.
- **Clearance governs waiting:** A takeover delay is travel before braking, so its allowance comes from the stopping budget, not from a configured timer. The subtotal before dispatch-to-admission travel allows 100.8 ms of waiting at the aisle speed, 37.5 ms once the 63.3 ms admission allocation is charged, and 751.6 ms at the 0.75 m/s slow-down, which still leaves 83.3 ms after an in-loop takeover and the charge. Price human responses at design percentiles, not averages. On the complete budget, the 605 ms in-loop design response caps the request speed at 0.797 m/s and the 4.5 s out-of-loop response caps it at 0.189 m/s. Dispatch the fallback at the earliest of the protocol, lease, evidence, and clearance deadlines, and stop before handing an unexpected hazard to an out-of-loop operator.
- **Single-writer actuator ownership:** Explicit source arbitration and exclusive enforcer command ownership eliminate conflicting actuator writes; low-level bus arbitration alone cannot establish safety authority. Every request, commit, and confirmation message must explicitly declare its channel mask. A partial transfer, such as reassigning arm control while the base keeps navigating autonomously, is admissible only while the console annunciates the untransferred channel’s owner; otherwise that channel holds. A four-phase protocol (Request, Acknowledge, Commit, Confirm) transitions authority deterministically. The commit transition revokes the autonomous policy token on the designated channels and latches its last admitted command. No proposer holds the proposal token during `BLEND`, while the enforcer owns the command; the arbiter grants the token to the operator only after the profile completes, and the enforcer then checks human proposals against the same physical envelopes as the policy’s.
- **Smooth command blending and haptic passivity:** A fixed-endpoint ${C}^2$ acceleration profile bounds peak jerk during authority transitions, and its additional stopping displacement must be reserved in the braking envelope. If the system incorporates bilateral force reflection, the console feedback loop requires its own passivity and stability argument evaluated under realistic network latency, packet loss, and actuator dynamics.
- **Independent stopping paths:** Dedicated, hardwired protective circuits execute risk-assessed mechanical and electrical stops independent of software. Network-delivered commands undergo cryptographic admission checks: corrupted or unauthenticated packets are dropped at ingress, whereas a failure to refresh a valid lease triggers an autonomous local fallback.
- **Causal data attribution:** Training on intervention logs requires preserving controller provenance and declaring target and eligibility rules. This chapter’s conservative direct-imitation mask ${\mathcal{M}}({t})$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^eq-intervention-causal-masking-loss|equation 8]]) preserves learner-visited drift states for failure analysis and automated relabeling, zeros out synthetic enforcer blending splines (${\tau_{\text{blend}}}$) and initial operator hunting, and admits only validated, stabilized human recovery as post-takeover demonstration targets. Other objectives may retain or weight early corrections differently ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^tbl-14-intervention-learning|table 8]]).
- **Authority as auditable evidence:** The authority record logs per-tick evidence tuples $\langle {t_{\text{MCU}}}, {S_{\text{auth}}}, {\mathbf{x}}, {\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}, {\mathbf{u}^*}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}, \text{ID}_{\text{operator}}, {T_{\text{lease,rem}}}\rangle$ into tamper-evident cryptographic hash chains, treating a missing entry as an explicit fault rather than an implied pass. The manifest’s four authority rules serve as primary test targets for verification fault injection; their passing logs, anchored by the authority record, are the evidence the release argument needs before it may admit remote takeover.

^callout-takeaways--14-2-b2069770

A person’s authority over an embodied physical AI system is granted on verifiable evidence and expires when that evidence lapses, as the fourth law, permission must be earned (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/V4 Governing the Machine#^pri-vol4-evidence-bounds-authority|4]]), requires of every privilege the machine honors. Fresh credentials, checked plant state, a feasible target and fallback reserve, and a successfully committed handshake support the grant of authority; the manual lease bounds how long the grant remains valid while its physical premises continue to hold. When operator inputs cease renewing the lease, authority immediately lapses to a verified local fallback governed by the hardware permission path. Preemption tiers do not persist across a lease expiration: a remote assistant who previously commanded the mobile base retains no residual authority once the lease expires. Re-establishing control under any proposer requires satisfying the reset conditions of the authority state machine and presenting fresh, validated credentials.
