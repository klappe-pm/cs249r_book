# V4-14.14 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention|V4-14 Supervisory Intervention]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Situation Awareness Levels**: Endsley ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-endsley1995toward|1995]]) set out situation awareness as three levels: perceiving the elements of a scene, comprehending what they mean together, and projecting their state forward. The levels fail separately. A remote assistant can see the pallet without projecting that the base will not stop short of it, which is why a console that reports where the operator is looking cannot shorten the response budget ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.07 Authority Handover Failure Modes#^sec-intervention-failure-modes|section 14.6]]).

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Supervisory Control Levels**: Sheridan and Verplank ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-sheridan1978human|1978]]) formulated a ten-level taxonomy of supervisory control, running from a human who performs the whole task without computer assistance to a computer that decides and acts while ignoring the human.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Model Human Processor**: Card et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-card1983psychology|1983]]) modeled a person responding to a stimulus as three processors in series, perceptual, cognitive, and motor, and gave each cycle time a typical value and a range rather than a single number. The range is the design input. A budget built on the typical cycles admits a speed that a slower but still normal operator overruns, so this chapter’s design row takes the slow end of every range.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Glass-to-Glass Latency**: The interval from light entering the remote camera’s lens to the matching image leaving the operator’s display, covering exposure, encoding, transport, decoding, and display refresh. It is the only video delay the operator experiences, and every stage of that path contributes to it, so a value measured on a wired link does not carry over to a warehouse wireless link. The site must measure its own link before the value enters the takeover budget.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Manual Reduced Speed**: ISO 10218-1:2025 limits the tool speed of an industrial robot in manual reduced-speed mode to $250\text{ mm/s}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-iso10218part1|International Organization for Standardization 2025a]]), the mode in which the pendant of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^fig-14-teach-pendant|figure 4]] couples motion to a three-position grip. Releasing or over-squeezing the grip only requests a stop from the safety circuit, and the performance level that circuit must reach, graded from PL a to PL e, is set by the machine’s risk assessment under ISO 13849-1 ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-iso13849part1|International Organization for Standardization 2023]]). The grip therefore bounds how fast the machine may be moving when a startled operator lets go, not which stop follows.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Nonzero Boundary Derivatives**: The $1.875/{\tau_{\text{blend}}}$ peak derivative factor assumes stationary endpoint derivatives. A general quintic can match the command value, first derivative, and second derivative at both endpoints, including nonzero values (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.05 Kinodynamic Feasibility#^sec-planning-quintic-splines|Quintic splines and C^2 boundary matching]]**). Its interior rate extrema must then be checked; the fixed-endpoint smoothstep factor does not automatically apply.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Mid-Blend Re-Intervention Abort**: If an operator commands a secondary protective stop while a bumpless transfer blend is actively underway ($0 < {\hat{t}} < 1$), the enforcer does not wait for ${\tau_{\text{blend}}}$ to complete. It uses the measured plant position, velocity, and acceleration, together with the active command and their uncertainty, to select a qualified state-matched fallback. A new polynomial seeded only from the intermediate command does not establish that the physical plant can execute it.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Admittance and Impedance Control**: Hogan ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-hogan1985impedance|1985]]) framed interaction control as programming the relation between force and motion at the contact. An impedance controller answers imposed motion with a commanded force; an admittance controller is its dual, turning a measured force into commanded motion, which suits stiff, highly geared arms whose position loops are accurate but which cannot render force directly. On such an arm a force-guided target is still a motion command, so it passes the same enforcer checks as a streamed pose.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Delayed Teleoperation**: Ferrell ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-ferrell1965remote|1965]]) found that operators working through a transmission delay fall back on a move-and-wait strategy, so task time grows with the delay. Force reflection through the same delay proved unstable until Anderson and Spong ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-anderson1989bilateral|1989]]) used scattering theory to make the communication channel passive, a construction that Niemeyer and Slotine ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-niemeyer1991stable|1991]]) recast as wave variables. For a force-reflecting console the delay budget is therefore a stability term as well as a latency term.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Ed25519**: The Edwards-curve signature scheme of Bernstein et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-bernstein2012highspeed|2012]]) provides rapid verification alongside compact 32-byte public keys and 64-byte signatures. Constant-time implementations eliminate secret-dependent branches and memory lookups to protect against side-channel timing attacks. While that defense primarily shields the signing teleoperation console, the receiving safety microcontroller only verifies incoming frames. Well-formed invalid signatures can still require the expensive verification path, even when malformed inputs admit early rejection. The verifier’s processing load therefore depends on admitted traffic and its verification paths, the vulnerability that the verifier-flooding analysis of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.10 Fallacies and Pitfalls#^sec-intervention-fallacies-pitfalls|section 14.9]] prices.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Event Data Recorder Baseline**: A passenger-vehicle event data recorder captures 5 s of pre-crash data at 2 Hz (phasing in from September 2028 to 20 s at 10 Hz), which even under the modernized rule yields only one sample per 100 ms across 100 permission ticks ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-nhtsa2024edr|National Highway Traffic Safety Administration 2024]]). Similarly, the data storage system mandated for automated lane-keeping systems under UN Regulation No. 157 logs system activations, overrides, and transition demands to establish driver attribution, but its timestamps require accuracy only within 1 s, or 1,000 permission ticks ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-unece2021r157|United Nations Economic Commission for Europe 2021]]). Because neither regulatory standard provides the time resolution needed to order an intervention against physical contact within a single control cycle, the autonomous machine must maintain its own deterministic, per-tick operational record.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Linked Hash Chains**: Haber and Stornetta ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-haber1991time|1991]]) proposed linking each timestamp to the cryptographic hash of its predecessor, ensuring that no historical record can be backdated or inserted without invalidating every subsequent link. The authority record inherits both this tamper-evident property and its limit, since an adversary possessing the signing keys and complete control over the storage device can recompute the entire chain. To protect against retroactive tampering, commitment anchors must be mirrored to an independent, replay-protected storage domain outside the logger’s mutable write path.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Token Bucket**: A limiter holds up to ${b}$ tokens, refills at rate ${r}$, and admits a packet only by spending a token, so a source may burst ${b}$ packets but never sustain more than ${r}$. Placed before the verifier, it bounds the forged signatures that can arrive within ${T_{\text{deadline,fallback}}}$ at ${b} + {r}\,{T_{\text{deadline,fallback}}}$. For the single-source queue modeled here, keeping that bound below the 55 forgeries that push a genuine veto past its deadline, or 20 once ${\Delta_{\text{da}}}$ is charged, sizes ${b}$ and ${r}$ from the stopping budget rather than from link capacity. A deployed ingress must also bound aggregate traffic across sources, include already queued work and legitimate traffic, and prevent forged source identifiers from bypassing those limits.

^fn13
