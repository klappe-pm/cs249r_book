# V4-14.08 Trustworthy Operational Logs

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention|V4-14 Supervisory Intervention]]

^sec-intervention-trustworthy-logs

Consider a robotic arm striking a warehouse racking upright during a supervisory takeover. If the operational log contains an unrecorded gap, investigators cannot determine whether the policy proposed the damaging trajectory, the enforcer admitted it, or the operator received authority too late to intervene. Rigorous incident reconstruction requires two foundational properties: declared loss handling and an operational record engineered to survive physical failure.

A trustworthy record must capture state on both sides of the causal boundary, paired with independent plant observations.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.14 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]] Motor phase currents, optical encoder positions, and wrist force-torque measurements provide physical observations distinct from software setpoints, subject to their calibration, bandwidth, and uncertainty bounds. Dispatched commands alone cannot prove delivered actuator torque or establish mechanical collision causality.

↩︎ A record of what was proposed, evaluated, dispatched, and measured is the third demand on the permission path established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.10 Where Safety, Security, and Accountability Meet#^sec-boundary-safety-convergence|Where Safety, Security, and Accountability Meet]]**.

At every cycle, the 1 kHz deterministic control loop records a structured authority and action tuple under declared loss handling. The minimal **evidence tuple** takes the form:

$$
\langle {t_{\text{MCU}}}, {S_{\text{auth}}}, {\mathbf{x}}, {\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}, {\mathbf{u}^*}, {\boldsymbol{\tau}}, \text{ID}_{\text{operator}}, {T_{\text{lease,rem}}}\rangle
$$

 This tuple records both sides of the proposal-permission boundary. Its command fields are the action taps of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.06 Action Label Selection Across the Control Boundary#^sec-data-four-stream-action-tap|The four-stream action tap tuple]]** taken at intervention time: ${\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}$ is the requested or mapped command (${a_{\text{req}}}$ or ${a_{\text{cmd}}}$, whichever the source emits), ${\mathbf{u}^*}$ is the enforced command ${a_{\text{enf}}}$, and the measured response ${a_{\text{meas}}}$ lives in ${\mathbf{x}}$. The fields are:

- ${t_{\text{MCU}}}$ is the monotonic event timestamp recorded in nanoseconds by the safety microcontroller hardware clock, mapped to other subsystem clocks through a qualified clock-conversion method with an explicit error bound. Expressing values in nanosecond units does not imply nanosecond-level inter-device synchronization accuracy.
- ${S_{\text{auth}}}$ records the active discrete authority state (`AUTO`, `PEND`, `BLEND`, `MAN`, `FALLBACK`, or `PROTECTIVE`).
- ${\mathbf{x}}$ captures the instantaneous measured plant state, including joint angles, velocities, motor phase currents, and IMU linear accelerations.
- ${\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}$ is the proposed plant control setpoint requested by the cognitive policy or human operator console.
- ${\mathbf{u}^*}$ is the control command admitted by the enforcer, including a feasible CBF-filtered proposal or the selected qualified fallback. Associated event metadata must distinguish admission, modification, and rejection; **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-qp-projection|Closed-form quadratic programming and torque projection]]** specifies the projection path.
- ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ is the actuator torque command dispatched across the real-time fieldbus to the motor inverters. It is still a command; delivered motor torque can be estimated from the measured phase currents in ${\mathbf{x}}$ using a qualified motor model and explicit uncertainty bounds.
- $\text{ID}_{\text{operator}}$ identifies the authenticated cryptographic entity holding active proposal rights.
- ${T_{\text{lease,rem}}}$ records the remaining time on the active execution lease (${T_{\text{lease}}}$ or ${T_{\text{lease,man}}}$, minus the elapsed duration ${t_{\text{MCU}}} - {t_{\text{refresh}}}$).

Omitting any of these fields breaks post-incident reconstruction. Without the proposal ${\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}$, a dispatched torque ${\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ cannot show whether the policy or operator asked for an erratic action. Without the enforcer output ${\mathbf{u}^*}$, the log cannot show whether the safety barrier modified that request. Without ${T_{\text{lease,rem}}}$, it cannot show whether authority had already lapsed into a fallback mode when the command was evaluated. Chained with cryptographic integrity proofs and clock-synchronization metadata, these tuples form the per-tick half of the authority record that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-authority-log|section 14.8]] specifies.

Three architectural requirements ensure that logged tuples serve as reliable forensic evidence. First, timestamps must date physical events rather than software handling. A software interrupt handler that samples the clock only after being scheduled records its own execution delay rather than the event time. Under variable scheduling jitter and interrupt latency, this delay can easily invert the apparent chronological order of an intervention command and a mechanical contact. Protective inputs and fieldbus ingress packets must therefore be timestamped directly at the hardware boundary through timer edge capture and network controller hardware counters. When clock-conversion error, sensor exposure windows, and sampling intervals overlap between two events, the log must explicitly record their relative order as indistinguishable rather than asserting an unverified sequence.

Second, the storage pipeline must be strictly tiered by guarantee level. Minimal evidence tuples stream to a bounded ring buffer in non-volatile memory on the safety microcontroller. The serialized tuple size multiplied by the control tick rate sets a continuous write bandwidth that hardware qualification tests must prove the device sustains under all operating regimes, with buffer overflows explicitly flagged rather than silently dropped. High-bandwidth perception streams, including camera frames, point clouds, and policy internal activations, flow to a best-effort buffer on the primary host. This high-volume traffic is never permitted to contend for the safety microcontroller’s memory or bus bandwidth.

↩︎ The multi-camera ingestion rate that this best-effort buffer must absorb is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.07 Teleoperation Demonstration Costs#^sec-data-telemetry-ingestion-storage|Ingestion rate and the preservation requirement]]**.

Third, loss must be declared explicitly. When buffer exhaustion or bus stalls occur, the logger must record a missing entry or overwritten frame as a declared gap in the evidence, never as an implied safe pass. A declared gap alerts both post-incident auditors and downstream training pipelines that the trajectory continuity was broken, whereas a silent drop makes two separated ticks appear contiguous in time.

Upon receiving an intervention trigger, such as an operator takeover command, an emergency stop assertion, or an enforcer barrier violation, the logger freezes a configured **pre-trigger window** (${T_{\text{pre}}} =$ 5 s) and transitions to recording a dedicated post-trigger segment. The pre-trigger duration matches the 4.5 s design out-of-loop takeover latency characterized in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-reaction-drift|section 14.2.2]], rounded up to whole seconds. This window preserves the design takeover interval, but policy divergence may begin before the operator notices it, so its duration alone does not establish that the complete causal chain remains in the log. Operational sites that measure longer takeover tails must lengthen ${T_{\text{pre}}}$ accordingly. Overwritten frames or dropped samples within this interval remain declared gaps in the evidence. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|Intervention and Recovery Data]]** uses this same window as its lookback horizon, searching it for the divergence point ${t_{\text{div}}}$ and detailing why learner-visited states during drift are essential for offline policy training. The authority record provides the cryptographic ownership stamps that allow the training pipeline to distinguish unprivileged policy proposals from enforcer interventions and operator overrides.

Finally, the operational record must reveal any subsequent tampering or truncation. Each entry carries a keyed cryptographic link over its predecessor,[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.14 Footnotes#^fn12|¹²]] while periodic authenticated anchors are written to replay-protected storage located outside the logger’s primary write path. Rewritable non-volatile storage with authenticated writes satisfies this requirement, avoiding the need for dedicated write-once media. Under this design, investigators can determine exactly which log prefix each anchor authenticates, verify that cryptographic keys and anchors remained in trusted custody, and flag any unanchored tail or missing block as a declared gap. Hardware-level tamper resistance and loss declaration protect record integrity and expose missing evidence; they do not establish that a sensor reading is accurate. To serve post-incident reconstruction and downstream policy training, however, these raw evidence streams must be bound into a formal, machine-checkable artifact: the authority record.
