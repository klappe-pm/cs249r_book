*Why can handing a moving robot to a human operator cause the collision the handover was meant to prevent?*

Most deployed robots keep a person in reserve for the situations their learned policies cannot handle, and that reserve is usually treated as the final safeguard. A person, however, needs a large fraction of a second to respond even while watching closely and several seconds after only monitoring. A machine moving at walking pace covers meters in that time, so a request for help can consume the very clearance the help was meant to protect. Taking over also creates hazards of its own. Two controllers that each believe they hold a joint can command it at once, a controller that believes the other still holds it can leave it unattended, and an abrupt switch between their commands jolts the drivetrain and the load it carries. A takeover that grants unchecked control lets a startled or lagging operator drive the machine into a rack. A log that does not mark who commanded each instant can neither settle what caused an incident nor keep the machine’s own near-failures out of the demonstrations it later learns from. Authority can change hands within a single control cycle, but the momentum already delivered across the causal boundary cannot be recalled, so a takeover must be engineered as a control action with its own deadline, its own limits, and its own record.

**Learning Objectives**

- Specify operator permissions through capability matrices that bind each authenticated role’s operations to physical channels, preemption tiers, and clearance-derived deadlines
- Select a state-dependent local fallback that remains physically feasible while human intervention is pending
- Calculate the latest fallback time and takeover speed ceilings from human reaction latency and remaining physical stopping clearance
- Evaluate the inherited chunk lease and the chosen manual lease against their modes’ stopping reserves and available fallback mechanisms
- Design authority handshakes that keep one owner on each actuator output and size fixed-endpoint ${C}^2$ command blends within each channel’s rate limit
- Diagnose mode confusion in split-channel handovers and specify the per-channel annunciation that keeps an operator’s model of ownership current
- Evaluate whether an authority record provides the cryptographic and kinematic evidence needed to reconstruct post-incident fault causality
