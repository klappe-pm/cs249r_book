# V4-14.04 Authority Transitions

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention|V4-14 Supervisory Intervention]]

^sec-intervention-authority-transitions

When a remote assistant takes manual control of the arm after the mobile base has slowed or stopped for a blocked aisle, authority must move between proposers on a single permission tick without two of them ever commanding the same channel, and it must not outlive a proposer that falls silent. The transfer names each actuator channel and assigns it to a single owner; linear blending of two competing proposers is no substitute for choosing who is in charge. The local enforcer remains the sole writer to the physical actuator bus, generating a checked transition profile only after an ownership change has committed on a control tick boundary.

Discrete handover is not the only way to share control. Shared autonomy systems keep both proposers active and blend their actions continuously. In standard policy-blending methods, the robot infers the operator’s intended goal and arbitrates between automated assistance and manual control; user studies show that this arbitration works only while the robot predicts the human’s goal with high confidence ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-dragan2013policy|Dragan and Srinivasa 2013]]). That confidence breaks down when there is more than one path around an obstacle. When an operator steers left around a pallet while the autonomous policy steers right, a convex combination of two sensible commands produces a third trajectory that hits the obstacle. In those cases, a qualified arbiter must select or construct one admissible proposal; the permission path here selects a single source deterministically at each tick. Continuous blending works well when the task has a single clear goal and every intermediate blend is admissible, as when an operator makes fine adjustments to an end-effector pose. Even in purely manual operation, the safety enforcer acts as an invariant filter rather than a proposer, projecting every human command onto the admissible control set regardless of who holds the channel. Before ownership can move between sources, however, each source’s hold on a channel must be bounded in time so that a proposer that falls silent loses authority instead of leaving its last command in force.

↩︎ The minimal-intervention principle, which keeps an admissible proposal and applies the smallest feasible correction otherwise, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-minimal-intervention|The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters]]**.

### Execution Leases and Three-Position Enabling Hardware

^sec-intervention-enabling-hardware

A stalled policy and a disconnected teleoperator leave the same immediate problem, because in both cases the moving machine still holds a previously admitted command. An inference stall, lost wireless link, or incapacitated operator must not leave that command active indefinitely. Expiring the proposer’s authority must also select a local response that brakes the machine or holds the load.

The permission path bounds autonomous chunks and manual sessions with **execution leases**, enforced on the safety microcontroller’s clock. The autonomous lease is the running 60 ms chunk lease. The manual lease is a separate value, chosen in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^sec-intervention-arbiter-heartbeats|section 14.3.4]] and checked against the remaining clearance in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.05 Transfer without Discontinuity#^sec-intervention-session-admission|section 14.4.6]]. An authenticated renewal must also carry valid state and evidence, because a heartbeat alone cannot refresh old observations. A wireless console that carries these renewals is a cableless control system in the sense of IEC 62745, which specifies the functions and interfaces of radio and infrared links between operator control stations, including portable ones, and a machine’s control system ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-iec62745|International Electrotechnical Commission 2017]]). Formally, let ${t_{\text{refresh}}}$ denote the local hardware timestamp of the most recent renewal accepted after authentication, freshness, state, and evidence validation. At physical time ${t}$, the lease-age condition is:

$$
{t} - {t_{\text{refresh}}} < {T_{\text{lease}}} \ \text{(autonomous)}, \qquad {t} - {t_{\text{refresh}}} < {T_{\text{lease,man}}} \ \text{(manual)}. \tag{5}
$$

 The clock advances toward expiry even when packets stop arriving. At expiry, the microcontroller revokes the affected proposal rights and selects the validated response from the current physical state. Authority can return only through the declared recovery and fresh-admission procedure; packet resumption alone cannot restore it.

^eq-intervention-lease-age

Electromechanical **enabling devices** provide an additional physical condition for manual motion, one that holds even when every network packet is valid. The industrial teach pendant in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^fig-14-teach-pendant|figure 4]] is the familiar carrier: a technician standing beside a robot uses it to jog the arm by hand, with a display, a jogging joystick, and a latched emergency-stop button on its face and an enabling grip on its rear handle. On the running machine, an enabling device belongs to a person beside the machine, such as the maintenance technician who jogs one arm joint from a pendant. The remote assistant’s console need not carry such a grip, because authenticated heartbeats maintain the manual lease, with presence assessed by the console’s chosen checks, which do not establish cognitive engagement ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^sec-intervention-arbiter-heartbeats|section 14.3.4]]). A site may add an enabling grip to the console as a second condition. The grip that matters is a three-position one.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.14 Footnotes#^fn5|⁵]] A startled or distressed operator may release a grip or squeeze it harder, and a two-position device that enables whenever pressed does not detect the latter response. By contrast, a three-position grip enables motion only in its intermediate position (Position 2). Releasing it (Position 1) or squeezing beyond the center detent (Position 3) withdraws the enabling signal; the configured safety circuit determines the resulting stop. These dual-channel contacts request the stop selected by the machine’s safety circuit, independently of the Linux application processor and its scheduler. Where that response includes Safe Torque Off (STO, IEC 61800-5-2), STO disables torque generation; the configured braking or holding mechanism must still establish the required plant response. The enabling circuit must also prevent a return through Position 2 after an over-squeeze from automatically restoring motion.

The pendant’s red mushroom button is a different device with different semantics. Under ISO 13850, the emergency stop is a complementary protective measure, not a substitute for safeguarding or for the stop the risk assessment selects. It takes priority over every other function in every operating mode, it latches until an operator deliberately resets it at the device, and that reset must not restart the machine ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-iso13850emergency|International Organization for Standardization 2015]]). Which physical response the stop invokes is a stop-category choice that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^tbl-12-iec60204-stopping-categories|Stop functions and physical response]]** maps to its evidence. The latch and the reset rule matter for supervisory control because they make a human stop sticky in exactly the way a network command is not. A remote packet can request a stop and a lease can lapse into one, but only the local reset of a latched device, followed by the fresh admission this chapter requires, returns the machine to motion.

Even while an operator holds an active lease and maintains Position 2 on the deadman grip, the deterministic enforcer evaluates human setpoints against the same barrier certificates, current limits, and kinematic envelopes applied to the learned neural policy. If an operator panics and commands a high-speed steering angle that would induce dynamic chassis rollover, or requests a joint torque that would shear harmonic drive gear teeth, the enforcer clamps or rejects the command. A human command therefore remains a proposal, and the third law, proposal is not permission (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]]), binds human supervisors and neural models alike. Intervention transfers the right to propose away from the learned policy, while the right to admit stays with the permission path. With every owner’s authority bounded by a lease, what remains to design is how the right to propose changes hands, at which control tick it commits, and what the machine does when a network handover stalls.

### Authority States and Single-Writer Actuator Ownership

^sec-intervention-discrete-states

If the autonomous policy and the remote console both write to a motor drive while a handover is in flight, the drive receives alternating setpoints on consecutive ticks. To prevent two sources from commanding an actuator at once, the arbiter resolves authority over each channel at every discrete time tick ${t}$ to a single, mutually exclusive state:

$$
{S}_t \in \{\text{Autonomous}, \text{Handover-Pending}, \text{Blend}, \text{Human-Manual}, \text{Fallback}, \text{Protective}\}.
$$

 These states separate proposer identity from actuator access. Each carries a short code name, used in the per-tick record and in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^fig-14-authority-handshake-fsm|figure 5]], and each names who holds the proposal token on the channel:

- `Autonomous` (`AUTO`): The learned model holds the proposal token and proposes setpoints, while the local enforcer verifies barrier certificates (${h}({\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$) on every control cycle before dispatching commands.
- `Handover-Pending` (`PEND`): A takeover handshake is negotiating across the network; the policy keeps the token and continues proposing commands under a strict clearance-derived countdown timer ${T_{\text{timeout}}}$. Active lease and evidence expiry run concurrently with that timer, so the arbiter dispatches fallback at the earliest of the protocol, lease, evidence, and clearance deadlines.
- `Blend` (`BLEND`, enforcer-owned bridge): No proposer holds the token. The enforcer assumes exclusive bus ownership, driving a smooth, ${C}^2$-continuous quintic interpolation between the latched autonomous setpoint and the validated human target setpoint.
- `Human-Manual` (`MAN`): The authenticated teleoperator holds the proposal token, streaming commands that pass through enforcer barrier checks under an active manual lease (${T_{\text{lease,man}}}$).
- `Fallback` (`FALLBACK`): Entered when an active lease expires, communication drops, or no admissible continuation remains. No proposer holds the token; the arbiter selects the validated HOLD or STOP rung for the measured state without waiting for remote commands. An ordinary least-change correction, by contrast, is enforcement within the current state and leaves the token with the current proposer.
- `Protective` (`PROTECTIVE`): Entered directly from an independent hardwired protective input or a critical silicon fault. No proposer holds the token. The safety circuit selects the validated protective stop; Safe Torque Off (STO) disables torque generation, and any required load-holding mechanism must be qualified for the current state and surviving resources.

At no point do two software masters write to an actuator simultaneously. Even during the `Blend` state, the enforcer alone owns the actuator channel, executing a precomputed profile between the latched references and validating dynamic constraints against the current state on each tick. Each edge in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^tbl-14-authority-state-matrix|table 4]] therefore names its guard and deadline, its single actuator owner, and the fallback taken when the guard fails or an active lease lapses.

| From → to                                           | Guard and deadline                                                                                      | Actuator owner / command                                                                                                                 | Failure response                                           |
|:----------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Autonomous → Handover-Pending                       | Policy requests supervision; deadline computed from clearance                                           | Enforcer admits policy proposals while feasible                                                                                          | Validated fallback if deadline reached                     |
| Autonomous or Handover-Pending → Blend              | Authenticated operator ACK, fresh state, feasible target and stopping reserve; commit on a control tick | At commit, revoke the policy’s proposal token, latch last approved command and checked manual target; **enforcer alone** writes actuator | Deny invalid target; retain valid active mode or fall back |
| Blend → Human-Manual                                | Profile completes and manual lease remains valid                                                        | Enforcer admits live human proposals within physical limits                                                                              | Validated fallback on lease or enabling-device loss        |
| Handover-Pending, Blend, or Human-Manual → Fallback | Timeout, stale lease, or invalid state                                                                  | The permission path executes the validated HOLD or STOP rung and records it                                                              | Re-arm only after validated recovery procedure             |
| Any state → Protective                              | Independent protective input or qualified critical fault                                                | Safety circuit/controller selects plant-specific protective stop                                                                         | Validated brake or hold, not torque removal alone          |

Table 4: **Authority state machine transition matrix**: Each row names the single actuator owner and the state-dependent fallback. The example uses a 1 ms permission tick. The `BLEND` state interpolates only latched, checked endpoints, under enforcer ownership.

### The Four-Phase Takeover Handshake

^sec-intervention-handshake-protocol

If a single network packet could switch a channel directly from `Autonomous` to `Human-Manual`, a delayed request could hand the actuators to an operator whose view of the aisle is already stale. With the same single packet, a dropped reply could leave the operator and the machine disagreeing on who is driving. Moving a channel between states instead takes a deterministic **four-phase takeover protocol** (`Request` $\to$ `Acknowledge` $\to$ `Commit` $\to$ `Confirm`) run by the safety microcontroller. This four-stage exchange separates the local hardware commit from the physical blend and from the final release of manual proposal rights, checks cryptographic freshness so replayed frames cannot trigger a switch, and changes ownership on a single tick boundary:

1.  **Notice (`Request` phase)**: Either the human supervisor notices policy drift and requests an override (for example, by pressing an enabling button or moving a joystick past its calibrated deadband), or the autonomous policy hits an epistemic uncertainty spike and requests teleoperation assistance. Upon receipt, the arbiter initializes the clearance-derived countdown timer ${T_{\text{timeout}}} = \max(0, \min({T_{\text{config}}}, {T_{\text{deadline,fallback}}}))$ of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^eq-intervention-latest-fallback|equation 4]].
2.  **Declutch (`Acknowledge` phase)**: The enforcer validates target prerequisites across the system. It verifies that sensor telemetry is fresh, network links are live, and the current plant state ${\mathbf{x}}({t})$ retains sufficient stopping reserve to brake safely if the transfer aborts. It then captures a synchronous state snapshot before preparing to revoke the autonomous policy’s proposal authority.
3.  **Transition (`Commit` phase)**: At the next synchronous control tick boundary, the enforcer executes the state transition. It revokes the policy’s proposal token on the committed channels, latches the last approved policy setpoint alongside the checked manual target setpoint, and assigns sole write authority to the enforcer in the `Blend` state. The enforcer drives the ${C}^2$-continuous transition profile, continuously checking that the trajectory remains feasible under measured plant dynamics.
4.  **Notification (`Confirm` phase)**: The permission path broadcasts confirmation of the committed owner and the active `Blend` state to the operator console. A dropped confirmation packet alarms the operator interface, but does not undo or stall the local commit on the robot. After the physical profile completes under valid state, sufficient stopping reserve, and an active manual lease, the arbiter separately grants the proposal token on the committed channels to the operator and transitions to `Human-Manual`. This release timing is dictated by physical profile completion rather than the network notification round trip.

Each phase in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^tbl-14-handshake-protocol|table 5]] carries a strictly budgeted bound, an explicit guard and owner, and deterministic failure handling. The transaction rejects spoofed override attempts and applies the failure response assigned to its current phase. Lost confirmation does not reverse a committed transfer; lease expiry or a failed physical guard invokes the validated state-dependent fallback.

| Phase / takeover stage     | Budgeted bound | Guard and owner                                                                                                | Failure handling                                                              |
|:---------------------------|---------------:|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Request (Notice)       |          10 ms | Authenticated role, checked nonce, and fresh clock-converted timestamp; no owner change                        | Reject invalid packet; keep current qualified mode                            |
| 2\. Acknowledge (Declutch) |           5 ms | Check state, manual target, and full stop reserve; isolate write path                                          | Reject request; invoke fallback if current mode cannot continue               |
| 3\. Commit (Transition)    |      1 ms tick | Revoke the policy’s proposal token, latch both checked endpoints, assign **enforcer** sole actuator write path | No partial commit within the requested channel mask; fallback on failed guard |
| 4\. Confirm (notification) |          15 ms | Announce committed `BLEND`; later announce `MAN` after profile and lease check                                 | Lost notice alarms operator but does not undo commit                          |

Table 5: **Four-phase takeover timing**: The phase budgets sum to 31 ms. This sum budgets the exchange through commit notification, not physical blending or release of manual proposal rights. The blend begins at commit and may continue after notification.

### Arbiter Execution and Manual-Lease Heartbeats

^sec-intervention-arbiter-heartbeats

Once a channel reaches $\text{Human-Manual}$, a dropped wireless link or a distracted assistant who leaves the console would let the last manual command keep running unless the machine checks that the operator is still present. The safety microcontroller bounds that risk with a hardware-timed **manual lease**. Depending on the console, presence detection may use a capacitive grip sensor, eye tracking, or continuous joystick deflection past a deadband, chosen for their sensor coverage and false-drop rates. Manual authority is sustained exclusively through authenticated keep-alive heartbeats streamed from the operator console to the safety microcontroller. The manual lease ${T_{\text{lease,man}}}$ of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^sec-intervention-enabling-hardware|section 14.3.1]] is the longest silence the microcontroller tolerates between them. Two bounds set its length. From below, the lease must outlast the heartbeat period plus the link’s jitter and at least one lost packet, or ordinary wireless loss would drop a healthy operator into fallback. That floor is why the heartbeats must run at a rate several times the lease’s inverse. From above, the lease replaces the chunk lease inside ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ while the operator holds `MAN`, so the travel it adds over the chunk lease, ${v}({T_{\text{lease,man}}} - {T_{\text{lease}}})$, must fit the clearance left after the takeover. The reference implementation chooses 100 ms. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.05 Transfer without Discontinuity#^sec-intervention-session-admission|Section 14.4.6]] checks that value against the upper bound at the in-loop request speed, and only the site’s measured heartbeat loss can show that it clears the lower one.

Each heartbeat packet carries a strictly increasing 64-bit sequence counter ${k}$, a monotonic hardware timestamp ${t^{\text{hb}}}_k$, an authenticated operator credential, and a keyed message authentication code (AES-128 CMAC, evaluated in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.06 Authenticating an Intervention#^sec-intervention-authenticating-intervention|section 14.5]]). In the age calculation below, ${t^{\text{hb}}}_k$ represents the sender’s timestamp translated into the local clock domain. The receiver tracks the bounded clock-synchronization uncertainty interval and computes freshness relative to the oldest plausible timestamp; an unqualified clock mapping cannot safely establish packet freshness. The nominal local-clock age is:

$$
{\Delta t_{\text{heartbeat}}} = {t_{\text{local}}} - {t^{\text{hb}}}_k.
$$

 These fields combine communication checks used in black-channel safety architectures ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-iec61784-3-2021|International Electrotechnical Commission 2021]]) with cryptographic security over an untrusted wireless link. The sequence counter detects transmission loss and replay attacks. The timestamp and bounded lease act as a communication watchdog against excessive latency. The cryptographic tag authenticates the claimed credential. The safety communication checks and the security mechanism require separate qualification.

↩︎ The proposal header that applies the same black-channel safeguards to every learned proposal is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-supervisory-timers|Supervisory timers and proposal wire contracts]]**.

A heartbeat packet may renew authority only while its conservative age falls within the configured freshness window. The active manual lease expires deterministically on the local hardware clock at its committed deadline; stale or out-of-order network traffic cannot extend that horizon. Similarly, an autonomous action chunk expires strictly according to its admitted local deadline. A packet that fails cryptographic verification is immediately discarded. It cannot renew the lease, but it does not revoke currently valid authority either; the existing timer runs down on its own. Upon lease expiration, the microcontroller automatically revokes the operator’s proposal token, drops into `Fallback`, and executes a pre-validated holding or stopping trajectory. Once a lease lapses, the state machine prohibits any immediate return to $\text{Autonomous}$ or $\text{Human-Manual}$. The robot must satisfy strict recovery conditions: it must come to a complete mechanical standstill, resolve and acknowledge the relevant faults without erasing their evidence, and undergo an authenticated four-phase re-admission handshake initiated from rest.

When multiple control sources propose concurrent commands, the permission path resolves candidate selection through a fixed priority hierarchy evaluated deterministically every 1 ms control cycle below the proposal boundary. This mechanism adopts the layered priority structure of subsumption architectures ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.13 Source References#^ref-brooks1986robust|Brooks 1986]]), but applies it strictly to input source selection rather than behavioral synthesis. The independent enforcer then admits, modifies, or rejects the winning candidate to ensure compliance with configured physical limits. In the $\text{Human-Manual}$ state with a valid lease, the human proposal takes precedence over learned model proposals, while remaining bounded by barrier certificates and actuator limits. In $\text{Autonomous}$, the learned policy generates candidate actions for safety screening. Because fixed-state arbitration requires bounded work per tick ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ computational complexity), it eliminates runtime negotiation or dynamic bargaining between software proposers.

↩︎ The proposal boundary, above which a learned component may only propose and below which the permission path alone admits commands, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.08 The Machine in Five Levels#^sec-boundary-machine-model|The Machine in Five Levels]]**.

Two properties of the protocol show only when the states and handshake are drawn together ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^fig-14-authority-handshake-fsm|figure 5]]). Every active state has an edge to `Fallback`, so no failure of the network or the operator can leave a channel without a defined owner, and the only edge out of `Fallback` is a reset from standstill. The hardwired `Protective` input sits outside the protocol entirely and preempts every state.

*State diagram with six boxes. Autonomous, Handover-Pending, Blend, Human-Manual, and Fallback sit inside a dashed enclosure. Arrows run from Autonomous to Handover-Pending on request, from Handover-Pending to Blend on acknowledgment and commit, directly from Autonomous to Blend on an operator acknowledgment, and from Blend to Human-Manual when the profile completes with a valid manual lease. Arrows from Autonomous, Handover-Pending, Blend, and Human-Manual lead to Fallback on lease expiry, timeout, invalid state, or lease lapse. A dashed arrow returns from Fallback to Autonomous on reset from rest. One arrow from the enclosure leads to the Protective state on a hardwired protective input or critical fault.*

Figure 5: **Authority states and their guards**: Each box is one value of the per-channel authority state, with its code name above its plain name. A handover runs from request through acknowledgment to a commit on a control tick; from then on the enforcer owns the blend between latched endpoints, and manual proposals begin only when the profile completes with a live manual lease. Timeout, lease lapse, or an invalid state leads to the validated fallback, whose only exit is a reset from rest with a fresh re-admission. The hardwired protective input preempts every state.

^fig-14-authority-handshake-fsm

Here the state machine decouples physical bus ownership from protocol progress. An acknowledgment prepares the transition. The local hardware commit (`REVOKE_PROPOSAL_TOKEN()`) strips the autonomous policy of its proposal token on the committed channels and grants the enforcer sole ownership of the blend. The enforcer admits live manual proposals only after the interpolation profile completes under an active manual lease. An unconfirmed transition therefore leaves only the console uncertain, as the `Confirm` phase states. A delayed handover request or a dropped heartbeat, in contrast, runs down its local timer and invokes the validated fallback without waiting for network consensus.

Assigning a single writer resolves authority unambiguously at each control tick, but an instantaneous switch can still produce hazardous dynamic transients. If the autonomous policy’s last admitted joint torque diverges significantly from the operator’s initial target, directly swapping setpoints across a single tick boundary requests an unachievable step change in torque, risking mechanical shock or actuator saturation. The enforcer avoids this discontinuity by using the latched boundary conditions to generate a dynamically feasible interpolation profile that preserves stopping margins throughout the transition.

**Checkpoint 14.2: Authority transitions and timeout bounds**

Before moving from who owns each channel to how its command changes value, verify your understanding of leases, transition state machines, and timeout dynamics:

**State-dependent fallback**: Can you explain why the moving base and the loaded arm need different validated responses when a lease lapses, and why Safe Torque Off alone provides neither?

**Finite-state transition semantics**: Can you trace the deterministic progression among the Autonomous, Handover-Pending, Blend, Human-Manual, and Fallback states, and name the guard and the single actuator owner on each edge?

**Timeout bound**: At the aisle speed the configured protocol ceiling is 500 ms and the clearance-derived deadline after charging the dispatch-to-admission allocation is 37.5 ms. Can you state which one sets ${T_{\text{timeout}}}$, by how many milliseconds it is the tighter, and how many whole 1 ms permission ticks the arbiter may wait before it dispatches fallback?

**Asymmetric preemption hierarchy**: Can you explain why the qualified local protection path must meet its measured deadline, while restoring autonomous authority requires a fresh admission handshake?

**Fallback on a lapsed handover**: Can you explain why a lapsed or unacknowledged handover goes to the validated stop rung rather than back to the degraded policy that requested it?

*If any transition paths permit unhandled timeouts or undefined intermediate states, the authority protocol is vulnerable to mode confusion and runaway actuation. Review this section before proceeding to smooth command blending.*
