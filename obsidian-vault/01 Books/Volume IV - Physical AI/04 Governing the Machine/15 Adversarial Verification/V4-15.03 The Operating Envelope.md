# V4-15.03 The Operating Envelope

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification|V4-15 Adversarial Verification]]

^sec-verification-operating-envelope

A mobile robot carrying a full payload down a warehouse aisle stops in a different distance when memory-bus contention delays its perception pipeline, when battery supply rails sag under acceleration, or when drive inverters heat up under sustained torque. Specifying the external operational design domain (ODD) alone therefore leaves the test conditions half-defined; an **operating envelope** couples those external conditions with the internal execution state of the computing, power, and actuation hardware. Every protective claim must declare both dimensions, the environmental bounds and the internal computational and electrical loads, under which it is guaranteed to hold.

For the mobile manipulator, the operating envelope declares the range of conditions over which the protective claim must hold. The campaign samples a discrete set of qualification points within that range; extending their results to untested points requires an explicit coverage argument. These points span an aisle speed of 1.3 m/s, payload masses ranging from an unloaded chassis to the full 50 kg rack capacity, and ambient operating temperatures from 5 °C to 35 °C. Surface friction must also be parameterized explicitly. In an optimistic all-weight traction screen, dry aisles require a friction coefficient of at least 0.204 to support the design braking deceleration of 2 m/s², whereas areas under the facility floor inspection regime guarantee only 0.12. The screen assumes that the driven wheels carry the entire weight; the actual base requires a qualified loaded deceleration that respects driven-wheel load share, configured braking current, and tip stability. For these inspected surfaces, the screen identifies a candidate restricted point at 1 m/s, whose friction premise must be logged in the enforcement telemetry. Verification trials on inspected floors must run against a physical test surface calibrated to this 0.12 threshold and must recalculate the oracles from the qualified loaded deceleration before testing the actual base.

Each physical configuration couples directly to the machine’s internal execution state. Trials must evaluate application-processor utilization driven to peak neural inference bursts, drive-bus voltages sweeping from a depleted 42 V up to a nominal 47.7 V, transmission jitter across EtherCAT or CAN FD buses, DRAM crossbar contention, and the thermal state of the safety microcontroller. The verification campaign also challenges transient edge conditions, chief among them a human takeover while the base is moving. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-clearance-deadlines|Clearance-derived fallback deadlines]]** screens a candidate in-loop takeover, with its 605 ms conservative response-and-exchange allowance, at the 0.75 m/s slow-down; the same moving handover is infeasible at the 1.3 m/s aisle speed and must be rejected. The slow-down remains a qualification candidate, conditional on operator response, admission delay, current clearance, and a feasible stopping continuation. The operative design stops the base before handing over. Both speeds therefore belong in the campaign, one to challenge a candidate moving transition and one to verify its rejection. Whenever an authority transition cannot preserve the verified stopping envelope, the enforcer must execute a local stop.

↩︎ The rule that an authority transition unable to preserve the stopping envelope ends in a local stop is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-clearance-deadlines|Clearance-derived fallback deadlines]]**.

**Definition 15.1: Operating envelope**

***Operating envelope*** is the declared set of external conditions and internal execution states, each point stated with its units and tolerances, over which a machine’s protective claim must hold and at which every trial of that claim is run.

1.  **Significance**: The envelope governs which terms of the inequality ${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$ are stressed during an empirical trial. For the running machine, an aisle speed of 1.3 m/s falls below the optimistic 1.44 m/s ceiling on a dry floor, yet exceeds the 1.23 m/s ceiling on an inspected surface. The identical velocity therefore passes the all-weight screen on one surface and fails it on another; passing still requires loaded qualification before admission.
2.  **Distinction**: The operational design domain describes the external environment encountered by the planning policy. The operating envelope expands this definition to incorporate internal machine states, including processor utilization, drive-bus voltages between 42 V and 47.7 V, and communication bus saturation. The same physical aisle yields different safety margins under memory contention or electrical depletion.
3.  **Common pitfall**: A frequent mistake is treating an operating envelope as a simple Cartesian product of decoupled intervals, assuming that testing each parameter independently at its minimum and maximum guarantees coverage. In cyber-physical systems, multi-dimensional boundary couplings are the rule rather than the exception: two parameters operating well within their nominal isolated bounds can combine to trigger an unrecoverable failure. Robust verification demands testing directly at these coupled, high-stress corners.

An operating envelope must be distinguished from the wider **survival envelope** that encloses it. Within the operating envelope, the learned policy and low-level tracking controllers regulate nominal behavior. When an external disturbance, sensor dropout, or compute stall pushes the machine across that boundary, nominal control yields to safety mechanisms that bring the robot to a bounded, safe stop before structural limits are violated. For the mobile manipulator, the survival envelope must be characterized separately for each fallback response and machine state. The standard resident stop executes controlled, powered deceleration before setting electromechanical holding brakes at zero velocity. In contrast, torque inhibit prevents commanded torque generation without necessarily electrically isolating the drive; it does not follow the controlled stopping profile or inherit its stopping-distance budget. If the remaining containment mechanisms cannot absorb the motion, structural damage may result when kinetic energy (${E_k} = \frac{1}{2} {m} {v_{\text{impact}}}^2$) exceeds what mechanical compliance and chassis end-stop dampening can absorb before hard impact. For the base in a test cell, the survival envelope is therefore the set of states from which the qualified combination of torque inhibit, passive resistance, holding mechanisms, and compliant end-stops can contain the remaining motion and energy. That boundary, not the clear distance of the stopping claim, is what the test cell’s own protection watches.

↩︎ The fallback rungs, from the controlled resident stop to hardware torque inhibit, are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-four-rungs|The four rungs of the fallback ladder]]**.

Establishing envelope boundaries requires deriving physical constraints from first principles rather than asserting arbitrary software timeouts. In rack-end operations, this boundary is governed by the stopping budget. Total stopping distance scales linearly with pre-braking latency and quadratically with vehicle speed (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^eq-body-stopping-distance|equation 2.2]]**). In the conditional design budget of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^tbl-enforcement-stopping-budget|The warehouse mobile manipulator's stopping budget]]**, a pre-brake subtotal of 133.6 ms measured from sensor capture, combined with the jerk-limited resident stop and the tracking inset, yields a partial-budget speed ceiling of 1.44 m/s on a dry floor. That ceiling holds only before the dispatch-to-admission interval is charged, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.07 Silicon Isolation Decisions#^sec-placement-dispatch-admission|The board-link budget and the dispatch-to-admission interval]]** allocated that interval without yet measuring it. Running at the candidate aisle velocity of 1.3 m/s consumes 969.0 mm of the available 1.10 m clearance. The unallocated spare of 131.0 mm, which the vehicle crosses in 100.8 ms, must still absorb that interval. Its allocation of 63.3 ms costs 82.3 mm of travel at this speed and leaves 48.7 mm, or 37.5 ms of waiting, the same remainder that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-clearance-deadlines|Clearance-derived fallback deadlines]]** found for a human takeover at aisle speed. The full 100.8 ms is therefore a ceiling, the longest dispatch-to-admission interval the spare could pay for. When later examples in this chapter quote the full spare, they quote an upper bound on the unbudgeted delay the machine can tolerate; once the allocation is charged, only the remainder is left to spend.

↩︎ The jerk-limited resident stop and its stopping distance are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-c2-stopping-suffix|The C^2 stopping suffix and jerk-limited braking]]**.

Two conditional oracle bounds follow from that budget. Before any qualification trial, the harness must show that the bounds cover the whole execution path from the reference event to rest, because an interval left unallocated is an open qualification gap rather than a margin.

![[90 Assets/Volume IV/15 Verification/Margin Renewal Onset Deadline Bar a47e2f5a1c.svg]]

*Stacked bar spanning the 82 milliseconds from the last valid renewal to powered deceleration onset: a 60 milliseconds lease, a 1 millisecond tick and a 1 millisecond bus cycle, and 20 milliseconds from delivered stop command to drive onset.*

*Of the 82 ms onset deadline, the lease takes 60 ms.*

1.  **Powered Deceleration Onset Deadline:** Powered deceleration must begin no later than 82 ms after the last valid renewal. This deadline traverses the full permission and actuation chain: the 60 ms lease duration, a single 1 ms tick of the safety permission loop, one 1 ms communication bus cycle, and the 20 ms drive latency from delivered stop command to physical deceleration onset. (For the power-rail fault scenario F4, the onset deadline is referenced instead to the assertion of the hardware undervoltage flag.)
2.  **Stopping Distance Bound:** The vehicle, tracked by external optical instruments independent of onboard odometry, must come to rest within the distance allocated from the reference event that triggers the fault. Three reference events are possible, and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.05 Fault Oracles and Acceptance#^fig-15-reference-events|figure 1]] places them on one time axis. A *capture-referenced* trial measures clearance and displacement from the exposure midpoint of a qualified sensor capture; the 969.0 mm subtotal starts there but omits the dispatch-to-admission interval, so it is incomplete until that interval is added. An *appearance-referenced* trial starts earlier, at the optical appearance of an unheralded hazard. It must timestamp that appearance and the first usable capture independently, and add the travel between them, as well as the dispatch-to-admission travel, to obtain a complete bound against the clearance measured at appearance. A *renewal-referenced* trial applies when a stalled proposer produces no new target at all; it scores both stopping distance and onset latency from the last valid lease renewal. No trial may claim a pass against an incomplete budget subtotal.

↩︎ The lease path, from the last admission through the permission tick and bus cycle to brake onset, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-lease-sizing|Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget]]**.

Measured from that event, the two F1 verification predicates are ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.03 The Operating Envelope#^eq-verification-f1-oracle|equation 1]]):

$$
{t_{\text{onset}}} \le {T_{\text{lease}}} + {T_{\text{tick}}} + {T_{\text{bus}}} + {T_{\text{act}}}, \qquad {d_{\text{F1}}}({v}) = {v}\,({T_{\text{lease}}} + {T_{\text{tick}}} + {T_{\text{bus}}} + {T_{\text{act}}}) + {d_{\text{suffix}}}({v}) + {\epsilon_{\text{track}}} \tag{1}
$$

 Here ${t_{\text{onset}}}$ is the measured interval from the last valid renewal to the physical onset of powered deceleration, ${d_{\text{F1}}}({v})$ bounds the measured travel from that renewal to rest, ${d_{\text{suffix}}}({v})$ is the jerk-limited resident stop profile from **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^eq-plan-c2-stopping-suffix|equation 11.17]]**, and ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$ is the tracking inset. The design budget’s pre-brake subtotal ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ begins earlier, at raw sensor capture, and its sum also carries the localization bound and the protective margin. Notebook [15.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/15_verification/15_verification.html#nbk-verification-reference-event-gap) evaluates both sums at the rack end.

**Napkin Math 15.1: The reference-event gap**

**Problem**: A proposer stall at the aisle speed is scored from the last valid renewal. What onset and travel bounds follow from the lease path and the resident stop, and how far below the capture-referenced subtotal of the design budget do they sit?

**Variables**:

- ${v} =$ 1.3 m/s (Chosen) and ${D_{\text{clear}}} =$ 1.10 m (Illustrative; **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^tbl-01-machine-card|The running machine's card]]**).
- ${T_{\text{lease}}} =$ 60 ms and ${T_{\text{tick}}} =$ 1 ms (Chosen); ${T_{\text{bus}}} =$ 1 ms and ${T_{\text{act}}} =$ 20 ms (Illustrative).
- ${a_{\text{brake}}} =$ 2 m/s² (Illustrative; stands in for a measured loaded deceleration) and ${j_{\max}} =$ 7.11 m/s³ (Derived; the reference stop polynomial’s peak jerk).
- ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}} =$ 40 mm and ${\delta_{\text{loc}}} =$ 50 mm (Illustrative); ${\delta_{\text{margin}}} =$ 100 mm (Chosen); evidence age ${t_{\text{age}}} =$ 51.6 ms (Derived; **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-latency-waterfall|The latency waterfall]]**).

**Math**:

*Onset bound.*

$$
t_{\text{onset}} \le T_{\text{lease}} + T_{\text{tick}} + T_{\text{bus}} + T_{\text{act}} = 60 + 1 + 1 + 20 = 82\ \text{ms}
$$

*Resident stop* (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^eq-plan-c2-stopping-suffix|equation 11.17]]**).

$$
d_{\text{suffix}}(v) = \frac{v^2}{2a_{\text{brake}}} + \frac{v\,a_{\text{brake}}}{2j_{\max}} = \frac{1.3^2}{2 \times 2} + \frac{1.3 \times 2}{2 \times 7.11} = 0.4225 + 0.1828 = 0.6053\ \text{m}
$$

*F1 oracle from the last valid renewal* ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.03 The Operating Envelope#^eq-verification-f1-oracle|equation 1]]).

$$
d_{\text{F1}}(v) = 1.3 \times 82 + 605.3 + 40 = 106.6 + 605.3 + 40 = 751.9\ \text{mm}
$$

*Capture-referenced subtotal*, which leaves 131.0 mm of the clear distance.

$$
v\,\tau_{\text{delay}} + d_{\text{suffix}} + \delta_{\text{loc}} + \delta_{\text{margin}} + \epsilon_{\text{track}} = 1.3 \times 133.6 + 605.3 + 50 + 100 + 40 = 969.0\ \text{mm}
$$

*The gap between them.*

$$
969.0 - 751.9 = 1.3 \times 51.6 + 50 + 100 = 67.1 + 150 = 217.1\ \text{mm}
$$

*Sensitivity.* In the optimistic all-weight screen at the restricted point on the inspected floor, friction caps the deceleration at 1.18 m/s²:

$$
d_{\text{F1}}(1) = 1 \times 82 + \left(\frac{1^2}{2 \times 1.177} + \frac{1 \times 1.177}{2 \times 7.11}\right)\text{m} + 40 = 82 + 507.6 + 40 = 629.6\ \text{mm}
$$

 A speed reduction of 23.1 percent shortens the oracle distance by only 16.3 percent, because the diminished traction cancels out the majority of the kinematic savings.

**Result**: F1 passes only if powered deceleration begins within 82 ms and the base travels at most 751.9 mm from the last valid renewal, 217.1 mm less than the capture-referenced subtotal.

**Systems insight**: Both formulations share the identical deceleration trajectory ${d_{\text{suffix}}}({v})$; they diverge entirely on their reference event. The design subtotal incorporates 67.1 mm of evidence-age travel alongside the localization bound and protective margin that protect a detected target. However, a stalled proposer produces no target. If the verification harness evaluated an F1 proposer stall against this generous capture-referenced subtotal, the distance predicate would tolerate a braking onset roughly 167 ms late, more than double the entire onset deadline. Verifying the invariant ${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$ remains sound only when travel is measured strictly from the physical or temporal event that the fault disrupts.

The partial budget beneath both bounds reproduces the arithmetic of the design record, serving as a conditional oracle specification rather than empirical timing closure. A physical trial must directly instrument and log successful admission timestamps, continuing evidence age, and physical deceleration onset. Nominal proposer loop cadences cannot guarantee that lease renewals succeed before the deadline expires. Because a safety lease begins strictly upon successful admission, the renewal verification campaign must qualify the maximum inter-admission interval demanded by the lease rule (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^eq-nervous-lease-rule|equation 4.4]]**) under realistic compute contention, bus saturation, and rejected proposals, rather than measuring only unvetted proposer dispatch timestamps.

Both oracle bounds also assume that the safety permission pipeline, required perception channels, and powered traction drives survive the injected fault. Capacitive hold-up for logic rails and brake coils cannot sustain the traction drives during heavy braking. The power-loss fallback (F4b) must therefore maintain and monitor a verified auxiliary power reserve and dynamic braking dissipation path throughout deceleration. If an injected fault disables these electrical reserves, the system drops into an unpowered fallback mode whose stopping envelope must be characterized independently. Neither oracle bound relies on corrective action from either learned proposer. When the chunk policy stalls, it cannot delay braking onset: the hardware safety monitor issues the stop when the lease timer expires, with physical onset following the allocated tick, bus, and actuator path, cleanly isolating safety enforcement from proposer latency. The F1 qualification test thus benchmarks the deterministic safety permission path rather than the learned policy. Finally, this stopping budget assumes a static obstacle located at the clearance boundary. Static verification trials do not validate protection against dynamic agents moving toward the machine; site crossing restrictions and occluded-motion tracking must cover that case. Declaring these qualification points alongside both formal bounds provides the complete specification required to execute an empirical verification trial.
