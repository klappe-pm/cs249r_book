# V4-15.02 The Claim a Fault Trial Must Test

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification|V4-15 Adversarial Verification]]

^sec-verification-fault-injection

The authority record at the close of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention#^sec-intervention|Supervisory Intervention]]** can log weeks of clean handovers and still say nothing about what the machine does when a duplicate writer appears on the bus, a stale packet replays, or a heartbeat drops. None of those faults occurred during normal service. Every preceding chapter of this book rests on protective claims of the same kind: a chunk lease that expires within its declared deadline, a permission path that halts the mobile base within its clear distance, or a safety microcontroller that outlasts a voltage brownout on the shared electrical supply. Because those fallback paths stay dormant until a subsystem breaks, an unexpected fault discovered in service has already crossed the causal boundary into the room, releasing kinetic energy that no software update can undo. Verification turns these design assertions into auditable evidence by provoking each fault deliberately before release, under the physical loads that make the protective response hardest to execute.

![[90 Assets/Volume IV/15 Verification/Spa Locator As 6221d32127.svg]]

*S·P·A locator triad highlighting the Act-to-Sense feedback edge.*

*A trial passes only when an independent sensor witnesses the act.*

Policy evaluation measures how capably a learned candidate policy completes tasks across declared environments. Verification, by contrast, tests the integrated machine as a whole: proposer, runtime enforcer, communication buses, power distribution network, and physical body. High task completion rates during policy evaluation do not show that an independent emergency stop engages when the neural proposer hangs; conversely, a reliable emergency stop does not show that the learned policy navigates aisles smoothly. Qualifying an embodied machine requires both kinds of evidence gathered on the same production hardware and software stack. The same distinction separates raw policy autonomy from whole-machine safety, because an enforcer applying a least-change correction can keep the robot safe during a mission while isolating and logging a hazardous proposal from the model.

⇄ **Contrast:** Policy evaluation, which scores the learned proposer’s task proficiency rather than the whole machine’s protective responses, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation#^sec-evaluation|Closed-Loop Evaluation]]**.

Treating passing assertions in a software simulator as sufficient proof of physical safety is the **digital twin delusion**.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.17 Footnotes#^fn1|¹]] A physics engine or digital emulator models timing jitter, power transients, and floor traction only to the extent that its authors anticipate and calibrate those effects; a simulated controller cannot fail on an unmodeled supply sag or bus collision. Passive operational logs invite an equally dangerous complacency: millions of incident-free operating hours show only that severe corner conditions did not arise during benign runs, leaving dormant failure modes untested.

Adversarial fault injection answers both traps by turning a design claim into a controlled disturbance executed inside an independent physical containment cell. To count as engineering evidence rather than an ad hoc stress test, a fault trial must declare its operating-envelope point, score telemetry against precommitted oracles that leave no room for after-the-fact negotiation, and preserve cryptographic records of the hardware and software state. The campaign then reports which points across the envelope those trials actually cover while the test harness records how each fault propagates across power rails, fieldbuses, and mechanical brakes.

Consider the warehouse mobile manipulator navigating an aisle at 1.3 m/s (chosen; see the Reader Guide) toward a rack end where a pedestrian may step out from a blind intersection. The learned proposer might abruptly stop renewing motion commands while the base continues rolling under momentum. An architectural authority diagram specifies which subsystem must take over, but that specification does not prove that the physical permission path survives the crash or that mechanical brakes engage before impact. An empirical trial that induces the fault and measures physical motion supplies evidence for the named protective response under its declared conditions. To structure that trial, the test harness must reflect how the machine partitions execution across its physical substrates. An application system-on-chip (SoC) equipped with an embedded accelerator executes the unprivileged learned proposers (the vision-language-action intent model and the chunk policy beneath it). A lockstep safety microcontroller executes the deterministic runtime enforcer. The safety microcontroller commands the base-drive motor inverters across a hard real-time fieldbus (EtherCAT, or Controller Area Network with Flexible Data-Rate, CAN FD) whose cyclic frames arrive on a strict, microsecond-scale schedule. Both processors draw power from the shared 47.7 V drive bus that powers the traction motors: the application SoC through a buck-boost converter, and the safety microcontroller through an isolated converter backed by a dedicated, held-up permission rail.

Weeks of uneventful operational logs at that speed say little about four severe hardware and timing faults that the architecture claims to contain:

1.  **Proposer Stall (F1):** The chunk policy, the fast proposer of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** that renews motion every 50 ms, has its latest proposal successfully admitted by the permission path, renewing its chunk lease, but immediately suffers a thread deadlock, driver lockup, or thermal throttling freeze.
2.  **Stale Observation (F2):** A camera frame or depth tensor arrives at the permission path with an aged or corrupted hardware timestamp after silent queuing in an image signal processor (ISP) buffer or direct memory access (DMA) ring.
3.  **Lost Fieldbus Frames (F3):** Electromagnetic interference (EMI) from high-current inverter switching corrupts or drops a burst of EtherCAT or CAN FD frames between the safety microcontroller and the wheel actuators.
4.  **Drive-Bus Droop and Feed Loss (F4a/F4b):** A supply injector steps the shared 47.7 V drive bus below the 33 V lockout threshold of the converter feeding the application processor under the declared drive load (F4a), or the permission rail itself loses its primary electrical feed (F4b).

These physical failure modes F1–F4, paired with the supervisory authority transfer faults A1–A4 (formalized in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.07 Deriving the Fault List#^tbl-15-fault-classification|table 2]]), organize the entire verification campaign. In theory, each threat has a dedicated countermeasure: the lease detects a hung proposer, the evidence-epoch check discards stale sensor frames, the fieldbus watchdog flags dropped communication cycles, and the isolated undervoltage supervisor manages electrical brownouts. In practice, adversarial verification injects each disturbance as the robot approaches the rack end, alongside the four authority handoff faults that the authority record hands over (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-evidence-tuples|Per-tick evidence tuples and the fault injection suite]]**). It scores every stopping trial against temporal and distance bounds derived from the machine’s own stopping budget.

An injected fault converts a physical trial into valid evidence for an auditable safety case only when the experiment satisfies the five requirements of the **cyber-physical test contract**:

1.  **A bounded claim:** The test specifies the exact invariant the machine must maintain. For the mobile base at the rack end, the machine must come to a complete stop within the 1.10 m clear distance.
2.  **Defined test conditions:** The specification bounds every relevant physical variable, including initial velocity, payload mass, floor friction, ambient operating temperature, bus traffic saturation, and DC supply voltages.
3.  **A precommitted oracle:** An automated evaluation harness inspects captured telemetry against the claim without human intervention, retrospective threshold tuning, or post-hoc excuses ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.05 Fault Oracles and Acceptance#^sec-verification-oracles-acceptance|section 15.4]] defines the oracle formally).
4.  **A stated sampling and injection procedure:** The test harness exercises the machine systematically across its operational envelope, deliberately triggering faults in sensors, buses, power rails, and actuators during peak mechanical and computational load.
5.  **Provenance:** The campaign logs complete cryptographic digests of model weights, calibration tables, sensor serial numbers, oscilloscope traces, and raw bus logs to enable matched re-execution. Software builds can be reproduced bit for bit; a physical trial can be replicated only when repeated under physical conditions that match declared tolerances.

The base’s stopping claim and the manipulator arm’s latch-force claim require separate operational envelopes, injection points, and acceptance criteria; this chapter follows the mobile base’s stopping claim. Fixing the contract starts with its second element, the declared conditions under which the claim must hold, which also locate the point beyond which nominal regulation must yield to an independent containment response.
