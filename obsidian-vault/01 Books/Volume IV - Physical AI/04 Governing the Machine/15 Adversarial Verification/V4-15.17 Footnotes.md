# V4-15.17 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification|V4-15 Adversarial Verification]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Digital Twin**: Grieves and Vickers ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-grieves2017digital|2017]]) trace the concept to a 2002 presentation on product lifecycle management, and Glaessgen and Stargel ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-glaessgen2012digital|2012]]) gave it the sense aerospace adopted, a simulation of one as-built vehicle, updated continually from that vehicle’s own sensors and history so that it mirrors the life of its flying twin. The original meaning therefore depended on continual calibration against one particular physical article. A simulator that has never been compared with a measured stop of this machine is a model, not a twin, and treating it as a twin is the delusion this chapter names.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Probe Effect**: Gait ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-gait1986probe|1986]]) identified the probe effect in concurrent software systems, where the overhead of monitoring instrumentation alters execution timing, inadvertently masking or introducing race conditions. On-chip hardware trace units, such as Arm’s CoreSight Embedded Trace Macrocell, stream cycle-accurate instruction flows over dedicated trace pins, while external protocol analyzers monitor bus transactions without altering bus arbitration. A verification trial must time the enforcer’s reaction using non-intrusive hardware instruments rather than software logging daemons executing on the processor under test.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Crowbar**: The name comes from the blunt industrial act of dropping a metal bar across power supply terminals. A crowbar circuit intentionally shorts a supply rail when an overvoltage occurs, forcing an upstream fuse or breaker to trip before hazardous energy reaches sensitive loads. Within a physical fault-injection test cell, this containment concept protects physical safety at the actuator stage: independent ground-truth sensors detecting an envelope violation trigger a hardware disconnect that severs actuator drive power over a dedicated electrical pathway that the vehicle under test can neither power nor override. Physical containment therefore never depends on the correctness of the very enforcer undergoing evaluation.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Scope of assurance standards**: ISO 26262-5 addresses road-vehicle electrical and electronic hardware. Its single-point and latent fault metrics concern the architecture and its allocated safety goals; they are not the failure-rate-weighted aggregate coverage against which notebook [15.3](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/15_verification/15_verification.html#nbk-verification-hardware-fault-injection-coverage-mean-time) later sizes an injection campaign. Fault injection is one way to test diagnostic assumptions, alongside analysis and other evidence. ANSI/UL 4600 frames an autonomous-vehicle safety case from multiple kinds of evidence. Neither standard makes a fixed number of hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) trials sufficient for release.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **STAMP and STPA**: Leveson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-leveson2004new|2004]]) proposed STAMP, which treats safety as a dynamic control problem rather than a component-reliability problem, recognizing that catastrophic accidents often arise from unsafe interactions among components that each operate strictly within specification. Leveson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-leveson2011engineering|2011]]) established the Systems-Theoretic Process Analysis (STPA) hazard-analysis method on this foundation, identifying unsafe control actions at subsystem interfaces, such as an integration seam where bus communication latency or digital filter lag erodes phase margin. Because no individual component has failed at such a seam, its unsafe control actions supply critical fault modes that inverting nominal design records cannot expose.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Adversarial Patch**: Brown et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-brown2017adversarial|2017]]) demonstrated that printing an image-independent physical patch and placing it within a visual scene forces an image classifier to ignore surrounding objects and predict the attacker’s target class. The perturbation enters entirely through the optical transducer without requiring software access, bus interception, or packet injection. In an embodied system, this attack operates wholly on the proposer’s side of the proposal boundary, where the permission path can constrain the physical consequences of an altered command but cannot observe the visual artifact that induced it.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Injection fixtures**: Power injectors switch or modulate the supply using solid-state devices, while bus injectors manipulate communication lines through high-impedance buffers. Test instrumentation must remain galvanically isolated from high-current motor transients; otherwise, witness systems will fail alongside the drive. Achievable edge rates and isolation ratings characterize the specific test fixture and must be recorded in the qualification dossier.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Bus-Off**: The Controller Area Network protocol enforces hardware fault confinement: when a controller’s transmit error count exceeds standard thresholds, it enters bus-off and ceases bus communication until cleared by an idle sequence ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-iso11898-1-2015|International Organization for Standardization 2015]]). While this prevents a damaged node from corrupting the bus, external disturbance injectors forcing bit collisions can exploit this mechanism to silence healthy nodes without a software fault. Physical fault injection must prove that the safety permission path detects this silenced node and initiates safe fallback within its timing budget.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **EtherCAT Working Counter**: As an on-the-fly datagram traverses each downstream slave, every node that successfully processes assigned process data increments a dedicated working counter field in hardware. The returning counter value informs the master within the exact cycle of transmission how many addressed devices successfully read or wrote their process data. A missing return, an invalid frame, or a working-counter mismatch provides a distinct communication-failure indication. The configured master checks can detect F3 within the cycle’s declared receive deadline, without waiting for a slave drive’s communication watchdog to expire; that detector bound and the subsequent stopping response must be qualified together.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Bayesian Optimization**: Originally developed for computationally expensive engineering simulations, Bayesian optimization fits a Gaussian-process surrogate across completed trials and selects the next sample point by maximizing expected improvement ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-jones1998efficient|Jones et al. 1998]]). Physical fault-injection rigs face the same cost structure, because each trial requires mechanical resets, hardware inspection, and thermal stabilization delays. Still, the surrogate’s confidence is only a model prediction: a region it estimates to be safe has not been physically tested simply because the surrogate predicts a positive margin.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Trial sampling and aging**: The binomial count assumes independent, identically distributed Bernoulli outcomes under the declared injection distribution; it does not require Poisson event arrivals. Repeated heating, mechanical wear, or contact changes can break independence and require a state-stratified experimental design.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Importance Sampling**: The technique originated in Monte Carlo calculations of nuclear radiation shielding in the early 1950s, where the few particles that penetrated dense shielding were exceptionally rare yet critical to safety. Kahn and Marshall ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-kahn1953methods|1953]]) formalized importance sampling alongside correlated sampling and statistical estimation to cut required sample counts. The method carries a sharp failure mode of its own: a proposal distribution that omits a valid failure path reports tiny sample variance and an erroneously low failure probability, a support failure that diagnostic checks must catch.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Return Period**: The term comes from civil hydrology, where a “hundred-year flood” means a discharge level with a 0.01 probability of being exceeded in any single year ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.16 Source References#^ref-gumbel1958statistics|Gumbel 1958]]). The hydrological caveat applies directly to robotics: because return levels extrapolate beyond observed records, they hold only when the threshold choice and sampling distribution are physically justified. Counting return periods across synthetic fault-injection runs gives no operational safety assurance until the hourly arrival rate in service is established.

^fn13
