*Why can a machine pass its normal operating tests and still fail when its protective response is needed?*

The claims that matter most in a physical machine describe what it does after something has already gone wrong, when the learned proposer stalls, an observation arrives old, a message is lost, or the supply sags while a loaded body moves toward a person. Normal operation almost never exercises those responses, so thousands of clean runs can coexist with a stopping mechanism that has never once been made to stop the machine. Without deliberate testing, the first real trial of that mechanism happens in service, where a late response delivers energy that no software correction can recall. Establishing such a claim requires creating the fault on purpose, measuring the physical response with instruments the fault cannot reach, and scoring it against acceptance conditions fixed before the test. Every condition left untested still limits what the result can support. Verification makes those limits explicit before release grants any action authority to cross the causal boundary.

**Learning Objectives**

- Derive test conditions and stopping oracles from the machine’s operating envelope
- Derive faults from design assumptions, physical hazards, authority-transfer failures, and attacks on the proposer and sensors
- Select qualification environments that exercise the causal mechanism behind each claim
- Design hardware injections with independent witnesses and precommitted acceptance conditions
- Interpret temporal safety margins and search for disturbances that violate them
- Evaluate the assumptions limiting simulation transfer, rare-event estimates, and formal proofs
- Assemble fault records that preserve failed, invalid, unobservable, and untested cases
