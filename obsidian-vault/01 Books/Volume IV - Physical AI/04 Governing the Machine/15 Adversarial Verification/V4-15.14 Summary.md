# V4-15.14 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification|V4-15 Adversarial Verification]]

^sec-verification-summary

The rack-end stopping claim begins as a physical distance and timing budget. Adversarial verification transforms this kinematic budget into a concrete, testable engineering contract by declaring the operating envelope across which it must hold, including the coupled corners that one-variable-at-a-time sweeps miss. A five-part test contract (bounded claim, declared conditions, precommitted oracle, stated injection procedure, provenance) decides whether any trial counts as evidence. Within it, each postulated fault is built from a five-element chain: the negated nominal condition, the injection point, the expected detector, the bounded physical response, and an observable pass condition scored on an independent witness. For example, when the chunk policy stalls while its host processor continues transmitting nominal heartbeats, the verification harness evaluates powered deceleration onset and travel to rest referenced strictly from the timestamp of the last valid lease renewal. An automated oracle fixed before the trial classifies each run into one of four mutually exclusive outcomes: `PASS`, `FAIL`, `INVALID`, or `UNOBSERVABLE`. Test coverage is then reported dimension by dimension, and the campaign is judged by its weakest dimension rather than by one aggregate percentage.

Developing a comprehensive fault catalog requires combining multiple analytical perspectives: inverting nominal design assumptions, tracing causal failure paths forward through physical mechanisms, evaluating authority boundaries, identifying shared root causes that induce coupled failures, and modeling adversarial disturbances at physical transducers. Some failure modes bypass communication buses and hardware sensors entirely. An adversarial physical patch, an optical spoof, or a maliciously crafted task instruction steers the learned proposer directly, testing the boundary of the proposal subsystem. Because the deterministic permission path monitors physical safety invariants rather than the semantic origin of a proposal, it intervenes only when an actuator command threatens a measured envelope limit. Consequently, such trials score strictly against the physical oracle. If an adversarial proposal induces an erratic or sub-optimal action that nonetheless remains entirely within the safe physical envelope, the physical predicates may pass, while the verification harness records the wrong action as a task-level coverage deficit rather than crediting it as a passed proposer-injection case.

Verification evidence must be gathered on the qualification rung that faithfully models the underlying physical mechanism. A software simulation cannot sag a power rail it does not model, whereas formal methods can verify compact, deterministic safety filters under explicit premises without establishing a closed-loop guarantee for the running machine’s learned proposers. On physical test benches, hardware fault injectors operate directly at interface boundaries, while instrumentation towers record ground truth using independent clocks and isolated power supplies to prevent observer coupling. During falsification, Signal Temporal Logic provides signed robustness margins that direct search algorithms toward worst-case counterexamples. However, simulated margins cannot transfer directly to physical claims without applying a discrepancy bound justified over the claimed conditions. Similarly, importance sampling converts targeted failure searches into operational failure rates only when the sampling proposal distribution is explicitly declared, covers the entire failure domain, and applies proper likelihood weights. In rare-event analysis, extreme value models support safety claims only to the extent justified by their distributional assumptions. A counterexample refutes a safety claim, whereas the absence of one leaves the unsearched domain open rather than certified. **Key Takeaways: Adversarial verification and fault injection**

- **A fault tests a physical claim:** Derive the injection, detector, response, and independent witness from the stopping or contact bound. Successful task execution alone does not exercise the protective response.
- **The environment bounds the evidence:** Select each qualification rung for the mechanism it exercises. Simulation, processor execution, electrical benches, and physical rigs contribute complementary evidence with different blind spots.
- **Search finds counterexamples, not universal passes:** Signed temporal margins help prioritize disturbances near a violation. An unsuccessful search does not establish a global minimum, and a fitted tail does not prove a hard physical limit.
- **Precommitment preserves the meaning of a pass:** Fix conditions and predicates before injection. Keep `FAIL`, `INVALID`, and `UNOBSERVABLE` distinct, because missing or invalid evidence cannot support the stopping claim.
- **Verification hands release bounded evidence:** Bind each fault record to its claim, builds, conditions, and raw measurements. Coverage gaps and model assumptions remain part of the fault records, so release can restrict authority where evidence ends.

^callout-takeaways--15-2-ad5fa39a

Under the fourth law, permission must be earned (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/V4 Governing the Machine#^pri-vol4-evidence-bounds-authority|4]]), and adversarial verification produces much of the evidence that earns it. Each fault record names the claim it supports, the envelope point it covered, and the oracle fixed before the run. The authority that the release decision can grant therefore reaches exactly as far as the passing records and stops at every `INVALID`, `UNOBSERVABLE`, or untested cell. The F1 record of this chapter shows the rule in its plainest form. Its timing path, candidate envelope point, and witnesses are specified, while its loaded braking premise remains unqualified and its outcome field is empty. Until measured trials on a physical rig with independent instrumentation fill that field, the stopping claim it describes grants no authority at all.
