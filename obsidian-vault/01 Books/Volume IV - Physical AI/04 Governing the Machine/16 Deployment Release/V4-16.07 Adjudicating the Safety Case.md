# V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release|V4-16 Deployment Release]]

^sec-release-case-worked-full

An algebraically exact stopping equation or contact-force bound still fails to protect a machine if the physical premise beneath its warrant has never been measured on hardware. Adjudicating a safety release case requires checking each warrant against the qualified measurements, justified analysis, or validated operating restrictions that support it. For the warehouse mobile manipulator, the adjudicator takes each of the six claims of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.04 Safety Cases and Claims#^tbl-16-cae-scorecard|table 2]] in turn, names the physical premise under its warrant, and grades the evidence behind that premise. This isolates the exact boundary where mathematical deduction ends and physical measurement must take over.

The door-opening branch of C1 shows the procedure in full. The contact enforcement record supplies the branch’s underlying mechanics (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|Intentional contact and in-contact energy barriers]]**). At the guarded approach velocity of 0.03 m/s, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-trip-indentation|equation 12.11]]** yields an impact force at physical braking onset of 39 N, well below the 100 N latch limit. From there, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-coupled-indentation|equation 12.12]]** leaves the latch plate with only 0.15 mm of additional allowable compression. Bringing the arm to rest within this narrow mechanical travel requires a deceleration of approximately 3 m/s² over a brief 10 ms stopping window. By contrast, at the unconstrained approach velocity of 0.10 m/s, the force at braking onset rises to 95 N, and avoiding structural damage demands a deceleration of 400 m/s², more than an order of magnitude above standard gravity. While this kinematic calculation specifies the stopping performance required for release, mathematical derivation alone cannot prove that the physical arm actuators can deliver it. Consequently, the operating envelope ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.03 Physical Criteria for Release#^tbl-16-odd-invariants|table 1]]) excludes the high-speed approach. Raising approach speeds sharply escalates contact braking demands, whereas the system’s training demonstrations offer zero empirical evidence for safe high-speed contact (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry|Choosing a tractable coverage table]]**).

*Log-scale ladder of three horizontal bars for the arm deceleration needed to stop inside the latch: 400 meters per second squared at a 0.10 meters per second approach, a gray reference bar for 1 g at 9.8, and 3 meters per second squared at the guarded 0.03 meters per second approach.*

*Raising the approach from 0.03 m/s to 0.10 m/s multiplies the arm stop needed 136-fold.*

Neither result is in doubt as arithmetic; the algebraic deduction is exact. Formal engineering release, however, cannot rest on closed-form kinematic models alone. The door branch fails authorization because its safety warrant depends on an unverified physical premise. Whether the physical manipulator, carrying its link momentum, payload mass, and reflected rotor inertia, can actually deliver 3 m/s² of deceleration within a fraction of a millimeter depends on transient joint torque limits, motor electrical time constants, and structural stiffness. Without calibrated hardware traces measuring these dynamic properties under load, this mechanical premise remains *unknown*, and the adjudicator must refuse the claim.

The remaining claim branches fail on similar grounds:

- **Base braking corridor (C1)**: The base stopping budget of 969.0 mm omits dispatch-to-admission delay and rests on illustrative values for braking deceleration, brake onset, path-tracking error, and localization uncertainty. Its remaining margin of 131.0 mm can be exhausted by the illustrative deceleration shortfall in step 4 of notebook [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/16_release/16_release.html#nbk-release-aisle-spare), and step 5 leaves still less once admission work is charged.
- **Object handover (C1)**: The mug handover branch fails on three separate premises. First, the calculated contact force falls below the criterion only for an unconstrained, free-moving hand, and it comes from a standardized biomechanical body model rather than a force measurement of this contact. Second, the evidence packet does not show that the coworker’s hand cannot be trapped against a rigid fixture, so the non-clamping geometry is unproven. Third, perceptual grounding remains unverified: a proposal that mistakes a clear glass jar for the target mug satisfies every geometric affordance check, and no trace in the runtime logs disambiguates the object.
- **Enforcer timing (C2)**: The timing warrant for the runtime safety enforcer assumes a worst-case execution time (WCET) of 250 μs against an unloaded baseline of 135 μs and a 400 μs deadline. The WCET is declared without architectural analysis and loaded stress evidence supporting it under peak memory bus saturation.
- **Traction and environmental state (C3)**: Claim C3 requires a qualified friction floor across every authorized aisle; 0.204 is only the optimistic all-weight traction screen, not a sufficient loaded stopping qualification. A clean concrete floor can be validated through baseline skid testing, but the onboard cameras cannot reliably distinguish dry concrete from a thin film of transparent oil, and no sensor on the machine can exclude liquid contamination ahead of the braking contact.
- **Perception pipeline latency (C4)**: The 51.6 ms dispatch age combines nominal datasheet readout times with uncharacterized pipeline delays rather than verified end-to-end telemetry.
- **Permission independence (C5)**: The placement record’s loaded test report, which would show the enforcer’s maxima under the proposer’s worst interference and test the common causes of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.03 Two Paths and the Resources They Share#^sec-placement-two-paths-one-die|Two Paths and the Resources They Share]]**, is described in this book but not produced. Without it, the credited enforcement isolation is unsubstantiated; even supporting isolation records would not by themselves establish statistical independence of every failure event.
- **Authority transfer (C6)**: The argument cites `PASS` fault records for authority transitions A1 through A4, and no such records exist yet. Local operation could drop the remote-channel cases, but not the local ones.

Because every claim rests on at least one unverified physical premise, the adjudicator must render a verdict of `REFUSE`. This refusal does not imply that the robot is inherently dangerous. The book’s supporting inputs are illustrative models rather than empirical validation logs, so the verdict is illustrative as well; it models how release is adjudicated and shows why ungrounded claims cannot license physical deployment. The fourth law, *permission must be earned*, requires an operational restriction wherever the available evidence cannot support a premise. Here, every branch contains an unverified premise. A principled refusal must therefore specify the exact measurements required to turn an unverified assumption into an accepted warrant. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^tbl-16-cond-measurements|Table 5]] lists these evidentiary requirements, naming the physical premise each measurement settles and the operational restriction a supporting result would lift.

^tbl-16-cond-measurements

Neural network inference latency primarily governs system availability rather than baseline safety. An inference overrun will not violate physical containment so long as the independent hardware safety path and powered fallback controllers remain operational; however, repeated deadline misses will degrade throughput and stall task progress. Conversely, resource contention across shared memory, PCIe buses, or CPU caches that delays the runtime enforcer or starves its sensor feeds represents a direct safety failure under claims C2 and C5. Finally, while local autonomous operation can safely omit validation trials for unused remote teleoperation links, deployment still requires verified software placement records, comprehensive common-cause fault coverage, and passing test records for every local authority transition.

### Worked Restricted Service with Stipulated Evidence

^sec-release-stipulated-service

Without calibrated hardware measurements, the mobile manipulator remains locked out under a `REFUSE` verdict. To see how an engineering organization moves from initial refusal to conditional deployment without relaxing its structural safety limits, consider a **stipulated** release case in which an engineering packet supplies the measured traces specified below. (The uncalibrated evidence currently available in this book continues to dictate `REFUSE`.)

The target application is *restricted inspected-route mug delivery*. The robot retrieves a declared mug from a singulated feeder and traverses a designated, surveyed warehouse aisle at a velocity capped at 1 m/s. It then pauses the mobile base to open an equipment cage latch using the guarded contact trajectory and hands the mug to a consenting human coworker under the `accept_item` primitive at 0.10 m/s. Operational authority remains strictly local. The feeder mechanism physically prevents accidental object substitutions, recognizing that singulation alone cannot authenticate identity. Similarly, strict access controls prevent personnel from stepping into the robot’s active stopping corridor during transit.

↪ The difference between a coworker who consents to a machine’s risk, as this one does, and people who bear it without choosing, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.07 Who Bears the Risk#^sec-responsibility-who-bears-risk|Who Bears the Risk]]**.

For claim C1, the safety case stipulates empirical stopping traces under full payload mass, measured tracking and localization variance, dynamic latch-stopping performance, and instrumented handover contact forces that satisfy the bounded thresholds in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^tbl-16-cond-measurements|table 5]]. For claims C2 and C5, the case stipulates independent hardware fault-injection logs and worst-case execution timing traces gathered under peak synthetic computational load. These records must account for common-cause hardware failures, support the enforcer’s permission deadlines and actuator reaction latencies, separate controlled motor deceleration from passive mechanical braking at rest, and confirm isolated drive-energy cutoff and sensor integrity under all credited fault modes.

For claim C4, the case stipulates synchronized end-to-end hardware timestamp telemetry, bounded clock skew, and a verified upper bound on dispatch-to-admission latency. This latency must be deducted from the dynamic stopping distance budget so that a strictly positive safety margin remains; a theoretical kinematic velocity ceiling cannot authorize transit speeds on its own. Because sensor data ages continuously throughout execution, the chunk lease starts only when a plan is admitted, never when it is computed. The qualification packet must also check the time between admitted plans against the lease renewal deadline rather than assuming that nominal policy inference frequency guarantees admission. For claim C6, the packet stipulates verified `PASS` audit records across every state transition exercised by the local autonomous executive, while disabling the unverified remote teleoperation interface entirely.

Claim C3 requires substantially more than visual inspection or reduced transit velocity. The case stipulates a qualified site friction survey showing a friction coefficient of at least 0.12 across the surveyed transit corridor, reinforced by active cleaning protocols, chemical containment barriers, and physical access gates that maintain floor integrity throughout the authorized inspection interval. Stopping trials at maximum payload must confirm deceleration on this specific friction surface with the loaded wheel distribution and drive settings of the signed configuration. The complete restricted stopping distance, including the verified dispatch-to-admission delay bound and a positive safety margin, must fit inside the surveyed physical clearance with a positive reserve, the same accounting as step 5 of notebook [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/16_release/16_release.html#nbk-release-aisle-spare). The operating velocity of 1 m/s sits below the partial ceiling of 1.23 m/s, but that comparison is only an initial feasibility filter. The active transit permit is revoked immediately upon any reported spill, expired maintenance inspection, or perimeter breach. If unmonitored oil or other fluid can enter the corridor during the deployment window, the stipulation is invalidated and the verdict reverts to `REFUSE`. A lower speed cannot repair that premise, because the restricted speed was sized for the inspected friction floor of 0.12, not for a film near 0.05 that removes most of it.

The task capability packet is similarly stipulated. Before evaluation begins, the facility operator defines binding acceptance criteria in the verification plan: a minimum mission completion rate, an upper bound on end-to-end cycle time, and a maximum allowable operator intervention rate, accompanied by sample-size requirements, confidence intervals, and statistical decision rules. A successful mission requires maintaining object identity and physical integrity from retrieval through coworker handover. Cycle time accounting includes transient safety stops, crossing delays, and autonomous replanning; human rescues and aborted deliveries are tracked as explicit mission failures. The safety case stipulates that closed-loop trials of this exact restricted configuration satisfy all three benchmarks. The qualification dossier separates raw neural policy autonomy, system-level task success, and safety interventions into independent metrics, the separation **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.03 What a Physical Success Rate Measures#^sec-evaluation-what-measured|What a Physical Success Rate Measures]]** drew between the policy’s own completions and the completions that protection made possible. Minimal-intervention safety filtering may run during nominal task execution; its clamps are counted separately, so they neither mask the policy’s shortcomings nor count as safety violations.

With these stipulated evidence packets in hand, the adjudicator can sign a verdict of `COND` (conditional release) for *restricted inspected-route mug delivery*. The physical records support claims C1 through C6 solely within the restricted operating envelope, while the task evaluation shows acceptable operational utility under identical constraints. The adjudicator binds both the physical safety dossier and the capability evaluation to the release manifest, leaving full-speed autonomous transit and remote teleoperation unauthorized. The result is a conditional safety case, not a blanket deployment permit. Invalidating any underlying assumption or breaching a boundary condition immediately returns the verdict to `REFUSE`; conversely, gathering qualified operational telemetry and physical measurements over time can justify expanding the envelope toward full-scale release.

**Checkpoint 16.2: Refusal and the path to conditional release**

Before approving a conditional release of the machine, verify your understanding of how a premise, rather than a calculation, decides a claim:

**Premise vs. arithmetic**: Can you explain why the calculated latch force at physical braking onset is not in doubt while the door branch of C1 is still refused?

**Unsupported approach**: Can you use **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-trip-indentation|equation 12.11]]** and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-coupled-indentation|equation 12.12]]** to show why the operating envelope admits only the guarded door approach?

**Refusal as a deliverable**: Can you name, for one refused claim, the measurement that would lift it and the restriction that would remain after a supporting result?

**Measured-deceleration estimate**: Suppose loaded stopping trials put the low tail of the deceleration at 1.6 m/s² under the same jerk bound. Can you rerun notebook [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/16_release/16_release.html#nbk-release-aisle-spare) at 1.3 m/s and decide whether that aisle speed can still be signed once ${\Delta_{\text{da}}}$ is charged?
