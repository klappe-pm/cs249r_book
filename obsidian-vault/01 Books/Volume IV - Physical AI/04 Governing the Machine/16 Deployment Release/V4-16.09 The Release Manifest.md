# V4-16.09 The Release Manifest

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release|V4-16 Deployment Release]]

^sec-release-manifest

A safety case that lives only in static documents or procedural sign-off sheets cannot stop a robot from booting with an unverified policy checkpoint or stale sensor calibrations. To make the verdict enforceable on hardware, the adjudicator’s decision is recorded in the **release manifest**, the fourteenth record in the book’s record chain. This signed, tamper-evident data structure binds every upstream engineering contract, the Goal Structuring Notation argument graph, the operating envelope limits, and live standing conditions to the adjudicator’s verdict by cryptographic digest. The target platform refuses physical motion whenever runtime binary images, silicon attestation measurements, or underlying qualification data diverge from these signed digests.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]]

↪ The residual-claims register, the record that follows the release manifest and ties each premise it leaves open to an operational restriction, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18.04 The Epistemic Frontier#^sec-frontier-residual-claims-register|A residual-claims register]]**.

The release manifest gathers the engineering contracts established throughout this book. Each upstream record defines an operating boundary or contributes empirical data that the safety case must check; a signature on an isolated contract does not validate the system as a whole. Instead, the manifest links reviewed snapshots of these records into a single argument graph:

1.  **Physical plant and timing base:** The limit record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.08 Measuring a Machine’s Own Limits#^sec-body-limit-record-schema|The six elements of a limit record]]**) documents torque, velocity, and thermal dissipation bounds alongside their qualification data. The proposer’s payload requirements (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-proposer-information|Information the proposer must supply]]**) specify candidate action horizons that feed the handoff record rather than standing as an isolated contract. The handoff record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^sec-nervous-handoffs-matrix|The Handoffs Matrix]]**) establishes deterministic fieldbus jitter, cycle deadlines, and real-time communication premises.
2.  **Data, policy, and behavioral bounds:** The provenance record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^sec-data-schema|The Dataset Schema]]**) captures dataset calibrations, sensor configurations, and operating-domain coverage. The policy manifest (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^sec-training-policy-manifest|The Policy Manifest]]**) binds frozen neural network weights to the empirical behavioral bounds proven for that candidate model. The evaluation record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|The Evaluation Record]]**) details closed-loop task success rates, human intervention tallies, and stress-tested operational conditions.
3.  **Perception, belief, and planning:** The observation contract (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.09 The Observation Contract#^sec-perception-observation-contract|The Observation Contract]]**) enforces observation freshness and multi-sensor synchronization windows. The belief record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.04 The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants#^sec-memory-handoff|The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants]]**) specifies geometric state estimates, state covariance, and validity domains. The intent lease (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.05 The Intent Handoff and Bounded Authority#^sec-intent-handoff|The Intent Handoff and Bounded Authority]]**) records task objectives, spatial tolerances, and preemption conditions. The trajectory record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-trajectory-contract|The Trajectory Record]]**) encodes motion profiles, kinematic seam continuity, execution deadlines, and safe stopping continuations.
4.  **Deterministic containment, execution, and verification:** The enforcement record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.12 The Enforcement Record#^sec-enforcement-admitted-command-schema|The admitted command schema]]**) logs the command stream admitted by runtime safety filters, cleanly separated from the signed enforcement contract that specifies barrier functions and fallback triggers. The placement record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.09 The Placement Record#^sec-placement-hardware-allocation|The Placement Record]]**) documents the qualified compute scheduling, memory isolation, and processor affinity. The authority record (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-authority-log|The Authority Record]]**) documents arbitration switches and fallback outcomes. The fault records (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.12 Fault Records for Release#^sec-verification-fault-manifest|Fault Records for Release]]**) index tested fault-injection scenarios alongside known coverage gaps.

Beyond linking these upstream contracts, the release manifest adds its own fields. It carries the root claim, the cryptographic digest of the Goal Structuring Notation argument graph, and binary hashes across all deployed software (spanning learned policy weights, the deterministic enforcer logic in both nominal and restricted modes, and the real-time operating system kernel). It also carries sensor calibration tables, the adjudicator’s digital signature, mandatory standing operational conditions, an accumulated lifecycle wear budget of operating hours and load cycles, and a calendar expiration timestamp. All evidence links must resolve to these exact reviewed record digests, organized hierarchically beneath a single evidence root structured as a Merkle hash tree ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-merkle1988digital|Merkle 1988]]). Each parent digest is computed from its children’s digests, so changing an evidence leaf changes the root that the manifest signs. Only when the hardware root of trust authenticates this complete cryptographic tree will it permit the motor power stages to energize. While traditional release notes or post hoc compliance certificates summarize safety after the fact, the release manifest is an active, machine-checked gate. The robot checks it before closed-loop control begins, and its runtime monitors revoke motor tracking within a bounded detection-and-response window if any underlying evidence or environmental condition expires.

↪ **Byte layout:** The release manifest’s fields, types, and digests are given in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-release|Release manifest]]**.

[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.09 The Release Manifest#^fig-16-release-record|Figure 3]] routes every release attempt through authentication and physical standing checks before an operating permit can issue. The release gate authenticates the manifest, binary images, and complete set of fault records before verifying current sensor calibration, mechanical maintenance thresholds, and operational design domain conditions. Only an exact cryptographic and physical match enables the plant’s certified startup sequence. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.09 The Release Manifest#^tbl-16-release-authorization|Table 6]] lists the gate result for each combination of signed case and standing check, including the single path by which stale calibration still reaches `COND`. A separately signed `COND` case can tolerate a named stale calibration only when its restricted envelope either locks out the motions that depend on that sensor, such as prohibiting base transit when the tote-rack scale is stale, or widens the uncertainty allowance and derates speed to cover worst-case calibration drift. During active motion, any breached condition commands an assessed safe fallback before any switch of software partition (the A/B banks of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-modular-safety-cases|section 16.9.1]]) or electrical isolation.

↪ **Protocol:** The gate’s step-by-step authorization protocol is given in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-release-protocol|Release-gate protocol]]**.

Each empirical evidence leaf in the assurance Merkle tree must identify its raw sensor trace, instrumentation hardware, calibration log, machine configuration, and automated test harness script. Analytical leaves retain their derivations and assumptions; procedural leaves retain the accountable procedure and its validation for the signed envelope. Measured latency profiles enter the safety case as empirical percentiles, whereas deterministic real-time execution guarantees demand the analytically justified upper bounds specified in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^sec-nervous-deterministic-fieldbuses|Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism]]**. Similarly, collaborative object-handover criteria require load-cell time series recorded against standardized anthropomorphic surrogates across specified contact geometries. By cryptographically anchoring these primary records, an independent auditor or verification engine can reproduce the experimental test suite and pinpoint the exact operating boundaries where each safety claim ceases to hold.

The release manifest bounds the physical envelope and hardware configuration under which its verdict remains valid. Modifying a neural policy checkpoint, altering a real-time thread priority, updating a device driver, or replacing an actuator with an unverified component revision immediately invalidates the manifest, requiring a re-adjudicated safety case. On the warehouse mobile manipulator, for example, the manifest enforces signed wear thresholds on harmonic-drive backlash and electromagnetic brake lining thickness derived from stopping-distance and payload trials. Operating envelope tripwires trigger immediate refusal: on-board load cells detect tote-rack overloading beyond rated payload capacity, while facility procedures prevent operation on unmapped or unsurveyed floor surfaces ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.03 Physical Criteria for Release#^tbl-16-odd-invariants|table 1]]). The manifest also encodes the isolated, energy-buffered power rail of the permission path as a standing invariant, running a diagnostic self-test on the hold-up capacitor bank before granting boot authorization. Whenever a runtime sensor detects that a state variable has breached a signed threshold, the hardware permission path interrupts closed-loop trajectory tracking within its certified detection-and-response deadline and brings the system to an assessed fallback state.

To preserve traceability across time, the manifest binds all operational telemetry directly to the exact hardware-software revision that generated it. High-resolution local ring buffers capture boundary excursions for post-incident diagnostics, whereas fleet-wide telemetry summaries reveal systematic mechanical drift across hundreds of operating hours. The curation pipelines examined in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-data-curation-flywheel|section 16.9.5]] must preserve this cryptographic lineage whenever field data is promoted to support a later release case.

During daily operation, edge telemetry logs motor torque residuals, control-loop cycle times, and safety enforcer interventions for trend analysis, while the hardware permission path checks physical invariants on every 1 ms permission tick. If a routine maintenance check measures a stopping distance exceeding the signed clearance budget, or if a camera calibration certificate expires, the release verdict is revoked automatically. Re-flashing an older firmware image cannot compensate for worn mechanical friction pads. Unless an engineer deploys a separately signed restricted case (`COND`) that explicitly accommodates that degraded physical state, the controller executes an assessed emergency stop and locks out autonomous motion until certified maintenance and re-adjudication take place ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.09 The Release Manifest#^tbl-16-release-authorization|table 6]]).

Once its powered stop has brought the base to rest, the warehouse mobile manipulator can reach a quiescent state only after the arm and payload are supported and the assessed holding brakes are engaged before drive power is removed. The base rests stably on the floor, but that support alone does not secure its gravity-loaded arm. For other morphologies, severing motor power is itself the hazard, because the machine collapses under gravity once torque is removed (perspective [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/16_release/16_release.html#capstone-release-humanoid-crucible)).

**Systems Perspective 16.1: When removing torque causes the harm**

**Question**: Which verdict applies when removing torque causes the harm?

A supported wheeled base at rest eliminates its translational kinetic hazard, though its arm and payload still require qualified support. In contrast, an upright bipedal humanoid retains substantial gravitational potential energy. While resting in a support gantry before standing up, a `REFUSE` verdict safely holds the robot in its mechanically supported state. Once balanced upright, however, revoking locomotion authority cannot simply de-energize the actuators without inducing an immediate dynamic fall; safe shutdown requires an active, validated arrest or controlled descent strategy.

- **Torque removal can cause a fall.** An unsupported biped with backdrivable transmissions will collapse if motor currents drop to zero: torque removal leaves gravity-loaded joints without support (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.07 Actuation Authority#^sec-nervous-actuation-authority|Actuation Authority]]**), and an upright biped without active balance diverges like an inverted pendulum (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^sec-nervous-coupled-budget|Coupling the distance, lease, and supply budgets]]**). Consequently, such physical plants cannot treat torque removal as a fail-safe stopping condition unless accompanied by certified passive catches, lock-up brakes, or an external tether.
- **The fall-arrest response is itself an actuation.** Executing a controlled squat, brace, or step-down to bring the robot to rest is itself an active trajectory that requires validated sensorimotor claims, verified timing margins, and fault-tolerance guarantees. In this regime, executing a safe `REFUSE` action depends on the very control systems maintaining upright stability.
- **Wear revokes the running permit.** Degradation in foot-sole rubber traction or increased backlash in planetary gearheads alters the dynamic margin required for emergency balancing. When telemetry signals that physical wear has crossed the threshold certified in the safety case, the runtime supervisor must revoke high-speed dynamic locomotion well before the safety-critical arrest maneuvers degrade below their qualifying safety margins.

**Checkpoint 16.3: Deriving a verdict from your own measurements**

[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^tbl-16-cond-measurements|Table 5]] lists the measurements needed to support a release case but supplies no raw trial results. Make a copy with columns for value, source, provenance, and premise status, fill those columns with empirical data from hardware trials on your own robotic platform or an illustrative benchmark scenario, and evaluate the resulting release verdict:

**Provenance and premise status**: For each row, record measured, derived, datasheet, or illustrative provenance; label selected operating requirements as chosen. A derived result retains its inputs and assumptions and remains illustrative when those inputs are illustrative. A selected substitute for an unmeasured brake value is illustrative, whereas a chosen speed cap needs qualified evidence that it fits. A manufacturer’s rating is datasheet evidence, not a measurement of this unit. Separately apply the Reader Guide’s five premise statuses: established by measurement or derivation, assumed, decided by accountable judgment, unsupported, or outside the threat model. Explain why each credited assumption or restriction is warranted for the signed envelope; a status label grants no authority. Runtime known-true, known-false, and unknown describe current envelope membership, not provenance.

**Verdict derivation**: A `COND` case requires adequate support for every relied-on premise: qualified measurements, justified analysis, or validated operating restrictions. Illustrative values alone still support only an actual `REFUSE`. Recompute the base’s budget and spare margin from your values. If every relied-on premise has adequate support, name the envelope the `COND` case signs and the premise that keeps the normal mode unsigned. If a relied-on premise lacks adequate support, derive the verdict the table *would* support given the missing evidence, and label that verdict hypothetical.

**Artifact label**: Mark the filled table as your own work, whose values license no physical machine.

A release manifest binds the verdict to an explicit, reviewed system configuration. Any later modification, whether retraining a perception head, tightening an actuator torque limit, or swapping a camera lens, breaks that closure and reopens some of the underlying claims. The question the rest of the chapter answers is which ones, and how the machine stays contained while the reopened claims are requalified.
