# V4-16.08 Adversarial Review

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release|V4-16 Deployment Release]]

^sec-release-adversarial-review

Confirmation bias steers an engineering team’s data collection toward nominal conditions where learned models succeed, leaving edge-case hazards untested until live operation exposes them. Suppose the measurements listed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^tbl-16-cond-measurements|table 5]] arrive and, as in the stipulated branch, appear to support a conditional (`COND`) authorization for the mobile manipulator. Before signing, the safety adjudicator submits the safety case to an independent **adversarial review** tasked with falsifying its warrants. Rather than ratifying a brief, the reviewer actively searches for unsupported warrants, uncharacterized physical transitions, and environmental edge cases that violate the release contract. If an argument collapses on paper when an adversary probes for physical violations, the machine cannot be expected to withstand the hazards of live service.

The review interrogates every evidentiary node in the argument tree using four structured probes. The first challenges **empirical sufficiency**: whether the physical trials reached the extremes of the operating envelope. Stopping trials with the loaded base on dry concrete, at a friction coefficient near 0.60, support the warrant that the base achieves its certified deceleration under ideal conditions. The restricted operational mode, however, admits facility floors that the site’s inspection regime certifies only down to 0.12, such as an aisle polished smooth by traffic. In those zones, the base may run at the restricted velocity 1 m/s, below the 1.23 m/s ceiling. If the trial set contains no loaded stops on such degraded floors at that speed, the warrant for the restricted mode is unsupported. A coverage audit finds on paper the gap that unvetted service would otherwise find against a warehouse rack. The reviewer also checks whether validation trials covered transient execution states across an A/B partition swap, when a newly flashed software bank takes over from the incumbent one ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-modular-safety-cases|section 16.9.1]]). The same check applies to cold-start sensor characteristics before cameras and inertial measurement units reach thermal equilibrium. It applies as well to the operating boundaries of canary units, the small subset of the fleet that first runs a candidate policy ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-candidate-staging|section 16.9.2]]). Finally, the reviewer asks whether site procedures actually keep fluid films out of the corridor throughout the authorized interval, since listing oil outside the envelope does not exclude it from the floor.

The second probe examines **domain transfer**, targeting claims where synthetic simulation substitutes for direct physical measurement. A vision-based human detector may achieve high recall across synthetic, ray-traced warehouse environments, yet fail on physical hardware when encountering coworkers partially obscured by stretch-wrapped pallets whose specular reflections blind the camera’s auto-exposure algorithm. Benchmark recall on curated offline datasets is an indirect proxy metric that scores isolated frames rather than the closed-loop physical causal chain running from visual detection to an arrested base. Likewise, a simulation that omits linkage compliance or fieldbus jitter cannot establish tolerance to those physical mechanisms. The reviewer demands empirical evidence from paired hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) trials on target SoC devices connected to chassis dynamometers[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn8|⁸]] and physical bus emulators under real facility illumination, dust, and reflective geometry.

The third probe attacks **structural independence**, asking whether the learned model and safety enforcer share hardware resources whose contention could delay sensor delivery or actuation cutoff. On modern robotics edge platforms where neural inference engines and real-time safety enforcers share a single system-on-chip die, internal crossbar interconnects, shared caches, and DRAM memory controllers, unconstrained direct memory access (DMA) transfers from large model forward passes can saturate memory channels. This memory contention induces tail latency spikes that starve deterministic safety enforcer execution ticks, directly invalidating the 51.6 ms observation-age chain of C4. Stress tests and hardware timing analysis under peak synthetic compute and bus loads must establish the worst-case interference bound; unit tests on an otherwise idle bus provide zero evidence of non-interference. The reviewer also checks whether writing frozen edge telemetry ring buffers to non-volatile storage competes with real-time fieldbus DMA buffers across the internal bus fabric. The reviewer inspects watchdog reset circuits for single points of failure and verifies whether the supercapacitor power hold-up circuit supplies enough energy to flush in-flight telemetry buffers to persistent flash when an emergency stop opens the main DC bus contactors.

The fourth probe identifies **unmonitored state drift**. Mechanical wear alters gearbox compliance[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn9|⁹]] and disrupts the dynamic relationship between commanded motor torque and physical joint motion. The reviewer therefore demands paired wear-state braking and contact traces before accepting the arm-stop premise of the door branch of C1 ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^sec-release-case-worked-full|section 16.6]]). A static motor-current threshold alone cannot prove that a mechanically worn drive train remains inside certified force and stopping limits. The reviewer also checks whether over-the-air updates preserve the persistent hardware calibration partition intact across schema migrations, or whether an OS reflash can silently reset camera extrinsics and joint zeroes to unverified factory defaults. Finally, the probe checks whether thermal bracket motion stays inside the localization allowance; fresh timestamps and unchanged calibration files on disk cannot establish that physical fact.

Reviewers turn these vulnerabilities into concrete tests by constructing explicit defeaters for each warrant. The hardest failure modes to catch combine minor anomalies that each stay inside their own tolerance bands, the compound faults that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.07 Deriving the Fault List#^sec-verification-compound-faults|Compound faults and coupled disturbances]]** derives from shared causes. Consider the mobile manipulator’s navigation camera operating with three simultaneous sub-threshold degradations. A light film of dust coats its lens. A mount whose fasteners relaxed within tolerance vibrates as the base crosses floor joints. An interconnect harness flexes at those joints and elevates the bit-error rate across its serializer/deserializer (SerDes) link. Each individual check reads normal. The optical transmission score exceeds its 85 percent alarm threshold. The mount deflection registers below its 0.15-degree hardware fault limit. SerDes cyclic redundancy check error counters remain below their warning tripwire. Their timing effects remain contained only if the independent permission path strictly enforces the event-specific observation-age and clock-uncertainty checks of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.09 The Observation Contract#^sec-perception-observation-contract|The Observation Contract]]** and retains its validated response time. Any hardware or bus interference that defeats that deterministic path is a safety violation under C5, not a benign availability-only event.

The same anomalies compound within spatial estimation. Dimmed contrast reduces tracked visual features, mount vibration adds motion blur and persistent pose bias, and frame drops extend intervals of uncorrected dead reckoning. A systematic pose bias can quietly erode the localization allowance without forcing estimated filter covariances or innovation residuals to breach trip thresholds. The release reviewer therefore challenges the combined error against the qualified uncertainty bound, rather than inferring containment from individually passing scalar diagnostics. A breach of the combined bound consumes part of the stopping reserve and invalidates the release warrant; exhausting the entire stopping margin requires a quantified combined-error scenario. Statistical residual monitoring protects against only the specific faults its qualification shows it can detect, leaving site operational restrictions or independent physical checks to cover the remaining biases.

The review also audits the execution integrity of the permission path, checking whether inference loops or background services can bypass, corrupt, or starve the enforcer or human overrides, the latter covered by claim C6 of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.04 Safety Cases and Claims#^tbl-16-cae-scorecard|table 2]]. Below the proposal boundary, both safety monitors and manual overrides maintain non-preemptible execution priority over learned models. The reviewer asks concrete systems questions: whether a runaway policy thread can exhaust shared memory channels, whether an accelerator-runtime exception can lock a kernel spinlock, and whether a telemetry logging burst can brown out a regulated power rail. The reviewer accepts **permission path** independence, and the held-up power rail that carries it on the mobile manipulator, only after reviewing physical fault-injection trials. These trials must show survival under bus saturation, active feed removal, and an abruptly killed proposer task (fault F1), and they must appear in the signed fault records, because C2 claims only the faults the permission path demonstrably survives. A user-space implementation without qualified scheduling, resource isolation, and response mechanisms cannot supply the justified execution bound and fault isolation the reviewer requires.

When an adversarial probe succeeds, the case cannot be salvaged by editing claim descriptions or inserting arbitrary safety margins. The broken warrant is traced to its root systems failure (an uncharacterized physical boundary, an inadequate dataset, or an unmodeled bus latency), and the defect returns to the owning engineering subsystem. For example, a motor-current observer that fails under gearbox compliance returns to the mechanical specification as wear limits and runtime compliance estimation, while bus contention that delays the enforcer returns to the compute architecture as memory partitioning and real-time scheduling. The case returns to the engineering team with its broken warrants named, and only updated boundaries, re-derived stopping distances, and renewed verification reopen formal adjudication.

Adversarial review must also recur periodically, because a case accepted once can erode in service without any single discrete event forcing a new audit. The *normalization of deviance* names that operational erosion, in which anomalies that cause no immediate harm come to be treated as acceptable operating noise rather than as evidence against a warrant ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-leveson2011engineering|Leveson 2011]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-vaughan1996challenger|Vaughan 1996]]) (war story [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/16_release/16_release.html#ws-release-challenger-experience-base-as-boundary)).[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn10|¹⁰]] On the mobile manipulator, this erosion unfolds silently across months of fleet operation. Localization residuals creep toward the edge of their tolerance. Observation-age watchdog trips are dismissed as nuisance stops. Stopping distances end slightly longer than the initial trials did. Enforcer trips are cleared on the floor without a trace review. When thresholds are widened, margins trimmed for throughput, and alarms suppressed because nothing has yet gone wrong, the gap between the signed envelope and the machine’s actual physical behavior widens until a routine disturbance exhausts what remains. The defense is to fix each watched quantity’s threshold and response before service begins, as the safety performance indicators of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-data-curation-flywheel|section 16.9.5]] do, so that a run of harmless readings cannot move it.

**War Story 16.1: Challenger and experience limits (1986)**

**Context**: The Space Shuttle’s solid rocket motors were assembled from cylindrical steel segments whose field joints were sealed by primary and secondary O-rings ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-rogers1986challenger|Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident 1986]]). No learned model appears in this chain. The adjudication failure it records is the exact failure this chapter’s verdicts exist to prevent: a launch approved for conditions that the evidence did not cover.

**Mechanism**: The ambient temperature at launch was $36^\circ\text{F}$, 15 degrees colder than any previous launch. O-ring resiliency falls with temperature, and a cold ring returns to shape too slowly to follow the joint gap as ignition pressure opens it. On the eve of launch, engineers at Morton Thiokol, the motor’s contractor, recommended against launching below $53^\circ\text{F}$, the O-ring temperature of the coldest previous flight, whose joints had shown the worst blow-by to date. That limit was the outer edge of flight experience, not a qualified bound. NASA managers countered with a qualification temperature that applied to the propellant’s bulk temperature rather than to the joint seals.

**Impact**: A combustion gas leak through the right booster’s aft field joint, beginning at or shortly after ignition, weakened or penetrated the External Tank. The vehicle broke up about 73 seconds after launch, and all seven crew members died.

**Response**: The presidential Commission attributed the failure to “a faulty design unacceptably sensitive to a number of factors,” and temperature heads its list of those factors. It recommended that the joint be changed and that the new design be certified by tests “over the full range of operating conditions, including temperature.”

**Systems lesson**: Thiokol management reversed its engineers’ recommendation at the urging of NASA’s Marshall Space Flight Center. Thiokol’s vice president of engineering testified, “We had to prove to them that we weren’t ready.” The burden of proof had inverted: rather than requiring proof that the joint was safe inside its evidence, adjudicators demanded proof that it was unsafe outside the evidence, where no data existed. The Commission also found that O-ring erosion had recurred on earlier flights and that NASA and Thiokol “accepted escalating risk apparently because they ‘got away with it last time’” ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-rogers1986challenger|Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident 1986]]), so the flight record itself had been read as evidence of safety. A release verdict keeps the burden where the envelope trichotomy puts it. A condition outside the evidence, such as a seal temperature below all flight experience, is unknown, and the verdict refuses it.

The Tempe collision analyzed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.09 When the Responsibilities Fail Together#^sec-boundary-when-fails|When the Responsibilities Fail Together]]** rested on an unmeasured premise of the same kind as Challenger’s seal temperature. Its vehicle operator was the countermeasure the whole system leaned on, so the premise was that the operator was watching, and nothing measured whether that held. A release case that relies on such a premise must bind its operational response, whether an outright refusal or a restricted case, to verified physical limits, as claim C6 does for authority transfer. Even a case that survives this adversarial review has had its warrants tested only in principle while its verdict still lives on paper. The release manifest turns the surviving argument and the verdict it supports into a signed, tamper-evident record that the machine itself checks before it moves.
