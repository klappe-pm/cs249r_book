# V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release|V4-16 Deployment Release]]

^sec-release-deployment-lifecycle

Continuing the stipulated warehouse branch, suppose the mobile manipulator’s restricted mug-delivery policy is retrained to reduce hesitation at the controlled pick station. Pushing that updated checkpoint directly to the fleet would invalidate the signed manifest and expose the machine to unverified bus loads and contact trajectories. Before the candidate neural policy can issue commands to physical actuators, it must pass back through the release decision. A signed safety case establishes which historical evidence remains valid, which hardware-in-the-loop tests must run again, and how the machine maintains physical containment throughout the update.

↩︎ Every updated checkpoint is a new policy whose earlier evaluations no longer apply, the rule established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.07 Real-Robot Fine-Tuning and Residual Control#^sec-training-real-machine-tuning|Real-Robot Fine-Tuning and Residual Control]]** that gives each one its own manifest and regression evidence.

### Modular Safety Cases and Over-The-Air Updates

^sec-release-modular-safety-cases

Retraining a policy directly alters capability evidence, and neural modifications often introduce side effects across the shared hardware platform: increased neural accelerator memory bus traffic, altered sensor frame processing latency, or modified contact force trajectories. An engineer must trace these cross-subsystem dependencies before deciding which containment records remain valid. While **over-the-air (OTA) updates** swap out a named software payload, **modular safety cases** isolate dependencies by recording how each claim relies on specific evidence items and platform assumptions ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-denney2015dynamic|Denney et al. 2015]]). Whenever an update alters an underlying dependency, the modular case shows exactly which safety claims are reopened.

The containment argument is the defense against physical harm. It binds together the plant model, physical sensor limits, worst-case execution budgets, and the independent permission path. Key engineering premises include the bounded tracking error tube ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$, the emergency stopping budget, the passivity barrier for physical contact, and hardware watchdog timing margins. Historical verification records for these containment claims remain valid only if the proposed update preserves the exact operating conditions under which they were measured. For example, replacing a backbone network with a higher-capacity model can increase memory bus contention or cause voltage droop on the power rail even if the deterministic safety-filter binary remains byte-for-byte identical. An unchanged enforcer binary does not imply an unchanged containment argument; its guarantees remain bounded by the validated dynamic envelope ${\mathcal{C}}$ and the system-level timing margins established during qualification.

↩︎ The tracking-error tube ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$ that the containment argument consumes as an invariant is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.03 The Tracker Handoff and Dynamic Reference Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-handoff|The Tracker Handoff and Dynamic Reference Envelopes]]**.

In parallel, the capability argument shows that the robot reliably fulfills its mission within its certified operational design domain. Candidate benchmarking, passive shadow traces, and geofenced closed-loop trials test whether a retrained policy reduces hesitation or improves task throughput. While the low-level enforcer can continuously clamp or reject invalid control proposals to maintain safety, an enforcer that constantly intervenes degrades the robot into an unusable, halting machine. Release engineering therefore evaluates capability and containment together. An update qualifies for fleet dispatch only when the candidate configuration delivers acceptable task performance without undermining the empirical premises supporting its containment envelope.

Modern physical AI platforms preserve configuration integrity during deployment through **dual A/B OS partitioning**. The update agent streams the candidate image onto the inactive storage bank (Bank B) while the incumbent controller continues executing from the active bank (Bank A). Activating the newly flashed partition requires an assessed stationary update state. The robot must be parked with its payload supported. Battery reserves must exceed with margin the energy needed for re-flashing and post-boot self-tests. The independent safety supervisor must hold all motor drives unpowered or mechanically locked so the robot cannot move during bootloader cutover. Hardware watchdogs govern the runtime decision to commit or abort this cutover ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^sec-release-verdicts-ota-watchdogs|section 16.9.4]]). A single fleetwide signing key is a single point of compromise, because an attacker who extracts that key from one compromised endpoint could sign arbitrary firmware for every robot in the field. Update architectures therefore use role-separated signing structures where distinct root, targets, and release keys live on separate, hardware-isolated machines. The Update Framework (TUF) pioneered this design, and Uptane extended it to automotive and robotics systems where heterogeneous electronic control units require independently authenticated firmware images ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-samuel2010survivable|Samuel et al. 2010]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-kuppusamy2016uptane|Kuppusamy et al. 2016]]).

Overwriting firmware must never destroy the physical calibration history of an individual robot. **Persistent hardware calibration parameter retention** separates machine-specific measurements from generic operating system binaries, preventing a **calibration clashing failure**. Camera intrinsic matrices (${\mathbf{K}}$), extrinsic sensor transformations ($[{\mathbf{R}} \mid {\mathbf{t}}]$), kinematic joint zero-point offsets, and motor torque constants (${K_t}$) reflect the mechanical and electrical geometry of an individual machine and drift over time with vibration, thermal cycling, and wear. Storing these values in a dedicated, wear-leveled, non-volatile parameter partition keeps calibration intact when an OS image is flashed. When a firmware update introduces a revised parameter schema, the migration utility writes the transformed schema into a new record while preserving the legacy block, allowing direct rollback to the prior image-parameter pair. Cryptographic checksums verify the bit-level integrity of these stored calibration blocks, while physical self-tests at field release verify that the stored parameters still match the physical machine.

Finally, **forensic traceability** links every running OS image, policy checkpoint, and calibration partition to its training provenance, compiler flags, and reviewed HIL regression records through the signed release manifest ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.09 The Release Manifest#^sec-release-manifest|section 16.8]]). When an unexpected field intervention or trajectory excursion occurs, these links let incident investigators reconstruct the exact configuration running at that millisecond and determine which safety warrants need re-examination. While a cryptographic digest confirms identity, isolating the physical root cause requires detailed sensor traces and causal analysis.

### Staging Candidate Policies Before Release

^sec-release-candidate-staging

Software simulation models timing and electrical behavior only to the accuracy of its mathematical approximations; it cannot catch memory-bus contention, fieldbus noise, or inverter thermal sag on the real processor and drive electronics. A candidate policy must therefore repeat the **hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) regression gates** affected by its changes on a physical test bench under full thermal and electrical load. The bench tests the specific interference and fault mechanisms on which release claims depend:

1.  **Worst-case execution time (WCET) and scheduling jitter**: Profiling whether safety filter evaluations complete within their hard real-time deadlines (${T_{\text{exec}}} \le {T_{\text{deadline}}}$) while neural workloads drive peak memory crossbar contention and DMA transfers, the falsification campaign of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.08 Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation#^sec-placement-testing-separation|Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation]]**.
2.  **Deterministic bus arbitration**: Measuring fieldbus communication jitter and cyclic transmission integrity across physical EtherCAT or CAN FD networks under simulated telemetry broadcast floods and electrical noise injection.
3.  **Power-stage thermal dynamics and current ripple**: Verifying that high-frequency control chatter or torque ripple proposed by the candidate policy does not induce excessive inverter switching losses, DC rail voltage sag, or thermal runaway in motor windings (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.06 Thermal Duty Cycles#^sec-body-thermal-duty-cycles|Thermal Duty Cycles]]**).
4.  **Hardware fault-injection survivability**: Validating that physical line breaks, power supply brownouts, or deserializer frame dropouts deterministically trigger the hardware watchdog and safe-stop sequence within certified timing budgets before motor power stages can be damaged.

A candidate policy advances through bench staging only when every required regression test passes and empirical records confirm that timing and physical margins remain intact. Observing zero deadline overruns during a finite bench campaign does not by itself establish a mathematical upper bound on worst-case execution time, nor does it show how the policy will behave on the warehouse floor. In cloud software engineering, blue-green deployment can reduce downtime by preparing a replacement cluster and redirecting traffic after readiness checks. In physical AI, however, blue-green cutover cannot happen mid-stride or at speed. Swapping active control authority between an incumbent controller and a candidate policy while mass is moving requires managing actuator state continuity, motor winding back-EMF, and dynamic tracking tubes (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.05 Transfer without Discontinuity#^sec-intervention-command-steps|Command steps and mechanical drivetrain shock]]**). De-energizing motor windings or inducing sudden torque steps causes severe mechanical shock loads (${N_{\text{gear}}}^2 {J_{\text{rotor}}} {\ddot{\theta}_{\text{link}}}$) and loss of traction. An unvalidated policy also cannot be granted partial motor authority in a populated warehouse to “canary test” edge-case performance.

Safe predeployment staging instead relies on **shadow mode deployment**, the shadow-operation evidence regime of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.05 Evidence Regimes#^sec-evaluation-four-regimes|The four evaluation evidence regimes]]** applied to a release candidate. The incumbent controller $\pi_{\text{incumbent}}$ supplies proposals, while the independent enforcer retains sole actuator-output ownership and checks each command before fieldbus dispatch. Concurrently in the background, the candidate neural policy $\pi_{\text{candidate}}$ executes on the host compute SoC, consuming identical live sensor streams ${\mathbf{o}}_{t}$ within its qualified shared-resource budget and computing what control command ${\mathbf{u}^{\text{cand}}}_t \sim \pi_{\text{candidate}}({\mathbf{o}}_{t})$ it would have proposed. The candidate writes its outputs to an isolated telemetry buffer that grants no actuator authority. Consumers can read that record, but neither the buffer nor the candidate can bypass the enforcer. Shadow inference and recording must stay within the interference and power budgets that preserve the active case. Discrepancies between candidate proposals and the incumbent’s admitted commands are logged as high-rate telemetry events without admitting any candidate command to the drives.

Shadow-mode telemetry monitors two instantaneous divergence metrics:

1.  **Action discrepancy** (${\Delta}{\mathbf{u}}_t$): The Euclidean (or angular) distance between the candidate proposal and the incumbent control action, measured here by the Euclidean norm:

$$
\|{\Delta}{\mathbf{u}}_t\|_2 = \| {\mathbf{u}^{\text{cand}}}_t - {\mathbf{u}^{\text{incumbent}}}_t \|_2.
$$

 Sustained spikes in $\|{\Delta}{\mathbf{u}}_t\|_2$ identify behavioral divergence where the candidate policy introduces unmodeled bias, high-frequency control jitter, or erratic steering commands. In addition, the discrepancy analyzer monitors the command-rate norm $\|{\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\text{cand}}}_t\|_2$, which is a torque-rate norm only when ${\mathbf{u}}$ is a torque command, to detect unmodeled control chatter that would excite mechanical resonances.

2.  **Safety barrier margin discrepancy** (${\Delta} {\dot{h}}_t$): The differential proximity to forward-invariant safety boundaries under a control barrier function ${h}({\mathbf{x}})$ (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^sec-enforcement-safe-sets-conditional-permission|Safe Sets as Conditional Permission]]**):

$$
{\Delta} {\dot{h}}_t = {\dot{h}}({\hat{\mathbf{x}}}_t, {\mathbf{u}^{\text{cand}}}_t) - {\dot{h}}({\hat{\mathbf{x}}}_t, {\mathbf{u}^{\text{incumbent}}}_t),
$$

 where ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}_t$ is the state estimated from the sensor streams ${\mathbf{o}}_{t}$. A negative ${\Delta} {\dot{h}}_t$ means the candidate has a smaller modeled barrier derivative than the incumbent. Both derivatives can still be positive, so the sign alone does not imply decreasing clearance or a violated boundary of ${\mathcal{C}}$ (defined by ${h}({\mathbf{x}})$).

The two discrepancies answer different questions. A large action discrepancy $\|{\Delta}{\mathbf{u}}_t\|_2$ can reflect an alternate useful action, while ${\Delta} {\dot{h}}_t$ indicates how the proposals differ in their modeled effect on the barrier. Neither sign alone supplies a verdict; review must consider the current margin, uncertainty, and the condition that the independent enforcer would require.

Shadow mode nevertheless has a limit that more traces cannot remove, the trajectory confinement of that evidence regime, which a release reviewer encounters as **counterfactual divergence**. Because admitted incumbent proposals determine the plant trajectory, the historical state trajectory $({s}_0, {\mathbf{u}^{\text{incumbent}}}_0, {s}_1, {\mathbf{u}^{\text{incumbent}}}_1, \dots)$ is conditioned entirely on incumbent actions, and the candidate’s proposals ${\mathbf{u}^{\text{cand}}}_t$ never alter it. Conflating open-loop agreement with deployment safety is the **open-loop replay fallacy**: assuming that high action agreement during passive trace replay guarantees closed-loop dynamic stability. The ${\mathcal{O}(S^2 \epsilon)}$ result in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability|Recovery and Compounding Error]]** is an expected-cost bound under its stated imitation-learning assumptions, not a universal law for physical tracking error. A candidate that agrees on incumbent trajectories can still visit unfamiliar states or excite unmodeled dynamics when it closes the loop, the covariate shift that interactive imitation learning was designed to correct ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|Ross et al. 2011]]). Shadow mode is an essential pre-filter for gross behavioral anomalies, but never a substitute for closed-loop dynamic qualification.

**Phased canary fleet rollouts**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn12|¹²]] expose the candidate to closed-loop operation under a separately supported restricted case. Selecting fewer machines limits exposure but does not bound the consequence of any one action. The restrictions must address the physical hazard:

1.  **Geofenced operational containment**: Canary units operate exclusively within dedicated, surveyed low-risk corridors, such as wide transit aisles devoid of blind rack corners, reduced pedestrian density, and high-friction surveyed concrete (${\mu} \ge$ 0.60).
2.  **Enforced kinematic derating**: The real-time safety enforcer clamps candidate policy velocity and acceleration below nominal ratings (${v} \le {v_{\text{canary}}} < {v_{\text{nominal}}}$), expanding stopping distance margins and suppressing transient kinetic energy.
3.  **Supervised human oversight**: High-readiness human supervisors equipped with wireless remote emergency-stop (tele-stop) pendants maintain line-of-sight monitoring over canary units, backed by an active-lease watchdog.
4.  **Targeted regression observability**: The trial measures tracking error variance ${\sigma_{\text{track}}}^2$, drive torque ripple, and enforcer intervention frequency to reveal service regressions. The permission path retains only the containment claims validated for the restricted envelope.

Expansion requires predeclared acceptance criteria tied to the service and physical claims. Intervention-rate non-inferiority[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.15 Footnotes#^fn13|¹³]] and tracking variance ${\sigma_{\text{track}}}^2 \le {\sigma_{\text{incumbent}}}^2$ are possible capability criteria, with their comparison conditions and statistical assumptions stated. The non-inferiority margin ${\delta}$ is fixed before the trial, so that the upper one-sided 95 percent confidence bound on the rate ratio ${\lambda_{\text{canary}}}/{\lambda_{\text{incumbent}}}$ must fall below $1+{\delta}$. With a zero margin, that upper-bound criterion requires evidence that the candidate is better, which may demand more exposure than a small canary provides. The trial arithmetic of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-trial-limits|Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons]]** sets how much operation a given ${\delta}$ requires. A sequential test can stop expansion as soon as the evidence suffices. None of these criteria replaces the evidence for stopping, observation, or containment. An enforcer intervention also needs causal review, because suppressing legitimate clamps to improve a rollout metric would weaken the release argument.

↩︎ The sequential probability ratio test, which stops a trial campaign as soon as the evidence crosses a precalibrated boundary, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-sequential-sprt|Sequential probability ratio testing]]**.

### From Qualification to a New Manifest

^sec-release-ota-staging

Reducing a policy’s hesitation rate at the pick station does not justify relaxing its contact-force limits or floor-friction restrictions. If the retrained mug-delivery candidate were promoted on task speed alone, it would bypass the containment premises that keep the restricted route safe. Instead, the reviewer repeats closed-loop capability evaluation under the restricted route and feed conditions, and repeats the loaded timing and fault tests whose dependencies changed. Unaffected plant evidence remains reusable only while configuration, calibration, wear, and site premises still match.

Assume, still within the stipulated branch, that those results satisfy the predeclared capability criteria and preserve the containment claims. The adjudicator issues a new manifest binding the candidate, retained and repeated evidence, current calibration, and the same restricted mission. At the assessed stationary cutover state, the gate authenticates that manifest and checks current standing conditions before admitting new proposals. Field monitoring then compares the running service with the same success, cycle-time, rescue, and protection criteria. Failed qualification leaves the incumbent case unchanged; a failed standing condition withdraws the affected grant.

### Over-The-Air Staging and Watchdog-Enforced Rollbacks

^sec-release-verdicts-ota-watchdogs

A candidate written to the inactive bank inherits no operating grant from its predecessor, and rolling back to the prior software image cannot repair a worn brake or a stale calibration. **Hardware watchdog rollback triggers** must therefore distinguish a failure to boot from a failure during motion. Before tracking is enabled, failed image authentication, startup tests, or fieldbus synchronization can reject the candidate and select the retained image, which must still pass current calibration and standing-condition checks before receiving a permit.

During motion, the independent supervisor first commands the assessed fallback within its measured response budget. Where that response ends in torque removal, the supervisor asserts Safe Torque Off after the required controlled stop; a plant needing a powered hold retains the authority needed for that hold. Unlike a conventional compute watchdog that resets the processor immediately upon a timeout, a missed enforcer heartbeat withdraws tracking permission and triggers the hardware-controlled stop without resetting the processor mid-motion. Only after the plant has come to rest in its validated update state may the boot selector change banks.

The image and its compatible calibration schema form the rollback unit. If either record is missing, corrupted, or physically stale, the gate selects `REFUSE` unless a separately signed restricted case covers the actual state and its limits, such as ${v} \le {v_{\text{restricted}}}$. Retaining the prior binary image provides an immediate rollback target, but operators must re-validate standing physical conditions before the recovered binary receives an operating permit.

### Event-Triggered Telemetry and Safety Performance Indicators

^sec-release-data-curation-flywheel

An enforcer trip can reveal a policy failure, a changed physical premise, or both, and the field record must let the reviewer distinguish those causes before choosing retraining, maintenance, or a narrower envelope. Because wireless summaries alone cannot reconstruct the observations and actuator response around the event, detailed traces are retained locally. **Edge event-triggered telemetry** captures the sensor, proposal, permission, actuator, and timestamp chain around an intervention or boundary excursion. A local rolling buffer preserves the lead-up, and capture continues through the physical response whenever the qualified recording power path survives. Buffer length, write bandwidth, and hold-up energy are sized from the required reconstruction window and verified under the same contention and power faults as the permission path. A logic-rail capacitor does not guarantee that the full sensor and storage path survives, and recording must never delay the stop.

UL 4600 calls the field measurements that keep a claim alive **safety performance indicators** (SPIs). Each is a metric tied to a named claim, with a threshold and a predeclared response when the threshold is crossed ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-ul4600|UL Standards & Engagement 2023]]). On the running machine, every standing condition of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.06 The Verdict’s Standing Conditions#^sec-release-standing-conditions|section 16.5]] has one. Measured stop distance at scheduled trials and enforcer trips per operating hour watch the base’s stopping claim, age refusals per operating hour watch the observation-age claim, and localization residuals against their allowance watch the sensor geometry. An SPI whose threshold is crossed withdraws or narrows the grant it watches; one that drifts toward its threshold triggers review before it is crossed, which is the defense against the normalization of deviance of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.08 Adversarial Review#^sec-release-adversarial-review|section 16.7]].

During routine operation, compact health summaries support fleet monitoring while required incident records retain full provenance locally until transfer succeeds. Learning-data filters may prioritize unfamiliar events for training, provided they never discard required safety evidence or hide repeated failures. Once the traces arrive, a reviewer distinguishes policy hesitation from calibration drift, surface contamination, and mechanism wear before assigning the repair: qualified recovery traces can enter the experience pipeline, whereas reconstructed simulations extend test coverage only over their modeled conditions. A captured event therefore changes the release argument only after its physical cause and operating conditions are established. Reconstructed scenes and retraining can improve a candidate policy, yet neither automatically repairs a physical warrant, so the revised claim must return through the relevant qualification stages before a new manifest grants authority. Because the traces the machine keeps also record the people around it, each record that identifies a person carries standing conditions of its own: a declared purpose, a retention period, and a list of readers, audited like any other premise.

↪ The declarations that govern records identifying people, and the access and retention audit that enforces them, are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.08 Data About People#^sec-release-recorded-people|Recorded people as a standing condition]]**.

### Fleet Evidence and Accountable Release

^sec-release-fleet-exposure-liability

Teams break the safety warrant when they pool dissimilar routes into a single fleet mileage total or treat an operator intervention count as a collision rate. Fleet exposure can reveal recurring failures, but interpreting it requires knowing the operating domain, the intervention definition, and the exact system configuration. The release reviewer uses fleet records to challenge the same capability and containment claims, rather than replacing them with a mileage ranking. One public fleet record shows what an interpretable version looks like. Waymo publishes its rider-only crash rates by outcome, each against a human-driven benchmark adjusted to the same locations and each with a confidence interval. It republishes them as rider-only mileage accumulates ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-waymo2026safetyimpact|Waymo 2026]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.14 Source References#^ref-kusano2025comparison|Kusano et al. 2025]]). [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.10 Maintaining Release Through Updates#^fig-16-fleet-evidence-intervals|Figure 4]] plots the latest release and the history of two of its outcomes.

Panel a is a horizontal interval plot of percent change in crash rate from minus 100 to 0 percent for six outcomes, each with a point, a horizontal 95 percent interval, and a crash count label. ^fig-16-fleet-evidence-intervals

In the June 2026 release, after 271 million rider-only miles, the injury estimate rests on 183 crashes and its interval spans a reduction of 79 to 85 percent. The serious-injury estimate rests on 3 crashes and spans 83 to 99 percent, and the fatal estimate rests on 2 and spans 18 to 98 percent. The same mileage therefore licenses a tight claim about common crashes and only a loose one about the outcomes a release decision cares about most, because rare events accumulate slowly however many miles the fleet drives. That is the fourth law, permission must be earned (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/V4 Governing the Machine#^pri-vol4-evidence-bounds-authority|4]]), measured on a real fleet. Each outcome’s claim may extend only as far as its own interval, and a reviewer reads the interval before the point estimate.

The signed release manifest records the engineering decision and its accountable owner, and together with the records it binds, it lets an investigator follow one admitted action back to the person who signed for it. The enforcement record logs the admitted command. The manifest in force at that moment names by digest the policy weights, the enforcer and real-time operating system binaries, the calibration tables, and the operating envelope behind it. The signature on that manifest, made with a key held in a hardware security module, is bound to the sign-off record that names the lead systems adjudicator who accepted that configuration or the update that installed it. The manifest does not itself establish regulatory compliance or determine legal liability. Applicable requirements depend on the machine, intended use, and jurisdiction; the release manifest must preserve the evidence needed for that separate assessment. Throughout this lifecycle, release remains a decision about a named machine under named conditions. Shadow traces, staged trials, and field records can strengthen the next argument only when their operating conditions and limits are explicit, while the manifest and independent permission path retain the restrictions that the evidence supports.
