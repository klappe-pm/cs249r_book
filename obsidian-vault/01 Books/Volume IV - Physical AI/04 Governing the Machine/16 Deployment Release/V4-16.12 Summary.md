# V4-16.12 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release|V4-16 Deployment Release]]

^sec-release-summary

A deployment release formalizes which physical claims the empirical evidence licenses for a specific physical machine, under what operating conditions, and for how long. For the warehouse mobile manipulator examined across these chapters, release resolves to three concrete physical inequalities. The mobile base stops within surveyed clearance. The manipulator unlatches the cage door below the latch’s mechanical force limit. A mug handover remains strictly below the contact criterion for an unconstrained human hand. A formal claim-argument-evidence safety case links each inequality to its supporting premises and experimental evidence within a clearly demarcated operating envelope. Because required premises combine conjunctively, an argument is no stronger than its least supported premise; empirical confidence cannot be averaged across heterogeneous requirements. Independent sufficient legs strengthen support in the stipulated probability model, which estimates neither this robot’s hazard rate nor calibrated assurance confidence. In the physical argument, a policy error cannot breach a monitored invariant only while the qualified enforcement, plant, sensing, timing, and fault premises hold; C5 isolation evidence does not by itself establish statistical independence. Recognized machinery safety standards govern this argumentation structure. Within the permission path, only the safety microcontroller’s lease-expiry timer and heartbeat watchdog are rated safety functions; the other formal protective claims rest on the certified drive, scanner, and arm safety functions beneath it.

An adjudicator evaluates the safety case to deliver one of three definitive verdicts: unconditional release (`UNCOND`), conditional release (`COND`), or refusal (`REFUSE`). If release is granted, standing conditions enforce operational boundaries in real time, automatically withdrawing authorization whenever any relied-upon premise becomes false or unobservable. Records that identify people carry standing conditions of their own, declared and audited like the physical premises (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.08 Data About People#^sec-release-recorded-people|Recorded people as a standing condition]]**). Against this book’s illustrative input datasets, every primary claim contains at least one ungrounded premise, compelling a verdict of `REFUSE`. A separately stipulated branch illustrates the empirical evidence that would justify restricted conditional service (`COND`). Before final authorization, an adversarial review must try to break the entire argument, and that scrutiny must recur throughout the operational lifespan. A cryptographically signed manifest binds the release verdict to reviewed software and calibration artifacts by cryptographic digest, failing closed if any binary or configuration changes. Any subsequent model or firmware update must return through the same safety case, because open-loop shadow operation cannot evaluate the physical consequences of unexecuted closed-loop control actions. **Key Takeaways: Deployment release and safety cases**

- **The envelope bounds the claim:** A release claim covers only the speeds, payloads, surface friction coefficients, clearances, and contact scenarios for which empirical evidence was systematically collected. Every dimension of the operational design domain requires an active runtime monitor or a strictly enforced site procedure.
- **Doubt accumulates along required premises:** A conjunctive argument ($\bigwedge_i {P}_i$) is no stronger than its weakest required premise and is often strictly weaker; confidence cannot be averaged across premises. High empirical accuracy in perception cannot offset an unmonitored braking margin or an uncharacterized tail latency. Independent sufficient legs reduce doubt under the stipulated probability model. Policy validation and an isolated permission path do not automatically constitute such legs; the physical containment claim still requires qualified plant, sensing, timing, and fault premises.
- **Standing conditions make release revocable:** Every relied-upon premise requires an active detector or operational procedure, an explicit checking cadence, and a validated safety action triggered whenever the premise evaluates to false or unknown. The signed safety case binds these obligations directly to the machine across its operational life.
- **Updates return through the release argument:** Shadow deployment reveals policy divergence without testing the closed-loop physical consequences of those divergent actions. Rigorous closed-loop qualification and a re-evaluated manifest still govern deployment authorization; every update must preserve machine-specific calibration and execute a validated stop or hold during software cutover.
- **An unknown premise refuses, and refusal names the next work:** In safety argumentation, unknown envelope membership refuses exactly as a measured violation does, while its cause is logged separately. On this book’s illustrative walkthrough, the mobile manipulator receives an explicit verdict of `REFUSE`. A structured refusal is productive: it isolates the missing measurements, identifies the claims they would ground, and defines the residual operational envelope that remains once those gaps are closed.
- **Useful service needs its own evidence:** The stipulated restricted mug-delivery workflow achieves `COND` only by satisfying concrete task-success, cycle-time, and human-intervention criteria alongside safety claims C1–C6. Safety filtering can preserve high task completion rates even when raw proposer policy autonomy is comparatively modest.
- **No verdict moves the proposal boundary:** Under both `UNCOND` and `COND`, learned policies merely propose actions while the hardware permission path decides physical admission. Within that permission path, only the safety MCU’s lease-expiry and heartbeat watchdog is a rated safety function; the admission checks are not rated. Entering a restricted operational mode requires its own signed release case, never an informal or ad hoc runtime downgrade.

The fourth law, *permission must be earned* (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/V4 Governing the Machine#^pri-vol4-evidence-bounds-authority|4]]), establishes that permission to operate must derive strictly from recorded evidence and expire whenever that evidence lapses. Standing conditions transform this principle into an active software and hardware mechanism. Each condition couples a required premise with a dedicated detector, a monitoring cadence, and an enforced safety response. Consequently, the loaded stopping trials that would support a conditional release for the warehouse mobile manipulator become binding empirical warrants whose invalidation immediately terminates authorization. Under this book’s illustrative records, that grant is never issued, because each of the six primary claims relies on at least one premise that remains ungrounded or unknown. The stipulated delivery branch demonstrates how empirical field trials could substantiate useful restricted service and specifies the runtime conditions necessary to sustain that grant. Just as a manual lease restricts human or teleoperation authority to the validity window of its evidence, and precommitment binds test evaluation to rules established before observations occur, deployment release binds fleet deployment to verifiable physical limits. Release is the widest operational authority granted in this book, and even it never reaches the actuators directly. Every motion it licenses still crosses the causal boundary only through the permission path and the rated functions beneath it.
