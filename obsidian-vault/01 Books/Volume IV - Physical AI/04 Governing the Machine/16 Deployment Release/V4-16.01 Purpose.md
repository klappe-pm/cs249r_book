*Physical AI Systems Stack*

*Physical AI stack focus for this chapter.*

*Why can a machine that completes every trial still lack the evidence to be allowed among people?*

A successful trial shows what a machine did under the conditions it happened to meet, while permission to operate is a claim about every condition it may meet next. Thousands of clean runs on a dry floor say nothing about the stop on a floor no trial touched, and a brake that stopped the machine last month may not stop it inside the same distance after its lining wears. A machine granted authority on demonstrations therefore carries untested premises into service, where the first test of each one is the event it was meant to prevent. Deciding to deploy requires stating which physical claims the evidence supports, under which conditions and for how long, refusing or narrowing operation wherever a premise remains unknown, and withdrawing the grant when brakes wear, sensors shift, or software changes. At the causal boundary, authority must expire when its physical justification does, because a later software correction cannot undo motion already produced.

**Learning Objectives**

- Construct a claim-argument-evidence (CAE) safety case as an auditable engineering assertion DAG linking bounded operating envelopes to qualified measurements, analysis, and operating conditions
- Distinguish input provenance from premise status, and select the verdict (`UNCOND`, `COND`, or `REFUSE`) the supported operating claim permits
- Join task-success, cycle-time, and intervention evidence to the physical measurements and restrictions supporting a conditional release
- Specify standing-condition cadences and validated responses, from brake wear to calibration drift, separately from execution leases
- Diagnose unsupported warrants, hidden contextual assumptions, and compound defeaters through adversarial review of a release argument
- Specify the records a release manifest binds by digest and the conditions under which its gate refuses motion
- Determine which claims a policy or software update reopens, and why shadow operation cannot substitute for closed-loop qualification
