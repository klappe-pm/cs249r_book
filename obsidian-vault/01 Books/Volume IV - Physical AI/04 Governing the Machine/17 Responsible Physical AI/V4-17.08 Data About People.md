# V4-17.08 Data About People

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17 Responsible Physical AI|V4-17 Responsible Physical AI]]

^sec-responsibility-sensed-data

When the warehouse machine logs a near miss at a rack end, its sensors and audit trails capture the people in and behind the cell alongside the telemetry of the base and arm. The records that make a machine answerable are also records about people, and the same evidence that settles who answers for an incident can, if kept forever and read by anyone, become a tool for watching the people it was meant to protect. How those records are declared, kept, and read is therefore part of the design, not an afterthought to it.

### Recorded People as a Standing Condition

^sec-release-recorded-people

Three of the release records carry personal data directly: the authority record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-authority-log|The Authority Record]]** names the operator behind each takeover tick by tick, the event-triggered traces of the black box hold camera frames of whoever stood in the aisle (including coworkers who never touched the console), and the demonstration archive of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^sec-data-schema|The Dataset Schema]]** binds each episode to the operator who produced it. If those traces are retained indefinitely or opened to unrestricted browsing, the safety evidence store turns into continuous workplace surveillance. A release must retain the evidence needed for incident reconstruction while keeping identifying data to a minimum and within justified retention limits. The release therefore carries one more premise, and it is administrative rather than physical: each record that identifies a person declares its purpose, its retention period, and who may read it, and the site operates within those declarations.

Where the machine runs in the European Union, the General Data Protection Regulation makes that premise law ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-gdpr2016|European Parliament and Council of the European Union 2016]]). Article 5(1) requires personal data to be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with them (point b), to be limited to what those purposes need (point c), and to be kept in identifiable form no longer than they require (point e). Article 25(2) makes the minimum the default, in the amount collected, the extent of processing, the storage period, and accessibility. Article 35 requires a data protection impact assessment before processing that is likely to result in a high risk to the people concerned. Article 88 lets member states and collective agreements set more specific rules for employee data, naming monitoring systems at the workplace among the safeguards those rules must address. Other jurisdictions differ in detail; the engineering premise does not.

Each declaration becomes a standing condition of the kind **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.06 The Verdict’s Standing Conditions#^sec-release-standing-conditions|The Verdict's Standing Conditions]]** defines, with a detector and a response. Incident reconstruction sets the retention of the black-box and authority logs: the traces around an intervention are kept for a declared, justified review period, with any extension documented under the applicable retention rules, and routine ticks with no event roll off the ring buffer as they already do. Access follows the purpose, so an incident reviewer may read a trace and an operator’s supervisor may not score from it. A trace promoted into the experience pipeline carries its purpose and retention with it, which is what the consent and retention field of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^tbl-05-provenance-schema|Demonstration dataset provenance schema]]** records. The detector is an access and retention audit on the evidence store. Its response follows from the rule that a verdict stands only while its premises are known-true, so a record used outside its declared purpose, or held past its period, suspends that use and returns the premise to the adjudicator. Stated this way, the premise keeps the traces an investigator needs to reconstruct a stop and bars their use for watching workers.

### Keeping Less

^sec-responsibility-minimization

Continuous video recording keeps every frame of every worker’s shift, creating a permanent surveillance archive when an incident investigation needs only the seconds around a fault. The first way to protect recorded people is therefore to record less of them. Event-triggered retention keeps only the window around an intervention or a boundary excursion: the 5 s before the trigger that the authority record needs to reconstruct a takeover, and the response after it, 30 s in all. At 20 such events per machine over a 16-hour operating day, the machine keeps about 1 percent of its footage, a 96× reduction, and everything else rolls off the ring buffer within seconds.

↩︎ The pre-trigger window the black box must hold to reconstruct a takeover is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.08 Trustworthy Operational Logs#^sec-intervention-trustworthy-logs|Trustworthy Operational Logs]]**.

The event window and the records retained from it have different lifetimes. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.08 Data About People#^fig-17-incident-evidence-lifecycle|Figure 3]] follows the bounded capture history into incident review and separates that use from a possible training use.

Figure 3: **The Lifetime of Incident Evidence**: A trigger selects the declared pre-trigger and response window while routine frames roll off the capture buffer. The incident record carries its purpose, retention, and readers. A training copy requires a separate review of consent, eligibility, and purpose; selection as incident evidence does not itself authorize training. The window and retention periods are those declared for the deployment, not universal durations.

^fig-17-incident-evidence-lifecycle

Retention limits apply to each copy, including a copy admitted to training. Deleting a routine capture frame does not remove a retained incident record, and approving incident access does not approve every later use of that record.

Two further steps reduce what the kept footage reveals. Faces and badge numbers can be blurred on the machine before a frame is stored, provided the investigation does not need them. The declaration then records which fields stay identifiable and why, for example that the operator’s identity is kept in the authority record but not in the camera trace. People whose demonstrations train the policy are a separate case: they are recorded on purpose and at length, so their consent, what their episodes may be used for, and how long those episodes are kept travel with the data from the moment of collection. The data block of the approval record lists each record class, the people it records, its purpose, its retention, what is removed before storage, who may read it, and the result of the last access and retention audit.

### When Safety Records Become Performance Scores

^sec-responsibility-workplace-monitoring

The records most likely to be misused are the ones that measure workers. The machine’s authority logs and black-box recorders capture the identity of every operator who initiates a manual takeover, how long each takeover took, and how often each operator intervened. If an employer uses those records to score employee performance, the records have crossed from safety into monitoring. Conformity assessment under the European Union Machinery Regulation addresses the machinery safety requirements for the declared use; it does not govern every later use of the machine’s records. One such use falls under a different law, the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act. Its Annex III lists AI systems intended to monitor and evaluate the performance and behavior of people at work among the employment uses subject to the high-risk classification rules of Article 6. Article 26(7) requires an employer deploying a high-risk system in the workplace to inform workers’ representatives and the affected workers before putting it into use ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-eu2024aiact|European Parliament and Council of the European Union 2024]]).

The declarations of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.08 Data About People#^sec-release-recorded-people|section 17.7.1]] are where the line is drawn. A record declared for incident reconstruction may not be read to rank operators, and an access audit that finds such a read suspends that use and returns the premise to the adjudicator. If a site does want to evaluate workers, that is a separate system with its own declaration, its own legal classification, and its own owner, and the approval record lists it as a separate decision rather than letting it grow out of the safety records. Protecting workers in the machine’s records still leaves the physical work the machine shifts onto them during daily operation.
