# V4-17.05 Permission in Stages

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17 Responsible Physical AI|V4-17 Responsible Physical AI]]

^sec-responsibility-staged-permission

Granting a machine full operating permission on day one exposes the public or the workforce before the configuration has accumulated the field evidence needed to support that exposure. Regulators of driverless vehicles therefore grant permission in stages. California’s rules separate testing with a safety driver, testing without one, and deployment to the public, each under its own permit and conditions ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cadmv2018testingregs|California Department of Motor Vehicles 2018a]], [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cadmv2018deploymentregs|2018b]]). The CPUC adds a second ladder for carrying passengers. Before August 2023, Cruise could carry paying passengers without a safety driver only in limited areas of San Francisco between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m., could carry paying passengers anywhere in the city at any hour only with a safety driver present, and could give free driverless rides anywhere at any hour. On August 10, 2023, the commission removed the time limit on driverless fared service ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cpuc2023cruiseapproval|California Public Utilities Commission 2023]]). Each rung named a configuration, a place, a time, and who could be aboard.

**Definition 17.2: Permission stage**

A ***permission stage*** is a grant, made by the party entitled to grant it, that allows a named configuration of a machine to operate in a named domain, among named groups of people, on named evidence, and under named reporting duties. A machine moves to the next stage only on evidence gathered at the current one, and a stage can be withdrawn for a configuration change, an incident, or a withheld record.

1.  **Significance**: Exposure grows one stage at a time, so the evidence for the next stage is gathered under the protection of the current one, and a failure at one stage costs only what that stage exposes.
2.  **Distinction**: A release verdict is the engineering decision about whether a configuration’s claims are supported; a permission stage is the grant that decides who may be exposed to that configuration and what must be reported about it.
3.  **Common pitfall**: A frequent misconception is that a stage, once reached, belongs to the company. It belongs to a configuration, and it carries duties that, if broken, withdraw it.

Each stage carries reporting duties, and the duties grow with the stage. California requires a manufacturer testing on public roads to report, within ten days and on a set form, any collision that causes damage, injury, or death, and to file an annual report of every disengagement, each transfer of control from the automation to the safety driver ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cadmv2018testingregs|California Department of Motor Vehicles 2018a]]). Since 2021, a federal order has required companies operating automated driving systems to report crashes to NHTSA. Under the version in force in 2023, a crash that involved a death, an injury treated at a hospital, or a pedestrian or other vulnerable road user had to be reported within one day, with an update at ten days ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-nhtsa2021sgo|National Highway Traffic Safety Administration 2021]]). The disengagement reports are the data behind **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.02 Shared Authority#^fig-14-disengagement-record|A decade of reported takeovers]]**, whose two series come from two different stages, testing with a driver and testing without one.

A warehouse has no public permit office, but the same ladder exists. The machine is commissioned with a spotter walking beside it, then allowed to run unattended in a zone people cannot enter while it operates, and only then released onto an open floor among workers. The facility owner, the integrator, and the workers’ representatives grant these stages, and the approval record names who granted each one, for which configuration, on what evidence, and with which reporting duties. The stage and the verdict are recorded separately. A `COND` verdict for restricted inspected-route delivery says which claims the evidence supports; the stage says whether that restricted service may run on the open floor yet.

### Leaving a Stage on Evidence

^sec-responsibility-stage-exit

A stage is left on evidence, not on a calendar date, and the evidence a lower stage produces is partly hidden by the protection that stage provides. A safety driver or a spotter masks failures: when the person intervenes, the machine’s own error never reaches its consequence, and the stage records an intervention rather than a collision. Counting failures at the supervised stage therefore means reconstructing, for each intervention, whether the machine would have failed without it, which is why the disengagement reports matter and why they must say why control was transferred.

↩︎ Interventions that end a trial before its outcome is known, and how they are counted, are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.07 Confidence from a Finite Campaign#^sec-evaluation-closed-loop-survival|Confidence from a Finite Campaign]]**.

The exposure arithmetic sets how long a stage must run. Under the zero-failure Poisson exposure bound of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.10 Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall#^sec-evaluation-limits|Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall]]**, ruling out an intervention rate at 95 percent confidence with zero observed events requires an exposure of $\ln(1/0.05)$, or nearly three times the target mean interval between interventions, so claiming fewer than one safety-relevant intervention per 1,000 operating hours after observing none requires the machines to accumulate about 2,996 failure-free operating hours. A fleet of 10 machines running 16 hours a day reaches that in 19 days. Those hours support the claim only for the configuration that ran them. A new policy, a new enforcer build, or a new site layout starts its own count, because the evidence belongs to a configuration, and so does the stage it supports.

### Withdrawal and Disclosure

^sec-responsibility-revocation

Permission is withdrawn the way it is granted, one stage at a time. The DMV’s October 2023 order suspended Cruise’s driverless testing and deployment permits but left in place its permit to test with a safety driver ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cadmv2023cruise|California Department of Motor Vehicles 2023]]), stepping the company back down the ladder rather than off it. The grounds the DMV cited show what a permit protects. One was that the vehicles were not safe for public operation, a finding about the motion. Another was that the company had misrepresented information related to the safety of its technology, a finding about the record.

The regulatory fines that followed were for the record. The CPUC counted the 15 days in which it lacked a full account, applied its daily maximum of \$7,500 to each, and fined Cruise \$112,500 ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-cpuc2024cruisedecision|California Public Utilities Commission 2024]]). In September 2024, NHTSA imposed a consent order with a \$1.5 million penalty for crash reports that omitted the dragging ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-nhtsa2024cruiseconsent|National Highway Traffic Safety Administration 2024]]). In November 2024, Cruise entered a deferred prosecution agreement and paid a \$500,000 criminal fine for submitting a false record to influence the federal investigation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.14 Source References#^ref-doj2024cruise|U.S. Attorney’s Office, Northern District of California 2024]]). Regulators withdrew authority from the company because of what its vehicle did and because of what the company did not tell them about it.

The **incident disclosure duty** is the obligation, attached to an operating permit, to report an incident completely and on time to the parties the permit names. It is a decision with an owner like any other: someone decides what an incident report contains, someone signs it, and someone decides when a record has been withheld long enough to break the permit. The permit block of the approval record therefore lists, for each stage, the regulator or grantor, the configuration the stage covers, the reporting duties with their deadlines, and a disclosure log of every report sent, with its author and the evidence attached. A release manifest that binds raw traces to the configuration that produced them, and that an assessor can open, is the engineering half of that duty. The disclosure log is the other half, because it shows what was actually sent, to whom, and when. Once a machine enters an open-floor stage among coworkers or pedestrians, however, its safety case rests on a sensor premise that aggregate stage-exit hours can hide: whether its detectors see every group of people in its path equally well.

↩︎ The release manifest that binds raw traces to the configuration that produced them is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.09 The Release Manifest#^sec-release-manifest|The Release Manifest]]**.
