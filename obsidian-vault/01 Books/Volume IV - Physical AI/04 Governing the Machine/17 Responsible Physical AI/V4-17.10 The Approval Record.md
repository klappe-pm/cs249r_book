# V4-17.10 The Approval Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17 Responsible Physical AI|V4-17 Responsible Physical AI]]

^sec-responsibility-approval-record

Left scattered across emails, slide decks, and meeting notes, the human and organizational premises behind a machine can lapse without blocking release: a staffing ratio changes on the night shift, a subgroup detection test is deferred, or a data-retention declaration expires while the robot keeps running. The **approval record** collects these premises in a single gate-checked artifact: it binds by digest the authority record, the enforcement record, the fault records, and the release manifest, and it adds the decisions behind them, each with a named owner.

**Definition 17.3: Approval record**

The ***approval record*** is the signed record that names, for each decision behind a machine’s admitted actions, the person who approved it and answers for it, and that declares the human roles the design relies on, the permission stage and its reporting duties, the groups exposed to the machine, the records kept about people, and the labor the deployment moves.

1.  **Significance**: The release gate reads the approval record as a standing condition, so a decision with no named owner refuses operation exactly as an unknown physical premise does.
2.  **Distinction**: The authority record shows who held each channel at each tick; the approval record shows who decided that the machine could be in that situation at all.
3.  **Common pitfall**: A frequent misconception is that a complete runtime log makes someone answerable. A log reconstructs events; only a record of decisions and their owners says who answers for them.

The approval record has seven blocks, a header that binds it to the release and one block for each kind of decision this chapter has set out ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.10 The Approval Record#^tbl-17-approval-record|table 2]]). Each block names the decision owner alongside the specific evidence, bounds, or reporting duties that keep the release valid.

Table 2: **The approval record**: Each block holds a kind of decision the runtime records cannot hold, and each entry names an owner. The header binds the record to the release it governs by digest, so a new release requires a new approval record.

^tbl-17-approval-record

↪ **Byte layout:** The approval record’s fields, their encodings, and the check the release gate applies to each are given in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-responsibility|Approval record]]**.

The approval record gates release because the release gate reads it as a standing condition of the manifest. Four findings refuse operation the way an unknown premise does: an admitted action class has a decision link with no named owner, a human role cited as a countermeasure carries a verdict of `INFEASIBLE` or `UNKNOWN`, an exposed group the machine relies on detecting lacks a per-group entry, or a declaration about recorded people has expired or failed its audit. Each finding returns the premise to the adjudicator with its cause logged. A decision whose owner leaves the organization expires with that person’s role, so the approval record is kept current the same way the standing conditions are: by checking, on a declared schedule, that every premise still holds.

↩︎ Standing conditions, each with a detector, a cadence, and a response that withdraws the grant, are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.06 The Verdict’s Standing Conditions#^sec-release-standing-conditions|The Verdict's Standing Conditions]]**.

Not every open entry can be closed before release. A decision may wait on a regulator, a per-group sample may still be filling, a staffing plan may depend on a hire. Those entries do not vanish into a footnote. Each one leaves the approval record with an operating restriction that holds until it closes, such as a lower speed near the group not yet measured or a zone the machine may not enter. It also enters the residual-claims register with an owner and a closure test, alongside the physical premises no sensor can check.

↪ The residual-claims register, which carries each open premise with its restriction, owner, and closure test, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18.04 The Epistemic Frontier#^sec-frontier-residual-claims-register|A residual-claims register]]**.

**Checkpoint 17.3: An approval record for the Cruise configuration**

**Decisions**: Using [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.02 Who Answered for the Cruise Collision#^sec-responsibility-who-answered|section 17.1]], can you list the decisions behind the October 2023 pullover (the post-collision response rule, the operating domain, the commercial stage, the reporting duties) and name the role that should own each?

**Unknown fields**: Which of those fields could have been marked `UNKNOWN` before the collision, and which would have refused operation under the gate rule of this section?
