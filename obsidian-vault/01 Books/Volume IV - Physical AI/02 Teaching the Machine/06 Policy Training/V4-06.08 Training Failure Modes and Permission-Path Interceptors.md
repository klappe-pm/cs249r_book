# V4-06.08 Training Failure Modes and Permission-Path Interceptors

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training|V4-06 Policy Training]]

^sec-training-failure-modes

A physical failure in a learned policy is rarely arbitrary. When an end-effector collides with a strike plate, that command reflects optimization over a specific dataset, loss formulation, and simulator dynamics, and the two failures that opened the chapter can now be traced back to theirs. The assumptions baked into that optimization leave a structural footprint on policy behavior, one that engineers can trace and mitigate before hardware ever runs. The **policy vulnerability inference chain** makes this causal trace explicit across five links: ($\text{Exposure} \to \text{Dependency} \to \text{Violated Assumption} \to \text{Observable Signature} \to \text{Permission-Path Interceptor}$). Training exposure establishes a learned dependency. An unmodeled deployment condition violates that dependency. The violation produces a measurable signature in the commands or physical plant telemetry. That signature names the specific runtime interceptor that the execution permission path requires. Reading the three latch checkpoints back through this chain shows how distinct optimization regimes produce different failure modes.

For `latch-bc-01`, behavioral cloning relies on the teleoperated openings. These demonstrations only support nominal plate alignment during the guarded approach; the data-collection campaign never demonstrated a displaced or misaligned plate. A sagging door frame directly violates that geometric dependency. The resulting failure signature is compounding covariate shift. Because behavioral cloning optimizes an open-loop supervised objective over expert trajectories, small drift errors compound at each control step. Individual motion proposals often remain physically plausible right up until the gripper mechanically binds against the frame. To prevent mechanical damage, a support monitor must evaluate runtime observations against the empirically supported demonstration cells and revoke policy authority outside them. Predeployment qualification follows directly from the known coverage gap that the scenario table of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry|Choosing a tractable coverage table]]** recorded: bench trials with deliberately displaced plates must confirm that the support monitor trips reliably before the mechanism binds.

For `latch-sim-02`, training inside a physics simulator instills strong dependencies on idealized contact dynamics and negligible actuation lag. A physical steel plate violates both assumptions, generating sharp contact forces and unmodeled transport delays that produce the overforce transients quantified in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.05 The Simulator as a System Component#^sec-training-contact-exploit|section 6.4.2]]. Paired contact probes expose this dynamical mismatch before hardware deployment. If the physical contact deadline leaves insufficient time for closed-loop deceleration, a reactive force tripwire arrives too late. Detecting a force excursion confirms that an impact occurred, but actuator inertia and mechanical compliance will continue driving the mechanism into the plate before the brakes can arrest motion. The interceptor is therefore the pre-contact stopping envelope of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.05 The Simulator as a System Component#^sec-training-contact-exploit|section 6.4.2]], which guarantees a full stop at the standoff before the plate and hands engagement beyond it to the separately qualified guarded contact mode.

For `latch-res-03`, adaptation concentrates real-robot experience on the contact states of a single hardware rig. Localized fine-tuning targets tight-tolerance contact dynamics on that specific fixture, but it does not qualify the frozen base across the rest of the operational state space. Unlike full-weight fine-tuning, which can introduce catastrophic forgetting, this gated residual retains the base weights and holds corrections at zero in free space; its primary regression risks arise from contact corrections and gate-boundary transitions. Before updated weights receive operational authority, they must clear a frozen regression suite that evaluates both nominal latch engagement and free-space flight. In addition, an architectural residual gate must hold corrections at zero outside the designated contact phase, preventing fine-tuned offsets from destabilizing the free-space approach. Physical exploration during real-machine tuning ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.07 Real-Robot Fine-Tuning and Residual Control#^sec-training-real-machine-tuning|section 6.6]]) also imposes mechanical shock and motor winding heat. The runtime supervisor must monitor these thermal and structural thresholds continuously to ensure that task reward improvements do not mask hardware degradation.

Subsequent training stages do not automatically heal the unresolved dependencies of earlier stages. In the simulation-and-residual lineage (`latch-sim-02` through `latch-res-03`), simulation artifacts persist until physical hardware evidence explicitly closes them. Conversely, the recovery-imitation branch adopts an alternative architectural path. The checkpoint `latch-bc-recovery-02` incorporates targeted, human-guided recovery demonstrations for a named displaced-plate condition, expanding the operational envelope without relying on an uncalibrated simulator. Its remaining engineering uncertainties, chiefly closed-loop trajectory stability and potential regression on nominal openings, are made explicit through targeted qualification requests.

Ultimately, each inference chain connects a specific training assumption to an empirical test and an active execution limit. The policy manifest preserves these connections alongside the checkpoint’s source data, simulator configuration, optimization objective, and architectural constraints. By maintaining this end-to-end traceability, verification suites can systematically evaluate candidate policies against the exact physical vulnerabilities created by their training history.
