# V4-06.10 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training|V4-06 Policy Training]]

^sec-training-fallacies-pitfalls

Minimizing loss on an offline demonstration split or maximizing cumulative return in simulation measures compliance with training assumptions rather than physical competence on hardware. When an objective rewards behavior that exploits a curated demonstration distribution or a simulator approximation, the ranking of candidates established in training can invert on deployment. The candidate that scored highest in simulation finishes lowest on hardware, through unsupported state drift, contact-force spikes past the structural limit, or torque oscillations that grow into resonant chatter ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.10 Fallacies and Pitfalls#^fig-06-policy-ranking-inversion|figure 9]]). Virtual environments forgive maneuvers that physical hardware penalizes with tripwires, resonance, or thermal overload, so the causal assumptions connecting a training metric to physical execution have to be tested before a policy receives actuator authority.

**Pitfall**: *Sanitizing recovery episodes to achieve lower offline validation loss.*

Suppose an archive of latch manipulation demonstrations includes human recovery maneuvers, and two separate policies train on competing subsets of this corpus. One policy trains on the full archive, including episodes where a human teleoperator backed off the gripper and re-aligned the jaws after the latch cam caught on the strike plate. The second policy trains on a sanitized subset stripped of all hesitation and retries, achieving a markedly lower loss on its held-out validation split. When deployed on the physical cell, both policies eventually encounter a misaligned cam. The first policy recognizes the contact anomaly, backs off, and seats the latch successfully. The second policy relentlessly drives along the nominal path, building contact force until the safety supervisor’s force tripwire halts the cell. Because the sanitized dataset excluded off-nominal recoveries, the lower validation loss proves only that predicting smooth nominal trajectories is easier to fit than handling physical disturbances. Pruning corrective demonstrations simplifies the loss score at the exact price of removing the feedback behaviors the physical controller needs off the nominal path. **Filtering out recovery trajectories to drive down offline prediction loss strips the policy of the corrective demonstrations required to escape compounding physical errors.**

**Architect’s countermeasure**: Retain all authority-record entries during dataset ingestion and admit, as training targets, only the stabilized, human-reviewed segments of attributable expert recoveries. Never prune demonstration corpora to minimize offline validation loss, and mandate closed-loop physical perturbation benchmarks to verify recovery competence before signing off on deployment candidate checkpoints.

**Fallacy**: *Transitioning from human demonstrations to simulation reinforcement learning removes the failures training left in the policy.*

An assembly team switches from behavioral cloning to simulation reinforcement learning to eliminate lateral drift during automated gasket seating. The resulting policy cures the drift in the virtual environment but commands joint torque oscillations at 65 Hz. The physics simulator permits this aggressive chatter because its rigid-body solver assumes massless motor rotors and instantaneous torque reversal. On the physical machine, this high-frequency command excites drivetrain mechanical resonance and rapidly heats the motor windings toward their thermal trip point via ${i_q}^2 {R}$ Joule losses. The drive electronics throttle current to prevent permanent motor damage, causing the assembly stroke to stall before seating completes. Shifting training regimes solved an imitation drift problem by introducing a high-frequency chatter exploit that the simulator did not penalize. Qualification must therefore evaluate the full physical task under sustained cyclic operation, verifying simulator dynamics assumptions against physical hardware telemetry. **Switching to simulation reinforcement learning substitutes numerical solver shortcuts and unmodeled mechanical dynamics for human demonstration deficits.**

**Architect’s countermeasure**: Require paired-trace validation across all four reality gap channels before deploying simulation-trained policies. Enforce low-level hardware rate limiters and digital low-pass action filters (${f_{\text{cutoff}}} \le$ 20 Hz) within the embedded motor drives to suppress high-frequency chatter originating from unmodeled rotor inertia.

**Fallacy**: *Assuming online gradient updates preserve predeployment safety envelopes.*

When a simulation-trained policy shows steady-state positioning error on a physical assembly fixture, an engineer might enable online policy-gradient fine-tuning directly on the robot. After 5,000 gradient steps, the arm successfully eliminates contact positioning error at the fixture, prompting the team to declare the updated model production-ready. Applying parameter updates ${\Delta}{\theta}$ directly to the policy network, however, produces an entirely new checkpoint with changed weights. The behavioral trials accumulated during predeployment qualification remain observations of the original checkpoint and do not establish performance of the updated one. Independent permission and fallback properties may remain applicable only while their plant, timing, authority, isolation, and realization premises hold; integration must check those premises after the update. While local contact accuracy improves, unconstrained backpropagation into shared representations triggers catastrophic forgetting in rarely visited state-space regions. As a result, the fine-tuned policy may lose stability during high-speed free-space slews or corrupt its emergency deceleration profiles. Because physical robot time and mechanical wear preclude repeating full-scale testing suites on hardware, regression suites must isolate and verify every operational envelope that gradient updates might perturb. **Online gradient updates require fresh behavioral qualification and reopen every assurance claim that depends on the changed checkpoint or execution conditions.**

**Architect’s countermeasure**: When a bounded correction suffices, use gated residual policy architectures (${\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}} = \pi_{\text{base}} + {g}({s})\,{\Delta}{\pi_\theta}$) with an externally enforced binary gate (${g}({s}) \equiv 0$ in free space) to localize real-world adaptation. Mandate automated, frozen non-regression suites that verify trajectory tracking and stopping profiles before granting execution authority to updated parameters.

**Fallacy**: *Believing that widening parameter distributions can substitute for missing physical mechanisms.*

When an assembly policy repeatedly fails on physical hardware, an engineering team might attempt to brute-force sim-to-real transfer by radically widening the simulator’s domain randomization bounds. They randomize link masses by ±50 percent and vary surface friction coefficients across the extreme interval from 0.05 to 1.5. On the physical test bench, however, the trained policy behaves poorly: it moves sluggishly through free space and hesitates indefinitely upon initial contact. Randomization perturbs coefficients exclusively within the governing differential equations already hardcoded in the physics engine. It cannot synthesize missing physical phenomena such as gearbox backlash, joint cable compliance, Stribeck friction, or sensor transport jitter. When dynamically incompatible virtual plants produce indistinguishable observations, a memoryless policy cannot choose separately for each plant; average-return optimization may favor a slow compromise or may sacrifice low-probability cases. While recurrent or history-conditioned architectures can adapt to wide parametric variations by estimating system properties online, even an adaptive policy cannot identify or compensate for physical mechanisms that never existed in its training simulator. **Expanding parameter randomization bounds merely shifts the probability distribution across existing simulator equations; it cannot synthesize unmodeled physical dynamics.**

**Architect’s countermeasure**: Calibrate baseline physics engine parameters against real hardware test fixtures before introducing domain randomization. Explicitly document whether parameter distributions derive from empirical instrument measurements or heuristic curricula such as automatic domain randomization. Enumerate known physical omissions, including transmission elasticity and actuator deadbands, in the policy manifest’s omission log, ensuring that hardware-level safety watchdogs bound operations within verified modeling assumptions.

**Fallacy**: *Assuming multi-step generative decoders can execute synchronously within inner actuator control loops.*

Aiming to capture multimodal trajectory distributions observed in offline demonstration logs, an engineer replaces the single-pass ACT feedforward decoder in `latch-bc-01` with an iterative 16-step diffusion denoising head. The team deploys the new architecture synchronously into the robot’s real-time inner control loop, scheduling a complete iterative sampling cycle for every 25 ms actuator setpoint. Under the hardware inference budget evaluated in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.03 Policy Training Regimes#^sec-training-decoder-costs|section 6.2.6]], executing all denoising steps consumes 48 ms, exceeding the allowable control step deadline. Because the synchronous dispatch pipeline stalls awaiting model convergence, the system routinely misses setpoint deadlines. The low-level controller must either freeze on stale command setpoints or trip safety watchdogs on every iteration overrun. The weight-streaming floor of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.05 The Cost of a Fresh Evaluation#^sec-brain-memory-wall|The memory wall]]** bounds each evaluation from below, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-action-chunking|Action chunking]]** shows that re-reading the action head on every evaluation can dominate the decoder’s cost. Each of the 16 dependent evaluations pays that floor, so the sum misses the setpoint deadline before scheduling jitter is even counted. **Deploying multi-step generative policies into high-rate actuator loops requires decoupling iterative trajectory generation from real-time setpoint tracking through asynchronous execution contracts.**

**Architect’s countermeasure**: Decouple iterative policy inference from high-frequency actuator control loops whenever measured inference latency approaches the control period. Formally partition the pipeline into an asynchronous planner generating trajectory chunks and an inner controller tracking them under qualified execution leases. Explicitly track prediction horizons, executed setpoint prefixes, and proposal renewal deadlines. Buffering trajectory chunks never grants permanent operational authority: the execution engine continuously validates physical safety invariants and triggers safe stopping profiles whenever a lease expires without a fresh proposal.
