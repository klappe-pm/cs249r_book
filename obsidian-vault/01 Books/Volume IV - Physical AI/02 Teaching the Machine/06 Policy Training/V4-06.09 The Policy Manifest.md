# V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training|V4-06 Policy Training]]

^sec-training-policy-manifest

Handing a candidate checkpoint to an evaluation team with only its weight file and a training curve hides whether its behavior on a displaced plate comes from human recovery demonstrations, uncalibrated simulator rollouts, or pure network extrapolation. The candidate selection must distinguish nominal behavior supported by demonstrations, newly taught recovery, and contact behavior that still depends on unverified simulation. The failure chains of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.08 Training Failure Modes and Permission-Path Interceptors#^sec-training-failure-modes|section 6.7]] make that distinction concrete for each checkpoint; the record accompanying the candidate into evaluation preserves the reasoning, including the assumptions that remain open.

The **policy manifest** records how a checkpoint was produced ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^tbl-06-training-schema|table 4]]). It lists the training stages and the data support the checkpoint inherits from the provenance records. It also lists the simulator versions, together with the **omission log** of mechanisms they leave out and mismatches paired probes have not closed. Finally, it records the wear incurred during adaptation and the operating domain the checkpoint declares. These fields state what evaluation must test and what runtime enforcement must accommodate. The runtime safety supervisor can check the manifest against its validated configuration before enabling a checkpoint. A declared boundary does not automatically generate a reliable detector or a safe clamp; those mechanisms require their own engineering and evidence.

The manifest separates optimization results from the conditions the policy may be asked to operate in. The **declared ODD** is the part of the ODD from **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-policy-coverage|Collection Policy Coverage]]** that the policy manifest claims: the supported cells of the data join, minus every state the omission log excludes. High training reward or low offline loss does not show that the policy is safe outside it. The declared ODD is therefore computed from the join and the log, never from the training curve, and a convergent metric cannot stand in for clearance over regimes nobody characterized.

The separate `latch-bc-recovery-02` manifest consumes the original provenance and the stipulated `latch-recovery-02` archive, adding the positively displaced-plate cell at the guarded approach to the candidate domain with the expert recovery labels and held-out sessions identified by digest. Its qualification request asks whether the updated policy completes that recovery and preserves nominal service under the same physical limits. The original archive remains unchanged, and negative displacement and the fast approach remain excluded. Current provenance bounds the evidence already available, whereas collecting and qualifying new experience expands the machine’s useful competence.

^tbl-06-training-schema

For the original latch campaign and its simulation-residual lineage, a compact manifest links the fields into one decision:

- `regime_sequence`: demonstration checkpoint `latch-bc-01` → `latch-sim-02`, a student initialized from `latch-bc-01` and distilled from a simulator teacher trained by reinforcement learning on privileged state (privileged contact wrenches ${\mathbf{w}}$ distilled into an 8-step observation-history buffer) → gated residual candidate `latch-res-03`. Retain each checkpoint hash, loss or reward specification, and stop rule.
- `base_checkpoint`: no pretrained robot policy underlies the latch checkpoints, which descend from `latch-bc-01` as `regime_sequence` records; any pretrained image encoder they use belongs here by digest. A candidate post-trained from the machine’s intent model would record that model’s weight digest, its pretraining mixture as a known unknown, and its action-space mapping, and would inherit the same declared ODD ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.03 Policy Training Regimes#^sec-training-pretrain-post-train|section 6.2.8]]).
- `policy_inference_contract`: specify checkpoint weights hash $\text{hash}({\theta})$, the machine’s 80M-parameter ACT architecture with one decoder pass per chunk, chunk prediction horizon ${H} =$ 16 setpoints at 25 ms, and the 40 ms p99 inference target. Record the selected execution prefix separately from the 50 ms proposal-renewal deadline and the selected chunk lease. Each renewed prefix requires valid evidence and a feasible continuation or fallback; the prediction horizon is not the active lease.
- `data_support`: cite the latch campaign’s provenance records and its scenario table by digest; their join supports the nominal strike plate at the guarded approach, and lists the displaced plate and the faster approach, whose cells hold no demonstrations, as known absences with no recovery label.
- `simulation_provenance`: record the actual solver build and contact parameters.
- `omission_log`: the example simulator models strike-plate contact with a penalty spring far softer than the plate and omits actuator transport lag, so the contact states those two departures govern are unverified for the checkpoints trained in simulation.
- `negative_event_registry`: cite by digest the authority-record entry, with its pre-trigger ring-buffer trace, for every fixture-shoulder strike, operator veto, or permission-path clamp with its event classification, without treating rejected learner commands as expert targets. Mark episodes an operator truncated, so that an update does not score an aborted command as a success.
- `adaptation_wear_log`: associate `latch-res-03` with the specific rig, above-threshold samples and separately identified contact events, motor-current history, modified residual weights, and free-space regression result.
- `declared_odd_boundary`: the supported cells minus the states the omission log excludes, here the nominal plate at the guarded approach of 0.03 m/s. The contact states governed by the two omissions stay outside the declared ODD for every simulation-refined checkpoint, `latch-sim-02` and `latch-res-03` alike, until paired probes close them. The displaced plate and the 0.10 m/s approach lie outside it, and the fast approach would stay outside even with demonstrations, because its contact deadline ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.05 The Simulator as a System Component#^sec-training-contact-exploit|section 6.4.2]]) leaves almost nothing beyond the contact-loop response. There the permission path must impose the guarded approach or refuse policy authority.

A qualification review would withhold operational authority from `latch-res-03` until paired contact probes quantify the contact-stiffness error and the omitted lag, and the combined controller passes contact, free-space, stopping, and wear-budget tests. With only the original campaign, the same rule leaves `latch-bc-01` as the checkpoint evaluation may sample, provided the support monitor of its chain refuses policy authority off the nominal plate ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.08 Training Failure Modes and Permission-Path Interceptors#^sec-training-failure-modes|section 6.7]]). Trained without a simulator, it carries no omission-log exclusions, so its declared ODD keeps the door’s contact states at the nominal plate and the guarded approach. The manifest records that decision and its evidence rather than converting a training score into permission.

The manifest references the collector, plant, calibration, and action taps through the provenance record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^sec-data-schema|The Dataset Schema]]**. The cited digests preserve which data and scenario table produced the checkpoint; later changes require a new record rather than silently updating its lineage. Simulator provenance similarly fixes the solver and timestep[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.14 Footnotes#^fn12|¹²]] used during optimization. Its separate omission log carries unresolved discrepancies from [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.06 Physical Channels of the Sim-To-Real Gap#^sec-training-sim-to-real-gap|section 6.5]] into evaluation’s coverage gaps and fault-injection tests.

Downstream engineering teams consume the manifest throughout the design, evaluation, and release lifecycle. The evaluation record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|The Evaluation Record]]** consumes it directly. Its target ODD is sampled from inside the declared ODD, and its catalog of coverage gaps inherits the known absences and the omission log, so evaluation suites probe the specific failure modes each training regime predicts. Targeted exploratory tests outside the candidate domain can also identify gaps, but they do not establish an operational claim there without new support and qualification. The permission path takes the declared ODD as the outer limit of the region in which it admits policy proposals, and uses the recorded support to parameterize out-of-distribution detectors. Verification and the safety case draw on the manifest to establish the provenance of every parameter update and to confirm that each omission is bounded by an independent physical interlock.

↪ The omission log becomes one of the upstream records from which fault records are compiled for release, developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.12 Fault Records for Release#^sec-verification-fault-manifest|Fault Records for Release]]**.

**Checkpoint 6.3: Policy manifests and declared ODDs**

Before releasing a trained policy checkpoint to physical verification, confirm the following relationships between the policy manifest, its omission log, and the declared ODD:

**Complete the latch record**: A fixture-shoulder strike appears after `latch-res-03` was trained with a soft penalty contact and no actuator lag. Which omitted mechanism belongs in `omission_log`, and which paired probe and admission decision must the manifest record?

**Confining the declared ODD**: Suppose demonstrations were collected at the fast approach and every omission-log entry were closed by paired probes. Why would the latch policy’s declared ODD still exclude the fast approach, which timing comparison decides it, and what must the permission path impose on a proposal that arrives at that speed?

**Negative event logging**: Why does the manifest keep the pre-trigger trace of an operator veto or fixture strike as labeled failure evidence rather than as an imitation target?

**Declared operational boundaries**: `latch-bc-01` proposes to open the door while the strike plate sits displaced from its nominal mount. How does the permission path use the `data_support` field to classify that state as ungrounded extrapolation rather than demonstrated competence, and why does a confident policy output not change the classification?

The manifest turns a training history into a claim with stated limits. The original record sends `latch-bc-01` to evaluation and keeps `latch-res-03` out of service until paired probes and regression tests close its gaps. The stipulated recovery record sends `latch-bc-recovery-02` with a wider, explicitly testable candidate claim. The common ways that decision goes wrong all let a training score stand in for that evidence.
