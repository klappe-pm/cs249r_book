# V4-06.07 Real-Robot Fine-Tuning and Residual Control

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training|V4-06 Policy Training]]

^sec-training-real-machine-tuning

Fine-tuning a simulation-trained policy directly on a physical robot to absorb a small fixture offset can fix the local contact error while overheating the motor windings, spending gearbox fatigue life on exploratory impacts, and silently corrupting the free-space approach trajectories that had already passed qualification. Mechanical assemblies rarely match their models exactly: on a warehouse cage door, for example, the physical strike plate may sit at a small mounting offset relative to its nominal position in the simulation model. By computing gradient updates directly from physical rollouts, on-robot fine-tuning adapts policy parameters to the true structural compliance, contact dynamics, and bus latencies of the hardware. However, because end-to-end policy gradients optimize cumulative task return rather than identifying individual system parameters, they do not isolate or quantify specific physical mechanisms. Unmodeled simulation discrepancies, such as an idealized contact spring or an omitted communication transport lag, therefore remain open in the engineering omission log until dedicated system-identification probes characterize them.

Furthermore, physical adaptation exacts a concrete hardware toll. In simulation, sampling aggressive state transitions near the boundaries of stability costs only compute cycles. On physical hardware, the exploratory actions and gradient updates that correct a contact error consume mechanical fatigue life and generate substantial heat in the motor windings. Every exploratory rollout risks driving the mechanism into an unrecoverable contact jam or an over-current trip, and unconstrained parameter updates can silently degrade nominal, previously certified behaviors.

Consider adapting the baseline policy `latch-sim-02` into `latch-res-03`, a residual candidate that adds a gated learned correction to the frozen `latch-sim-02` setpoints. This adaptation is motivated by the physical reality of the hardware fixture: the physical strike plate sits 1.5 mm away from its modeled location in simulation. The physical mechanism must guide a spring-loaded latch past this plate, where non-smooth Coulomb friction, cam surface geometry, and spring preloads produce sharp stick-slip transitions. The policy outputs setpoints every 25 ms (40 Hz). To adapt its control commands to the strike plate’s contact dynamics, an on-robot fine-tuning routine collects 20,000 interaction steps, totaling 500 s of active contact and near-contact operation.

During exploration, stochastic perturbations can inject force transients faster than the low-level controller can reject them. If 1.5 percent of the sampled steps breach the 15 N contact tripwire, the fine-tuning run records 300 above-threshold samples. However, engineers must not mistake these raw sample counts for independent mechanical collisions. Consecutive over-threshold steps typically belong to a single sustained contact episode rather than multiple distinct impacts. Accurately counting mechanical shocks and evaluating their damage potential requires temporal event segmentation alongside continuous force-time telemetry.

Every mechanical impact transmits impulsive loads directly into the drivetrain. Translating that cumulative stress into an estimated expenditure of actuator service life requires an empirical, drive-specific load spectrum and fatigue life model. Actuator thermal dynamics impose a second, equally rigid operational boundary. Stochastic exploration dither elevates the root-mean-square current passing through the motor coils. Without explicit cooling intervals scheduled between training episodes, this continuous Joule heating can push motor windings to their thermal derating threshold within minutes.

Demonstrating improved performance on a specific contact fixture does not prove that the overall manipulation task remains sound. An optimization step yielding a parameter update ${\Delta}{\theta}$ produces an entirely distinct model checkpoint. Empirical evaluations gathered on the prior policy ${\theta}_0$ remain historical observations of ${\theta}_0$ alone; they do not substantiate safety claims for the updated weights. Gradient updates that successfully minimize contact forces on the strike plate can inadvertently distort free-space approach trajectories or degrade emergency stopping profiles in operational regions absent from the fine-tuning rollouts. Because physical trials consume valuable robot runtime and cause mechanical wear, requalification protocols must be strictly prioritized to verify both the targeted contact improvement and the baseline capabilities the update could destabilize.

Each updated checkpoint must carry its own explicit configuration manifest and regression evidence before deployment. Moreover, while optimization progresses on physical hardware, the safety permission path must actively evaluate every candidate action proposed by intermediate policies. Restricting where learned corrections may act keeps unconstrained weight updates from corrupting verified behaviors.

### Residual Policy Architectures and Bounded Authority

^sec-training-residual-architectures

To let `latch-res-03` adapt to the strike plate without perturbing its free-space approach, residual architectures freeze the base policy weights and multiply the learned additive correction by an independently enforced binary gate:

$$
{\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}({s}) = \pi_{\text{base}}({s}) + {g}({s})\,{\Delta}{\pi_\theta}({s}), \tag{11}
$$

 where $\pi_{\text{base}}$ is the frozen base controller, ${\Delta}{\pi_\theta}$ is a learned contact correction trained via reinforcement learning on the physical machine against the latch reward of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.03 Policy Training Regimes#^eq-training-latch-reward|equation 1]], and ${g}({s})\in\{0,1\}$ is an independently evaluated deterministic gate that equals one only within the certified contact envelope.

^eq-training-gated-residual

Both the base policy and the composite controller require explicit verification, because freezing an unverified base policy such as `latch-sim-02` does not make it qualified for deployment. Instead, the role of the gate ${g}({s})$ is structural: it forces the residual correction to evaluate *identically to zero* in free space and across all non-contact states. There, an absence of reward gradients during training would still allow a function approximator to output nonzero values on out-of-distribution inputs. Before any command reaches physical actuators, the permission path verifies the validity of the current state estimate (the machine’s fused estimate of its own configuration, developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09 Spatial Memory#^sec-memory|Spatial Memory]]**) and audits the proposed composite action.

Relying on a soft training loss, such as an action-norm penalty $\|{\Delta}{\pi_\theta}({s})\|^2$, merely biases the optimizer toward modest corrections during nominal rollouts; it offers no deterministic runtime guarantee. In contrast, the independent gate explicitly revokes residual authority whenever the robot operates outside designated regimes. Even with an architectural gate, the verification suite must evaluate transient switching behaviors at gate boundaries, state-estimation latency, execution timing jitter, and joint torque limits under the combined controller.

Imposing an output magnitude ceiling, $\|{g}({s}){\Delta}{\pi_\theta}({s})\|\le{\Delta_{\max}}$, restricts only one dimension of authority. A production permission path must also enforce slew-rate limits on the correction delta, prevent joint limit violations, verify multi-axis dynamic feasibility, and invoke the qualified fallback when active execution loses valid state, evidence, or lease. A late replacement proposal does not revoke a still-valid active prefix; that prefix may continue only within its own validity conditions. Furthermore, the authorized residual envelope must lie strictly within the verified stability margin of the *combined* closed-loop system across its intended operating envelope; checking an isolated torque limit against an idealized, single-axis specification is never sufficient. For the latch fixture, for instance, the architecture activates residual corrections exclusively during confirmed physical contact, enforces a hard zero via the gate throughout the free-space approach, and clamps joint torque slew rates. Following each optimization step on hardware, an automated regression suite must re-verify free-space trajectory tracking, contact engagement forces, emergency stopping, thermal duty cycles, and drivetrain shock profiles before granting operational authority to the new checkpoint. An improperly verified residual, even when clipped in magnitude, can still induce limit-cycle oscillations or drive the actuator gearboxes into saturation. Freezing the base policy and gating an additive residual is the most localized way to refine a policy with rewards; other post-training regimes modify the decoder’s input noise or update every weight in the network, widening the regression scope accordingly.

### Reinforcement Learning After Pretraining

^sec-training-rl-post-training

The gated residual is one member of a broader family of post-training regimes. Every method that refines a pretrained policy using environmental rewards must specify which parameters are allowed to change, because updating shared weights to double throughput on one fixture can silently degrade baseline behaviors elsewhere. That choice determines how much regression evidence is required to re-qualify the resulting checkpoint. Modern robotic systems use four primary post-training strategies ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.07 Real-Robot Fine-Tuning and Residual Control#^tbl-06-state-of-practice|table 3]]):

- **Residual control** freezes the base policy weights and learns an additive correction term, exactly as demonstrated by `latch-res-03`. This strategy has been successfully deployed to refine frozen chunked imitation policies for high-precision assembly, training the residual policy via PPO in simulation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.13 Source References#^ref-ankile2025residual|Ankile et al. 2025]]). Residual architectures are straightforward to bound because independent hardware permission logic can directly gate, saturate, and rate-limit the additive output.
- **Noise-space steering** freezes every parameter of an underlying diffusion or flow-matching policy, training an auxiliary policy only to predict the initial latent noise vector supplied to the reverse diffusion trajectory. Every proposed trajectory still passes through the frozen generative decoder, so steering is restricted to outputs that decoder can generate. This restriction does not establish that those outputs lie within demonstrated support or satisfy physical limits. Furthermore, the optimizer requires only black-box sampling access to the base policy ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.13 Source References#^ref-wagenmaker2025dsrl|Wagenmaker et al. 2025]]). Here, the support of the base distribution constrains the proposed behaviors; the independent permission path must still determine whether those proposals may execute.
- **Advantage-conditioned post-training** updates all network weights using supervised learning conditioned on advantage estimates. A learned critic evaluates the advantage of candidate actions across human demonstrations, autonomous rollouts, and real-time human interventions; the policy is then trained on this mixed corpus conditioned on target advantage and queried for high-advantage actions at run time. Rather than weighting gradients by the advantage or discarding failed trials, this formulation supplies an advantage indicator token as an additional conditioning input during supervised training so the network learns to associate suboptimal trajectories with negative advantage and successful recoveries with positive advantage, allowing runtime inference to prompt the policy with the positive-advantage indicator. Applied to generalist robot manipulation models, this approach more than doubled throughput and halved failure rates on the reported physical manipulation tasks ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.13 Source References#^ref-physicalintelligence2025recap|Physical Intelligence et al. 2025]]). However, because gradient updates modify every weight in the network, all nominal behaviors remain subject to drift.
- **Policy-gradient fine-tuning in simulation** applies reinforcement learning techniques ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.05 The Simulator as a System Component#^sec-training-parallel-simulation|section 6.4.1]]) to update the weights of a pretrained vision-language-action policy using sparse or binary success rewards in parallel simulation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.13 Source References#^ref-li2025simplevlarl|Li et al. 2025]]). Like advantage conditioning, this paradigm modifies all network parameters; in addition, it inherits every physical approximation and contact-solver idealization present in the underlying simulation environment.

Regression scope follows where the composite output can change and which execution assumptions the update affects. An independently enforced residual gate localizes the changed output, but regression still covers its active region, boundary transitions, combined closed-loop behavior, and timing and resource effects. Noise-space steering can change sampling throughout the base domain despite frozen decoder weights, so every affected operating mode requires regression unless a separate gate establishes a narrower scope. Full-weight updates can also alter behaviors across the entire operational envelope. A newly fine-tuned full-weight model demands complete end-to-end regression testing across all previously validated operational modes, and the system’s `adaptation_wear_log` must document every modified parameter group. Whether adaptation rollouts accumulate physical machine wear on hardware or incur modeling dependencies in simulation, each rollout must be recorded in the policy manifest. No model can legitimately claim expanded operational authority without concrete validation data recorded in the system audit log.

Bounded residual authority restricts the influence of an exploratory policy during execution, but it cannot alone prevent mechanical damage or eliminate latent vulnerabilities introduced during earlier training phases. Every stage of policy synthesis, from demonstration ingestion and simulation contact mechanics to residual adaptation, leaves a distinct behavioral signature on closed-loop execution. When an embodied machine develops high-frequency chattering, thermal derating, or semantic drift, systems engineers can trace that failure behavior directly back to the training assumptions, solver compromises, and architectural trade-offs that created it.
