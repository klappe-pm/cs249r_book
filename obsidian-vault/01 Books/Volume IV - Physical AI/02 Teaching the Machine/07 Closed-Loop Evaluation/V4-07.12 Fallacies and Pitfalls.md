# V4-07.12 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^sec-evaluation-fallacies-pitfalls

An empirical evaluation supports engineering claims only within the precise physical boundaries and statistical assumptions of its test protocol. In embodied machine learning systems, severe evaluation errors arise when teams extrapolate localized results across untested operational envelopes or attribute system-level safety guarantees to a learned policy when deterministic enforcement mechanisms actually did the work.

**Fallacy**: *Repeated success in a single nominal condition proves broad operational readiness.*

Consider a door-opening evaluation campaign that repeats a single nominal configuration twenty times: a standard strike plate, an unencumbered approach vector, a stationary mobile base, and an empty payload rack. Under the binomial model, twenty flawless runs yield the 86.1 percent lower confidence bound derived in notebook [7.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/07_evaluation/07_evaluation.html#nbk-evaluation-derivation-20-run-lower-bound) for that specific setup. However, they provide no statistical evidence for the remaining operating conditions, such as misaligned latch hardware, floor irregularities, or a loaded tote rack that shifts the manipulator’s center of mass and reduces actuator torque margins. Repeating identical trials merely sharpens precision on a single nominal operating point while leaving deployment-critical boundary conditions unprobed. An engineering claim of operational readiness must explicitly define the target ODD, distribute test budgets across the physical parameters governing that domain, and bound uncertainty within each stratified cell. **Nominal repetition sharpens precision on a single cell; it provides zero evidence for the rest of the operational design domain.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Explicitly partition the declared operational design domain into a structured condition matrix, allocate trial quotas across all operational strata, and report confidence intervals per cell rather than pooling unstratified runs.

**Fallacy**: *Zero failures in 1,000 trials proves compliance with a 99.9 percent reliability specification.*

Suppose a latch manipulation policy opens a door 1,000 consecutive times without a single safety trip, evaluated against a specification requiring 99.9 percent success at 95 percent confidence. The observed sample success proportion is perfect (${\hat{p}} = 1.00$). Standard Wald intervals based on the asymptotic Central Limit Theorem collapse into the degenerate point $[1.0, 1.0]$ because the estimated variance ${\hat{p}}(1-{\hat{p}})/{n}$ vanishes identically at the boundary. However, an exact non-asymptotic Clopper-Pearson inversion (${p_{\text{lower}}} = 0.05^{1/{n}}$) demonstrates that the true one-sided lower confidence bound reaches only 0.9970, falling decisively short of the 99.9 percent target ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^tbl-07-confidence-bounds|table 3]]). Certifying compliance with that target requires 2,995 consecutive clean runs. A parallel fallacy recurs in early-stage pilot testing. Observing zero failures across 20 trials (${p_{\text{lower}}} =$ 86.1 percent) cannot demonstrate even a modest 90 percent success bound (29 clean runs required), much less an industrial 99 percent standard (299 clean runs required). Test budgets must be derived directly from the required confidence level and reliability target before execution begins, and validation reports must document exact non-asymptotic bounds rather than empirical success ratios. **Zero failures across an insufficient sample size represent an evidence deficit, not empirical proof of compliance.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Reject asymptotic Wald intervals near parameter boundaries. Precalculate the minimum sample budget using the exact non-asymptotic Clopper-Pearson formulation (${n} = \lceil \ln{\alpha_{\text{sig}}} / \ln {p_{\text{target}}} \rceil$) or its continuous-time counterpart for exposure hours (${T} \ge 3.0/{\lambda}$), and report exact one-sided lower confidence bounds in all compliance audits.

**Fallacy**: *High offline action agreement on static logs guarantees closed-loop physical stability.*

An evaluation report citing close agreement with expert actions on recorded teleoperation logs, whether framed as negligible action mean squared error (MSE), high discrete token prediction accuracy, low validation perplexity, or high action cosine similarity, measures dataset imitation on static buffers and nothing more. As demonstrated by the mobile aisle policy in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.04 Closed-Loop Dynamics#^sec-evaluation-closed-loop-dynamics|section 7.3]], a policy can match its offline demonstrations with near-zero error and still exhaust its lateral clearance through the kinematic integration of a minute, persistent heading bias. Compounding this error, covariate shift drives the system into unmodeled state spaces once trajectory drift departs from the demonstration support.

Physical contact introduces even sharper failure modes. At non-Lipschitz mechanical interfaces, sub-millimeter positional deviations that barely register in aggregate MSE trigger destructive reaction forces (${F} = {k_{\text{contact}}} {\Delta} {x}$) and mechanical wedging. This produces dramatic ranking inversions where policies with lower offline MSE fail completely while policies with higher MSE succeed. Directional metrics such as cosine similarity fail for complementary reasons: they ignore command magnitude entirely. A policy commanding the correct directional vector with 25 percent too little force stalls below a latch’s breakout threshold, whereas commanding 150 percent of the required force can trip protective torque boundaries. Offline metrics serve well as initial screening filters to discard defective training checkpoints. Empirical closed-loop capability, however, must be evaluated using physical observables, namely task-completion rate, ACR, MTBI, and ${D_{\text{int}}}$, tracked against the complete execution denominator, with protection outcomes recorded separately. Even then, these finite-sample empirical metrics do not constitute a formal proof of closed-loop stability. **Offline validation loss, MSE, and cosine similarity measure dataset mimicry; closed-loop physical dynamics decide survival.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Treat offline loss, action MSE, and cosine similarity strictly as preliminary triage filters. Require closed-loop physical rollouts with full-denominator accounting to support behavioral claims, and require separate model-based Lyapunov or invariant set analyses for any formal stability guarantee.

**Pitfall**: *Peeking at accumulating rollouts and terminating testing early inflates false safety certification.*

When an engineering team halts an evaluation campaign after 30 consecutive clean runs because intermediate results look favorable, and then reports the confidence bounds of a fixed-sample protocol, they commit sequential peeking. Halting on a winning streak distorts the underlying statistical distribution. An adaptive stopping rule inflates the false-positive rate, rendering standard fixed-sample confidence calculations invalid. While a predeclared zero-failure test can terminate upon the first observed failure to reject an inadequate policy, terminating early to accept a candidate requires an explicit sequential design. Sequential probability ratio testing provides a mathematically sound alternative. Ville’s martingale inequality bounds the probability of false acceptance by $e^{-{\Lambda_{\text{up}}}}$ for any declared acceptance threshold ${\Lambda_{\text{up}}}$, which resolves to ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}/(1-{\beta})$ under classic Wald boundaries ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-sequential-sprt|section 7.7.3]]). **Choose the stopping rule before collecting outcomes, and report the error guarantee for that rule.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Pre-register sequential decision boundaries (${\Lambda_{\text{up}}} \approx \ln((1-{\beta})/{\alpha_{\text{sig}}})$, ${\Lambda_{\text{lo}}} \approx \ln({\beta}/(1-{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}))$) before running trials. Update the cumulative log-likelihood ratio ${\Lambda_m}$ after each physical rollout, and award the declared SPRT acceptance or rejection decision only when the statistic crosses its corresponding threshold. A physical hazard or invalidating protocol evidence can terminate the campaign independently. Keep the complete trial log and the termination reason, and claim no favorable result that the declared statistical rule does not support.

**Pitfall**: *Crediting the learned policy with surviving transport stalls that the enforcement architecture handled.*

Suppose a mobile manipulator completes 50 nominal aisle navigation trials, leading the engineering team to credit the learned transformer policy with inherent robustness against serializer/deserializer (SerDes) bus jitter. Later, a qualification trial injects a synthetic mailbox stall that exceeds the 60 ms chunk lease. The lease expires, and the deterministic permission path detects the deadline miss and commands an immediate controlled dynamic stop. This stop is analogous to an industrial Category 1 stop (a controlled deceleration under drive power, with torque removed once the machine stands still), as classified in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-stop-categories|Industrial stop categories and the safe torque off trap]]**. This safe recovery is executed entirely by the enforcement architecture, not the learned model. Nominal task completion does not prove that the neural network weights can detect, absorb, or recover from communication dropouts. Rigorous fault injection must evaluate the permission path’s detection latency and stopping distance margins under stress, and the final evaluation report must attribute safety enforcement directly to the hardware and runtime mechanisms that actually executed the stop. **Do not credit unverified neural weights with safety guarantees provided by the deterministic permission path.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Conduct systematic fault injection, including synthetic communication stalls and sensor frame drops, to independently benchmark the permission path’s braking envelope, and attribute safety mechanisms strictly to the hardware and software layers that executed them.

**Fallacy**: *Zero physical collisions or hardware damage in testing proves high autonomous policy reliability.*

When an autonomous vehicle or mobile manipulator completes an extended test campaign without collisions, teams frequently declare that the policy demonstrated exceptional reliability (for example, claiming an MTBF equal to the total logged run time). However, if human safety operators disengaged the autonomy system or the permission path intervened repeatedly to avert impending collisions, the zero-damage outcome (${N_{\text{damage}}} = 0$) occurred only because the protective safety envelope actively censored catastrophic trajectories. What the testbed truly recorded was a sequence of intervention events. True autonomous capability must be evaluated through ACR and MTBI. Frequent human takeovers render commercial deployments economically unviable, whereas small automatic clips by the permission path may represent routine boundary regulation. An evaluation report must classify and audit these intervention tiers separately. Censoring interventions or tallying assisted rollouts as unassisted policy successes confuses the protective competence of the supervisory envelope with the autonomous competence of the learned policy. **Zero collisions under active safety supervision must be interpreted alongside interventions; they do not by themselves establish policy reliability or complete protection coverage.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Enforce strict full-denominator accounting. Record every human takeover, permission-path clamping action, and virtual tripwire breach as an autonomy disengagement (${A}_i = 0, {{n_{\text{int}}}}_i \ge 1$). Report these unassisted autonomy metrics alongside overall task completion rates, mission cycle times, and protective safety statistics.

**Pitfall**: *Qualifying a policy on a single reference robot assumes zero unit-to-unit hardware variance across the fleet.*

Consider an engineering team that logs 300 failure-free trials on a single laboratory robot (Unit 01) and certifies the policy for fleet deployment. When deployed onto Unit 02 on the manufacturing floor, the identical policy repeatedly jams against fixtures or develops severe actuator chatter. As detailed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.03 What a Physical Success Rate Measures#^sec-evaluation-what-measured|section 7.2]], physical unit-to-unit variations (transmission lost motion, harmonic drive compliance and stiffness, structural link flexure under load, camera mounting misalignments, and motor winding tolerances) substantially alter the plant transfer function across robot builds. Qualifying a policy on a single “golden unit” merely calibrates the model to that individual chassis’s electromechanical profile rather than establishing fleet-wide reliability. Furthermore, running evaluations during a single shift overlooks non-stationary environmental factors: diurnal sunlight glare sweeping across vision sensors, floor traction changes driven by ambient humidity, or thermal drift from motor winding resistance heating (${I}^2 {R}$) that alter plant dynamics without altering a single neural network parameter. **Evaluating a single robot chassis characterizes that specific unit’s mechanical quirks; fleet release requires stratifying trials across the manufacturing distribution.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Stratify physical qualification protocols across multiple chassis serial numbers sampled from the fleet manufacturing distribution ${\mathcal{M}}$. Audit unit-specific lost motion and kinematic link offsets prior to evaluation, and reserve final acceptance testing for untouched production units that were never used during policy development or hyperparameter tuning.
