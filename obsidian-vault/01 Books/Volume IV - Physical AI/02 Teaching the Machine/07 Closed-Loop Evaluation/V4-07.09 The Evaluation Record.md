# V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^sec-evaluation-logs

When a twenty-run door campaign finishes with twenty clean latches, the pass count alone cannot tell an auditor which strike-plate cell was tested, whether aborted runs were dropped from the denominator, or which network weights were loaded on the robot. The **evaluation record**, the fifth link in the record chain, preserves the formal reasoning that connects empirical observations to a bounded operational claim. It consumes the policy manifest, citing it by cryptographic digest, and incorporates the known absences listed in the provenance record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^sec-data-schema|The Dataset Schema]]**. Across the seven fields of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^tbl-07-evaluation-record-schema|table 5]], it then documents precisely which operational conditions were tested, how physical outcomes were counted, which statistical assumptions justify the conclusions, and where that empirical support ends. Its `runtime_monitor_specs` field establishes explicit detection requirements for runtime safety engineering. These specifications require an independent implementation whose sensing latencies, execution timing, and fallback transitions undergo separate validation. By binding measured physical outcomes to declared operational boundaries and isolating human supervisory observations with their source, the record enables review of the learned proposer without granting its weights direct authority over hardware actuators.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.16 Footnotes#^fn13|¹³]]

^tbl-07-evaluation-record-schema

Engineering claims and acceptance predicates in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^tbl-07-evaluation-record-schema|table 5]] must be established prior to data collection. For the door’s strict autonomy claim, success requires opening the latch mechanism without triggering the 15 N contact tripwire or invoking a permission-path override. The target ODD is restricted to a declared subset of the manifest domain, explicitly separating the tested fixture from doors with alternative strike plates, latch frictions, or return spring rates. Fixing these boundaries before testing prevents post-hoc rationalization, ensuring an evaluation team cannot alter success criteria after observing an anomalous run.

Rigorous configuration tracking ensures that subsequent performance comparisons remain physically interpretable. Cryptographic software hashes pin the evaluated policy weights and inference engine binaries, while chassis serial numbers, measured mechanical tolerances, and calibration logs bind the physical plant. When a camera lens is swapped, motor firmware is updated, or transmission backlash develops in an actuator, the physical plant changes. Recording these modifications allows engineers to assess whether historical evaluation evidence remains valid for the current hardware assembly. A shared component identifier establishes audit traceability, but it cannot guarantee physical equivalence when mechanical wear or manufacturing tolerances alter dynamic response.

The evaluation record preserves the trial protocol, which must be fixed before the first physical run begins to eliminate the optional-stopping hazard of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-trials-per-claim|section 7.7.1]] and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-sequential-sprt|section 7.7.3]]. The protocol predeclares the required sample size ${n}$ derived from the claim’s target reliability and confidence level, the discrete outcome classifications, and the explicit stopping rule. Under a zero-failure protocol, the campaign halts immediately upon encountering its first failure, formally rejecting the target acceptance condition rather than retroactively adjusting sample sizes. If the campaign completes all ${n}$ trials without failure, it reports the exact non-asymptotic bound ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}^{1/{n}}$. A pooled binomial bound requires each trial condition to be drawn independently and identically distributed from the target operational distribution. A stratified quota grid instead demands separate inference within each stratum and explicit weighting before any aggregated claim can span the target ODD. The record documents how runs were sampled across the cells of the target ODD, the pseudorandom seed sequence governing mobile base placement, and the automated reset sequence executed between consecutive attempts to re-latch the mechanism and return the base to its 2 m standoff. When the mobile base navigates back to this standoff autonomously, real-world physical side effects accumulate. Each drive cycle warms the motor windings and draws down the battery, and wheel slip creates odometry drift, the dead-reckoning error that grows when wheel-encoder and inertial measurements are integrated. These physical couplings introduce statistical dependence between adjacent attempts. The record therefore specifies whether trials were conducted with independent physical resets or chained in series, cataloging every manual intervention, hardware abort, and protocol deviation.

The outcome log gives every run four distinct classifications: task completion, strict proposer autonomy, safety intervention history, and physical protection status. Decoupling these dimensions prevents optimistic misclassification. For example, a trial in which the permission path clamps motor torques to prevent an impact can satisfy task completion (${Y}_i = 1$) while failing strict proposer autonomy (${A}_i = 0$). Conversely, an emergency protective stop that halts the manipulator before collision safely mitigates the physical hazard, yet it leaves the latch closed, yielding a task failure (${Y}_i = 0$). Omitting censored trials is strictly forbidden: timeouts, communication dropouts, and hardware aborts must remain in the trial denominator. The log reports ACR, MTBI, spatial intervention density, and recovery reset durations, stratified by intervention class. Alongside these reliability statistics, the log archives raw physical telemetry: cycle durations, peak contact forces, actuator temperatures, operational clearances, and limit crossings. Preserving these fields ensures that downstream reviewers understand both useful task delivery and the physical wear or supervisory burden required to sustain it.

The statistical specification formally defines the point estimator, confidence method, nominal confidence level, assumed sampling distribution, and underlying mathematical validity conditions. For fixed-sample campaigns, the record mandates exact non-asymptotic formulations, such as the Clopper-Pearson binomial lower bound (${p_{\text{lower}}} = {\alpha_{\text{sig}}}^{1/{n}}$) and, for hazard rates, the exposure bound that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.10 Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall#^sec-evaluation-limits|section 7.9]] develops next, rejecting degenerate normal approximations that fail near boundary probabilities. If the campaign employs sequential testing, the record preserves the competing operational hypotheses, decision thresholds, complete outcome sequence, terminal boundary decision, and calibrated error rates. As detailed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-sequential-sprt|section 7.7.3]], sequential testing allows valid early stopping without inflating Type I or Type II error rates, provided the stopping boundaries are enforced rigorously. Alongside the supported claim, the record documents unsupported claims and known coverage gaps. This catalog incorporates known absence regions from the demonstration datasets and unmodeled simulator dynamics from the policy manifest, appending any operational conditions left unvisited during physical testing. For coverage gaps amenable to onboard perception, the runtime monitor specifications define the monitoring signal, detection threshold, and validated fallback action across the five monitoring channels that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.10 Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall#^sec-evaluation-limits|section 7.9]] sets out once it has shown which gaps testing cannot close. Any coverage gap that cannot be sensed at runtime remains in the catalog as a hard operational constraint, restricting where and how the physical machine may be deployed.

Consider the twenty-run door campaign that opened this chapter. The learned latch policy engaged and opened the cage door twenty times from the 2 m standoff at the 0.03 m/s guarded approach speed, with the mobile base stationary and zero overrides from the runtime permission path. An auditor inspecting the evaluation record interprets this evidence through the exact non-asymptotic Clopper-Pearson bound of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.07 Confidence from a Finite Campaign#^sec-evaluation-closed-loop-survival|section 7.6]]. At a 95 percent confidence level, twenty clean runs establish a lower confidence limit of 86.1 percent. This result rejects the degenerate interval $[1.0, 1.0]$ that the Wald normal approximation reports for a spotless record.

The completed record exposes the structural limits of the claim, showing how far evidence narrows on its path from offline data curation through policy training. The scenario table of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry|Choosing a tractable coverage table]]** crossed three strike-plate positions with two approach speeds, yielding six cells in total, yet the retained demonstrations supported only one: the nominal strike plate at the guarded approach. The policy manifest designated that single cell as the declared ODD of `latch-bc-01`, and the target ODD encompassed the entire cell. Twenty clean physical trials were drawn from it. They bound the failure probability there at 13.9 percent, the one-sided upper confidence limit under the tested iid distribution ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^tbl-07-confidence-bounds|table 3]]). Every condition excluded by this narrowing enters the coverage gap catalog as an inherited deficit rather than an empirically closed claim. The five operational cells that contained zero demonstrations received zero physical trials, leaving every displaced-plate condition unvalidated. The policy manifest’s omission log does hold two unclosed simulator omissions (soft penalty contact dynamics and missing network transport lag). **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^sec-training-policy-manifest|The Policy Manifest]]**, however, scoped them to the simulation-refined checkpoints, the distilled student `latch-sim-02` (initialized from `latch-bc-01`, then distilled from a simulator teacher) and the residual candidate `latch-res-03` built on it. Because `latch-bc-01` was trained directly on hardware demonstrations without a simulator, its gap catalog does not inherit those omissions. The record names them only to state that its bound does not transfer. The simulation-refined checkpoints require their own empirical campaigns, and the evaluation record of `latch-bc-01` can neither resolve their simulation discrepancies nor lend its statistics to their modified weights. Physical testing introduces an additional operational gap. The mobile base carried zero payload in its 50 kg tote rack during these trials, so the record provides no empirical evidence for machine dynamics under loaded conditions. Finally, the record makes plain why twenty clean runs cannot justify high-reliability deployment. Establishing a 99 percent lower confidence limit on task success at 95 percent confidence requires at least 299 consecutive zero-failure trials. The evaluation record thus gives a reviewer a quantitative basis for rejecting the assertion that the policy is ready for unmonitored production deployment.

### Qualifying a Recovery Update

^sec-evaluation-recovery-update

Retraining a policy to recover from a displaced strike plate modifies every layer of its neural network weights, so the twenty clean nominal trials logged for `latch-bc-01` no longer certify the updated model even on the centered strike plate. A separate, explicitly stipulated teaching branch therefore evaluates `latch-bc-recovery-02`, the candidate policy trained on the curated `latch-recovery-02` archive of displaced-plate recovery demonstrations, while keeping the original nominal campaign unchanged. This added experience covers one displaced-plate cell, the positive-offset plate in the scenario table of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry|Choosing a tractable coverage table]]**, at the same 0.03 m/s guarded approach speed. The negative-offset plate and all three cells at the faster approach remain known absences. It provides a concrete engineering justification to test expanded competence, though training convergence alone does not qualify the policy for operational deployment.

↩︎ The curated recovery archive from which `latch-bc-recovery-02` is trained is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|Intervention and Recovery Data]]**.

The evaluation engineer freezes the new network weights and reserves fresh physical runs for both the nominal cell and the recovery cell. The nominal campaign verifies that the policy has not regressed against the original service baseline, while the displaced-plate campaign evaluates the proposed capability expansion. Each campaign maintains its own full denominator, declared within-cell sampling distribution, fixed execution time limit, strict autonomy predicate, and contact force thresholds. Exploratory rollouts and the earlier checkpoint’s twenty trials cannot be credited toward either candidate’s denominator.

Suppose, solely for this worked evidence branch, that each cell supplies 2,995 independent zero-failure trials under the predeclared protocol, with on-time door completion, zero counted overrides, and no contact-limit violations. Each cell individually satisfies the 99.9 percent one-sided lower success target at 95 percent confidence. These represent separate marginal bounds; establishing a simultaneous claim across both cells requires an explicit confidence allocation because when two cells are tested separately at that confidence level, each cell carries up to a ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$ chance of a false pass and the union bound allows up to $2{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$ total risk that at least one of the two claims is false. Splitting the significance level ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$ equally between the two cells using a Bonferroni allocation assigns each cell ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}'} =$ 0.025, raising the per-cell requirement to 3,688 clean runs. At the illustrative 40 s cycle duration, each 2,995-run marginal campaign consumes 33.3 h of testing, and the Bonferroni count lengthens it in proportion, before accounting for recovery-specific resets, thermal cooldowns, or scheduled maintenance. Variations in the physical cycle time alter the total testing duration, but they do not alter the underlying statistical sample size required to certify the claim.

Under these stipulated premises, with 2,995 clean runs per cell, the evaluation record supports two marginal 99.9 percent claims for the updated policy candidate, one on the nominal cell and one on the displaced-plate cell of the tested robot. It does not support the simultaneous claim that both hold at once. That claim would need 3,688 clean runs per cell. The original policy candidate retains its narrower operational boundary. Strike plates at other lateral offsets, higher approach velocities, loaded chassis dynamics, and uncalibrated robot units remain outside this certified envelope. If the runtime permission path executes protective adjustments while tasks still complete, the evaluator records successful whole-machine service alongside reduced strict proposer autonomy, recomputing statistical bounds directly from those distinct outcome classes. System operators must not conceal permission-path interventions to artificially inflate autonomous completion rates, nor should they conflate a safe intervention with an unmitigated service failure.

This teaching branch completes the policy improvement cycle without claiming that an unperformed physical experiment took place. Incorporating recovery demonstrations alters the policy candidate; conducting independent regression and expansion trials alters what can be formally claimed about that candidate. The numerical results presented above follow from stipulated trial counts rather than empirical measurements of the physical machine described in this book.

Rather than serving as a blanket safety certification for the autonomous machine, an evaluation record establishes the formal empirical boundaries and statistical uncertainty of an individual learned component operating under controlled conditions. In **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.12 Fault Records for Release#^sec-verification-fault-manifest|Fault Records for Release]]**, the fault-record set ingests these evaluation records as structured evidence, combining empirical policy bounds with fault-injection results and permission-path latency bounds for the safety case that decides whether the integrated machine may operate.

↪ The distinction between a demonstration and calibrated evidence in a release safety case is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.04 Safety Cases and Claims#^sec-release-assurance-grammar|Claims, arguments, and warrants]]**.

**Systems Perspective 7.2: Evaluating a machine that falls**

An evaluation record must account for physical damage when every failure in an unsupported cell is a fall that damages the test article.

A failed guarded latch engagement can leave the wheeled mobile manipulator and mechanism intact for subsequent trials when qualified precontact limits, passive protection, and intervention response make the engagement recoverable. A reactive contact tripwire alone cannot prevent every initial impact. When a bipedal humanoid loses balance, however, a single failure can strike the floor, destroying actuators, structural links, and onboard sensor payloads.

- **Fall frequency constitutes an independent estimand**: Because a catastrophic fall damages the physical test article, the evaluation record must track fall events using a dedicated denominator and an independent statistical confidence bound, rather than burying structural collapse inside a blended task completion metric.
- **Disturbance strata must be fixed prior to testing**: Environmental perturbations (low-friction spills, compliant turf, curb thresholds, and horizontal impact impulses) along with their respective sampling weights must be declared in the sampling protocol before commencing physical evaluation. Testing exclusively on polished concrete provides no support for unvisited disturbance regimes.
- **Human demonstrator stability does not transfer to robot dynamics**: Retargeted human motion reflects the human demonstrator’s balance and vestibular feedback, whereas teleoperation and kinesthetic demonstrations collected on the robot involve the robot’s physical plant. Either modality can omit balance and recovery states under relevant disturbances, including dynamic reaches under heavy external loads. Those omissions belong in the coverage gap catalog. Investigating them requires appropriate containment rather than exposing an unqualified test article to destructive trials.

By keeping these cell boundaries explicit, the evaluation record separates the nominal and recovery behaviors it validated from the operating regimes it did not. Rare physical hazards and unobserved dynamic mechanisms remain distinct evidence obligations that an autonomous task campaign cannot dismiss, even when every monitored trial completes successfully.
