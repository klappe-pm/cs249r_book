# V4-07.02 From Clean Runs to Bounded Claims

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^sec-evaluation-door-campaign

The checkpoint `latch-bc-01` arrives from offline imitation learning with a policy manifest that declares exactly one supported condition, the nominal strike plate at the guarded approach. The manifest marks where the policy’s training evidence ends, and evaluation asks what physical trials can establish inside that line. The recovery candidate `latch-bc-recovery-02`, whose wider claim new experience still has to earn, receives its own campaign later in the chapter ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-recovery-update|section 7.8.1]]). `latch-bc-01` is flashed onto the warehouse mobile manipulator for a nominal validation campaign. In each trial, the mobile base begins 2 m (illustrative; see the Reader Guide) from the spring-latched cage door, drives forward, and comes to a complete halt. With the base at rest, the manipulator executes a guarded approach at 0.03 m/s, unlatches the mechanism, and opens the door without crossing the 15 N contact tripwire or drawing a human intervention. Suppose the robot completes twenty consecutive runs this way, an empirical success rate of ${\hat{p}} = 1.00$ (100 percent). On a static vision or language benchmark, an unblemished score might suggest that the model is ready for production. On physical hardware, twenty clean runs leave the deployment decision open. They establish only a wide lower confidence bound, one that a policy failing a substantial fraction of its attempts can still satisfy, and they reveal nothing about failure modes too rare to appear in twenty draws.

↩︎ The policy manifest, which records how a checkpoint was produced and the operating domain it declares, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^sec-training-policy-manifest|The Policy Manifest]]**.

The gap between that perfect score and a deployable claim comes from three ways in which physical evaluation departs from the static protocol of machine learning. In that protocol, engineers draw a held-out dataset once from a stationary distribution, score each example independently, and treat the aggregate as a faithful descriptor of model quality. First, a physical policy selects its own test distribution. Every admitted action alters the environment and determines the observations that follow, so no pre-recorded dataset can stand in for the states the policy will visit. This is the second law, endogenous data (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/V4 Teaching the Machine#^pri-vol4-endogenous-drift|2]]), acting on evaluation. The same feedback that pushes a cloned policy off its demonstrations also decides which states a test campaign samples. Second, every trial consumes test-cell time, battery charge, and mechanical life, which keeps sample counts small and each sample expensive. Third, a physical trial delivers kinetic energy across the causal boundary, and that energy cannot be recalled. When an interlock, a contact tripwire, or a safety operator aborts an errant motion before damage occurs, the trial ends early and leaves a censored observation that is neither an unequivocal success nor an unmitigated crash. Aerospace, automotive, and industrial automation engineers combine physical tests with model-based arguments about controller stability and protection. The learned proposer in this campaign arrives without such a certificate, so its task reliability must be bounded by finite empirical sampling under the declared conditions.

Closed-loop evaluation therefore asks what a finite campaign of physical trials can establish about a learned proposer, and what it must leave for the rest of the machine to handle. Four obligations answer that question, and they order this chapter. A success rate is meaningful only when bound to a declared population of hardware states, environmental conditions, and trial procedures, with every protective intervention kept out of the policy’s credit ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.03 What a Physical Success Rate Measures#^sec-evaluation-what-measured|section 7.2]]). The evidence must come from a regime that can expose the failure in question, which for most physical failures means routing proposals through the real permission path rather than scoring them against logs ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.04 Closed-Loop Dynamics#^sec-evaluation-closed-loop-dynamics|section 7.3]] and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.05 Evidence Regimes#^sec-evaluation-evidence-regimes|section 7.4]]). A finite record becomes a defensible bound only under a protocol fixed before the first run, and each additional “nine” of demonstrated reliability costs roughly ten times the trials ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.06 Fixing the Trial Protocol#^sec-evaluation-trial-protocol|section 7.5]] through [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-trial-limits|section 7.7]]). Finally, the evaluation record, the link in the record chain after the policy manifest, must state which conditions were tested and which were not. It must also turn every untested condition, together with every hazard too rare for any campaign to sample, into an obligation for the runtime permission path ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|section 7.8]] and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.10 Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall#^sec-evaluation-limits|section 7.9]]).

The first obligation begins with the twenty runs themselves. Before that perfect fraction can mean anything, the campaign must fix its outcome rules, define the operational population it sampled, and separate what the policy achieved from what supervision supplied. A defensible evaluation record never credits the learned proposer with successes that hardware interlocks or supervisory overrides actually purchased.
