# V4-07.16 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Design of Experiments**: Fisher’s agricultural field trials introduced randomization and blocking so that variation in the soil could not masquerade as a treatment effect ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-fisher1935design|Fisher 1935]]). A stratified test matrix blocks on payload, approach speed, and lighting for the same reason, and randomizing the run order keeps winding heat and spring relaxation from entering the comparison as if they were properties of the policy ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-policy-comparison|section 7.7.4]]).

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Mean Masking of Steering Spikes**: Averaging squared steering error over thousands of straight-aisle frames also hides a brief, large prediction spike, which barely moves the mean. Whether such a spike matters depends on whether its integrated motion spends the clearance before the permission path responds, a question only a plant and timing model can answer.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Unicycle Model**: The simplest planar vehicle model, in which pose $({x_{\text{base}}}, {y_{\text{base}}}, {\theta_{\text{head}}})$ evolves as ${\dot{x}} = {v}\cos{\theta_{\text{head}}}$, ${\dot{y}} = {v}\sin{\theta_{\text{head}}}$, ${\dot{\theta}_{\text{head}}} = {\omega_z}$. This formulation captures the essential nonholonomic constraint of the aisle: because a wheeled base cannot translate instantaneously sideways, any heading discrepancy directly translates forward velocity toward the rack.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Bus Jitter in Shadow Mode**: Inference that is steady on a bench stretches on the machine when direct memory access (DMA) transfers, shared memory, and fieldbus arbitration compete for the same paths. Shadow operation logs that jitter on the real buses, under real thermal load, without letting the late proposals move the chassis.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Right Censoring**: In clinical survival analysis, a patient who departs a study before the terminal endpoint has an unknown time of death rather than an established long life. Kaplan and Meier ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-kaplan1958nonparametric|1958]]) formalized an estimator that tracks such subjects within the population at risk only while they remain actively observed. An intercepted robotic trial is right-censored under identical logic: the intervention proves that the policy required supervisor takeover, not that the robot would have stabilized itself had the supervisor remained passive.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Exact Binomial Bound**: Clopper and Pearson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-clopper1934use|1934]]) inverted the binomial tail directly instead of approximating it with a normal distribution. Here “exact” denotes that algebraic inversion rather than exact coverage probability; because binomial counts are discrete, the true coverage equals or exceeds the nominal confidence level for all values of ${p}$. Under the declared binomial sampling model, the procedure places the lower bound at or below the true reliability in at least the nominal fraction of repeated campaigns; an individual campaign can still overstate it.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Rule of Three**: The heuristic originates in biostatistics, where Hanley and Lippman-Hand ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-hanley1983nothing|1983]]) evaluated the risk to future patients following a clinical trial with zero adverse events across ${n}$ participants. An embodied evaluation campaign asks an identical question of the next robot trial, and the same $3/{n}$ ratio governs the bound.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Wilson Interval**: Wilson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-wilson1927probable|1927]]) inverted the score test around the parameter ${p}$ rather than substituting the empirical estimator ${\hat{p}}$, allowing the interval to maintain a nonzero width even when ${\hat{p}} = 1.0$. This formulation resolves the boundary collapse of the Wald normal approximation. However, because its coverage guarantees hold on average across the parameter space rather than strictly at every point ${p}$, physical qualification protocols default to the exact Clopper-Pearson bound.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Sequential Testing in Wartime**: The test grew out of work at Columbia University’s Statistical Research Group during the Second World War, as a way to accept or reject production lots after inspecting fewer items, and it carried a restricted classification until Wald ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-wald1945sequential|1945]]) set it out in the open literature. The robot campaign faces the same economics, because every trial consumes hardware, and stopping as soon as the evidence is decisive saves trials at the declared error rates.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Ville’s Inequality**: Ville’s thesis was a critique of von Mises’s frequency definition of a random sequence, the “collective” of its title, and it brought martingales into probability through a gambling argument ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-ville1939etude|Ville 1939]]). The same argument now underwrites anytime-valid tests, whose error bound holds however often the evaluator reads the running score, the freedom a campaign needs when it is watched run by run.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Lady Tasting Tea**: Fisher’s best-known illustration of the test concerns a lady who claimed she could tell whether the milk or the tea had been poured into the cup first ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-fisher1935design|Fisher 1935]]). With eight cups, four of each kind, there are 70 ways to choose which four had milk first, so a perfect score by guessing has probability 1/70, a figure that only exact counting of the arrangements gives. Twenty runs per arm sit in the same small-sample regime, where a normal approximation misstates the ${p}$-value.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Statistical Power in Physical Trials**: The probability $1 - {\beta}$ that a test detects a difference that is really there, here the chance that a campaign separates two checkpoints whose success rates truly differ by the stated effect.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Safety of the Intended Functionality (SOTIF)**: ISO 21448 covers hazards that arise without an electrical or mechanical fault, originating instead from functional insufficiencies or reasonably foreseeable misuse ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-iso21448|International Organization for Standardization 2022]]). That is precisely the failure class of a learned proposer: its neural network weights can remain perfectly intact while still proposing an unsafe trajectory. Consequently, the evaluation record catalogs unsupported operational conditions rather than faulted components. The record serves as an engineering evidence artifact rather than a format mandated by the standard itself.

^fn13

<sup>14</sup> **Ultra-Reliability Targets**: The figure comes from civil aviation, where a catastrophic failure condition must be “extremely improbable,” on the order of $10^{-9}$ per flight hour ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-faa1988ac251309|Federal Aviation Administration 1988]]). IEC 61508 sets a comparable band for its highest safety integrity level, SIL 4, of $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-8}$ dangerous failures per hour in continuous operation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-iec61508|International Electrotechnical Commission 2010]]). The exposure-wall examples borrow these magnitudes as analytical targets and are not claims of compliance with either document.

^fn14

<sup>15</sup> **MTBF and a Constant Rate**: The constant-rate model used here describes the flat middle of the bathtub curve after burn-in; it makes the reciprocal rate an MTBF and supports the zero-event bound ${T} \ge 3/{\lambda}$. Wear-out mechanisms such as bearing fatigue raise the rate as revolutions accumulate, so a rate bounded this way says nothing about wear-out life, which the ${L_{10}}$ rating below addresses separately.

^fn15

<sup>16</sup> **Extreme Value Theory**: Fisher and Tippett ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-fisher1928limiting|1928]]) found the three limiting forms that the largest member of a large sample can take, Gnedenko ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-gnedenko1943distribution|1943]]) proved that under general conditions no other limits exist, and Gumbel ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-gumbel1958statistics|1958]]) made the theory an engineering tool for floods and material fatigue. Each of those applications fitted many block maxima.

^fn16
