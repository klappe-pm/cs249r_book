# V4-07.11 Evaluation Pitfalls and Metric Masking

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^sec-evaluation-failure-modes

A physical qualification report can present a spotless pass rate and an exact confidence bound while still misrepresenting what the machine can safely do, if the test procedure quietly narrowed the operating domain, tuned weights on the test unit, or discarded inconvenient runs before the bound was computed. Systematic errors undermining policy evaluation rarely stem from deliberate fraud; they arise when an evaluation protocol answers an easier question than the claim asks, creating statistical or procedural mirages that mask physical vulnerability.

### Six Evaluation Pitfalls in Practice

^sec-evaluation-procedural-pathologies

An audit of an evaluation record checks six links between the headline metric and the physical trials that produced it, because breaking any one of them leaves the reported pass rate intact while invalidating the claim.

The first link breaks through environment selection, whenever the test bench is narrower than the claim. The twenty door runs all met the nominal strike plate at the 0.03 m/s guarded approach with the base stopped and the tote rack empty. If the report states their success rate as the machine’s ability to open the site’s cage doors, the claim silently expands to the five unsampled cells of the scenario table and the loaded machine ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.10 Verification Ceilings and the Exposure Wall#^sec-evaluation-limits|section 7.9]]). Environment selection is detected by comparing a condition log, which records the measured strike-plate position, approach speed, payload, and floor condition of every trial, against the boundaries of the claimed domain. A log that covers one cell establishes nothing about the others.

Even when the workspace matches the claim, metric drift can substitute an offline surrogate for physical closed-loop task completion, scoring candidates by action agreement, validation loss, action Mean Squared Error (MSE), or action cosine similarity against recorded expert buffers. Offline scoring is cheap, so the substitution happens gradually, and it inherits the ranking inversions of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.04 Closed-Loop Dynamics#^sec-evaluation-closed-loop-dynamics|section 7.3]]. Metric drift is prevented by keeping the primary evaluation metrics closed-loop physical observables, namely task-completion rate, ACR, MTBI, and ${D_{\text{int}}}$. Protection violations, including unmitigated tripwire crossings, remain a separate recorded outcome and an explicit acceptance condition; they never justify dropping runs.

Keeping the metric physical still fails if repeated tuning against a fixed evaluation setup introduces unit-specific test set contamination. Adjusting the checkpoint or observation filtering after each batch on Unit 01 makes that robot and door part of development, not an independent holdout. The resulting improvement may depend on their particular backlash, calibration, and lighting. The countermeasure is to audit the history of policy versions, tuning decisions, and chassis identities, and to reserve fresh units and conditions for any claim that requires generalization beyond the development setup.

During execution on a clean holdout unit, optional stopping and multi-look inflation can still corrupt the stopping rule: an engineer who watches a sequence of physical runs and halts as soon as the tally crosses a target reports a confidence the procedure does not have. Each look at the accumulating result is another chance for a false positive. Five independent 5 percent looks would have a 22.6 percent chance of at least one false positive. Repeated looks at one accumulating run are dependent, so the inflation for a real stopping rule must be computed for that rule. Optional stopping cannot be detected from summary tables or final pass rates. It is detected only by auditing the pre-registered trial protocol, verifying that sample sizes, stopping conditions, and evaluation milestones were fixed before the first physical run began. The calibrated alternative is a sequential rule declared in advance ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-sequential-sprt|section 7.7.3]]).

After the runs finish, a fifth distortion occurs when the denominator is quietly filtered, breaking the full-denominator condition of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.06 Fixing the Trial Protocol#^sec-evaluation-trial-protocol|section 7.5]]. A team that discards runs ending on timeouts, emergency stops, sensor communication drops, or permission-path overrides as invalid test artifacts reports success over the surviving trials only. Every metric of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.03 What a Physical Success Rate Measures#^sec-evaluation-what-measured|section 7.2]] then moves in the flattering direction: ACR rises, MTBI grows, intervention density falls toward zero, and the reset time spent clearing a jammed gripper disappears from the budget. If the arm opens the door on nine of ten attempts and the tenth needs an operator takeover to stop the gripper wedging against the latch, removing that attempt raises the apparent success rate from 90 percent to 100 percent. The batch then reports an infinite MTBI. Infrastructure aborts, such as dropped wireless packets, stalled kernel threads, or bus brownouts, also stay in the denominator. A base that stalls in the aisle because an operating system thread locked has failed its mission, whichever subsystem caused it, and removing such aborts evaluates an abstraction rather than the deployed machine. When physical constraints or safety limits truncate a run before its deadline ${T_{\text{deadline}}}$ expires, the trial stays in the trial log as not unassisted (${A}_i = 0, {{n_{\text{int}}}}_i \ge 1$), and for the unassisted-failure event it is right-censored rather than omitted. Reading survival on truncated trajectories as operational competence is the censored trial fallacy of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.05 Evidence Regimes#^sec-evaluation-evidence-regimes|section 7.4]]. Denominator filtering is detected by cross-referencing low-level actuator telemetry and permission-path logs against the trial log, confirming that every power-on cycle, autonomy engagement, and physical reset corresponds to a recorded outcome.

**War Story 7.1: The 2017 NHTSA Autopilot crash rate mirage**

**Context**: In January 2017, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) closed Preliminary Evaluation PE 16-007, which it had opened after a fatal Florida crash of a Model S operating in Autopilot mode ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-nhtsa2017pe16007|National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) 2017]]). The closing report compared airbag-deployment crash rates in the subject Tesla vehicles before and after Autosteer was installed and stated that “the Tesla vehicles crash rate dropped by almost 40 percent after Autosteer installation,” from 1.3 to 0.8 crashes per million miles.

**Mechanism**: The rate divided airbag deployments by miles driven before and after installation, and the report’s own footnote noted that the rates “are for all miles travelled before and after Autopilot installation and are not limited to actual Autopilot use.” The denominator therefore never measured exposure to Autosteer itself, and it was incomplete as well. A replication by Quality Control Systems Corporation found that the odometer reading at installation appears to have been reported for fewer than half of the vehicles, and that exact exposure before and after installation could be computed for only 13 percent of them ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-qcs2019nhtsa|Quality Control Systems Corp. 2019]]). The mileage NHTSA left uncounted, because it could be assigned to neither period, was concentrated among the vehicles with the least exposure before installation.

**Impact**: In the one cohort with complete exposure in both periods, the airbag-deployment crash rate rose 59 percent after installation, from 0.76 to 1.21 per million miles, a result the replication qualified with “if these data are to be believed.” Its authors concluded that the overall 40 percent reduction was “an artifact of the Agency’s treatment of mileage information that is actually missing in the underlying dataset.” The replication does not measure Autosteer’s effect either. It shows that the published denominator could not support the reported reduction.

**Response**: The correction came from outside the agency and two years after the closing report, because the per-vehicle mileage records that exposed the denominator were released only after a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the Department of Transportation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-qcs2019nhtsa|Quality Control Systems Corp. 2019]]).

**Systems lesson**: In physical AI evaluation, an unverified denominator produces a statistical mirage. Aggregating open-world mileage without engagement telemetry, operational design domain boundaries, and complete exposure records turns missing data into a safety claim. An evaluation audits the integrity of its denominator with the same rigor it applies to the failure events in its numerator.

At the extreme of denominator filtering, teams sometimes substitute visual demonstrations for statistical measurements altogether. A successful video shows that the machine reached the goal under one sequence of conditions; without a sampling record, it does not establish how often that outcome recurs. Smooth ${SE(3)}$ kinematics (rigid-body motions in three dimensions) can conceal small physical margins. A representative campaign needs predeclared sampling from the target ODD or separate per-stratum analysis, full-denominator accounting, and a sample size tied to its stated claim.

None of these pathologies is visible in the final pass rate. Each is caught only by auditing the record against the protocol that was declared before the first run, which is why the evaluation record keeps the protocol, the condition log, and the complete outcome sequence rather than the summary alone.
