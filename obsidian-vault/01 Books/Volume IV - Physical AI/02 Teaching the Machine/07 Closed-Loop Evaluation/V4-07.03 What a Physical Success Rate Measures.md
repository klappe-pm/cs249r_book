# V4-07.03 What a Physical Success Rate Measures

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation|V4-07 Closed-Loop Evaluation]]

^sec-evaluation-what-measured

A latch-manipulation policy that reliably opens a door during morning bench tests can jam repeatedly when confronted with a strike plate displaced by only three millimeters. Its measured success rate does not belong to the neural network weights in isolation; it characterizes the coupled dynamical system formed by the policy, the mechanical plant, the onboard compute platform, the physical environment, and the trial protocol itself. While a reproducible offline benchmark score indicates how well a model fits a frozen dataset, a physical success rate measures how the embodied closed-loop system behaves across a distribution of physical executions.

Evaluating a policy requires executing a sequence of discrete physical runs. A single trial is fully determined only when every physical parameter capable of altering the state trajectory is either explicitly controlled or systematically sampled. Formally, a single trial condition is the tuple ${c} = ({\mathbf{x}}_0, {e}, {m}, \pi_{\theta}, {\kappa})$, drawn from an operational testing distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$:

- The initial state vector ${\mathbf{x}}_0$ specifies the machine’s starting condition, including its spatial pose (position and orientation), linear and angular velocities, and joint encoder positions.
- The environment vector ${e}$ captures external physical variables, such as the tire-floor friction coefficient (${\mu}$), ambient illuminance, obstacle geometry, and ambient air temperature.
- The machine state ${m}$ captures physical and hardware properties sampled from the fleet manufacturing and wear distribution ${\mathcal{M}}$. This encompasses unit-to-unit mechanical tolerances, battery state of charge, payload mass, tire wear, lost motion and compliance in the arm’s transmissions, and sensor calibration matrices.
- The computational configuration $\pi_{\theta}$ represents the frozen policy weights ${\theta}$, numerical quantization formats (such as FP16 or INT8), real-time thread scheduling deadlines, and software driver versions.
- The termination protocol ${\kappa} = ({T_{\text{deadline}}}, {\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}, {\phi})$ defines the execution deadline ${T_{\text{deadline}}}$, the spatial goal region ${\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}$, and the binary evaluation predicate ${\phi}$ that maps the realized trajectory to a terminal outcome ${Y} \in \{0, 1\}$.

In static machine learning benchmarks, inputs $({x}_i, {y}_i)$ are sampled independently from a static data distribution ${p_{\text{data}}}({x}, {y})$. In Physical AI, the continuous trajectory $\text{Traj}({c}) = ({\mathbf{x}}_0, {\mathbf{u}}_0, {\mathbf{x}}_1, {\mathbf{u}}_1, \dots, {\mathbf{x}}_{T})$ is generated sequentially by the closed-loop physical state transition kernel:

$$
{\mathbf{x}}_{t+1} \sim {\mathcal{T}}({\mathbf{x}}_t, {\mathbf{u}}_t; {m}, {e}), \quad {\mathbf{a}}_{t} = \pi_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_t), \quad {\mathbf{u}}_t = \text{Gate}({\mathbf{a}}_{t}, {\mathbf{x}}_t)
$$

 Here $\text{Gate}$ stands for the permission path, which admits, clips, or replaces each proposal ${\mathbf{a}}_{t}$ before it becomes the applied command ${\mathbf{u}}_t$. Its permission and controller parameters remain fixed and are archived with the evaluated configuration; changing them changes the evaluated closed loop. The true success probability under this evaluation protocol is the expected value of the binary task outcome averaged over the joint distribution of trial conditions:

$$
{p}({\mathcal{D}}) = \mathbb{E}_{{c} \sim {\mathcal{D}}}[{\phi}(\text{Traj}({c}))] \tag{1}
$$

 where $\text{Traj}({c})$ is the physical trajectory generated under condition ${c}$. Altering floor friction, ambient temperature, or battery voltage modifies the physical transition kernel ${\mathcal{T}}$ even when the neural network weights ${\theta}$ remain untouched. The operational testing distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$ is therefore an inseparable component of the quantity being estimated.

^eq-eval-estimand

↪ The formal integral over the closed-loop trajectory distribution, including the plant transition kernel and the permission map, is developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Machine Learning Background#^sec-appendix-ml-trajectory-distribution|Closed-loop trajectory distributions and evaluation estimands]]**.

In test engineering, the abstract distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$ is realized through a **stratified test matrix**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.16 Footnotes#^fn1|¹]] across controllable parameters, systematically varying payload mass, approach speed, illumination, and initial spatial standoff, while logging uncontrollable environmental parameters such as ambient temperature and floor friction. Unstratified random sampling across the parameter space oversamples benign nominal states and leaves critical boundary regions unvisited. Each stratum of the matrix (one payload, one approach speed, one plate position) is then its own population for the statistical bounds that follow, with its own ${\mathcal{D}}$ and its own success probability. A claim about the whole matrix needs explicit weights across strata, which the evaluation record of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|section 7.8]] declares before the first run.

**Definition 7.1: Closed-loop evaluation estimand**

***Closed-loop evaluation estimand*** is the expected physical task outcome ${p}({\mathcal{D}}) = \mathbb{E}_{{c} \sim {\mathcal{D}}}[{\phi}(\text{Traj}({c}))]$ defined over the joint distribution of initial states, mechanical plant wear, environmental physics, frozen policy weights, and evaluation predicates:

$$
{c} = ({\mathbf{x}}_0, {e}, {m}, \pi_{\theta}, {\kappa}) \sim {\mathcal{D}}
$$

 rather than an intrinsic property of the neural policy weights evaluated in isolation.

1.  **Significance**: In static machine learning, held-out accuracy is treated as a property of the model $\pi_{\theta}$. In physical AI, substituting a worn tire, letting battery voltage sag, or shifting ambient illuminance alters the physical state transition function without changing a single weight. The sampling distribution of operational conditions ${\mathcal{D}}$ is an inseparable part of the quantity being estimated.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike open-loop validation metrics (such as validation loss, cosine similarity, or mean squared trajectory error) that score predictions against frozen recorded states, the closed-loop estimand integrates over the endogenous feedback loop. In that loop, past actions physically move the machine and directly change the future sensor observations it receives.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Reporting a single success rate percentage without predeclaring the support, density, and environmental bounds of the test distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$. Any physical policy can be made arbitrarily successful by testing exclusively in benign, unperturbed states.

### Hardware Instance Variance and Environmental Unobservables

^hardware-instance-variance-and-environmental-unobservables

Freezing the neural network weights $\pi_{\theta}$ and the termination protocol ${\kappa}$ fixes only two of the five entries in the trial condition ${c}$; the plant parameters ${m}$ and environmental parameters ${e}$ still vary from machine to machine and from morning to afternoon. On a mobile manipulator, this dispersion comes from two sources: **hardware instance variance** across individual builds and **environmental unobservables** in the operating workspace.

First, robotic production lines do not manufacture identical copies. Nominally identical units diverge in lost motion, thermal behavior, and controller response (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.11 Dataset Degradation and Physical Distribution Shift#^sec-data-fleet-divergence|Rig-to-rig divergence across nominally identical fleets]]**). The tolerances that matter for evaluation also include drivetrain compliance and hysteresis that grow with wear ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.15 Source References#^ref-harmonicdrive2006csf|Harmonic Drive LLC 2006]]), link deflection that shifts the tool center point, and camera-mount offsets between the sensor frame ${\mathcal{F}_{\text{cam}}}$ and the base frame ${\mathcal{F}_B}$. They further include torque constants ${K_t}$ that vary across windings and drive voltage that loses headroom against back-electromotive force (back-EMF) as the battery discharges. Each of these changes the trial condition without changing a weight, so qualification campaigns characterize every unit’s lost motion, joint stiffness, and camera extrinsics on an inspection bench rather than assuming nominal catalog values.

Second, environmental unobservables alter physical interaction dynamics without changing camera pixels in ways that a neural network can easily recognize. Ambient daylight shifting across a warehouse alters illuminance, causing camera auto-exposure routines to lengthen shutter times and inducing motion blur during base turns. Direct solar glare at dawn or dusk saturates complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) sensor arrays, washing out optical contrast around the latch interface. On the floor, dust accumulation and ambient humidity alter the tire-floor friction coefficient (${\mu}$), lengthening braking distances and corrupting wheel odometry. Within the manipulator arm, repeated door openings generate Joule heating (${I}^2 {R}$) in motor windings and warm harmonic drive lubricants, decreasing viscous damping and causing thermal expansion across structural links. Consequently, the tool center point meets the latch with a millimeter-scale spatial offset on a hot afternoon compared to a cold morning.

Trial reset procedures introduce another major source of unintended drift in ${c}$. When human operators reposition the mobile base after a difficult or aborted run, subconscious bias often leads them to favor forgiving, nominal configurations, systematically excising difficult boundary states from the sample. Conversely, an automated reset routine that drives the identical recovery path repeatedly heats actuator windings cyclically, introducing progressive thermal degradation across runs. Worst of all, an operator who catches a stumbling robot mid-fall and discards the run as an invalid trial erases the most informative corner-case failures from the evaluation record.

Statistical evaluation must therefore maintain a strict distinction between sampling variation and shifts in the underlying estimand. When ${N}$ independent trials are drawn from a stationary distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$, the sample proportion ${\hat{p}} = \frac{1}{{N}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} {Y}_i$ fluctuates around the true expectation ${p}({\mathcal{D}})$ due to ordinary binomial noise, which standard statistical estimators bound. However, loading a payload onto the tote rack or moving the door’s strike plate transitions the experiment to an entirely different distribution ${\mathcal{D}'}$ with its own success probability ${p}({\mathcal{D}'})$. Pooling runs across these two conditions without declaring their sampling weights conflates distinct physical configurations into an uninterpretable average. Independent draws from a predeclared mixture support a claim about that mixture; a quota-based stratified matrix instead requires separate inference within each cell and explicit weighting for an aggregate claim. Fixing the condition tuple ${c}$ settles which population a campaign samples, but a single binary outcome ${Y} \in \{0, 1\}$ still hides whether the learned proposer succeeded on its own or was rescued by the permission path.

### Intervention-Based Physical Metrics

^intervention-based-physical-metrics

Even when the trial condition ${c}$ is fixed, a single binary pass rate cannot distinguish a policy that operated the door latch on its own from one that was rescued on every attempt by permission-path clips or remote teleoperators. Consider four divergent operational outcomes during a door-opening task:

- A policy proposes an excessive contact force, but the permission path clips the command, allowing the door to open smoothly while remaining within mechanical limits.
- The permission path detects an impending joint limit and halts the manipulator entirely, preserving hardware safety while leaving the task incomplete.
- A human safety operator intervenes via remote teleoperation to clear a jammed latch pin, after which the robot resumes autonomous execution and completes the run.
- The raw policy executes the entire trajectory unassisted, completing the manipulation task within nominal limits without intervention.

Every protective actor in these four scenarios other than the human operator is the permission path, acting by clipping a proposal, replacing it, or stopping the machine. The first scenario shows a well-functioning integrated machine where permission-path clips compensate for raw proposer imperfections, whereas the second protects the hardware at the expense of task completion. The third achieves the objective only by drawing on costly human labor, leaving only the fourth as genuine proposer autonomy.

Evaluating physical AI systems therefore requires decomposing performance into three independent channels: whole-machine task completion within operational limits, raw proposer autonomy, and supervisory intervention frequency. The task-completion rate, the fraction of trials whose outcome ${Y}_i$ (defined in the first item below) equals 1, measures the first channel. The autonomous completion rate measures the second, and mean time between interventions and intervention density measure the third. Reset latency belongs to no channel; it prices what each outcome costs the test cell. Runtime safety adjustments are an intended feature of the architecture, so a small corrective clip by the permission path should not brand a completed run as a system failure. Nevertheless, tracking proposer autonomy separately reveals how often the learned model attempts to violate physical boundaries. Telemetry pipelines must log adjustments, command rejections, protective stops, and human overrides using predeclared event-counting rules, ensuring that a sustained safety clamp over several seconds is recorded as a single event rather than hundreds of spurious high-frequency faults.

1.  **Autonomous completion rate (ACR)**: Let ${{n_{\text{int}}}}_i \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ be the total count of supervisory interventions during trial ${i}$, whether initiated by human safety operators, the permission path’s clips and stops, or emergency tripwires, and let ${Y}_i \in \{0, 1\}$ denote goal achievement within the execution deadline ${T_{\text{deadline}}}$. The unassisted autonomous success indicator is:

$$
{A}_i = {\mathbb{I}}({Y}_i = 1 \land {{n_{\text{int}}}}_i = 0)
$$

 ACR is the sample proportion of trials completed entirely without supervisory intervention:

$$
\text{ACR} = \frac{1}{{N}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} {A}_i = \frac{{N_{\text{unassisted}}}}{{N}}
$$

 ACR measures strict raw proposer autonomy: a single recorded intervention forces ${A}_i = 0$. In contrast, the whole-machine outcome ${Y}_i$ can remain $1$ when the permission path clips the proposer’s commands and the task still completes within service bounds.

2.  **Mean time between interventions (MTBI)**: For any declared intervention class, MTBI quantifies the mean active operating duration between supervisory events:

$$
{T_{\text{MTBI}}} = \frac{{T_{\text{active}}}}{{N_{\text{int}}}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} {{t_{\text{run}}}}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} {{n_{\text{int}}}}_i}
$$

 where ${{t_{\text{run}}}}_i$ is the active operating duration of trial ${i}$ and ${N_{\text{int}}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} {{n_{\text{int}}}}_i$ is the total count of recorded interventions within that class. MTBI reflects the operational burden imposed by supervisory systems. Frequent automated filter adjustments signal aggressive policy proposals or model distribution shift, whereas frequent manual recoveries directly drive up staffing costs. Neither a favorable MTBI nor an absence of physical damage proves that a policy is ready for unattended deployment.

3.  **Intervention density (${D_{\text{int}}}$)**: In spatial navigation and continuous trajectory tracking, interventions must be normalized against physical travel distance or discrete execution steps:

$$
{D_{\text{int}}} = \frac{{N_{\text{int}}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} {d}_i} \quad [\text{interventions per kilometer}] \quad \text{or} \quad {D_{\text{int}}} = 1{,}000 \times \frac{{N_{\text{int}}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} {N_{\text{steps}, i}}} \quad [\text{interventions per } 1{,}000 \text{ steps}]
$$

 where ${d}_i$ is the physical path distance traversed during trial ${i}$, and ${N_{\text{steps}, i}}$ is the number of control cycles executed. Normalizing by exposure makes intervention rates comparable across routes or operating conditions. Locating concentrated degradation also requires retaining each event’s location and condition and comparing these rates across strata, such as narrow doorways or tight mechanical latches.

4.  **Reset latency (${T_{\text{reset}}}$)**: In physical qualification campaigns, the primary bottleneck in testing throughput is governed by the time required to restore the robot and workspace after a trial completes or aborts:

$$
{T_{\text{reset}}} = {t_{\text{physical\_restore}}} + {t_{\text{calibration}}} + {t_{\text{telemetry\_flush}}}
$$

 The standard door-opening run requires 25 s of active execution. While its nominal reset cycle is brief ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.08 Trial Budgets, Stopping Rules, and Comparisons#^sec-evaluation-trial-costs|section 7.7.2]]), a run that jams the latch pin requires an operator intervention of approximately 60 s (illustrative) to clear the jammed pin, recalibrate joint encoders, and return the mobile base to its initial standoff pose. For jammed runs, this manual reset consumes 70.6 percent of total test-cell occupancy. High reset latencies create organizational pressure to cut corners, for instance by skipping motor thermal cooldowns or manually nudging the robot into easier initial poses, and each shortcut breaks the statistical stationarity of the campaign.

Engineers must not conflate MTBI with mean time between failures (MTBF). While an MTBF metric tracks functional breakdowns or mechanical failures, MTBI, ACR, and ${D_{\text{int}}}$ capture supervisory burden and proposer autonomy within an operating envelope. A robot fleet can maintain an unblemished record of zero hardware damage simply because vigilant human safety drivers or a conservative permission path intervene whenever an unsafe state nears. Recording a run that was truncated or guided by external supervision as an unassisted operational success masks policy shortcomings behind the protective capability of the safety system. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.05 Evidence Regimes#^sec-evaluation-evidence-regimes|Section 7.4]] shows why such runs are censored observations and names this error the censored trial fallacy.

Evaluation protocols keep these metrics separate so that engineers can observe the trade-offs between safety filtering, autonomous capability, and operational throughput. These outcome channels fix which quantities must be recorded across experimental trials. The next challenge is to design a closed-loop evaluation harness that exposes these behaviors systematically under dynamic feedback.
