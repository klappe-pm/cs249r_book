# V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-policy-coverage

Every logged latch record carries an ingestion cost, an attributed action label, and a hardware timestamp, yet those fields say nothing about which physical situations the archive covers. In an illustrative contact task where a gripper aligns with a workpiece edge across contact angles ${\theta_{\text{contact}}} \in [-15.0^\circ, +15.0^\circ]$ and normal forces up to $20.0\text{ N}$, binning at $1.0^\circ$ and $1.0\text{ N}$ forms an operational grid of 600 discrete cells. Suppose two collection policies each record $1{,}000{,}000$ transitions at $100\text{ Hz}$ with identical sensors. A cautious teleoperator keeps contact within $\pm 2.0^\circ$ of perpendicular under $4.0\text{ to }6.0\text{ N}$ of normal force, populating 8 cells and leaving 98.7 percent of the grid empty. An exploratory policy approaches across the full angular range, slips, and loads the contact between $1.0\text{ and }18.0\text{ N}$, filling 510 cells and leaving 15.0 percent empty. Although both datasets have identical record counts, storage sizes, and hardware timestamps, they answer different operational questions. Record count therefore fails as a measure twice over: correlated control steps hold few independent samples ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.05 Physical Telemetry Sources and Collection Paths#^eq-data-effective-sample-size|equation 2]]), and even independent samples support only the cells where they land.

That disparity arises because a physical dataset comes from running a closed-loop policy on hardware and logging the resulting transitions, violating the independent-and-identically-distributed assumption of standard empirical risk minimization ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.03 Mechanics of Endogenous Covariate Shift#^sec-data-covariate-shift|section 5.2]]). Instead, it traces an **empirical state-action occupancy distribution** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-spencer2021feedback|Spencer et al. 2021]]) ${p^{\pi_{\text{col}}}}({s}, {\mathbf{a}})$ generated by a specific collecting policy $\pi_{\text{col}}$ under its own control biases, interface delays, and risk tolerance. Formally, imitation learning minimizes prediction error weighted solely by this empirical occupancy:

$$
{\mathcal{L}_{\text{BC}}}({\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{({s}, {\mathbf{a}}) \sim {p^{\pi_{\text{col}}}}} \left[ \ell({\pi_\theta}({s}), {\mathbf{a}}) \right].
$$

 The learned policy ${\pi_\theta}$ therefore receives supervisory gradient updates only over the empirical support hull $\text{supp}({p^{\pi_{\text{col}}}}) = \{ {s} \in {\mathcal{S}} \mid {p^{\pi_{\text{col}}}}({s}) > 0 \}$. Outside this narrow support hull, the network’s outputs are unconstrained by training demonstrations.

Supervisory labels exist only where the collecting policy visited and commanded an actuator action. In unvisited states, the dataset contains neither an optimal control response nor proof that a safe trajectory exists. Supervised loss functions cannot distinguish an inherently unstable physical configuration from a benign unvisited state or an outright collision hazard. Whether an autonomous policy generalizes in such regions, fails silently, or triggers a safety stop cannot be deduced from the archive alone; resolving that uncertainty requires targeted recovery demonstrations, expert relabeling, or explicit run-time gating. Consider a policy trained exclusively on the cautious teleoperation data. If unexpected surface friction perturbs the gripper to contact angle ${\theta_{\text{contact}}} = +4.5^\circ$, outside the eight demonstrated cells, the policy network evaluates an input unconstrained by demonstrations. One control step later, its ungrounded action drives the contact to $+6.0^\circ$ at $11.0\text{ N}$, another state lacking corrective labels in the training corpus. Compounding errors push the manipulator progressively farther from the nominal corridor until the gripper jams against the fixture. This cascade illustrates endogenous covariate shift (definition [5.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/05_data/05_data.html#dfn-data-endogenous-covariate-shift)) triggered by a coverage gap.

### Choosing a Tractable Coverage Table

^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability-geometry

The contact-angle example uses a compact grid because it isolates the parameters relevant to a single mating feature. Extending that dense discretization across all robot state variables triggers the **curse of dimensionality in continuous physical interaction**.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.17 Footnotes#^fn9|⁹]] Consider a mobile manipulator with a 7-degree-of-freedom (7-DoF) arm, a 2-DoF differential-drive base, a 6-axis force-torque sensor at the wrist, and a 6-DoF target workpiece pose. The continuous state space has dimension:

$$
{d_s} = 7\text{ (joint angles)} + 7\text{ (joint velocities)} + 2\text{ (wheel speeds)} + 6\text{ (wrench components)} + 6\text{ (workpiece pose)} = 28.
$$

If an engineering team partitions each dimension into a crude ${n_{\text{bin}}} = 10$ bins ($36^\circ$ per angle bin and $10\text{ N}$ per wrench bin, far too coarse to guide contact tasks whose tolerances are a few millimeters), the discretized state space contains ${n_{\text{bin}}}^{{d_s}} = 10^{28}$ cells. A fleet of 1,000 robots logging at $1\text{ kHz}$ since the universe began, about 13.8 billion years ago, would hold 4.35 × 10²³ records, enough to visit at most 0.004 percent of those cells even if every record landed in a different one. At a resolution fine enough to guide contact (${n_{\text{bin}}} = 100$ bins per axis), the count grows to $10^{56}$ cells. Brute-force observation cannot cover a continuous physical state space; a coverage table must describe the task with a few parameters instead.

![[90 Assets/Volume IV/05 Data/Margin State Grid Coverage Ladder 65865bcc65.svg]]

*A fleet logging since the universe began could visit at most 0.004 percent of even the coarse grid.*

Evaluating dataset completeness against the empirical extremes of collected values is fundamentally circular. A collection logger that records forces exclusively between $4.0\text{ N}$ and $6.0\text{ N}$ trivially reports full coverage of that observed interval. Coverage must instead be evaluated against the operational conditions that deployment demands, formulated before any demonstration data is collected.

**Definition 5.3: Operational design domain**

***Operational design domain (ODD)*** is the declared set of conditions (object poses, surface conditions, payloads, speeds, and disturbances) under which a machine is intended to operate, stated independently of any data collected within it.

1.  **Significance**: The ODD serves as the denominator for every coverage claim. For example, the latch archive, as the join later in this section shows, populates only a single cell of the scenario table its door mechanism requires, a fraction that no volume of redundant nominal episodes can increase. Subsequent verification, evaluation, and release records can assert operational authority only over explicitly supported regions of the ODD.
2.  **Distinction**: Deployment requirements define the ODD, whereas the collecting policy defines empirical occupancy. Coverage measured against observed telemetry extremes merely restates empirical occupancy in ODD terminology, producing an illusion of completeness. A **scenario table** partitions the continuous ODD into structured discrete cells so that deployment requirements and empirical occupancy can be joined.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Defining the ODD post hoc based on conditions where the machine has already operated successfully rather than where it is required to function. Letting the dataset define its own requirements masks known unvisited failure modes.

In high-dimensional continuous systems, a scenario table avoids voxelizing raw camera pixel streams or continuous joint spaces, which would trigger an intractable combinatorial explosion. Instead, it parameterizes low-dimensional task invariants and environmental boundaries, such as workpiece pose offsets ($\pm 5\text{ mm}$), surface friction coefficients, approach speeds, lighting variations ($100\text{--}1000\text{ lux}$), and payload masses ($0\text{--}2\text{ kg}$). These parameters ground verification in concrete physical operating constraints. While this relational join identifies where the dataset holds empirical experience, populating a cell does not demonstrate that a model trained on those samples executes successfully. A populated cell provides supervisory targets, but verifying autonomous competence still requires closed-loop policy evaluation.

The missing recovery actions identified in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^fig-05-latch-corridor|figure 1]] demonstrate why even a tractable scenario table must list off-nominal perturbations as cells of their own. Repeatedly executing a flawless motion samples progress along the nominal path, whereas corrective demonstrations supply actions that return the machine after an unexpected displacement. A sound scenario table distinguishes recovery experiences from nominal runs rather than treating repeated nominal cycles as coverage of the adjacent state space.

Unsupported operational regimes are exposed by a **support envelope relational join**, which cross-references empirical occupancy ${p^{\pi_{\text{col}}}}({s},{\mathbf{a}})$ (where the machine went) against the scenario table ${\mathcal{S}_{\text{req}}}$ (where the machine must be able to go). The table declares a count unit (transitions, independent episodes, or retained demonstrations), an eligibility and retention rule, and a minimum in that same unit. For each cell, the join tallies ${N_{\text{cell}}}$ under this policy and partitions the table into cells meeting the minimum (${N_{\text{cell}}} \ge {N_{\min}}$), low-count cells, and unvisited blind spots (${N_{\text{cell}}} = 0$). A populated cell has a nonzero count; it need not meet the minimum. The contact-angle example counts transitions, while the latch campaign’s table counts retained demonstrations. These bins define collection categories. The threshold ${N_{\min}}$ represents a declared data requirement rather than a test of policy competence; neither an occupied cell nor a large sample count confers autonomous deployment authority.

A held-out validation split drawn from the same collecting policy can evaluate action prediction accuracy over that occupancy while remaining completely blind to gaps in the required operational domain. The comparison in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^tbl-data-collector-coverage|table 4]] separates collection coverage metrics from downstream policy outcomes. Its loss row is the root-mean-square error, in radians, of the predicted action on held-out records, and the cautious archive posts the lower value precisely because a narrow, repetitive archive is easier to fit. Low validation loss does not demonstrate recovery capability in unvisited conditions, just as wide exploratory coverage does not prove that collected corrective labels are dynamically stabilizing. Closed-loop evaluation must test the candidate policy directly against the declared task population.

The cage-door latch campaign illustrates this relational join for the running machine. Its six-cell example records occupancy, without supplying per-cell counts or a minimum-count requirement. Every retained episode approaches the strike plate at the guarded speed of 0.03 m/s with the plate aligned within ±0.5 mm of nominal, and every successful demonstration stays below the illustrative 15 N detection threshold. This observation does not itself qualify the collection response. Evaluated against an uncritical table containing only the nominal plate position at the guarded speed, the archive appears exhaustive. Against the scenario table the deployment actually demands, three plate positions (nominal and ±3 mm) crossed with two approach speeds (0.03 m/s and 0.10 m/s), the identical dataset populates only one cell of six. Every off-nominal plate displacement and every fast approach at 0.10 m/s contains zero demonstrations. The raw logged telemetry does not change between these two assessments; the difference lies entirely in the standard of audit applied. The five unvisited cells therefore travel with the dataset into training as documented operational absences, establishing that the current demonstrations provide no empirical foundation for competence claims in those regimes. Targeted collection can acquire the missing recovery trajectories, but downstream training and closed-loop testing must verify what the resulting policy achieves. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.08 Collection Policy Coverage#^fig-05-collector-coverage-table|Figure 6]] sets the two audits side by side and adds a third, the stipulated recovery collection `latch-recovery-02` of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|section 5.8]], which fills one more cell and still leaves four known absences.

**Checkpoint 5.2: Empirical occupancy and scenario table joins**

Before evaluating policy coverage across an operational design domain, audit dataset occupancy and scenario-table counts against these criteria:

**Empirical support vs. validation loss**: Explain why an imitation policy can achieve near-zero validation loss on held-out test data while leaving 98 percent of the ODD’s required cells unsupported.

**Unsupported states**: What can an unvisited scenario cell establish about policy behavior, and what additional evidence or refusal decision is needed?

**Scenario table joins**: Distinguish cells meeting the declared minimum, low-count cells, and unvisited cells when joining telemetry against an operational scenario table.

**Table growth**: If the latch scenario table adds a third approach speed between the two it holds, how many cells does it contain, and how many of them are known absences for the original archive?

Logging additional nominal demonstrations along an unperturbed trajectory cannot eliminate coverage blind spots. Exposing the policy to recovery dynamics requires forcing the system toward its operational boundaries, either through deliberate demonstrator noise injection or through expert supervisory interventions ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-kelly2019hgdagger|Kelly et al. 2019]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-laskey2017dart|Laskey et al. 2017]]). Before collecting either, a team must know why each empty cell is empty, and recorded interventions add a harder question, because they mix the learner’s commands and the expert’s in one trace ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|section 5.8]]).
