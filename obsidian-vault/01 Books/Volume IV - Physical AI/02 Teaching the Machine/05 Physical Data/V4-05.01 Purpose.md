*Why can a large archive of successful demonstrations leave a robot unable to recover from its first mistake?*

A successful demonstration shows how one machine completed a task under one set of conditions. It shows almost nothing about what to do after a slip, a shifted fixture, or a worn gripper, yet a learned policy’s first error carries it into exactly those states, where the archive holds no instruction. Repeating the demonstration enlarges the archive without filling that gap. The recorded motion also carries the operator’s delay, the machine’s limits, and every correction the permission path applied, so a training process that cannot separate these influences learns a safeguard’s intervention or a mechanical limitation as if it were skill. Gathering the missing experience costs operator time and component life, and the collection itself must stay within the machine’s physical limits. Physical data must therefore be engineered, from the choice of experiences to the meaning, timing, and physical origin of every label and the conditions the archive leaves uncovered. Because every collected action crosses the causal boundary, its irreversible costs decide what a machine can learn and where its evidence stops.

**Learning Objectives**

- Explain how endogenous covariate shift yields the conditional quadratic imitation-cost bound, and distinguish dataset occupancy from evaluated closed-loop competence
- Select the action tap a dataset imitates and align multi-rate sensor and action streams to hardware exposure-midpoint timestamps within the task’s spatial tolerance
- Estimate usable demonstration yield from collection time, operator rest, and physical reset overhead, and budget the mechanical component wear a campaign consumes
- Identify collection gaps by joining empirical state-action occupancy against a required scenario table
- Separate failed autonomous actions from admissible human recovery across the divergence, takeover, and stabilization timestamps of an intervention episode
- Evaluate whether an embodiment’s mechanical, sensing, and timing assumptions transfer to another machine without falling into the normalization fallacy
- Detect silent calibration and plant drift in a demonstration archive using kinematic reprojection and repeated excitation trajectories
- Design an immutable provenance record linking each observation to its four-stream action taps (${a_{\text{req}}}, {a_{\text{cmd}}}, {a_{\text{enf}}}, {a_{\text{meas}}}$) and to the collecting plant
