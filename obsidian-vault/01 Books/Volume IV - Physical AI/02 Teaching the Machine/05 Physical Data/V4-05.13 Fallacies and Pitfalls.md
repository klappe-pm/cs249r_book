# V4-05.13 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-fallacies-pitfalls

In pure software systems, data collection can often treat storage growth as an unvarnished positive, since more web pages, text tokens, or labeled images generally broaden coverage. In physical artificial intelligence, however, datasets are tightly coupled to the mechanical dynamics, calibration state, and physical limits of the collection hardware. A dataset that passes every structural schema check, with zero dropped packets and consistent tensor dimensions, can still leave a deployed machine entirely blind to critical operational states. The operational distribution of physical data reflects three intertwined factors: the collecting policy (whether driven by human teleoperation or automated planning), the physical capabilities and wear of the collection rig, and the curation filters applied before admission to the archive. Neglecting these physical constraints leads to predictable engineering fallacies and costly operational pitfalls.

**Fallacy**: *Massive sample volume compensates for lack of operational state-space coverage.*

Suppose a surface-tracking policy must operate reliably across 500 distinct combinations of normal force, surface friction, and contact angle, but the archive covers only 45. Logging another collection run that adds $72{,}000$ control steps by repeating nominal trajectories through those same 45 states inflates the database, but it leaves physical coverage unchanged. High-frequency sampling in familiar regions merely records the same dynamic trajectory more densely, adding zero evidence about the unvisited friction regimes or steep contact angles. Ingestion pipelines that report growing data volume and zero dropped packets can create a false sense of security while leaving downstream policies brittle. A collection campaign earns its cost by addressing a declared coverage, label-quality, or evidence deficit. A populated cell may still need more or better demonstrations, while nominal repetition alone supplies no evidence about unvisited regimes.

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Gate dataset admission on a support envelope relational join against an explicit scenario table, rather than step counts or raw byte volume. Require that new data collection campaigns specify which coverage, label-quality, or evidence deficit they target, and track scenario coverage (${N_{\text{supported}}} / {N_{\text{req}}}$) as a primary engineering KPI alongside validation loss.

**Pitfall**: *Assuming physical demonstration labels survive mechanical or contact hardware maintenance.*

Archived demonstrations do not retain physical validity when the underlying hardware changes, even if array shapes and data schemas remain identical. Consider what happens when a technician replaces a parallel gripper’s compliant fingertips with stiffer pads and re-torques an adjacent camera bracket. The historical dataset still passes every automated pipeline check: sensor channels, array dimensions, and datatypes match expected specifications. If the camera bracket shifts, a known transformation matrix allows mathematical reprojection of visual coordinates. Contact mechanics, however, cannot be reprojected geometrically. The stiffer pads alter normal compliance and contact friction, causing grasp slip at substantially lower shear thresholds than the compliant material tolerated. Telemetry recorded before the swap captures the physical behavior of an obsolete body; training a closed-loop force controller on those historical tactile profiles will cause grasping failures on the modified hardware. Data provenance must track the full mechanical and calibration state of the collection rig so that any hardware alteration immediately triggers targeted review and recollected baselines.

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Bind every trajectory file to an immutable hardware state hash that captures camera extrinsics, motor serial numbers, and contact surface material specifications. When maintenance events alter contact stiffness or camera mountings, invalidate historical tactile and force labels for that cell until an automated revalidation protocol verifies dynamic equivalence.

**Pitfall**: *Filtering out aborted safety interventions to sanitize the demonstration dataset.*

Purging aborted demonstrations to produce a flawless training set introduces severe survivorship bias into physical policies. Consider an automated peg-insertion pipeline spanning 1,000 recorded attempts. Of these, 180 runs triggered a safety cutoff when contact forces reached or exceeded a $15.0\text{ N}$ trip threshold, leaving 820 nominal runs that succeeded with an 82 percent yield. A curation script discards the 180 aborted and recovery episodes, preserving only the smooth runs where forces stayed comfortably below $8.0\text{ N}$. When deployed in production, minor surface misalignment inevitably pushes an insertion force to $9.5\text{ N}$. Although this force remains below the $15.0\text{ N}$ trip threshold, the policy encounters a state outside its training support. Because the sanitization script erased every observation between nominal tracking and failure, the archive supplies no supervision for these near-boundary dynamics, so recovery behavior remains unsupported. Pruning near-boundary and intervention data strips away the exact state-action pairs the policy needs to regain stability after a disturbance.

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Implement three-way intervention curation at data ingestion. Quarantine learner commands between divergence (${t_{\text{div}}}$) and takeover (${t_{\text{takeover}}}$) for safety auditing only. Preserve the visited boundary states for expert relabeling via corrective aggregation (DAgger). Admit the subsequent human recovery as an expert sequence only after the operator’s measured stabilization (${t_{\text{stabilize}}}$), masking the blend and reorientation that precede it.

**Pitfall**: *Merging demonstrations collected across kinematically asymmetric leader interfaces.*

Merging trajectories across disparate teleoperation input interfaces can quietly bake kinematic dead zones into the dataset. Suppose two operators collect snap-fit assembly demonstrations using different leader devices to puppet identical follower arms. The first leader mechanism features detented wrist mechanics that restrict roll to two discrete positions (${\theta_{\text{roll}}} \in \{0.0^\circ, 90.0^\circ\}$). The second leader uses a continuous gimbal that mechanically limits roll travel to $[-15.0^\circ, +15.0^\circ]$. Merging the two collections yields 4,000 episodes and increases overall sample volume, but neither interface can express orientations between $20.0^\circ$ and $70.0^\circ$. Even though the follower arm can navigate that full range, the combined archive contains zero training targets within this $50.0^\circ$ gap. Pooling data from different input devices cannot fill gaps that neither device can mechanically traverse. Downstream imitation policies trained on the combined dataset will fail whenever initial conditions or part tolerances demand intermediate orientations.

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Apply the cross-embodiment compatibility contract to the leader interface itself, checking before recording that the leader and follower share the required reachable poses, joint motion ranges, and ability to move in the required directions. Enforce continuous orientation coverage checks across task-space degrees of freedom during data QA to detect mechanical dead-zones.

**Fallacy**: *Physical data collection scales linearly with labor hours just like internet scraping scales with compute.*

Treating physical data collection as an easily parallelized, linear process like web scraping ignores the mechanical entropy and human fatigue inherent to physical experimentation. Teams often extrapolate campaign timelines from early pilot runs, for example by projecting that an initial pace of 50 successful episodes per operator-hour will scale linearly across weeks of shifts. In reality, continuous operation degrades both hardware and teleoperator consistency. Repetitive contact impacts cause backlash and tooth wear in strain-wave gearboxes, thermal expansion and sensor drift cause uncalibrated joint encoders to drift away from physical ground truth, and human fatigue introduces latency spikes and jerky trajectories. As wear accumulates, the proportion of episodes passing quality filtering steadily drops, even while scheduled labor hours remain constant. Pilot runs conducted on pristine hardware systematically overstate long-term collection yield. Campaign planning must budget for routine recalibration, mechanical maintenance, ergonomic rests, and discarded runs alongside raw operator hours.

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Budget collection campaigns in net validated demonstration yield (${Y_{\text{usable}}} = \lfloor {T_{\text{motion}}} / {t_{\text{cyc}}} \rfloor \cdot {R}$) and component fatigue cycles (${L_{10}}$ ratings for strain-wave gears and internal flex harnesses), rather than gross scheduled operator hours. Estimate the overhead multiplier from the campaign’s calibration, reset, retention, and rest assumptions; validate it against sustained collection yield.
