# V4-05.17 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **ALVINN (Autonomous Land Vehicle in a Neural Network)**: Pomerleau’s road-following network learned to steer from a human driver who kept the van centered, so its training images held almost no off-center views ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-pomerleau1989alvinn|Pomerleau 1989]]). His remedy shifted each camera image sideways in software and corrected the steering label to match, synthesizing recovery examples about 20 years before Dataset Aggregation (DAgger, [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|section 5.8]]) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-pomerleau1991efficient|Pomerleau 1991]]). A warp could fill that gap because the missing state was only a viewpoint of a flat road, whereas a gripper displaced toward the fixture also lacks contact forces that no image transform can manufacture, so its recoveries must be demonstrated or collected.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Bilateral Telemanipulation**: Raymond Goertz’s 1949 master-slave manipulator at Argonne National Laboratory moved tongs behind radiation shielding in exact correspondence with a master handle, and its mechanical linkage returned the forces at the tongs to the operator’s hand ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-goertz1949master|Goertz 1949]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-hokayem2006bilateral|Hokayem and Spong 2006]]). Remote manipulation therefore began with contact force inside the loop. A demonstration recorded through an interface that drops that force channel cannot contain the contact corrections the operator would have made by feel.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Effective Sample Size**: The factor $(1-{\rho})/(1+{\rho})$ is the effective-sample-size correction for the mean of a first-order autoregressive series, the correction climatologists apply to serially correlated weather records before testing a trend ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-thiebaux1984interpretation|Thiébaux and Zwiers 1984]]). At the correlation used here it turns an hour of permission-tick records into about 1,800 independent ones, so a step count overstates the independent information available for estimating this statistic’s mean by three orders of magnitude before coverage is even considered.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Hardware Input Capture**: On real-time MCUs, a timer’s input-capture unit latches encoder counts and current-shunt conversions at a hardware edge, and Direct Memory Access (DMA) moves them to memory without waiting on software. Sampling jitter stays within microseconds, so the denominator of a numerically differentiated velocity or acceleration carries no scheduling noise.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Precision Time Protocol (PTP)**: IEEE 1588 was first published in 2002 for test-and-measurement and industrial-automation networks, and its current revision dates from 2019 ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-ieee15882019|IEEE 2020]]). It reaches sub-microsecond agreement only when both endpoints timestamp packets in hardware at the network interface. Its weak point is the symmetric-path assumption in the offset formula, because a link whose two directions differ in delay biases the computed offset by half that difference, and every observation-action pairing in the archive inherits the bias.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Hardware Exposure Triggering**: A dedicated GPIO or TTL pulse from the real-time microcontroller starts every camera’s exposure on the same electrical edge, instead of a software trigger issued through the host’s driver stack. Triggered global-shutter cameras then begin exposing within microseconds of one another, which leaves the exposure duration, not the trigger path, as the dominant term in the midpoint timestamp.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Passivity**: A passive system cannot deliver more energy through its ports than it has received or initially stored. A well-posed, power-conserving interconnection of passive components preserves their combined passivity inequality. Stronger stability conclusions require bounds on stored energy and appropriate strictness or detectability conditions, as developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-passivity|Passivity and Energy-Bounded Interaction]]**. Anderson and Spong ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-anderson1989bilateral|1989]]) showed that a delayed communication channel need not be passive and restored passivity for any constant delay with a scattering transformation, and Niemeyer and Slotine ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-niemeyer1991stable|1991]]) recast the idea as wave variables. The price is paid in force fidelity at contact, and the demonstrations record that price.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **${L_{10}}$ Rated Life**: The convention comes from rolling-bearing fatigue theory, where Lundberg and Palmgren ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-lundberg1949dynamic|1949]]) modeled the scatter of bearing life as a probability of survival and rated a bearing by the life that 90 percent of a population reaches. A rating chosen that way is a population statistic, not a guarantee for one part. A fleet of ten collection arms, each run to its cable’s rated life, should therefore expect about one cable to fail before the budget says it will.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Curse of Dimensionality**: Bellman coined the phrase for dynamic programming, whose value tables grow exponentially with the number of state variables ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-bellman1957dynamic|Bellman 1957]]). A raw physical coverage table meets the same combinatorial barrier. Dynamic programming scaled by approximating value functions over compact feature spaces; a physical coverage table stays tractable the same way, by listing the few task parameters that determine success rather than the dense continuous state.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Corrective Aggregation Error Bound**: The bound is Theorem 3.2 of Ross et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|2011]]). It holds for some policy in the sequence of ${N}$ iterations, with ${N}$ of order ${u} {S}$ up to logarithmic factors, and ${u}$ bounds the change in the expert’s cost-to-go after any single action. **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Machine Learning Background#^sec-appendix-ml-compounding|Imitation Learning and Compounding Covariate Shift]]** states the finite-sample form, in which the same constant appears as ${u_{\max}}$.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Event Data Recorder**: US vehicle rules under 49 CFR Part 563 require an automotive event data recorder to preserve a fixed window of telemetry recorded prior to a crash trigger, recognizing that the root cause of an incident precedes the trigger event itself ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-nhtsa2024edr|National Highway Traffic Safety Administration 2024]]). Human supervisory takeovers have the same temporal structure, because the initial divergence ${t_{\text{div}}}$ always precedes the override timestamp ${t_{\text{takeover}}}$. A logging pipeline that begins capturing telemetry only upon trigger activation has already discarded the critical drift data required for policy curation.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Camera Calibration**: In practice ${\mathbf{K}}$ is estimated from a few views of a planar checkerboard, the method Zhang ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-zhang2000flexible|2000]]) introduced, and the extrinsics come from a separate hand-eye calibration. Both describe one mounting at one time. The reprojection residual is therefore a calibration test repeated every session, not a setup step performed once.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Survivorship Bias**: During the Second World War, Abraham Wald of Columbia’s Statistical Research Group estimated the vulnerability of combat aircraft from the damage on the planes that came back; the planes that were hit in the most vulnerable places were the ones missing from the sample ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-mangel1984wald|Mangel and Samaniego 1984]]). A demonstration archive filtered to successes has the same hole. The attempts that reached the failure boundary are exactly the ones absent, so the archive understates how often the boundary is reached and holds no states from it.

^fn13
