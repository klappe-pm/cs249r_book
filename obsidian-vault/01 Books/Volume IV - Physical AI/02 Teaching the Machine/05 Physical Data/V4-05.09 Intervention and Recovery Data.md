# V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-intervention-recovery

A scenario cell, such as the cage-door latch displaced by $+3\text{ mm}$, may hold zero demonstrations in the training archive. That zero count alone cannot tell an engineer whether the robot never drifted there, whether the 15 N contact tripwire aborted every attempt that reached it, whether a safety supervisor took over beforehand, or whether an ingestion script threw the failed episodes away. Treating those four absences as interchangeable either trains the policy to imitate a safety abort or discards the exact boundary states needed for recovery. An absent state in an empirical log arises from one of four physical mechanisms during collection:

1.  **Unvisited nominal path**: The collecting policy never visited the state because nominal rollouts remained entirely within the primary task corridor. This absence represents unmonitored nominal operation, verifiable by cross-referencing trajectory logs against the scenario table.
2.  **Permission-path interception**: An active safety barrier or deterministic reflex in the permission path detected an impending boundary violation and truncated the motion before reaching the state, as the 15 N contact tripwire does during a misaligned latch attempt. The permission path logs record this barrier trip directly.
3.  **Human supervisory takeover**: A human safety operator perceived a developing hazard and disengaged the autonomous policy before contact occurred. The permission path’s log flags this disengagement explicitly.
4.  **Post-collection censoring**: The machine entered the hazardous state and collided with an obstacle or jammed a joint, but an automated ingestion script discarded the episode as corrupt or incomplete. Dataset inspection cannot detect this absence because the failure transitions were purged before the training arrays were serialized to disk.

Each mechanism produces an identical zero count in the scenario table, yet each calls for a different response. An unvisited cell calls for targeted collection. An intercepted state belongs to the permission path, which already refuses it, and is not a target for the policy to learn. A takeover leaves learner-visited states that curation can turn into corrective supervision. A censored episode is a pipeline defect, recoverable only if the raw recording still exists. Conflating them leads to wrong conclusions about closed-loop policy stability, and the third mechanism needs the most care.

Human takeovers and automated disengagements introduce a selection trap into fleet data. An intervention occurs when the autonomous policy enters a region of state space it handles poorly and its tracking error or uncertainty crosses a threshold. A trajectory recorded after takeover therefore shows an expert recovering from an edge-case disturbance, not nominal task execution. The distribution of states visited during these rescues, ${p^{\text{takeover}}}$, is conditioned on policy failure. Treating intervention logs as if they were drawn from the nominal distribution ${p^{\pi^*}}$ distorts the training distribution toward states that exist only because the prior policy failed.

Appending a rescued episode directly to a behavioral cloning dataset can make unsafe policy commands look like expert demonstrations. Before takeover, the learned policy may issue commands that compound heading error or contact force; after takeover, the expert issues corrective commands from states the learner visited. Those drift states are valuable for corrective aggregation (expert labeling of the states the learner itself reaches, developed below) when an expert can label a safe action there. If a pipeline trains on the entire recorded trajectory without curation, it commits **learner-action contamination**. The failing policy’s own commands enter the dataset as expert labels. The network then learns to answer the visual cues of an approaching hazard (such as the gripper sliding off the handle or the base drifting toward a rack upright) with the high-risk commands that produced them, which accelerates divergence rather than correcting it. Learner-action contamination corrupts the training targets directly by injecting errant commands, whereas **causal confusion** occurs when a policy trained on uncorrupted expert targets locks onto spurious correlations ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-dehaan2019causal|Haan et al. 2019]]). The driving example from that work is a brake indicator on the dashboard. The light turns on because the expert braked, so it predicts the braking label almost perfectly, and a policy can learn to brake when the light is on rather than when an obstacle appears. At the latch, a measured joint current that climbs because the operator has already begun to push could play the same role, a consequence of the label that a policy mistakes for its cause.

Separating these labels requires locating the **divergence point** where the autonomous policy departed the nominal path, rather than treating takeover as the first error. The divergence precedes the takeover by at least the time a supervisor needs to respond. A remote operator who was not watching continuously must notice the hazard, reorient to the scene, and take control. The running machine’s card in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-running-machine|The running machine]]** budgets that out-of-loop takeover at 4.47 s, from published take-over studies plus console video and handshake (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-reaction-drift|Human reaction time and travel before braking]]** derives it). Edge loggers therefore keep a circular ring buffer spanning a fixed lookback window ${T_{\text{pre}}} =$ 5 s, that takeover time rounded up to whole seconds. Because dynamic memory allocation during safety-critical events introduces non-deterministic latency spikes, the buffer is pre-allocated in locked memory (`mlock`) or non-volatile storage and populated deterministically at the permission tick rate (1 kHz). When a human safety supervisor hits an override button or an MCU safety enforcer trips a barrier, the pipeline latches the intervention timestamp ${t_{\text{takeover}}}$ and freezes the preceding ring buffer. An out-of-loop supervisory takeover takes longer than the teleoperation loop’s continuous correction ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.07 Teleoperation Demonstration Costs#^sec-data-teleop-costs|section 5.6]]); at an illustrative 100 Hz, that delay spans hundreds of control cycles, all logged under the learner’s authority.

↪ The authority record, which logs on every control cycle which entity held authority and the root cause of any preemption, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-authority-log|The Authority Record]]**.

Detecting the divergence instant ${t_{\text{div}}}$, estimated below as ${\hat{t}_{\text{div}}}$, in offline ingestion pipelines requires searching backwards in time from the physical override timestamp across this synchronized pre-trigger telemetry buffer. For the latch, ${e_{\text{div}}}({t})$ is the lateral gripper-position error between measured and planned kinematics in the declared fixture frame, and ${\delta_{\text{tol}}}$ is its positive tolerance in meters. The divergence estimate is:

$$
\begin{aligned} {\hat{t}_{\text{div}}} = \min \Bigl\{ {t} \in [{t_{\text{takeover}}} - {T_{\text{pre}}}, {t_{\text{takeover}}}] \;\Big|\; &|{e_{\text{div}}}({t})| > {\delta_{\text{tol}}} \\ &\lor\; \text{Tr}({\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\pi}}({t})) > {\sigma_{\text{thresh}}}^2 \Bigr\}. \end{aligned} \tag{7}
$$

 The pipeline scans the lookback window for the first threshold crossing. The policy ensemble covariance is computed after normalizing each action component by its declared scale, so its trace and variance threshold are dimensionless. In unstructured contact manipulation where no static reference trajectory exists, divergence detection instead evaluates declared physical residuals against admissible safety envelope boundaries, such as velocity-to-obstacle clearance margins or anomalous contact force derivatives $d{F}/d{t}$. The detection thresholds are scenario-specific and calibrated to the sensor noise floor. The minimum defines an estimate only when the retained window contains a threshold crossing from below; an empty set or a residual already above threshold at the start of the window leaves the onset unresolved.

The protocol of **intervention curation** separates learner commands, expert labels on learner-visited states, and post-takeover recovery at timestamps the permission path logs for every takeover, the entries of the authority record that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14 Supervisory Intervention#^sec-intervention|Supervisory Intervention]]** formalizes. The takeover instant ${t_{\text{takeover}}}$ is the permission tick at which the authority commit revokes the policy’s ownership of the arm, and the three curation rules below are measured from it:

1.  **Quarantine drift commands:** The actions issued by the failing autonomous policy during the drift window $[{t_{\text{div}}}, {t_{\text{takeover}}})$ are retained in the forensic audit log with their safety flags, but masked out (${\mathcal{M}_{\text{train}}} = 0$) from imitation training targets.
2.  **Preserve drift states for relabeling:** The physical states visited during $[{t_{\text{div}}}, {t_{\text{takeover}}})$ capture off-nominal boundary conditions that are absent from nominal demonstrations. These states are admitted to training only when an automated expert, such as a planner with privileged state, supplies corrective labels (${\mathbf{a}}^*({s})$); a person replaying a logged state cannot reliably say what they would have commanded while closing the loop on it.
3.  **Admit only stabilized recovery:** The post-takeover trajectory is not one demonstration. It opens with the enforcer’s blend, in which the enforcer, still the only writer to the drives, ramps its single admitted command smoothly from the learner’s last checked reference to the operator’s. The actuators therefore never see a step, and no two sources ever command them at once. The operator’s reorientation follows. Neither interval is expert behavior, and both stay masked. Only the segment after the operator’s measured stabilization ${t_{\text{stabilize}}}$, the instant the operator’s corrections settle into a steady return toward the corridor, up to recovery at ${t_{\text{rec}}}$, and only where the episode passes task-validity review, becomes an expert recovery demonstration. It teaches the policy how to steer from boundary states back to the nominal manifold. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-recovery-eligibility|Causal masking and recovery eligibility]]** defines the blend, reorientation, and stabilization intervals and the criterion that ends reorientation.

For the latch campaign, consider a stipulated follow-up collection, `latch-recovery-02`, at the positive 3 mm strike-plate offset and the guarded 0.03 m/s approach. The same 15 N tripwire remains active. An expert takes over from a misaligned approach, withdraws and reseats the gripper, then releases the latch. The stipulated archive contains valid, reviewed recovery sequences; this is a worked teaching branch, not a report of collected trials or measured improvement.

The three rules decide what this archive keeps. The unsuccessful learner commands are retained for diagnosis, expert relabeling is used only where a corrective target is available, and the expert’s recovery is admitted only from its stabilized, reviewed segment. A new provenance digest binds those labels to the displaced fixture, the original plant contract, and the collection safeguards. The original archive remains immutable. Its successor now contains experience for the positive-offset guarded cell, while the negative-offset and faster-approach cells remain known absences. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06 Policy Training#^sec-training|Policy Training]]** uses this archive to construct the candidate `latch-bc-recovery-02`, and closed-loop evaluation must test recovery and nominal-task regressions before any expanded competence claim follows.

↪ The qualification of `latch-bc-recovery-02` against fresh nominal and displaced-plate trials is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-recovery-update|Qualifying a recovery update]]**.

The states between divergence and takeover are the second law, endogenous data, made concrete. The learner’s own control actions chose them, and the original nominal archive supplied no corrective supervision there; they are exactly the off-corridor states of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^fig-05-latch-corridor|figure 1]], now visited and logged. Curated, they supply the missing return actions. Training on nominal expert demonstrations alone leaves the compounding-error problem of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability|section 5.3]] in place, bounded in the worst case by the quadratic growth of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^eq-data-quadratic-drift|equation 1]], ${\mathcal{O}(S^2 \epsilon)}$. Labeling the states the learner itself reaches changes how that bound grows.

In **corrective aggregation**, such as Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|Ross et al. 2011]]), an expert labels states visited by the learner, including eligible pre-takeover drift states. Training on states sampled from the learner’s induced distribution (${s} \sim {p^{\pi}}$), rather than solely the expert’s distribution (${s} \sim {p^{\pi^*}}$), directly counters the covariate shift that nominal demonstrations leave unaddressed. The resulting performance guarantee depends on a constant determined by plant dynamics and task geometry rather than the learning algorithm. After ${N}$ rounds of aggregation, at least one policy ${\hat{\pi}}$ in the sequence satisfies

$$
{J}({\hat{\pi}}) \le {J}(\pi^*) + {u}\,{S}\,{\epsilon_N} + \mathcal{O}(1), \tag{8}
$$

 where ${\epsilon_N}$ represents the surrogate loss of the best single policy on the aggregated learner-visited states (a different quantity from the per-step disagreement ${\epsilon}$ of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^eq-data-quadratic-drift|equation 1]]). The symbol ${u}$ is the **recoverability constant**, the maximum increase in the expert’s remaining cost-to-go incurred by a single errant action before the expert resumes control.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.17 Footnotes#^fn10|¹⁰]] This bound scales linearly with the task horizon ${S}$ only while ${u}$ remains bounded, which is to say only while the expert can undo any single mistake at a bounded price. If ${u}$ scales in proportion to ${S}$, the product ${u} {S}{\epsilon_N}$ reverts to $\mathcal{O}({S}^2{\epsilon_N})$, wiping out the linear advantage over behavioral cloning; Ross et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|2011]]) observed this exact failure mode in their predecessor forward-training scheme.

^eq-data-dagger-bound

For a horizon of ${S} =$ 500 steps (5 s at 100 Hz) and ${\epsilon} =$ 0.001, the behavioral cloning bound ${S}^2{\epsilon} =$ 250. The aggregation term ${u}\,{S}{\epsilon_N}$ in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^eq-data-dagger-bound|equation 8]] evaluates to 0.5 for a recoverable task with ${u} = 1$ and matching per-step error. In this setting, the quadratic cloning bound equals half the episode’s maximum budget, while the leading aggregation term is less than the cost of a single control step. This comparison is conceptual, since the two error terms evaluate losses over different state distributions and both bounds omit problem-dependent leading constants. The quadratic bound becomes vacuous whenever ${\epsilon}$ exceeds $1/{S} =$ 0.002, at which point ${S}^2{\epsilon}$ exceeds the maximum possible episode cost of ${S}$. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^fig-05-compounding-error-flywheel|Figure 7]] joins the two halves of this section, the feedback loop that makes cloning’s worst case quadratic and the curation timeline that turns one takeover into labels the linear bound can use.

The worst case, where ${u}$ grows in proportion to the remaining horizon, is the first law, irreversibility (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/V4 The Machine Anatomy#^pri-vol4-irreversibility|1]]), written in the language of imitation bounds. Once the plant enters an unrecoverable state, such as a bent latch, a sheared cable harness, or a dropped brittle workpiece, no subsequent expert command can restore the lost objective. In these failure modes, the cost penalty spans the entire remaining task horizon (${u} \sim {S}$), and aggregating additional learner states cannot recover linear error bounds. A policy cannot learn recovery from states where recovery is physically impossible; the deterministic permission path must prevent entry into those states altogether. The latch campaign draws this boundary explicitly. Withdrawing and reseating a misaligned gripper is recoverable, so `latch-recovery-02` records corrections there. Contact forces approaching the illustrative 100 N component damage threshold are unrecoverable; the illustrative 15 N tripwire is a detection threshold, not a contact-force ceiling. Its use requires a qualified collection-response envelope accounting for detection, command and actuator response, and peak-force overshoot below the damage threshold. Without that evidence, the lower threshold alone cannot authorize collection. Those damaging states belong to the safety-enforcer domain rather than the policy learning targets.

Gathering expert supervision on learner-visited states takes several architectural forms, and the curation rules detailed above instantiate one of them. Classical DAgger requires an expert to query and label every state visited during autonomous execution, an impossible requirement for a human supervisor when the policy issues commands at control rates. The framework of **human-gated aggregation** resolves this bottleneck by allowing a human supervisor to take over only when necessary, appending labels exclusively from the resulting intervention episodes ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-kelly2019hgdagger|Kelly et al. 2019]]). The three curation rules refine this approach by keeping quarantined learner commands, relabeled drift states, and expert recovery actions under separate provenance. Where standard human-gated aggregation retains the entire human takeover stream, rule 3 also filters out the transitional controller blend and operator reorientation intervals. Two refinements address the scarcity of supervisor attention. One queries the expert only when policy uncertainty or novelty exceeds a threshold, spending a fixed intervention budget where the policy is least sure ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-hoque2022thriftydagger|Hoque et al. 2022]]). The other upweights the sparse recovery segments relative to the dense nominal trajectories during training, so a few minutes of rescue are not drowned by hours of nominal motion ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-mandlekar2020humanintheloop|Mandlekar et al. 2020]]).

A team can also avoid online interventions entirely, and this is the wide-tube remedy promised in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability|section 5.3]]. Injecting small noise perturbations into the teleoperator’s commands pushes the robot off the nominal path during ordinary collection, so the operator’s corrections back toward the corridor appear in the initial demonstration set itself ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-laskey2017dart|Laskey et al. 2017]]). Each perturbation consumes hardware life from the fatigue budget in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.07 Teleoperation Demonstration Costs#^sec-data-yield-fatigue-budgeting|section 5.6.3]] and must stay within recoverable bounds. Ingestion pipelines therefore log the collection mode of every episode explicitly (nominal, noise-injected, or gated intervention), because the three modes put different kinds of states and labels into the archive.

Learner-action contamination compounds across policy generations. Suppose a first policy drifts toward a joint torque limit until a supervisor intervenes with an override pendant. A successor trained on those uncurated drift-window commands as expert demonstrations reinforces the behavior that produced the departure, drives harder toward the limit in deployment, and forces operators to intervene earlier and more often. A pipeline that monitors only offline validation loss misses the cascade, because loss on the mislabeled validation split keeps improving as the network memorizes the corrupt labels while physical stability degrades.

Preventing this cascade requires the permission path to record every takeover or safety-barrier trip as an explicit entry in the authority record.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.17 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]] A boolean disengagement tag overlaid on video is not enough. The ingestion pipeline requires a populated pre-trigger ring buffer matching the declared lookback window (5 s), hardware-synchronized sensor observations and actuator taps, monotonic override timestamps, unique authority identifiers, and precise timestamps marking the blend completion and supervisor stabilization. Without this synchronized pre-trigger telemetry, ingestion pipelines cannot locate the divergence point ${t_{\text{div}}}$ and therefore cannot isolate errant learner commands from the boundary states eligible for expert relabeling.

**Checkpoint 5.3: Intervention curation and error bounds**

Before curating human intervention logs for policy retraining, audit compounding error dynamics and timestamp attribution against these criteria:

**Takeover vs. divergence timestamps**: Why retain learner-visited drift states for possible expert relabeling while excluding their unsafe learner commands from expert imitation targets?

**Retraining cascade progression**: Describe how training successive policy generations on uncurated intervention logs accelerates policy degradation despite improving training metrics.

**Conditional error bounds**: Which assumptions distinguish the behavioral-cloning quadratic worst-case cost bound from DAgger’s linear-horizon result?

**Bound threshold**: For an episode twice as long as the 5 s comparison, at the same 100 Hz control rate, above what per-step error ${\epsilon}$ does the quadratic bound stop constraining the cost?

Even with expert labels on learner-visited states, the resulting demonstration archive remains tethered to the kinematics and dynamics of the collecting machine. Pooling demonstrations across robot arms, bases, and end-effectors raises the cross-embodiment questions in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.10 Cross-Embodiment Transfer#^sec-data-cross-embodiment|section 5.9]].
