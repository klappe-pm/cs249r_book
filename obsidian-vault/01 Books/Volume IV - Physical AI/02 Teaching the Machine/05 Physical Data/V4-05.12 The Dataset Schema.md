# V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-schema

Consider an audit of two trajectory files from the latch campaign, each holding the exact same number of state-action pairs logged at the same frequency. File A records trajectories from four authorized operators across several days, with no permission-path clamps and consistent fingertip pads throughout. File B records a single operator, logs 620 steps where safety logic clamped joint velocities, and spans an unannounced force-sensor re-zeroing midway through the session. A standard validation script checking only tensor dimensions and value ranges accepts both files without complaint. Treating them as interchangeable training data introduces silent, corrupting bugs. File B requires explicit intervention attribution and recalibration metadata before an engineer can interpret its action labels correctly. Neither file’s tensor geometry reveals which physical latch conditions the recorded data actually covers.

The dataset schema organizes the contextual evidence required to make ingestion decisions safely. It preserves the physical origin and timing of every signal, logs collection costs and dropouts, and documents which physical conditions the archive covers or omits. These fields reflect the mechanical and operational realities of the collection cell. While complete metadata cannot guarantee that a learned policy will operate successfully in closed loop, omitting it ensures that downstream pipelines will misinterpret physical signals.

The **provenance record** is the immutable, machine-readable artifact bound directly to a demonstration archive. It links every recorded trajectory to the specific plant, sensor calibrations, component wear state, four action taps, collection denominators, and scenario-table join (${N_{\text{supported}}} / {N_{\text{req}}}$), including its count unit, eligibility and retention rule, and minimum, under which data collection occurred. Standard tensor shapes and training loss curves cannot validate a physical dataset. By recording an archive’s empirical boundaries, the provenance record allows the policy manifest (the record that training attaches to each candidate policy) and the safety case to distinguish demonstrated historical experience from the operational competence that physical testing must establish. Datasheets for datasets ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.16 Source References#^ref-gebru2021datasheets|Gebru et al. 2021]]) standardize documentation for static corpora; embodied archives additionally require tracking physical wear, permission-path interventions, and tap histories across the causal boundary. This record must travel with the trajectory data itself. Because detachable sidecar files easily separate from their payloads during format conversions, engineers serialize the record as an immutable, hashed header within the container’s root metadata, such as an MCAP top-level metadata record or a Zarr root attribute dictionary.

↪ The sixteen-record chain, in which the provenance record is the third record, is developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-record-register|The record register]]**.

The provenance record chains Part I’s hardware records directly to Part II’s training and evaluation records. It inherits the actuator and thermal bounds from the limit record and the clock domains and jitter budgets from the handoff record, while the proposal boundary determines which entity held control at each of the four action taps. As episodes are logged, the collection runtime adds the plant identifier, calibration matrices, component wear counts, yield denominators, and scenario-table join. The runtime then hands that complete record forward to the policy manifest, so closed-loop evaluation can test the policy against the exact conditions its data covered.

↩︎ The handoff record, which extends each limit with the handoff and clock domain that locate it in the machine, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^sec-nervous-handoffs-matrix|The Handoffs Matrix]]**.

Every trajectory admitted into the training corpus must identify its collection source, collector policy, and execution authority. For human teleoperation, the record logs the operator identifier, the interface hardware, and transport timing characteristics, for example a 6-DoF leader arm sampled at $100\text{ Hz}$ with $0.8\text{ ms}$ of input jitter. Storing an operator identifier, or capturing human faces on peripheral camera streams, makes an episode a record of people as well as of the robot. The schema therefore documents the legal and institutional basis under which each person was recorded, the authorized deployment purposes, and strict retention schedules. This governance ensures that an episode collected to teach latch insertion is not repurposed to evaluate the worker who demonstrated it. When data originates from an autonomous or mixed controller, the record stores the exact policy checkpoint, the planner version, and the active enforcement configuration, including Cartesian bounding boxes, joint velocity limits, and contact-force clamping thresholds. Trajectories must also record the active retention rule, documenting whether the runtime committed an episode automatically upon task completion, retained it after explicit operator review, or flagged it for inclusion as an instructive failure recovery.

↪ Purpose limits and retention schedules for records that capture people are enforced in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.08 Data About People#^sec-release-recorded-people|Recorded people as a standing condition]]**.

A physical machine cannot be identified by a generic catalog name or model string. In real hardware, two nominally identical robot arms behave differently because of manufacturing tolerances and component aging, so the data record specifies the individual plant through its intrinsic and extrinsic sensor calibrations, embodiment contract ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.10 Cross-Embodiment Transfer#^sec-data-embodiment-contracts|section 5.9.1]]), motor-drive firmware revisions, and component wear state. Even replaceable contact surfaces alter the governing dynamics. A parallel gripper fitted with compliant silicone pads of Shore 30A hardness (a durometer scale for rubbers, on which higher numbers are stiffer) produces vastly different friction and compliance than the same mechanism fitted with worn Shore 60A polyurethane pads under identical joint commands. Maintenance milestones such as cable retensioning, drive-belt replacements, gearbox swaps, and camera remounts therefore belong directly in the machine state record. If technicians recalibrated joint offsets between morning and afternoon collection shifts, the dataset exposes that boundary, so downstream imitation learning does not attempt to resolve two conflicting kinematic mappings into a single policy.

Collection denominators reconcile every physical attempt as a retained success, a retained failure, or an excluded recording, while exposing the wall-clock time spent resetting the scene. Alongside those yield counts, the four action taps of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.06 Action Label Selection Across the Control Boundary#^sec-data-four-stream-action-tap|section 5.5.1]], override flags, and exclusion codes preserve the critical distinction between demonstrated behavior and permission-path intervention. When safety logic intervenes to clamp velocity or limit contact forces, the data pipeline must attribute those adjustments correctly rather than misinterpreting supervisory interventions as expert demonstrations.

The scenario join records dataset-supported cells (configurations containing retained demonstrations) and catalogs known absences where no empirical data exists. The count-policy digest binds the count unit, eligibility and retention rule, and declared minimum; when a minimum is specified, the record distinguishes occupied cells from those meeting it. Here, dataset support reflects historical logging coverage and must not be conflated with the evaluated support of a deployed policy. For the latch campaign, the record below connects collection cost to coverage; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.12 The Dataset Schema#^tbl-05-provenance-schema|table 6]] maps its field groups to the decisions they support.

- **Denominators:** 52 attempts per scheduled session at a net retention rate of 0.68, so 5,000 retained successful demonstrations cost 7,353 attempts over 141.4 h of scheduled cell time.
- **Retention rule:** an attempt whose contact force crosses the 15 N tripwire triggers the qualified collection response and is kept as a labeled failure, outside the demonstration count, when its telemetry is valid; a recording that fails quality filtering is excluded with its exclusion code.
- **Scenario table:** plate offset (nominal, +3 mm, −3 mm) crossed with approach speed (0.03 m/s, 0.10 m/s), six cells, held with the count and retention policy in the record as a digest rather than as a copy.
- **`supported_cells`:** the nominal plate at 0.03 m/s, the only cell the original retained demonstrations occupy.
- **`known_absences`:** the other five cells, every displaced plate and every approach at 0.10 m/s.

The policy manifest of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^sec-training-policy-manifest|The Policy Manifest]]** cites each contributing archive by digest, including `latch-recovery-02` in the stipulated follow-up branch. The original record continues to describe the original campaign; new experience receives new provenance rather than rewriting old absences. Operator and override history also informs supervisory evaluation, while coverage and calibration evidence contributes to the release argument. Binding the metadata to the trajectory container helps preserve these relationships through ingestion, but cryptographic hashes do not automatically prevent misuse. Data loaders and ingestion pipelines must verify that trajectory arrays remain bound to their declared schema and calibration headers before training begins, because skipping any part of that check leads to the recurring collection failures cataloged next ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.13 Fallacies and Pitfalls#^sec-data-fallacies-pitfalls|section 5.12]]).
