# V4-05.02 Endogenous Experience

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-endogenous-experience

In most machine learning, the dataset exists before the engineering begins. A text corpus or an image benchmark is gathered independently of the model, costs little per additional example, and does not change in response to anything the model predicts. A physical machine has no such corpus. Collecting demonstrations on the robot consumes operator time and component life; robot-free and synthetic sources avoid that robot wear but still require qualification for the target machine. Every telemetry record reflects who commanded the action, what the permission path admitted, and how the physical body moved. Because each executed action changes the physical state of the system, it dictates what subsequent observations can reveal. Physical data is therefore an engineered artifact. The central challenge of physical data engineering is deciding which experiences to collect, and what to record about them, so that a policy learns the task rather than the idiosyncrasies of its collection. Answering this challenge requires four elements: accounting for collection costs in operator time and hardware wear, defining an unambiguous meaning for every recorded action label, establishing a metric for state-space coverage independent of the archive’s internal contents, and explicitly auditing what the archive omits.

Consider a warehouse mobile manipulator tasked with opening a spring-latched cage door. The robot can flawlessly enforce its low-level motion limits and yet fail because it lacks the operational experience to unlatch the door. During a demonstration, a human operator commands motion, the safety permission path checks and filters it, and the robot’s physical body drives against the mechanical latch. The onboard sensor log captures the output of that entire coupled loop. The limits and handoffs recorded earlier for this machine, in the limit record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.08 Measuring a Machine’s Own Limits#^sec-body-limit-record-schema|The six elements of a limit record]]** and the handoff record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^sec-nervous-handoffs-matrix|The Handoffs Matrix]]**, therefore become preconditions on the training data fed to the learned policy proposer. Every logged command was shaped by them before the body moved. Choosing which physical experiences to collect demands a rigorous accounting of how control inputs shift future sensory states. Choosing what to imitate requires cleanly separating the operator’s raw request from the safety-filtered command and the measured physical response.

A policy fit by supervised imitation to nominal, clean teleoperated demonstrations of door openings (behavioral cloning, defined below) can execute flawlessly in simulation yet jam the latch during its first autonomous deployment. The primary culprit is the distribution shift that the machine’s own closed-loop actions induce. Unlike passive inference on a fixed benchmark, an action executed within an embodied Markov decision process (MDP), in which the next state depends on the current state and action, physically alters the robot’s spatial trajectory, camera viewpoint, and contact states. That displacement changes the probability distribution of every subsequent sensory observation (${s}_{t+1} \sim {p}({s} \mid {s}_{t}, {\mathbf{a}}_{t})$).

↪ The Markov decision process, with its transition model and reward, is developed for the latch task in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.03 Policy Training Regimes#^sec-training-rl-primer|The latch task as a reward problem]]**.

**Definition 5.1: Endogenous covariate shift**

***Endogenous covariate shift*** is the closed-loop distribution discrepancy wherein an embodied agent’s executed actions alter its subsequent physical states and sensory observations (${s}_{t+1} \sim {p}({s} \mid {s}_{t}, {\mathbf{a}}_{t})$), driving the system into state-space regimes unsupported by its training demonstrations.

1.  **Significance**: Explains how offline behavioral cloning can fail during closed-loop physical execution: a perturbation may drive the system into states where the policy lacks training support.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike exogenous dataset shift caused by changing ambient lighting or seasonal weather, endogenous shift is actively generated by the agent’s own closed-loop actions altering its subsequent observation distribution.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Attempting to fix closed-loop policy failure by collecting more nominal expert demonstrations, which only increases data density along the success manifold without providing the corrective recovery data needed to escape boundary excursions.

This distribution shift is how the second law, endogenous data (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/V4 Teaching the Machine#^pri-vol4-endogenous-drift|2]]), shows up in data collection, where the machine’s own actions choose the states its archive must later answer for. An archive composed exclusively of flawless executions without recovery trajectories becomes an operational liability. The moment the autonomous policy commits its first sub-millimeter tracking error, the robot enters states never encountered during training. Two distinct physical mechanisms govern how fast that departure grows ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.03 Mechanics of Endogenous Covariate Shift#^sec-data-covariate-shift|section 5.2]]).
