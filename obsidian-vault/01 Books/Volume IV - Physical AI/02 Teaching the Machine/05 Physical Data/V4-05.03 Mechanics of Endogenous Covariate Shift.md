# V4-05.03 Mechanics of Endogenous Covariate Shift

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05 Physical Data|V4-05 Physical Data]]

^sec-data-covariate-shift

When an image classifier misidentifies a dog as a cat, the mistake does not alter the pixels of the next photograph in the test set. When a door-opening policy misses the latch centerline by a millimeter, that motion shifts the camera viewpoint, changes the contact geometry, and alters every observation that follows. Classical empirical risk minimization assumes the first setting, drawing independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples from an exogenous, stationary distribution ${\mathcal{D}}$. Even when passive machine learning studies *covariate shift*, it treats the shift as an open-loop observational change: the marginal input distribution ${p(x)}$ shifts (such as evaluating a daytime-trained vision model at night) while the conditional data-generating function ${p(y \mid x)}$ stays fixed. Because a passive model’s predictions ${\hat{Y}}$ exert no physical back-action on the world, an inference error never mutates the next input. Imitation learning on a machine operates inside the closed-loop counterpart to this setting.

↩︎ The closed loop through which a flawed proposal carries the machine into states missing from its training distribution is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-tensors-to-silicon|From a proposed action to motion]]**.

In an embodied physical system, two distinct mechanisms distort what the learner encounters. The first shifts the states that the policy visits even when the hardware stays unchanged; the second alters how the physical plant responds to any given control input:

1.  **Endogenous state distribution shift**: The policy’s executed actions ${\mathbf{a}}_{t} = {\pi_\theta}({\mathbf{o}}_{t})$ directly drive the physical state transition ${s}_{t+1} \sim {p}({s} \mid {s}_{t}, {\mathbf{a}}_{t})$, choosing the sensory observations ${\mathbf{o}}_{t+1}$ the robot sees next. A single control error moves the body into an unvisited region of the state space and changes the incoming observation distribution on the next tick.
2.  **Active physical plant drift**: The physical plant is a continuous dynamical system governed by differential equations of motion ${\dot{\mathbf{x}}} = {\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{x}}, {\mathbf{u}}; {\theta_{\text{plant}}}({t}))$ whose parameters drift over operational time. Actuator Joule heating (${i_q}^2 {R}({T_{\text{winding}}})$) raises winding temperature and copper resistance via ${R}({T_{\text{winding}}}) = {R}_0(1 + {\alpha_{\text{Cu}}} {\Delta T})$, compressing the motor drive’s voltage headroom and capping peak torque at high joint speeds. Over extended operating cycles, continuous loading degrades strain-wave gearbox lubrication, lowering torsional stiffness and expanding mechanical lost motion. Compliant elastomer gripper pads wear down and deform, shrinking the Coulomb friction cone ($\lVert {\mathbf{f}_{k, \parallel}} \rVert \le {\mu} {f_{k, \perp}}$). Finally, physical workpieces vary in mass, center of gravity, and surface texture across trials, perturbing both the inertial loads the arm accelerates and the gravitational torques the joints sustain.

Together, the policy’s closed-loop actions and the plant’s response induce an *endogenous state visitation distribution* ${p^{\pi}}({s})$, the expected fraction of time steps that a rollout of policy $\pi$ spends in state ${s}$. When an agent is trained by offline behavioral cloning on demonstrations from an expert policy $\pi^*$, empirical risk minimization fits the imitation objective only over the expert’s occupancy distribution ${p^{\pi^*}}({s})$. During autonomous execution, the learned policy instead acts under its own induced distribution ${p^{\pi}}({s})$. Wherever ${p^{\pi}}$ departs from ${p^{\pi^*}}$, the policy encounters states that hold no demonstrated corrective action.

**Definition 5.2: Behavioral cloning**

***Behavioral cloning*** is imitation learning posed as supervised learning: a policy is fit to map each recorded observation to the action the demonstrator took there, with the loss weighted by the demonstrator’s own state occupancy ${p^{\pi^*}}$.

1.  **Significance**: Training error is measured only on states the demonstrator visited, while deployment cost accrues on the states the learned policy visits. Under a 0–1 action loss (one whenever the policy’s action differs from the expert’s, zero otherwise) the worst-case excess cost can therefore grow with the square of the horizon ${S}$, the episode length in control steps, rather than linearly, as the next section derives.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike corrective aggregation, which obtains expert labels at states the learner itself reaches ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.09 Intervention and Recovery Data#^sec-data-intervention-recovery|section 5.8]] develops it), behavioral cloning never observes the consequences of its own mistakes; unlike reinforcement learning, it needs demonstrations but no reward.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Reading a low held-out loss as evidence of closed-loop competence. A held-out split drawn from the same demonstrations shares their occupancy, so it cannot contain the states the policy’s first error reaches.

Because behavioral cloning weights its loss solely by the demonstrator’s occupancy ${p^{\pi^*}}$, a single step off the demonstrated path places the policy in states where training never penalized an error. How fast that first departure compounds over an episode depends on whether the archive holds any actions that steer the machine back ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.04 Recovery and Compounding Error#^sec-data-curse-dimensionality-recoverability|section 5.3]]).
