*Why can a correctly chosen action still demand more than the machine’s body can deliver?*

A mobile manipulator can recognize an obstacle correctly and still strike it if its body cannot stop within the remaining distance. The same machine can hold a payload without moving while its motor windings overheat, or lose its controller when a sudden demand for current pulls the supply voltage too low. These failures arise from limits that prediction accuracy alone cannot reveal. Physical AI engineering requires connecting a proposed action to the motion, heat, and electrical demand it creates, then establishing which limit the assembled machine reaches first. Measurements must also establish the conditions under which those limits hold, because a change in payload, floor surface, cooling, or battery state changes what the machine can deliver. The body’s limits therefore govern what the permission path may admit before a command crosses the causal boundary and produces irreversible physical work.

**Learning Objectives**

- Calculate observation freshness limits from measurement age and the machine’s allowable motion
- Calculate stopping distance and the admissible speed ceiling from pre-brake delay, credible deceleration, and clearance overheads
- Evaluate actuator and transmission choices against response limits, reflected inertia, and mechanical impedance
- Estimate sustainable motor duty cycles from heat generation, cooling, and operating conditions
- Budget electrical supply margins for simultaneous actuation, computation, and regenerative braking
- Specify the elements of a limit record that states a measured limit’s conditions, uncertainty, margin, runtime detector, and evidence category
- Evaluate maneuver-specific binding budgets from qualified evidence and justify holding up the permission supply through covered faults
