# V4-02.14 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02 The Physical Body|V4-02 The Physical Body]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **MIPI CSI-2 (Camera Serial Interface 2)**: The Mobile Industry Processor Interface camera link carries raw frames from the image sensor to the system-on-chip and marks the start and end of each frame with short packets. Latching a hardware timestamp on the frame-start packet ties the frame to a fixed offset from its exposure rather than to the moment software first sees it, which is what lets transduction time, not arrival time, anchor the age budget.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **EtherCAT**: An industrial Ethernet fieldbus in which one frame passes through every drive on the segment, each node reading and writing its own slice of the frame as it passes. Frame length and node count, not contention for the wire, fix the cycle time, which is why the fieldbus row of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]] has equal $\text{p50}$ and $\text{p99}$. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^sec-nervous-schedulability-analysis|Comparing bus occupancy on CAN FD and EtherCAT]]** sizes that cycle for the running machine.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Quantile Convolution**: In general, the quantile of a sum of delays does not equal the sum of their individual quantiles (${Q_p}({X} + {Y}) \ne {Q_p}({X}) + {Q_p}({Y})$). Even when stages are independent, adding their $\text{p99}$ values is not a worst-case bound, and shared buses and queues correlate the stages so that the end-to-end tail must be measured across the whole pipeline.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Transport Delay and Feedback Stability**: Transport delay makes a controller act on historical state. Whether delayed corrections destabilize the loop depends on the plant, controller, and total response. **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-zoh|Discrete-time sampling and zero-order hold phase lag]]** develops the phase-margin calculation; delay alone does not establish a particular oscillation or damage outcome.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **IEEE 802.1AS (gPTP)**: The generalized Precision Time Protocol of IEEE 802.1AS is a profile of the IEEE 1588 Precision Time Protocol ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-ieee15882019|IEEE 2020a]]) for time-sensitive Ethernet networks ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-ieee8021as2020|IEEE 2020b]]). It synchronizes clocks by exchanging packets timestamped in hardware at the network interface, which keeps the operating-system scheduler’s jitter out of the measurement. The age ${t_{\text{age}}} = {t_{\text{current}}} - {t_{\text{transduction}}}$ means something only when the camera, the host, and the drives read one timebase; an offset between their clocks enters the freshness budget as an error that no timestamp reveals.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Protective Separation Distance**: For speed and separation monitoring of robot arms, the collaborative-application requirements that ISO 10218-2:2025 incorporates from ISO/TS 15066 (which itself remains published and under revision) write the protective separation distance as ${S_p} = {S_h} + {S_r} + {S_s} + {C} + {Z_d} + {Z_r}$: the person’s travel, the robot’s travel during its reaction time and while stopping, an intrusion distance set by the sensing, and the position uncertainties of person and robot ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-isots15066|International Organization for Standardization 2016]], [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-iso10218part2|2025]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-marvel2017implementing|Marvel and Norcross 2017]]). The robot’s terms map onto [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^eq-body-stopping-distance|equation 2]], with ${v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ as ${S_r}$, ${v}^2/2{a_{\text{brake}}}$ as ${S_s}$, and ${\delta_{\text{loc}}}$ as ${Z_r}$, although ISO 10218-2:2025 excludes the hazards specific to a manipulator riding a mobile platform, the running machine’s case. For a learned policy, an unbounded compute tail lengthens the reaction time, which enlarges both ${S_h}$ and ${S_r}$ and forces the protective field outward.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Kinematic Velocity Root**: Grouping the fixed distance terms into the available braking distance ${D_{\text{avail}}} = {D_{\text{clear}}} - {\delta_{\text{loc}}} - {\delta_{\text{margin}}}$ gives the quadratic inequality $\frac{1}{2 {a_{\text{brake}}}} {v}^2 + {\tau_{\text{delay}}} {v} - {D_{\text{avail}}} \le 0$. Multiplying by $2 {a_{\text{brake}}}$ gives ${v}^2 + 2 {a_{\text{brake}}} {\tau_{\text{delay}}} {v} - 2 {a_{\text{brake}}} {D_{\text{avail}}} \le 0$. Applying the quadratic formula and taking the positive root sets the speed ceiling.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Field-Oriented Control (FOC)**: Holding the stator current at right angles to the rotor field is the arrangement that a brushed DC motor’s commutator enforces mechanically. An AC machine controlled this way behaves like a DC motor whose torque is set by a single current, ${i_q}$. That is what lets a policy command torque at all, because its request becomes a current target whose arrival the winding inductance and the voltage headroom then pace.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Inverter Dead Time**: A short hardware-inserted interval in which both switches of an inverter half-bridge are off, so that the high-side and low-side switches of one leg never conduct together and short the DC bus, a failure called shoot-through. The interval distorts the applied voltage most at low speed and near each current zero crossing, so delivered torque departs from the request exactly where slow, fine contact force is commanded.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Inertia Matching**: At ${N^*}$ the reflected rotor inertia equals the load inertia, the mechanical twin of impedance matching in a circuit, where a source delivers the most power to a load whose impedance equals its own. The analogy carries the same caveat: the matched ratio maximizes acceleration, not efficiency or safety, so practical arms choose lower ratios to protect gear teeth from shock, keep the joint backdrivable, and raise top speed.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Strain Wave Gearing**: Musser ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-musser1959strain|1959]]) patented the mechanism, now sold as the Harmonic Drive: an elliptical wave generator flexes a thin toothed cup inside a rigid ring that carries two more teeth, so a single stage yields a high ratio with near-zero backlash. The flexing that makes the ratio possible is also the joint’s torsional compliance, which makes the link elastic.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Limit Cycle**: A self-sustained oscillation of fixed amplitude that a nonlinear feedback loop settles into, here excited by the backlash deadband as the motor crosses the gap, the teeth strike, and the controller corrects back across it. Unlike an instability, it does not grow, so it can run unnoticed through a shift while every cycle adds a tooth impact to the fatigue count.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Thermal Aging as an Exchange Rate**: The engineering practice predates the chemistry. Montsinger ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-montsinger1930loading|1930]]) proposed loading transformers by the temperature their insulation reaches rather than by current alone, and Dakin ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-dakin1948electrical|1948]]) later explained the exponential dependence as Arrhenius reaction kinetics. The consequence is an exchange rate rather than a threshold. Time spent above the rating costs life at a rate that grows exponentially with the excess, so the limit record must count time at temperature rather than merely the peak.

^fn13

<sup>14</sup> **Duty Type and RMS Torque**: IEC 60034-1 rates a motor for a duty type: S1 is continuous running at constant load long enough to reach thermal equilibrium, and S3 is an intermittent periodic duty of identical load-and-rest cycles too short to reach it ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-iec60034part1|International Electrotechnical Commission 2022]]). Because copper loss grows with ${\tau_{\text{motor}}}^2$, the torque that heats the winding over a cycle short compared with the thermal time constant is the root-mean-square value ${\tau_{\text{rms}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{{T}}\int_0^{T} {\tau_{\text{motor}}}^2\,d{t}}$, and it is the RMS, not the peak, that must stay under the continuous rating. A peak rating means nothing without its duty type, which is why [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.06 Thermal Duty Cycles#^fig-02-motor-torque-speed-envelope|figure 6]] draws the S1 and S3 regions separately.

^fn14

<sup>15</sup> **Periodic steady-state thermal peak**: Over a repeating cycle of period ${t_{\text{cycle}}} = {t_{\text{on}}} + {t_{\text{off}}}$, summing the heating and cooling exponentials gives the periodic peak temperature rise: ${\Delta} {T_{\text{peak}}} = {P_{\text{peak}}} {R_{\text{th}}}\frac{1 - e^{-{t_{\text{on}}}/{\tau_{\text{th}}}}}{1 - e^{-{t_{\text{cycle}}}/{\tau_{\text{th}}}}}$. As the cycle period ${t_{\text{cycle}}} \to 0$, this peak approaches the average-power screen (${D_{\text{cycle}}} {P_{\text{peak}}} {R_{\text{th}}}$); for any finite burst length, the peak winding temperature exceeds the average.

^fn15

<sup>16</sup> **Source Resistance**: The lumped source resistance is ${R_{\text{bus}}} = {R_{\text{cell}}} + {R_{\text{harness}}} + {R_{\text{switch}}}$, the sum of battery cell, harness and connector, and protection-switch on-state resistances. The cell term is the one that moves. It rises as the pack ages and as it cools, so a sustained screen passed at commissioning can fail on a cold morning years later. That drift belongs in the rail’s limit record as a condition, with a detector that measures the drop under load.

^fn16
