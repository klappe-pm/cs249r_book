# V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02 The Physical Body|V4-02 The Physical Body]]

^sec-body-measurement-freshness

Software benchmarks in machine learning frequently report latency as isolated accelerator execution time, for example 18.5 ms for the vision backbone of the arm’s wrist-camera grasp path on an edge neural processing unit (NPU), a different pipeline from the navigation backbone that the stopping budget of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-kinematic-roofline|Delay consumes stopping clearance]]** charges. This narrow focus fosters the illusion of an agile 54.1 Hz control loop. In physical AI systems, however, that compute kernel represents only one intermediate segment in an end-to-end silicon-to-plant dataflow pipeline. A sensor measurement begins decaying the moment physical transduction occurs; what governs physical performance is its total age when torque actually appears at the actuator joint. That physical age spans the entire interval from the moment photons strike a photodiode array, magnetic flux sweeps past a Hall sensor, or strain deflects a piezoresistor to the moment motor inverter switches build stator magnetic flux that alters link acceleration. If a perception policy evaluates an obstacle bounding box in 18.5 ms, while exposure integration, sensor readout, Peripheral Component Interconnect Express (PCIe) transport, kernel scheduling, fieldbus dispatch, and motor current rise consume an additional 16.5 ms at the median, the true physical age of the observation when the joint accelerates is 35 ms. The distribution tail extends farther still.

Throughout that capture-to-onset window, the physical environment continues to evolve unobserved. In distributed data systems, read-after-write consistency windows govern whether a client observes stale or linearized state. In physical AI, the physical plant acts as an autonomous, unpausable concurrent writer: spatial position ${\mathbf{p}}({t})$ evolves along continuous trajectories at velocity ${\mathbf{v}}({t})$. An observation age ${t_{\text{age}}}$ converts directly into unobserved physical displacement ${\Delta} {\mathbf{p}} \approx \int_0^{{t_{\text{age}}}} {\mathbf{v}}({t})\,d{t}$. High-speed machines inevitably act on historical state.

The rate of world-state change dictates how long a measurement remains valid before acting on it introduces intolerable error. Let ${e_{\max}}$ denote the maximum acceptable error in a tracked state, such as a joint angle or the position of a part on a conveyor, and let ${v_{\max}}$ denote the fastest credible velocity at which that state can evolve. The freshness deadline ${\Delta t_{\text{fresh}}}$ is the upper bound on measurement age before a control action based on that measurement becomes invalid, defined by the inequality

$$
{\Delta t_{\text{fresh}}} \le \frac{{e_{\max}}}{{v_{\max}}} \tag{1}
$$

^eq-body-freshness-deadline

**Definition 2.1: Freshness deadline**

***Freshness deadline*** (${\Delta t_{\text{fresh}}}$) is the largest age, counted from the capture of a measurement to the onset of the actuator’s response, that the measurement of a moving target may reach before the target’s motion alone spends the allowable position error ${e_{\max}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^eq-body-freshness-deadline|equation 1]]).

1.  **Significance**: It converts a spatial tolerance into a time budget that every stage from exposure to torque onset must share. A 12 mm tolerance on a target moving at 0.20 m/s leaves 60 ms for sensing, inference, transport, and current rise together.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike an inference-latency budget, which times one stage, the deadline bounds the age at onset; unlike a control-loop period, it is set by the target’s speed and the tolerance rather than by the loop rate. When the machine moves toward a stationary obstacle instead, the same age becomes the first term of the pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ and is paid as blind travel ${v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ in the stopping distance.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Treating the deadline as a property of the sensor or the model. It changes whenever the target moves faster or the tolerance shrinks, so a pipeline qualified on a slow conveyor can miss on a faster one without any change in its own timing.

↪ The same deadline applied to a stored belief, which lapses once its region falls out of view, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^sec-memory-belief-through-occlusion|Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay]]**.

The running machine meets this bound at its pick station, where the arm grasps a mug from a moving conveyor. The belt carries the mug at speeds up to ${v_{\text{conv}}} =$ 0.20 m/s, and the gripper mechanism tolerates ${r_{\text{tol}}} =$ 15 mm of position error. Initial handle-pose estimation already consumes ${e_0} =$ 3 mm of this margin. Consequently, the mug’s physical motion may spend at most ${e_{\max}} = {r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0} =$ 12 mm, with ${v_{\max}} = {v_{\text{conv}}}$. Notebook [2.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/02_body/02_body.html#nbk-body-freshness-deadline) evaluates this resulting deadline against the ages an integrated camera-to-joint pipeline delivers in practice. The deadline governs any target that moves while its observation ages. For moving workpieces, it limits motion-induced tracking drift under bounded relative velocity and valid initial observations. Sensing and state-estimation noise consume separate slices of the tolerance budget, just as ${e_0}$ does here. Conversely, when the robot approaches a stationary obstacle, such as a person standing in a warehouse aisle, the target does not move. Instead, the mobile base drives toward the person during the latency interval. Observation age then converts directly into unguided vehicular travel before braking begins, which [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^sec-body-kinetic-momentum|section 2.3]] charges against the kinetic stopping envelope.

### Transduction Physics, Quantization Noise, and Nyquist Limits

^sec-body-sensor-transduction

If a 12-bit magnetic encoder jumps by a single count between two 1 ms servo ticks, backward differencing reports a sudden 88°/s velocity spike. If an unshielded cooling fan vibrates an inertial unit above half its sampling rate, the vibration folds into the low-frequency band and looks like chassis acceleration. Every sensor trades error tolerance against time: it must resolve the physical state of interest, and the analog filtering, sampling, or serial scanning needed to extract a clean signal must fit inside the freshness deadline. Four physical mechanisms set this trade:

1.  **Optical Encoders and Velocity Quantization Noise:** An encoder measures joint position in discrete angular increments. An ${n}$-bit encoder discretizes the continuous $2\pi\text{ rad}$ circle into an angular resolution of ${\delta} {\theta_{\text{enc}}} = \frac{2\pi}{2^{n}}$. For an 18-bit absolute optical encoder, ${\delta} {\theta_{\text{enc}}} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad}$ ($0.0014^\circ$); for a 12-bit magnetic encoder, ${\delta} {\theta_{\text{enc}}} \approx 1.53 \times 10^{-3}\text{ rad}$ ($0.088^\circ$). Feedback controllers, which compute each command from the measured difference between the desired and the actual state, estimate joint velocity from successive position samples across a discrete control cycle ${\Delta} {t}$:

$$
{{\dot{\theta}_{\text{enc}}}{}}_{k} \approx \frac{{{\theta_{\text{enc}}}}_{k} - {{\theta_{\text{enc}}}}_{k-1}}{{\Delta} {t}}
$$

 Backward differencing acts as an effective high-pass filter, scaling high-frequency position fluctuations by $1/{\Delta} {t}$. Under an idealized model where successive quantization errors are independent and uniformly distributed across one encoder count, each sample has variance $({\delta}{\theta_{\text{enc}}})^2/12$. Differencing two independent samples sums their variances to $({\delta}{\theta_{\text{enc}}})^2/6$, yielding a velocity-error standard deviation of ${\sigma_{\dot{\theta}_{\text{enc}}}} = \frac{{\delta}{\theta_{\text{enc}}}}{\sqrt{6}\,{\Delta} {t}}$. At a $1\text{ kHz}$ servo update rate (${\Delta} {t} = 1\text{ ms}$), a single-count jump on a 12-bit encoder ($1.53\text{ mrad}$) registers as an abrupt velocity spike of $1.53\text{ rad/s}$ ($88^\circ/\text{s}$). If routed directly into velocity feedback loops or neural policy state inputs, these discrete impulses cause severe torque chatter and excite mechanical resonances. Downstream low-pass filters or state observers (models that infer a quantity such as velocity from a stream of measurements) smooth this quantization noise, but filter group delay introduces phase lag, consuming milliseconds of the freshness budget.

2.  **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and Nyquist Aliasing:** An inertial measurement unit measures linear acceleration and angular velocity. Sampling must isolate macroscopic body dynamics from high-frequency structural vibration. Under the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-nyquist1928certain|Nyquist 1928]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-shannon1949communication|Shannon 1949]]), continuous signals require a sampling frequency strictly greater than twice their highest spectral component (${f_s} > 2 {f_{\max}}$). Mechanical sources, including gear mesh harmonics, cooling fans, and motor inverter switching, generate vibrations reaching kilohertz frequencies. Without adequate analog anti-aliasing filters before the analog-to-digital converter (ADC), structural vibrations above the Nyquist frequency fold back into baseband frequencies ($1\text{--}20\text{ Hz}$). The state estimator, which combines sensor readings into an estimate of the machine’s motion, cannot distinguish these aliased artifacts from genuine chassis acceleration, introducing cumulative drift into dead-reckoning integrations. Raising downstream software loop rates cannot reconstruct information already corrupted at the sensor hardware interface.

3.  **Tactile Sensor Arrays and Scanning Bandwidth:** Tactile arrays measure contact pressure across a robot gripper using discrete sensing elements called taxels. To minimize cabling across robotic wrists, hardware architectures multiplex taxels across shared row and column lines. Scanning these elements sequentially forces an entire frame to span the multiplexing period. This serial scan time adds directly to the measurement age and limits the bandwidth for detecting rapid contact events, such as the onset of localized slip. Increasing the taxel density without scaling the underlying ADC readout rate produces older tactile observations.

4.  **Cameras and Rolling-Shutter Readout:** A rolling-shutter CMOS camera exposes and transfers pixel rows sequentially, so a single frame spans its entire readout duration. Image timestamps must therefore anchor to a defined reference, such as the exposure midpoint of a designated row, rather than the frame-arrival interrupt.

Across all four mechanisms, a measurement begins aging at physical transduction, milliseconds before any software driver receives a buffer. Bounding how much of the freshness deadline remains at actuation therefore requires tracing the signal across every stage between transduction and motor current.

### The End-To-End Sense-To-Actuation Latency Pipeline

^sec-body-latency-pipeline

A measurement’s physical age begins at **capture**, the moment of physical transduction, rather than when an operating system process first receives the data buffer. Three subsequent boundary events demarcate the pipeline from transduction to mechanical work. *Dispatch* is when perception publishes dated perceptual evidence to the action proposer. *Admission* is when the real-time permission path validates and accepts a proposal built using that evidence. *Onset* is when electrical current in the motor stator windings begins driving physical acceleration at the joint. The four events recur throughout the book, and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^tbl-04-event-timing|The capture-to-brake-onset timing budget of the running machine]]** later budgets the intervals between them for the running machine. For the freshness deadline, capture and onset form the bounding endpoints, and the deadline limits the cumulative delay between them.

An illustrative camera-to-joint pipeline reveals how latency compounds across physical and digital boundaries ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]]). Between capture and dispatch, the CMOS sensor integrates exposure and reads out raw scanlines over MIPI CSI-2.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.14 Footnotes#^fn1|¹]] Direct memory access (DMA) transfers the frame into host memory, and the vision backbone executes. In this representative architecture, the policy network executes in the same inference pass as the backbone, spanning the dispatch boundary. Between dispatch and admission, inter-process communication (IPC) transmits the proposed trajectory to the safety microcontroller, a dedicated real-time processor that executes the deterministic permission path. Between admission and onset, an EtherCAT fieldbus[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.14 Footnotes#^fn2|²]] delivers target setpoints to individual motor drives, where power-inverter gate drivers switch to ramp up stator current. Neural inference occupies one of the eight stages. Under memory bus contention and scheduling delays, the peripheral system stages expand the latency tail substantially more than the neural network execution itself.

Systems engineers sometimes estimate worst-case pipeline latency by summing the 99th-percentile ($\text{p99}$) latencies of individual stages. However, the percentile of a sum does not equal the sum of the percentiles.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.14 Footnotes#^fn3|³]] In embedded system-on-chip (SoC) architectures, shared hardware resources couple stage delays. For example, PCIe DMA bursts during camera readout contend for memory-controller bandwidth with neural accelerators, while CPU cache evictions during kernel message passing induce thread scheduling jitter. The end-to-end distribution of sense-to-torque latency must therefore be profiled empirically on the integrated hardware platform under concurrent production workloads. Furthermore, an empirical percentile remains a statistical measurement; it does not replace the formal worst-case bounds required for hard real-time safety guarantees. Under continuous inference load, each stage in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]] carries its own tail, and the two bottom rows separate the arithmetic column sums from the end-to-end age the system actually delivers.

| Pipeline Stage                     | Physical Mechanism / Contention Source                                              | $\text{p50}$ (ms) | $\text{p99}$ (ms) |    Max (ms)    |
|:-----------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------:|:-----------------:|:--------------:|
| **1–2. Sensor Exposure & Readout** | $60\text{ Hz}$ rolling shutter exposure midpoint                                    |       11.2        |       16.6        |      16.7      |
| **3–4. Host Transport & DMA**      | PCIe DMA transfer into host memory                                                  |        1.4        |        3.2        |      6.8       |
| **5. Vision Backbone & Policy**    | Onboard accelerator inference (memory throttling tail)                              |       18.5        |       24.1        |      38.4      |
| **6. OS Scheduling & IPC**         | Kernel preemption and mailbox hand-off to safety microcontroller                    |        0.6        |        2.1        |      12.3      |
| **7. Fieldbus Transmission**       | Deterministic EtherCAT cyclic packet exchange                                       |         1         |         1         |       2        |
| **8. Actuator Current Rise**       | Gate driver switching and stator flux buildup                                       |        2.3        |         3         |      4.5       |
| **Arithmetic Stage Sum**           | Sum of each column’s stage values; not an end-to-end quantile                       |      **35**       |      **50**       |    **80.7**    |
| **End-to-End Age**                 | System-level profile, capture to onset; max bounded within the illustrative profile |      **35**       |     **46.2**      | $\le$ **80.7** |

Table 1: **Illustrative sense-to-actuation latency decomposition**: Stage and system-level timings show why the arithmetic sum of stage percentiles is not the end-to-end tail. The example’s $\text{p99.9}$ of 68.4 ms exceeds the 60 ms freshness deadline of the mug on the pick-station conveyor, although its median fits.

^tbl-02-latency-breakdown

The 50 ms sum of individual stage percentiles overstates the system-level $\text{p99}$ latency of 46.2 ms because pipeline stages rarely experience worst-case contention simultaneously. For the same reason, the sum of the stage maxima enters the end-to-end row only as an upper bound within the stated illustrative profile and conditions. A measured maximum needs a separately justified allowance or bound before it can support a hard real-time deadline. Coupling pushes the other way. Stages that slow down together, because they contend for the same memory controller or scheduler, fatten the end-to-end tail toward the stage sum, while stages that vary independently usually leave that sum pessimistic, as here. The end-to-end median in the table is set equal to the stage-median sum, a simplification of the illustrative profile that a measured one need not share. Notebook [2.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/02_body/02_body.html#nbk-body-freshness-deadline) evaluates how this illustrative profile behaves against the conveyor’s physical deadline.

**Napkin Math 2.1: The freshness deadline**

**Problem**: The arm grasps a mug riding the pick-station conveyor, acting on camera observations of the handle. Suppose the grasp path had the illustrative age profile of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]]. Does it deliver those observations within the freshness deadline, and how far does the mug move at the median and at the tail of the observation age?

**Variables**:

- Grasp tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}} =$ 15 mm, of which the initial estimate spends ${e_0} =$ 3 mm (illustrative)
- Conveyor speed ${v_{\max}} = {v_{\text{conv}}} =$ 0.20 m/s (illustrative)
- Median backbone time 18.5 ms, median end-to-end age 35 ms, and $\text{p99.9}$ age 68.4 ms (illustrative profile of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]])

**Math**:

1.  **Deadline**: ${e_{\max}} = {r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0} =$ 12 mm, so ${\Delta t_{\text{fresh}}} = {e_{\max}}/{v_{\max}} =$ 12 mm $/$ 0.20 m/s $=$ 60 ms ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^eq-body-freshness-deadline|equation 1]]).
2.  **Backbone alone**: 18.5 ms is less than 60 ms, so the one stage a benchmark times fits with room to spare.
3.  **Median chain**: 0.20 m/s $\times$ 35 ms $=$ 7 mm of mug travel, 5 mm inside the tolerance.
4.  **Tail**: 0.20 m/s $\times$ 68.4 ms $=$ 13.7 mm, 1.7 mm past the tolerance.

**Result**: Inference and the median chain both meet the deadline, but the stated $\text{p99.9}$ age exceeds it. The profile therefore does not establish that 99.9 percent of grasps meet the freshness deadline.

**Systems insight**: The whole path from exposure to torque onset spends the freshness deadline, not the model alone, and its tail, not its median, decides whether the grasp holds. When the machine instead drives toward a stationary obstacle, the same age enters the pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ as its first term and is paid as blind travel ${v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}}$, the first term of the stopping distance that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^sec-body-kinetic-momentum|section 2.3]] sets against the clear distance.

Excessive feedback delay can also degrade closed-loop tracking or destabilize motor control loops,[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.14 Footnotes#^fn4|⁴]] but the freshness calculation requires no frequency-domain model: at the latency tail, the mug has already moved beyond mechanical capture tolerance. System architectures must therefore preserve the observation’s true physical capture timestamp. Hardware timers synchronized through **IEEE 802.1AS**, the generalized Precision Time Protocol (gPTP),[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.14 Footnotes#^fn5|⁵]] or hardware capture strobes associated with the MIPI CSI-2 receiver provide a common physical timebase. The sensor’s exposure and readout characteristics determine how that hardware timestamp maps to the measurement midpoint. Once latched, this capture timestamp accompanies the observation through every subsequent compute kernel.

At the admission boundary, the real-time permission path computes the observation’s total elapsed age, ${t_{\text{age}}} = {t_{\text{current}}} - {t_{\text{transduction}}}$, and adds the bounded remaining admission-to-onset interval for fieldbus dispatch and motor current rise. Capture and clock-synchronization uncertainty must also fit the qualified age budget. The permission path rejects the proposed trajectory update if its projected onset age exceeds the freshness deadline. Rejecting a stale trajectory replacement does not require cutting motor torque immediately; an executing command may continue while its qualified physical margin remains valid. If that margin expires before fresh, valid observations arrive, the permission path triggers a qualified fallback maneuver within the remaining stopping distance ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.13 Source References#^ref-brooks1986robust|Brooks 1986]]).

**Systems Perspective 2.2: The publish timestamp anti-pattern**

A widespread architectural bug in robotics systems is timestamping sensor observations inside user-space publishers, such as a Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2) node transmitting over Data Distribution Service (DDS) middleware. Latching timestamps in application software discards the duration of sensor exposure, rolling-shutter integration, sensor readout, DMA transfers, and kernel queueing. The latency budget therefore loses stages 1 through 4 of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^tbl-02-latency-breakdown|table 1]] (exposure, readout, transport, and DMA), along with any kernel queueing before the publisher runs. Hardware strobes must latch timestamps directly at the physical silicon interface (such as MIPI CSI-2 Start-of-Frame interrupts or Ethernet physical-layer transceiver timestamps synchronized via IEEE 802.1AS) before data enters operating-system queues.

^callout-perspective-2-2-8e55c8fa

Bounding measurement age ensures that control policies act on valid observations rather than historical artifacts, but freshness alone cannot guarantee collision avoidance. Throughout that capture-to-onset interval, the body continues moving under unguided momentum, consuming physical clearance before braking can begin. Halting that physical mass requires dissipating its stored kinetic energy across real distance, an irreversible mechanical process governed by momentum rather than processor clock rates.
