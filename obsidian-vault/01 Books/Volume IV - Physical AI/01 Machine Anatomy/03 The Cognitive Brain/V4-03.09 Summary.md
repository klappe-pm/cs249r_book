# V4-03.09 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03 The Cognitive Brain|V4-03 The Cognitive Brain]]

^sec-brain-summary

Learned representations give the mobile manipulator what hand-authored geometry could not, the ability to recognize and act on objects no engineer enumerated, and the machine pays for that breadth in memory traffic, arithmetic, and time. A fresh evaluation splits into a compute-bound prefill and a bandwidth-bound decode, so weight-only quantization shortens one term and leaves the other, and a large intent model on an embedded processor renews only a few times per second. Supply and validity are therefore separate budgets. A chunk keeps the arm supplied between renewals, but every setpoint in it descends from one observation, so the time the target stays within tolerance, not the length of the chunk, bounds how much of it may run. What the slow proposer hands the fast one decides what the permission path can check, and because a proposal can arrive on time and still be wrong, every proposal must carry its capture time, validity limit, and provenance so that it remains refusable. Six core quantitative relations govern these trade-offs across the chapter, each bounding a distinct stage of proposal generation and expiration ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.09 Summary#^tbl-03-key-equations|table 6]]).

**Key Takeaways: Learned proposers and deliberation limits**

- **Memory traffic and arithmetic both bound fresh inference**: When an evaluation cycle streams nonresident weights from memory, the total weight footprint and sustained memory bandwidth establish a hard latency floor. Fetching the intent model’s 14 GB of FP16 weights requires 98.0 ms. Input token prefill operates in the compute-bound regime, whereas per-token autoregressive decoding is bandwidth-bound. Consequently, even with INT4 weights, a single evaluation consumes approximately 336 ms. This supports the selected intent renewal rate of 2 Hz, which is far too slow to drive real-time actuator chunk renewal directly.
- **Chunking supplies motion without refreshing evidence**: Generating a chunk of 16 setpoints amortizes a single backbone evaluation across 400 ms of candidate motion. Although this amortizes memory bus traffic across waypoints, the robot executes that motion without incorporating new visual evidence until the subsequent policy evaluation completes.
- **Supply and validity bound different horizons**: In the conveyor pick task, actuator continuity requires a minimum supply horizon of 4 setpoints to bridge neural renewal intervals, whereas target drift constrains the sensory evidence horizon to approximately 219 ms. A deeper queue of setpoints cannot extend that validity. A setpoint stays eligible only while the evidence behind it holds and the active chunk lease runs, and when renewal stops, the chunk lease bounds how far the arm continues.
- **Retained context competes with current sensing**: Retaining the hypothetical five-second history at the stated sampling rate occupies 21.0 GB of key-value cache memory. Because storing and rereading past attention state directly contends for DRAM bandwidth with incoming sensory observations and proposal generation, temporal context retention must be explicitly managed within the system timing and memory budget.
- **Proposer interfaces dictate runtime verifiability**: An opaque latent embedding passed from a high-level intent model to a low-level policy cannot be cross-referenced with measured workspace geometry or aged against target drift. Conversely, an explicit Cartesian target pose accompanied by an observation timestamp provides a checkable contract. This auditable geometric interface allows the machine’s permission path to enforce its intent lease alongside the high-rate chunk lease.
- **Reported confidence requires independent physical validation**: A softmax probability or normalized confidence score emitted by a learned model cannot guarantee physical execution success under real-world distribution shift. While calibration validates score reliability across known operating regimes, runtime safety demands independent geometric constraints, kinematic limits, and physical contact monitoring.
- **Proposals must remain rejectable**: Trajectory proposals must bundle explicit observation timestamps, kinematic assumptions, and validity envelopes alongside their raw setpoints. The Nervous System combines these proposer constraints with real-time body state measurements to validate trajectory headers, manage handoffs between successive chunks, and enforce fail-safe execution windows.

^callout-takeaways--3-2-ec6e1013

The conveyor pick separates two kinds of inadequacy that call for different fixes. A proposer that cannot renew before its evidence expires has design remedies: fewer input tokens, a smaller or differently quantized model, more bandwidth, or a slower target. A proposer that renews in time and still proposes a wrong grasp is not repaired by any of them, which is why its output stays a proposal however fast it arrives. Both cases end at the first law (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/V4 The Machine Anatomy#^pri-vol4-irreversibility|1]]). Motion executed on evidence that has gone stale, or toward an affordance that does not exist, delivers energy that cannot be recalled. The time a proposal may keep driving the machine therefore has to be set below the proposer, from the body’s stopping budget, rather than by the proposer’s own schedule.
