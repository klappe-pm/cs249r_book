# V4-04.07 Actuation Authority

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System|V4-04 The Nervous System]]

^sec-nervous-actuation-authority

A shared mailbox cannot protect a mobile manipulator if the host processor retains a diagnostic route, an auxiliary bus master, or a JTAG debug port that writes directly to a drive. The silicon partition established in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.03 The Independence Requirement and the Two-Processor Hypothesis#^sec-nervous-silicon-partitioning|section 4.2]] therefore requires exclusive physical control over every electrical path capable of energizing the motor windings. Actuation authority is this exclusive physical ability to modify drive states, encompassing PWM configuration registers, current references, gate-driver outputs, and bridge-enable lines. Under this architecture, the host publishes candidate proposals ${p}_t$ and the safety microcontroller’s permission path alone turns them into admitted commands ${u}_t$. The difference between holding that authority and merely appearing to is a question of wiring ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.07 Actuation Authority#^fig-04-privilege-enforcer-registers|figure 7]]). When the permission path is the sole writer of the drive registers and can trip a drive inhibit, every motion passes its checks. When the host can also write those registers, one unchecked write reaches the motors, and proposer and drives share a single failure domain.

(b) When the application processor can write the drive registers directly, an unchecked write reaches the motors, and proposer and drives share a single failure domain.

↪ How exclusive authority over each actuator channel passes between the learned policy, an operator, and the fallback is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.04 Authority Transitions#^sec-intervention-discrete-states|Authority states and single-writer actuator ownership]]**.

The microcontroller continuously evaluates physical safety invariants: verifying that commanded joint positions lie within certified reach envelopes, that joint velocities stay within qualified limits, and that projected decelerations remain safely within dynamic stopping clearance (${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$). When a proposal satisfies all invariants, the microcontroller admits the command and interpolates waypoints into cyclic position setpoints for each bus cycle, which the drives track through their internal velocity and current loops. When a candidate proposal violates an invariant or expires, the enforcer refuses it ($\bot$). A refusal does not automatically revoke a previously admitted, actively executing trajectory chunk. Instead, the active chunk continues running as long as its state, sensor evidence, and chunk lease remain valid. When those conditions lapse or fail, the enforcer terminates execution authority and transitions the system into a controlled deceleration or hold. In parallel, a separate hardware trip zone stands ready to cut motor drive torque upon catastrophic electrical faults, though de-energizing an inverter does not by itself brake moving mass.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.15 Footnotes#^fn12|¹²]]

↪ The refusal authority and the state invariants it checks are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.07 Refusing Proposals and Enforcing State Invariants#^sec-enforcement-authority-refuse|Refusing Proposals and Enforcing State Invariants]]**.

At the causal boundary the root cause of an aberrant proposal does not matter. A remote memory exploit in a host operating system daemon, an adversarial optical perturbation that induces a vision model hallucination, and an arithmetic overflow in an acceleration tensor engine can all produce invalid proposals for the same boundary filter to reject. A proposal that passes the physical checks may still pursue the wrong task, so admission does not establish semantic correctness. Because the enforcer evaluates physical invariant bounds against direct sensor feedback from optical encoders and current shunts rather than inspecting internal neural network weights, it requires no internal model of why the proposer failed. Because the host lacks bus-master privileges to actuator registers, a compromised host has no bypass around this check. Complete protection against a rogue host nonetheless requires four physical foundations: independent sensing, an accurate kinematic and dynamic limit model, sufficient actuator capability, and a physically feasible fallback trajectory.

↩︎ The causal boundary, where a command becomes current in a winding and mass begins to move, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-tensors-to-silicon|From a proposed action to motion]]**.

Controlled stopping and torque removal impose different engineering obligations. Powered braking actively uses the drive inverters to decelerate the plant to zero velocity before disabling torque. Immediate torque removal, by contrast, de-energizes the motor windings instantly, which can leave a wheeled base coasting uncontrollably or allow a vertically loaded manipulator arm to collapse under gravity. Maintaining a held position requires either continuous motor torque or a certified mechanical restraint. At the actuation authority interface, the systems designer must explicitly designate the physical fallback response and reserve both the execution time and thermal energy budget that it demands.

A refusal that leaves no admissible active continuation carries an unavoidable physical cost. When the enforcer rejects an invalid proposal and commands dynamic braking on a fast-moving joint, the physical braking path must supply sufficient torque, clearance, and thermal dissipation capacity to arrest the moving mass. Over an arrest interval spanning several hundred milliseconds, the motor winding resistance, dynamic brake chopper resistor, or friction brake lining must absorb the joint’s accumulated kinetic energy within the thermal limits detailed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.06 Thermal Duty Cycles#^sec-body-thermal-duty-cycles|Thermal Duty Cycles]]**. This dissipation must occur without degrading winding insulation or permitting the manipulator to breach its physical clearance boundary.

↪ The stopping-distance model this braking path must satisfy is developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-stopping|Kinematic Stopping Envelopes and Information Age Lag]]**.

If the safety microcontroller itself hangs or suffers a clock failure, it cannot execute a software-directed stop. A hardwired, out-of-band interlock must therefore supervise the system and enforce a qualified fallback state. Safe Torque Off (STO) accomplishes this by disabling gate-driver optocouplers or charge pumps through hardware logic, preventing the inverter from generating rotating magnetic fields.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.15 Footnotes#^fn13|¹³]] STO does not isolate the high-voltage DC bus from the mains, nor does it guarantee that momentum will dissipate within the stopping clearance allocated for powered deceleration. Complete electrical isolation requires an upstream safety contactor, and containing gravitational drop or mechanical drift requires a certified electromechanical brake. A dual-channel interlock circuit (the relay network of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.07 Actuation Authority#^fig-real-estop|figure 8]]) decouples torque inhibition entirely from the host software stack. Safety qualification must still verify that the system remains resilient against common-cause failures in power rails, clock crystals, and harness wiring.

In [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.07 Actuation Authority#^fig-real-estop|figure 8]], dual positive-break E-stop contacts (${S}_1$, ${S}_2$) drive independent force-guided relays (${K}_1$, ${K}_2$), which route dual STO enable signals into the motor drive. Series-wired normally closed external device monitoring (EDM) contacts prevent system restart if either relay fails to open. At the bottom, a dedicated brake supply drops a spring-applied mechanical brake directly onto the motor shaft upon power loss, enforcing torque removal (${\tau_{\text{motor}}} = 0$) and holding restraint without software or processor intervention. However, engineers must not confuse a static holding brake with a dynamic stopping brake. Under normal operational sequences, the drive decelerates the motor to zero velocity electrically before engaging the spring brake to hold position. Dropping a holding brake onto a spinning shaft during a high-speed emergency stop subjects the brake pads and hub to severe friction heating and mechanical shock, requiring independent thermal rating and stopping-distance validation. The stop-category selections and timing sequences depend directly on these electromechanical limits.

**Systems Perspective 4.1: Hardware authority invariant**

If an unverified learned policy has direct memory-mapped write access to actuator gate-driver registers, the machine has no mediated proposal path, and its next out-of-distribution input reaches the motors unchecked.

Write privilege is a physical reality dictated by memory mapping, bus arbitration, and physical trace layout, never a configurable software flag. The host processor holds no write privilege to drive registers. While hardware partitioning determines which processor may issue physical commands to an actuator, the engineer must still decide what physical quantity those admitted commands specify, because each choice leaves a different set of failure modes for the permission path to catch.

### What a Proposal May Command

^sec-nervous-command-interface

A digital motor drive accepts setpoints across a nested cascade of control loops: position, velocity, and winding current, with current running innermost at the highest bandwidth. Sending raw current targets directly to the drive bypasses its position and velocity loops and leaves the permission path to predict how gravity, inertia, and external contact will move the joint. Sending pure position targets lets the drive bound its own speed and current, at the cost of ramping winding current against an unmodeled obstacle until the inverter saturates or the mechanism breaks. The level at which a proposal enters this cascade therefore decides which physical states the drive hardware regulates on its own and which quantities the permission path must verify before admitting the command. Four command interfaces dominate physical AI systems, each placing a different prediction and checking burden on the enforcer.

A *position target* ${q_{\text{des}}}$ delegates the entire trajectory tracking problem to the drive’s internal cascaded loops. Because the drive firmware enforces its own configured speed and current limits, the enforcer’s verification burden remains relatively compact. It validates that the target lies within the geometric reach envelope, bounds the discrete step between consecutive targets (which position gains convert directly into velocity and torque demands), and ensures adequate dynamic stopping clearance. However, a pure position target cannot modulate interaction force. When encountering an unmodeled rigid obstacle, a stiff proportional-integral position loop interprets physical contact as a tracking error to be eliminated, ramping winding current until the inverter saturates or the mechanical structure breaks. The impedance-control analysis in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.05 Actuator Transmission Limits#^sec-body-transmission-compliance|Transmission compliance, contact impedance, and tooth fracture]]** quantified this exact failure mode on a cage-door latch.

A *velocity target* ${\dot{q}_{\text{des}}}$ shifts kinematic integration to the host proposer, bypassing the drive’s position loop. Because the proposal omits an explicit geometric endpoint, the enforcer cannot simply validate a target coordinate against static workspace limits. Instead, the enforcer forward-integrates the commanded velocity over the duration of the active chunk lease to predict the joint’s worst-case state at lease expiration. It then checks both the reach envelope and the dynamic stopping distance against this forward projection. As with pure position control, contact force remains unregulated, and unmodeled collisions will produce maximum drive torque.

A *torque target* ${\tau_{\text{des}}}$, delivered as a direct current reference, bypasses both outer loops. The drive regulates only electromagnetic winding current. Consequently, all kinematic state evolution, including velocity, position, and braking distance, becomes an emergent consequence of plant dynamics, gravity, and external contact forces rather than an enforced setpoint. The enforcer must bound the resulting state evolution and preserve a feasible fallback, using a plant model or effort-space constraints, while constraining torque slew rates and tracking the cumulative mechanical energy injected into the system. Torque control offers maximum responsiveness for dynamic locomotion and manipulation, but it imposes the heaviest computational burden on the permission path while providing the least explicit intent.

An *impedance target* specifies a virtual equilibrium pose alongside stiffness gains ${K_p}$ and damping gains ${K_d}$, so the actuator behaves as a virtual spring-damper ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-hogan1985impedance|Hogan 1985]]), the same model introduced in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.05 Actuator Transmission Limits#^sec-body-transmission-compliance|Transmission compliance, contact impedance, and tooth fracture]]**:

$$
{F} = {K_p} ({x_{\text{des}}} - {x}) + {K_d} ({\dot{x}_{\text{des}}} - {\dot{x}})
$$

 Its dynamic dual, an *admittance target*, measures external contact force via load cells and commands motion in response. Both formulations explicitly regulate the dynamic relationship between motion and force, a coupling that position, velocity, and torque interfaces leave unconstrained. This capability makes impedance and admittance formulations standard in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The enforcer’s verification role expands accordingly: it must evaluate both the equilibrium setpoint and the commanded stiffness gains. Under quasi-static contact, the maximum contact force is bounded by the product of the commanded stiffness and the largest displacement the environment can impose. Consequently, a stiffness parameter that exceeds the structural limits of the workpiece or fixture is just as inadmissible as a trajectory crossing a workspace wall. However, steady-state gains do not govern transient impact forces, which depend heavily on approach velocity, rotor inertia, structural compliance, and control-loop latency. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|Intentional contact and in-contact energy barriers]]** establishes the energy and contact-force barriers needed to bound these transient impact phases.

On the reference mobile manipulator, the enforcer admits position waypoints, interpolating them into fine setpoints for each bus cycle while the motor drives close their own internal velocity and current loops. When the manipulator engages the sprung latch of an equipment cage door, the host switches to an impedance command, introducing stiffness and damping parameters that the real-time tick must validate before transmission. No command interface eliminates the verification burden of the permission path: shifting the command interface inward, from position setpoints toward raw current references, strips away explicit geometric intent and forces the safety layer to predict complex electrodynamic behavior. Write privilege and command filtering protect the physical plant only if the enforcer also maintains physical independence from the host whose proposals it evaluates. If the safety microcontroller and the host application processor share a power rail, a common system clock, or a unified memory bus, a failure in the host can brown out the microcontroller, corrupt its timing, or deadlock the bus. Any of these silences the safety layer without issuing a single illegal drive command.
