# V4-04.09 Invariant Checking

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System|V4-04 The Nervous System]]

^sec-nervous-invariant-checking

A proposer fast enough to keep pace with the permission tick invites a shortcut. Learned locomotion controllers already emit joint targets within an order of magnitude of a millisecond, and a designer may be tempted to let such a policy write the motor registers directly. Rate does not confer authority. A fast network emitting joint targets or torques carries the same statistical uncertainty as a large model generating waypoints a few times a second. Even when policy and enforcer share one period, the network’s output remains a proposal that must pass the proposal boundary before it changes current in a winding. What the permission path can still guarantee when a learned policy commands at control rate, and what it gives up, is examined in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.09 Admission, Cyclic Checking, and Monitor Independence#^sec-enforcement-thesis-bends|Where the thesis bends]]**.

The interface must reject an inadmissible candidate without waiting for the producer, and recheck the active command against current physical state. Position and velocity limits (${\mathbf{q}} \in [{\mathbf{q}_{\min}}, {\mathbf{q}_{\max}}]$ and $|{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}| \le {\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\max}}$) are necessary but do not by themselves establish a feasible stop. A torque-slew constraint, $|{\dot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}| = |({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_k - {\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{k-1})/{\Delta} {t}| \le {\dot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\max}}$, can limit command changes, but a command that meets the slew limit must still satisfy the other physical limits. Contact may additionally require accounting for exchanged energy, $\int {\boldsymbol{\tau}}^\top {\dot{\mathbf{q}}}\,d{t}$.

↪ The passivity framework that accounts for energy exchanged in contact is developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-passivity|Passivity and Energy-Bounded Interaction]]**.

Choosing and composing those filters is the work of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement#^sec-enforcement|Safety Enforcement]]**, which constructs the admissibility calculation and selects a feasible continuation, hold, or stop. What the Nervous System fixes is the execution contract those filters must run under, whatever they compute. On the running machine the contract has numbers. The recheck of the active command must finish inside the tick’s 400 μs deadline under the response bound of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^eq-nervous-response-budget|equation 3]]. It must read measured wheel and joint encoder state rather than commanded setpoints (the fallacy of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.11 Fallacies and Pitfalls#^sec-nervous-fallacies-pitfalls|section 4.10]] shows what the commanded setpoints miss). It must also leave the safety microcontroller the only writer of the drive registers. Neither fast inference nor a fast rejection routine establishes that contract on its own.

The same reasoning covers the one proposer that is not learned. A remote operator is a third proposer. Its commands pass the same permission path, and network delay spends the same pre-brake budget. Neither speed nor a human origin gives a proposal authority; only passing the permission path’s checks does, and each check spends part of a budget that the next section lays out handoff by handoff.

↪ How an operator’s reaction and link delays are charged against the stopping budget is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-reaction-drift|Human reaction time and travel before braking]]**.

**Checkpoint 4.2: Hardware isolation and invariant checking**

Before turning to the handoffs between proposer and actuator, verify your understanding of the exchange, hardware isolation, and pre-actuation invariant defenses:

**Nonblocking exchange**: Can you explain how the seqlock reader detects a torn read in one bounded attempt, and why a writer stopped mid-write cannot extend the active spline’s state, evidence, or lease validity?

**Independence as a hypothesis**: Can you state the independence requirement the permission path must meet, and explain why the two-processor split satisfies it only once loaded tests on the machine’s silicon support the claim?

**Chunk lease cost**: Can you explain why the chunk lease must be sized from stopping distance and clearance rather than chosen as a software timeout, why a heartbeat never renews it, and how much clear distance lengthening it from 60 ms to 100 ms would spend at the 1.5 m/s drive limit?

**Command interface**: Can you state what the permission path must check or predict for a position, a velocity, a torque, and an impedance target, and why a velocity target leaves it to predict motion over the whole lease?

**Rate matching and authority**: Can you explain why a policy that runs at the plant’s own rate still produces only proposals, and what timing, state, and fallback obligations remain?
