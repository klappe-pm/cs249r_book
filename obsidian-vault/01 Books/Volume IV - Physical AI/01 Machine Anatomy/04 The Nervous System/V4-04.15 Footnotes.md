# V4-04.15 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System|V4-04 The Nervous System]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Field-Oriented Control (FOC)**: The drive rotates the three measured phase currents into a frame that turns with the rotor’s magnetic field, where torque and flux become two slowly varying currents that ordinary proportional-integral loops can hold. The rotation needs a fresh rotor angle in every carrier period, so the loop cannot wait on anything slower than its own sensors. That is why the current loop runs inside the drive, beside the encoder and the current shunts, and why no proposer, however fast, ever sits inside it.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Inference Latency Distribution**: The median and $\text{p99}$ alone do not fix the exceedance probability at a given deadline, which must be measured or modeled separately. Tail-model assumptions and exceedance estimates require representative workload data and an account of dependence and uncertainty; **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Machine Learning Background#^sec-appendix-ml-evt|Extreme Value Theory and Tail Risk]]** develops these conditions.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Worst-Case Execution Time (WCET)**: A static WCET is a bound proved over every path through the binary and every state the processor’s caches and pipeline can be in; analyzers compute it by abstract interpretation of the code against a model of that hardware ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-wilhelm2008worst|Wilhelm et al. 2008]]). Caches, branch predictors, and shared buses make that model either pessimistic or intractable, so a timed path earns a tight bound by avoiding them. The bounded sources listed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^sec-nervous-rta-variance|section 4.5.1]] therefore favor tightly coupled memory and fixed pipelines, giving up average speed for a bound that analysis can prove.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Phase Margin**: Bode ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-bode1945network|1945]]) introduced gain and phase margins to measure how far a feedback amplifier sits from instability, turning the stability criterion of Nyquist ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-nyquist1932regeneration|1932]]) into a practical design margin. In cyber-physical loops, delay erodes the modeled phase reserve and can destabilize the loop under its nominal assumptions. Establishing actual damping and stability requires the complete sampled plant, compensation, and timing response. Because a pure transport or compute delay ${T_d}$ subtracts ${\omega_c} {T_d}$ radians of phase regardless of cause, bus cycles, compute stages, and zero-order holds all draw from the same finite phase budget.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Dead-Time Delay**: The interval in which both switches of a half-bridge leg are off, so the leg never shorts the DC bus, is the inverter dead time introduced in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.05 Actuator Transmission Limits#^sec-body-electrical-time-constants|Electrical time constants, back-EMF, and current clamping]]**. Dedicated pulse-width modulation timers insert it in hardware ($100\text{--}500\text{ ns}$) on every transition, and drive firmware applies online volt-second compensation for the voltage error it introduces.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Versioned Read**: Lamport ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-lamport1977concurrent|1977]]) showed that a reader can detect a concurrent, overlapping write without acquiring mutual exclusion locks. By incrementing a version counter before and after updating payload data, and having the reader sample the counter in reverse order, any mismatch reliably flags a torn read.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Wait-Free**: Herlihy ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-herlihy1991waitfree|1991]]) defined an implementation as wait-free when every operation finishes in a bounded number of its own steps, whatever the other processes do. That is stronger than lock-free progress, which guarantees only that some operation completes while a particular one may retry indefinitely. The one-attempt seqlock read is bounded but can come back empty-handed; the slot mailbox is bounded and always returns a complete snapshot, and the extra slot is the price of that second property.

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Filter Group Delay**: Digital smoothing filters commonly applied in machine learning pipelines introduce significant phase lag when placed inside feedback control loops. An ${N}$-tap moving-average finite impulse response (FIR) filter imposes an average group delay of $({N}-1)/2$ sample intervals. Consequently, a 9-tap moving average operating at the 1 ms permission tick delays feedback signals by 4 ms. At the illustrative 20 Hz crossover frequency from [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.04 Multi-Rate Cadences#^sec-nervous-time-constants-phase-lag|section 4.3.1]], this delay burns 28.8$^\circ$ of phase, 5.8× the design allowance. In real-time control paths, decimation and filter topologies must be selected for minimal phase lag rather than cosmetic smoothing.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Rate-Monotonic Scheduling**: Liu and Layland ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-liu1973scheduling|1973]]) established that static-priority rate-monotonic scheduling guarantees periodic task schedulability when total processor utilization satisfies ${U} \le {n}(2^{1/{n}} - 1)$, converging to $\ln(2) \approx 0.693$ for large ${n}$. The utilization bound is sufficient, not necessary, so a task set that exceeds it may still meet every deadline; with shared-resource blocking and deadlines shorter than periods, only the exact response-time test of **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-timers|Real-Time Timers, Watchdogs, and Schedulability]]** decides.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Distributed Clock**: EtherCAT Distributed Clocks (DC) synchronize distributed drive nodes to a reference clock by measuring propagation delays around the ring. Receive-time latches at each node let the coordinator discipline every node’s timer, so the hardware `SYNC0` events that start the drives’ update cycles occur in close alignment. Clock alignment alone does not bound the complete control cycle, which must also account for controller response time and PWM gate update latency.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Time-Sensitive Networking**: IEEE 802.1Qbv Time-Sensitive Networking provides bounded Ethernet delivery by configuring time-aware traffic shapers across network switches. High-priority control frames travel inside dedicated, pre-scheduled transmission windows, while asynchronous high-bandwidth traffic (such as uncompressed camera streams) is held back at the switch gates. This scheduled time-division multiplexing lets camera streams and microsecond servo commands share a physical link without packet collisions or switch-queue buildup.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Hardware Trip Zone**: Microcontroller hardware trip zones couple analog overcurrent and overvoltage comparators directly to timer output logic. Upon fault detection, configured trip logic may force gate signals high-impedance or activate a validated braking mode. These have different mechanical consequences: torque removal can leave the plant coasting, while dynamic braking requires a rated energy path.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Safe Torque Off (STO)**: An STO implementation removes torque-producing drive signals through a hardware interlock. An IEC 61800-5-2 integrity rating depends on the actual devices, diagnostic coverage, wiring, and validation, not on the circuit pattern alone ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-iec61800part52|International Electrotechnical Commission 2016]]).

^fn13

<sup>14</sup> **Freedom from Interference (FFI)**: ISO 26262 defines FFI in its vocabulary, Part 1, as the absence of cascading failures between two or more elements that could lead to the violation of a safety requirement ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-iso26262|International Organization for Standardization 2018]]). Part 6 lists the software interference it covers (timing and execution, memory, and exchange of information), whereas shared power, clocks, and heat are common-cause and cascading faults that Part 9 treats in its dependent-failure analysis and that IEC 61508 addresses as independence between elements ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-iec61508-2-2010|International Electrotechnical Commission 2010b]]). A neural policy sharing silicon with the permission path therefore needs both arguments, FFI for its software and a dependent-failure analysis for the resources underneath.

^fn14
