# V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04 The Nervous System|V4-04 The Nervous System]]

^sec-nervous-lease-sizing

A learned policy runtime can stall for many reasons: a page fault, thermal throttling on the vision accelerator, memory fragmentation, or an uncaught exception in perception preprocessing. The robot does not stop when its software does. A loaded base keeps its momentum (${p} = {m} {v}$) and covers 1.5 mm every millisecond at the drive limit, while the downstream controller keeps converting the remaining waypoints of the last admitted chunk into setpoints. To keep an unrenewed chunk from driving the machine into an obstacle, the permission path must end that chunk’s authority on its own hardware clock, whether or not the proposer ever reports its failure. How long it should wait before doing so is a question about distance, not software.

**Definition 4.2: Chunk lease**

***Chunk lease*** is the interval, counted on the permission path’s own clock from a chunk’s admission, during which that admitted chunk may keep driving the actuators without a successful replacement; when it expires, the permission path ends the chunk’s authority and commands the state-matched fallback, a stop profile chosen from the machine’s measured speed and pose.

1.  **Significance**: The lease enters the pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ of the stopping budget, so each millisecond of it costs ${v}$ of clear distance, 1.5 mm at the running machine’s drive limit. The machine’s 60 ms lease, with the tick and bus cycle that detect and deliver its expiry, adds 93 mm to the stopping budget (notebook [4.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/04_nervous/04_nervous.html#nbk-nervous-watchdog-stopping-lease)).
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike a heartbeat timeout, which shows only that the host is alive, a lease is renewed only by a freshly admitted proposal. Its length is derived from the plant rather than configured: above the proposer’s renewal floor and below the stopping budget’s ceiling. Unlike an evidence deadline, it bounds how long one motion segment may execute, not how long the observation behind it stays valid.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Lengthening the lease to ride out proposer hiccups. A longer lease buys continuity with clearance, and a lease that reaches the pre-brake ceiling commits the machine to motion it can no longer stop in time.

In classical distributed systems, a lease grants bounded access to a shared digital resource, such as a distributed lock or cache entry, expiring automatically if the leaseholder crashes ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-gray1989leases|Gray and Cheriton 1989]]). In physical AI, the leased resource is the kinetic authority to move mass through space. Because physical motion cannot be undone, the duration of a lease must be formally derived from the physical stopping budget. The distance covered during the lease, the detection latency, the fieldbus transit, and the brake onset, together with the braking distance itself, must fit within the available clearance ${D_{\text{clear}}}$ after the localization uncertainty and protective margin are subtracted. Rearranging the stopping-distance relation of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^eq-body-stopping-distance|equation 2.2]]**, ${v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}} + {v}^2/2{a_{\text{brake}}} + {\delta_{\text{loc}}} + {\delta_{\text{margin}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$, and dividing the remaining pre-braking distance by vehicle velocity yields the **pre-brake ceiling**, representing the maximum tolerable delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ before physical braking deceleration must commence:

$$
{\tau_{\text{delay}}} \le \frac{{D_{\text{clear}}} - {\delta_{\text{loc}}} - {\delta_{\text{margin}}} - {v}^2/2{a_{\text{brake}}}}{{v}} \tag{2}
$$

 where ${v}$ represents operating velocity, ${a_{\text{brake}}}$ denotes verified braking deceleration, and ${\delta_{\text{loc}}}$ and ${\delta_{\text{margin}}}$ represent the localization uncertainty and safety clearance of that formulation. As operating speed ${v}$ decreases, the required braking distance shrinks quadratically while the velocity divisor decreases linearly, causing the pre-brake ceiling to expand toward infinity at rest. At maximum operating speed, however, every millisecond allocated to the lease path consumes exactly ${v}$ units of stopping clearance.

^eq-nervous-prebrake

Panel (a) of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.04 Multi-Rate Cadences#^fig-04-multirate-cadence-buffer|figure 4]] draws the lease on the running machine’s own clocks. The chunk policy renews twice and then stalls. The tick keeps executing the last admitted chunk only until the lease counted from that admission lapses, and the next tick, one bus cycle, and the brake onset then bring the fallback stop. Notebook [4.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/04_nervous/04_nervous.html#nbk-nervous-watchdog-stopping-lease) evaluates [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^eq-nervous-prebrake|equation 2]] for the mobile manipulator at its drive limit and prices that sequence inside it.

**Napkin Math 4.1: The price of a lease**

**Problem**: In the illustrative aisle scenario, the warehouse mobile manipulator cruises at its drive limit toward a person who has stepped out from a rack end and stopped. The permission path admits one last chunk and then receives no admissible replacement. How much time remains before braking must begin, and how much of it, and of the clear distance, does the chunk lease spend?

**Variables**:

- Speed ${v} =$ 1.5 m/s, the drive limit (illustrative); braking deceleration ${a_{\text{brake}}} =$ 2 m/s², loaded on a dry floor (illustrative).
- Clear distance ${D_{\text{clear}}} =$ 1.10 m (illustrative), less localization error ${\delta_{\text{loc}}} =$ 50 mm (illustrative) and protective clearance ${\delta_{\text{margin}}} =$ 100 mm (chosen).
- Brake onset ${T_{\text{act}}} =$ 20 ms (illustrative); permission tick ${T_{\text{tick}}} =$ 1 ms (chosen); bus cycle ${T_{\text{bus}}} =$ 1 ms (illustrative).
- Chunk lease ${T_{\text{lease}}} =$ 60 ms; renewal period ${T_{\text{chunk}}} =$ 50 ms with a 5 ms variation allowance ${T_{\text{jitter}}}$ (all chosen).

**Math**: The braking term and the ceiling of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^eq-nervous-prebrake|equation 2]]:

$$
\frac{v^2}{2a_{\text{brake}}} = \frac{(1.5\ \text{m/s})^2}{2 \times 2\ \text{m/s}^2} = 562.5\ \text{mm}
$$

$$
\tau_{\text{delay}} \le \frac{1100 - 150 - 562.5\ \text{mm}}{1.5\ \text{m/s}} = 258.3\ \text{ms}
$$

 The stopping-budget terms that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^sec-body-kinetic-momentum|Kinetic Momentum]]** already charged (braking, the localization and clearance overheads, and onset travel ${v}\,{T_{\text{act}}} =$ 30 mm) hold 742.5 mm of the clear distance. The lease path, lease plus the tick that notices its expiry plus the bus cycle that delivers the stop, is the next term:

$$
v\,(T_{\text{lease}} + T_{\text{tick}} + T_{\text{bus}}) = 1.5\ \text{m/s} \times (60 + 1 + 1)\ \text{ms} = 93\ \text{mm}
$$

$$
742.5 + 93 = 835.5\ \text{mm}, \quad 1100 - 835.5 = 264.5\ \text{mm}
$$

 In time, from the last admission to brake onset, and against the ceiling:

$$
62 + 20 = 82\ \text{ms}, \quad 258.3 - 82 = 176.3\ \text{ms}
$$

 Continuous tracking also sets a floor under the lease:

$$
T_{\text{chunk}} + T_{\text{jitter}} = 50 + 5 = 55\ \text{ms} < T_{\text{lease}} = 60\ \text{ms}
$$

**Systems insight**: A chunk lease is a term in the stopping budget, not a timeout chosen for convenience. Each millisecond added to it costs 1.5 mm of clear distance at the drive limit, distance that the observation age and the stop profile still need, so the lease sits just above its renewal floor rather than anywhere under the ceiling.

The renewal floor is the lease duration needed to cover the budgeted inter-arrival interval between successful admissions without starving trajectory execution. Because this floor cannot rely solely on the model’s 99th-percentile ($\text{p99}$) inference latency, it must accommodate bus transit, validation overhead, and mailbox synchronization jitter. If a renewal fails to arrive before the lease expires, the system terminates trajectory tracking rather than granting an unearned extension. The 176.3 ms remaining under the pre-brake ceiling is not spare margin either. It must still absorb the observation’s age at admission, which [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^sec-nervous-coupled-budget|section 4.9.1]] charges against it, and the jerk limits of the braking profile.

Every charge in this budget is an interval between the four boundary events introduced in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^sec-body-latency-pipeline|The end-to-end sense-to-actuation latency pipeline]]**. *Capture* dates an observation at its exposure midpoint, *dispatch* hands that evidence to the proposer, *admission* accepts a chunk built on it and starts the chunk’s lease, and *brake onset* marks the first physical braking force at the drives. In the worst-case stall scenario, an obstacle enters the path just after *capture* of the camera frame that produces the last healthy chunk, which therefore travels from *capture* through *dispatch* to *admission* without reflecting the hazard. When the subsequent renewal stalls, the robot continues tracking that last admitted chunk from *admission* until its lease expires and *brake onset* engages, chaining all three intervals end to end. Between these four events lie three intervals, each derived in exactly one chapter ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^tbl-04-event-timing|table 1]]). Only row 3, the stalled-proposer response, is derived here, from the lease notebook. Rows 1 and 2 enter Part I as allocations: the observation age that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-latency-waterfall|The latency waterfall]]** will build stage by stage, and the dispatch-to-admission interval that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.07 Silicon Isolation Decisions#^sec-placement-dispatch-admission|The board-link budget and the dispatch-to-admission interval]]** will charge to the board link and the validator. Later chapters charge time against these events and this table rather than keeping their own stage lists.

### Supervisory Timers and Proposal Wire Contracts

^sec-nervous-supervisory-timers

Granting a fresh lease at admission without charging the time the observation already spent in inference and board-link transit lets the drives keep tracking a trajectory whose sensory evidence is already stale. Waiting on a single timeout while a hung policy thread sits inside a live host kernel lets them keep tracking a trajectory whose producer has stopped updating. Evidence keeps aging from capture onward, and admitting a chunk starts its lease on the microcontroller’s clock without rolling that age back. Neither the dispatch timestamp nor the newly granted lease accounts for the dispatch-to-admission interval, so the evidence-age check must charge row 2 of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^tbl-04-event-timing|table 1]] explicitly. Authority over an admitted chunk then ends at the earliest of four events: its evidence freshness deadline passes, its lease expires without a successful replacement, the host heartbeat times out, or an independent safety reflex such as the protective scanner path detects an obstacle. None of these waits for the others, and which fires first depends on the scene. At the conveyor, the tracked evidence horizon that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** computed is several times longer than the lease, so there a stalled chunk loses its authority to the lease. The chunk lease bounds how long one admitted motion segment may execute. How long the strategic goal stays meaningful is a separate grant, the intent lease of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|The Intent Lease Specification and Contract]]**, and neither is the nominal planning horizon.

↩︎ The freshness deadline that bounds how old evidence may be at actuation is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^sec-body-measurement-freshness|Measurement Freshness]]**.

Three independent supervisory timers guard the exchange, and each monitors a distinct failure domain:

1.  **The chunk lease (${T_{\text{lease}}} =$ 60 ms):** Bounds how long an admitted proposal may command actuators. Only a freshly validated, admitted proposal chunk renews the lease. Its duration is derived directly from the physical stopping budget, sitting between the renewal floor and the pre-brake ceiling (notebook [4.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/04_nervous/04_nervous.html#nbk-nervous-watchdog-stopping-lease)).
2.  **The host heartbeat (${f_{\text{heartbeat}}} =$ 100 Hz, timeout 30 ms):** Asserts that the application processor operating system kernel and user-space runtime remain alive. Expiry of this timeout revokes the active proposal without waiting for the chunk lease. Heartbeat pulses **never renew the chunk lease**, because a live process can easily suffer algorithmic livelock or produce corrupted, out-of-distribution setpoints.
3.  **The microcontroller self-watchdog (${T_{\text{self}}} =$ 5 ms):** Monitors whether the permission path completes its required checks within the watchdog interval. The permission loop feeds this hardware watchdog only upon successfully completing all verification checks in a tick; a hung enforcer trips the watchdog even if timer interrupts continue firing ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.11 Fallacies and Pitfalls#^sec-nervous-fallacies-pitfalls|section 4.10]]).

A watchdog refresh confirms only that the specific task servicing the timer is still executing. In *Bookout v. Toyota* (2013), litigation centered on whether a timer-tick interrupt could continue petting a watchdog while application-level control tasks had hung ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-barr2013toyota|Barr 2013]]). Although federal investigations by NASA and NHTSA identified no electronic mechanism capable of causing large unintended accelerations ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-nasa2011toyota|NASA Engineering and Safety Center 2011]]), the dispute highlights a real-time principle. Watchdog refresh points must be coupled to the completion of safety-critical algorithmic cycles rather than independent periodic interrupts.

Real-time safety requires wrapping the proposer’s payload in a transport and authority envelope. The system enforces chunk leases through the **proposal header**, a structured wire contract that enables the permission path to validate candidate trajectories without placing trust in host application software. The header encapsulates a monotonically increasing sequence number and the evidence timestamp of the source sensory frame (including clock conversion error bounds). It also carries the host dispatch timestamp and the requested lease duration (which the permission path caps at the lease it derives, so a proposer can ask for less authority but never more). A cryptographic parent-record digest supports provenance tracking. The remaining fields are the coordinate frame identifier, the payload type enum, and a 32-bit cyclic redundancy check (CRC-32). For the default payload type the payload consists of ${H}$ kinematic waypoints specifying joint coordinates and velocities. The payload-type field is where other command forms announce themselves, so an impedance target ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.07 Actuation Authority#^sec-nervous-command-interface|section 4.6.1]]) carries its stiffness and damping gains beside its equilibrium waypoints. In this layered architecture, the learned policy expresses kinematic intent, the permission enforcer converts admitted waypoints into position setpoints, and the drives regulate joint velocity and motor current. The payload deliberately omits raw torque commands; while direct-torque policies require alternate wire representations and specialized admission checks, their outputs remain untrusted proposals. In all cases, an admitted proposal retains execution authority only until the earlier of its lease expiration and its evidence freshness deadline, regardless of whether cancellation packets arrive across the network.

↩︎ The proposal payload, the candidate motion and the evidence times it carries, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-proposer-information|Information the proposer must supply]]**.

Industrial safety networking adopted the same transport safeguards decades before the advent of learned robotic proposers. Functional-safety fieldbus standards such as IEC 61784-3 establish the **black-channel principle**. Transmission media, network controllers, and intermediate switches are treated as untrusted, fallible channels, which requires safety-critical packets to encapsulate self-contained integrity and timing protections verified exclusively at communication endpoints ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-iec61784-3-2021|International Electrotechnical Commission 2021]]). Fieldbus profiles like PROFIsafe and Safety over EtherCAT (FSoE) exemplify this architecture. FSoE protects frames against corruption using CRCs, against repetition and packet loss using sequence counters, against misrouting using unique connection identifiers, and against transit delay using watchdog timers. Missing or delayed frames force the receiver into a deterministic safe state ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-etg2019fsoe|EtherCAT Technology Group 2019]]). The proposal header mirrors this design field for field: checksums guard integrity, sequence numbers detect drops, payload identifiers ensure proper dispatch, and the chunk lease replaces the watchdog timer. The physical link between host application processor and safety microcontroller operates as an untrusted black channel; end-to-end header checks allow the permission path to enforce its declared physical admission contract without trusting host operating system integrity. Classical real-time theory provides the complementary semantic foundation. Kopetz defines a computing system’s internal representation of a dynamic physical entity as a *real-time image*, which remains *temporally accurate* only during a bounded physical duration following the initial observation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-kopetz2011real|Kopetz 2011]]). The proposal’s evidence deadline represents this temporal accuracy window applied to sensory inputs, while the chunk lease bounds the duration of derived actuation. This architecture adds one thing to these precedents, the grounding of temporal validity. Whereas industrial fieldbuses configure watchdog timeouts empirically and real-time images reflect passive plant dynamics, the chunk lease is derived analytically from the physical stopping budget above and the proposer’s renewal floor below ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^eq-nervous-prebrake|equation 2]], notebook [4.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/04_nervous/04_nervous.html#nbk-nervous-watchdog-stopping-lease)).

↪ **Byte layout:** The proposal header’s and chunk payload’s fields, types, and offsets are given in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-header|Proposal header and chunk payload]]**.

Every record crossing the proposal boundary expresses temporal quantities referenced to the safety microcontroller’s monotonic clock. Upstream producers operating on independent oscillator domains map timestamps using a versioned affine transformation and transmit explicit synchronization error bounds, which the safety consumer adds to all age evaluations. Wall-clock calendar time is restricted to diagnostic logging and software release expiration; it never enters real-time safety gating.

Individual boundary records guard against three orthogonal failure modes through three distinct mechanisms, only two of them cryptographic. A checksum across the header and payload, a CRC rather than a cryptographic function, detects transmission bit flips and memory corruption during transit, leaving origin authentication unaddressed. Similarly, a cryptographic digest of the parent record establishes computational lineage without authenticating the sender. Cryptographic message authentication codes (MACs) appear exclusively where sender identity conveys explicit operational authority: network teleoperation commands, immutable authority audit logs, and release artifacts verified during deployment. Learned model proposals omit cryptographic signatures entirely, because the permission enforcer validates candidates based on physical admissibility and temporal freshness rather than origin. A cryptographic key stored inside an untrusted host environment provides no assurance of kinematic safety.

↪ How a teleoperation command’s authentication tag is verified and priced against the fallback deadline is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.06 Authenticating an Intervention#^sec-intervention-authenticating-intervention|Authenticating an Intervention]]**.

The reader’s possible failures fall into four classes ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^tbl-04-boundary-fallbacks|table 2]]), and none of them lets a failed producer make the permission path wait. The classes keep the exchange from blocking. They do not by themselves contain the plant, because the fallback each one selects still needs bounded timing and a validated stop. Even a valid admitted chunk still has to be stepped from the proposer’s coarse waypoint spacing down to the permission tick’s rate.

### Rate Interpolation and Boundary Fallback Execution

^sec-nervous-rate-interpolation

Holding each admitted waypoint constant for its full 25 ms spacing until the next waypoint arrives injects staircase position steps into the drives rather than a smooth trajectory, delaying reference tracking by half the step spacing on average and exciting high-frequency structural resonances. Bridging the learned policy’s renewal rate (20 Hz) and the real-time permission tick (1 ms) without those disturbances requires guarding against three sampled-data failure modes: reference lag, spectral aliasing, and buffer starvation. Because the zero-order hold’s latency sits entirely on the feedforward reference path outside the feedback loop, it appears as tracking error rather than eroding feedback phase margins ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.04 Multi-Rate Cadences#^sec-nervous-time-constants-phase-lag|section 4.3.1]]). Confusing reference lag with closed-loop phase lag often leads engineers to misdiagnose tracking errors as plant instability. To eliminate discrete steps across chunk boundaries, the permission enforcer constructs a quintic Hermite spline bridge matching boundary position, velocity, and acceleration. These six boundary constraints guarantee ${C}^2$ continuity across chunk transitions, meaning position, velocity, and acceleration all stay continuous. Evaluating this admitted spline at 1 kHz yields continuous position references, smooth velocity feeds, and feedforward acceleration profiles for the underlying joint controllers.

↩︎ Action chunking, which produces the trajectory segments this interpolator densifies, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-action-chunking|Action chunking]]**.

Spectral aliasing occurs when unmodeled high-frequency mechanical vibration or sensor noise, sampled too slowly, reappears as a false low-frequency signal in the slower streams that estimators and the tick consume. The architecture mitigates aliasing through front-end analog filtering followed by multi-rate digital decimation,[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.15 Footnotes#^fn8|⁸]] low-pass filtering a fast sample stream before keeping only one sample in several, within the high-rate microcontroller domain. Classical sampled-data control models assume constant, deterministic sampling periods. Temporal jitter, a sampling interval that varies from cycle to cycle, degrades phase margin more severely than a constant delay of equivalent mean duration ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.14 Source References#^ref-cervin2003control|Cervin et al. 2003]]). Discrete trajectory execution also assumes setpoints replenish continuously before downstream buffers drain. The permission path enforces these invariants via cycle-deterministic hardware timer interrupts and real-time buffer-depth accounting. If the upstream planner stalls and the trajectory buffer reaches its terminal waypoint, the enforcer rejects stale extrapolation and commands a validated local stopping profile.

An overrun costs something different at each of the four tiers:

- **Current loop (20 kHz):** Its jitter allowance is below $10\,{\text{µ}}\text{s}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.10 The Handoffs Matrix#^tbl-04-synthesis-grid|table 3]]). A timing overrun, gate-drive desynchronization, or current spike trips a hardware fault line that disables the inverter’s PWM stages.
- **Permission tick (1 kHz):** A deadline overrun by the enforcer software sends the machine to the local deceleration stop.
- **Chunk policy (20 Hz):** Inference latency is stochastic (40 ms at p99), so some chunks arrive late. A late chunk costs nothing until the active chunk’s lease or evidence deadline runs out, and the enforcer never waits on the proposer’s optimistic estimate of when it will finish. When either deadline passes, the enforcer commands the state-matched fallback.
- **Intent planner (2 Hz):** Its goals expire under a separate *intent lease*, a longer grant of authority over the goal itself rather than over any motion (developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|The Intent Lease Specification and Contract]]**). When it lapses, the system holds station instead of chasing a stale goal.

The lease notebook charged the tick and the bus cycle as fixed terms, 1 ms and 1 ms, as though each were certain to finish on time. That premise is the next thing to establish. It requires a worst-case response bound for every stage between the mailbox and the drives, and no benchmark or faster processor substitutes for one.
