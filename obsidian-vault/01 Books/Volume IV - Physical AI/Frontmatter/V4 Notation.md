# V4 Notation

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]]

^notation

Physical AI draws on three disciplines at once. Machine learning supplies the foundation models, vision-language-action architectures, diffusion policies, and action chunking that propose what a robot should do. Robotics and control supply the mechanics that decide what it can do: articulated dynamics, the Lie group ${SE(3)}$, contact and friction, Lyapunov stability, control barrier functions, and actuator electromechanics. Embedded and real-time systems supply the machinery that decides when it happens, from interconnect and memory contention through zero-order hold, real-time scheduling, leases, hardware watchdogs, and functional safety.

Because each discipline developed its mathematical conventions independently over decades, combining them creates symbol collisions that cyber-physical systems engineering has long lived with. For example, writing $\nabla_{{\theta}} {\pi_\theta}({\mathbf{a}} \mid {\mathbf{q}})$ when ${\theta}$ also denotes joint angles, or writing ${\mathbf{a}}_t$ for an action when ${\mathbf{a}}$ denotes Cartesian acceleration or barrier constraints, confuses spatial mechanics with parameter optimization and control execution authority.

The sections that follow state the cross-discipline disambiguation rules, the governing relationships of embodied systems, and the symbol tables used throughout this book. Every symbol of the series core keeps its foundational meaning: ${T}$ is time, ${\text{BW}}$ bandwidth, ${L_{\text{lat}}}$ latency, ${R_{\text{peak}}}$ peak rate, ${O}$ operations, ${I}$ arithmetic intensity, ${D_{\text{vol}}}$ bytes moved, ${\mathcal{L}}$ loss, ${\eta}$ learning rate, and $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$ asymptotic cost. Serving and memory arithmetic on the robot’s onboard computer uses those symbols, with a subscript naming the instance (${\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}$, ${O_{\text{prefill}}}$).

## Governing Relationships

^sec-notation-governing-laws

Physical intelligence operates subject to thermodynamic, mechanical, kinematic, and communications constraints. The relationships below state those constraints in the symbols this book uses. They are working relationships, not laws: the book’s four bedrock laws are the ones **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.11 The Four Bedrock Laws#^sec-boundary-bedrock-laws|The Four Bedrock Laws]]** states.

### The Sense-To-Actuation Delay Budget

^sec-notation-embodied-iron-law

Delay converts directly into travel before any retarding force begins. The pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ runs from the capture of the frame that first shows a hazard to retarding force, excluding the dispatch-to-admission interval ${\Delta_{\text{da}}}$, which the stopping budget charges against its timing allowance. The stop it leaves, whose travel is ${d_{\text{suffix}}}({v})$, must fit inside the clear distance, and the spare that remains must pay for ${v}\,{\Delta_{\text{da}}}$:

$$
{\tau_{\text{delay}}} = {t_{\text{age}}} + {T_{\text{lease}}} + {T_{\text{tick}}} + {T_{\text{bus}}} + {T_{\text{act}}}
$$

$$
{v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}} + {d_{\text{suffix}}}({v}) + {\delta_{\text{loc}}} + {\delta_{\text{margin}}} + {\epsilon_{\text{track}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}
$$

Inference time is spent on the proposal side of the proposal boundary. It ages the evidence behind a proposal, which the permission path checks against the proposal’s expiry. It is neither a term of ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ nor part of the permission loop’s period ${T_{\text{tick}}}$, because the fallback does not wait for the proposer.

The appendices regroup the same interval for two specific stop paths, and each form defines its own terms. For a stop triggered by lease expiry, ${\tau_{\text{delay}}} = {T_{\text{lease}}} + {t_{\text{detect}}} + {T_{\text{hold}}} + {t_{\text{age}}} + {T_{\text{act}}}$; for a stop triggered by a hazard detector, ${\tau_{\text{delay}}} = {t_{\text{age}}} + {t_{\text{detect}}} + {T_{\text{compute}}} + {T_{\text{bus}}} + {T_{\text{act}}}$.

### Leases

^sec-notation-leases

A lease is authority that lapses unless it is renewed. The book uses six kinds:

| **Lease**                | **Symbol**                         | **Distinction**                                                                                                                                                                      |
|:-------------------------|:-----------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Execution lease**      | remaining ${T_{\text{lease,rem}}}$ | Any authority an admitted command source holds to keep driving the actuators without renewal; it begins at admission, and its expiry invokes the state-matched fallback.             |
| **Chunk lease**          | ${T_{\text{lease}}}$               | The execution lease of the autonomous chunk policy, renewed by each admitted chunk; it enters ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$.                                                               |
| **Manual lease**         | ${T_{\text{lease,man}}}$           | The execution lease of a human operator in manual mode, renewed by authenticated heartbeats; it replaces the chunk lease in ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ while the operator drives.       |
| **Administrative lease** | ${T_{\text{admin}}}$               | The host-silence bound inside an intent lease: how long the application processor may hold a goal without renewing it.                                                               |
| **Intent lease**         | expiry ${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$  | The record that grants a grounded target and effort ceilings, never a path; it expires at the earliest of its evidence horizon, its administrative lease, and its stopping deadline. |
| **Hardware lease**       | (mechanism)                        | The countdown timer on the safety microcontroller that enforces any of these expiries even when the process that issued the authority has hung.                                      |

### Single-Device Execution Time

^sec-notation-conventions-iron-law-ml-systems-ce9d

In a serial model without overlap, one evaluation of a learned proposer on the robot’s onboard accelerator takes a time that splits into three terms: moving bytes through memory, executing operations, and a fixed overhead. The chunk-cadence and proposer-inference relationships below reuse these symbols, with a subscript naming the instance (${\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}$, ${O_{\text{prefill}}}$):

$$
{T} = \underbrace{\frac{{D_{\text{vol}}}}{{\text{BW}}}}_{\text{Memory Time}} + \underbrace{\frac{{O}}{{R_{\text{peak}}} \cdot {\eta_{\text{hw}}}}}_{\text{Compute Time}} + \underbrace{{L_{\text{lat}}}}_{\text{Latency Overhead}}
$$

| **Symbol**               | **Definition**     | **Unit** | **Notes**                                                                                                                                         |
|:-------------------------|:-------------------|:---------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${T}$**                | **Execution Time** | seconds  | Total execution time of one evaluation; bare ${T}$ is always time.                                                                                |
| **${\text{BW}}$**        | **Bandwidth**      | bytes/s  | Sustained bytes per second across a named path; never bare ${B}$, which is batch size ${B}$.                                                      |
| **${D_{\text{vol}}}$**   | **Data Volume**    | bytes    | Bytes moved through memory in one evaluation; never bare ${D}$, which is dataset size.                                                            |
| **${\eta_{\text{hw}}}$** | **Efficiency**     | —        | Fraction of the peak rate an evaluation achieves, $0 \le {\eta_{\text{hw}}} \le 1$; never bare ${\eta}$, which is the learning rate.              |
| **${L_{\text{lat}}}$**   | **Latency**        | seconds  | Fixed overhead per evaluation (kernel launch, transfer setup); the subscript keeps it distinct from the loss ${\mathcal{L}}$.                     |
| **${O}$**                | **Operations**     | FLOPs    | Floating-point operations one evaluation executes; a subscript names the phase (${O_{\text{prefill}}}$). Asymptotic cost is $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$. |
| **${R_{\text{peak}}}$**  | **Peak Rate**      | FLOP/s   | Peak arithmetic rate of the processor at the stated precision; never bare ${P}$, which is parameter count.                                        |

### Action-Chunk Cadence and Proposer Inference

^sec-notation-chunking-cadence

Large neural policies that stream their weights from memory may exceed a kilohertz control period. Action chunking predicts an action trajectory of horizon ${H}$ at a lower cognitive frequency ${f_{\text{policy}}}$, while the chunk’s waypoints are consumed at ${f_{\text{exec}}}$. In the serial model with one weight-streaming pass per chunk and no additional fixed overhead, memory streaming must fit inside the chunk execution window after reserving the compute term ${T_{\text{compute}}}$ of the single-device decomposition:

$$
{t_{\text{stream}}} = \frac{{M_{\text{weights}}}}{{\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}} \le \frac{{H}}{{f_{\text{exec}}}} - {T_{\text{compute}}}
$$

A decoder that streams its nonresident weights once per emitted token spends, for ${S_{\text{out}}}$ emitted tokens, the inference time

$$
{t_{\text{infer}}} \approx \max\!\left(\frac{{O_{\text{prefill}}}}{{\eta_{\text{hw}}}\,{R_{\text{peak}}}},\ \frac{{M_{\text{weights}}}}{{\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}}\right) + ({S_{\text{out}}} - 1)\,\frac{{M_{\text{weights}}}}{{\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}}
$$

which caps the fresh-evaluation rate at ${f} \le {\text{BW}_{\text{mem}}}/{M_{\text{weights}}}$ when every evaluation streams the weights. ### Stopping, Braking, and Safe-Set Invariants

^sec-notation-stopping-invariant

For the stopping-based safety envelope, the machine must retain enough clearance to arrest its approach before crossing the protected boundary. The relationship below specializes the resident stop to one-dimensional motion starting and ending at zero acceleration, with symmetric jerk ramps and a constant-deceleration segment. The stopping distance combines travel during the pre-brake delay with braking travel; the delay-budget inequality adds the spatial margins:

$$
{d_{\text{stop}}}({v}) = {v}\,{\tau_{\text{delay}}} + {d_{\text{suffix}}}({v}), \qquad {d_{\text{suffix}}}({v}) = \frac{{v}^2}{2{a_{\text{brake}}}} + \frac{{v}\,{a_{\text{brake}}}}{2{j_{\max}}} \quad ({v} \ge {a_{\text{brake}}}^2/{j_{\max}})
$$

|:--------------------------------|:-------------------------------|:-------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${d_{\text{stop}}}$**         | **Stopping Distance**          | $\text{m}$                     | Distance required to bring the plant to rest from speed ${v}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **${T_{\text{stop}}}$**         | **Stopping Duration**          | $\text{s}$                     | Elapsed time from braking onset to standstill in the protective-separation calculation; distinct from controller sampling period.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **${D_{\text{clear}}}$**        | **Clear Distance**             | $\text{m}$                     | Clear distance from the machine to the boundary it must not cross; the subscript keeps it distinct from dataset size ${D}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **${v}$**                       | **Speed at Detection**         | $\text{m/s}$                   | Speed of the machine at the moment the hazard is detected; in stopping, pre-brake, and impact algebra, the scalar travel or approach speed ${v}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **${v_{\max}}$**                | **Speed Bound**                | $\text{m/s}$                   | Largest speed the machine, a tracked object, or an approaching body is credited with in a budget; the bounding speed in ${\Delta t_{\text{fresh}}} \le {e_{\max}}/{v_{\max}}$.                                                                                                                                                                                              |

| **${a_{\max}}$**                | **Profile Acceleration Bound** | $\text{m/s}^2$                 | Largest acceleration or deceleration a planned or interpolated profile may command; for the base’s resident stop it is set to ${a_{\text{brake}}}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **${a_{\text{net}}}$**          | **Net Braking Deceleration**   | $\text{m/s}^2, \text{rad/s}^2$ | Linear or angular deceleration left after a gravity or load bias torque ${\tau_{\text{bias}}}$ is subtracted from the actuator’s braking capacity, in the direction of travel.                                                                                                                                                                                              |

| **${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$** | **Tracking-Error Bound**       | $\text{m}$                     | Bound on the deviation between the commanded and the executed trajectory, stated by the enforcement record.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

### Zero-Order Hold and Phase Margin

^sec-notation-zoh-law

At crossover frequencies well below the sampling frequency, holding discrete control commands constant over sampling period ${T_s} = 1/{f_s}$ contributes approximately half a sample period of delay. With fixed modeled enforcer and fieldbus delays, the relationship below estimates phase-margin erosion; its phase-lag term is in radians, so all phase margins must use the same angular unit before subtraction:

$$
{\tau_{\text{ZOH}}} = \frac{{T_s}}{2}, \qquad {\Delta \phi} = {\omega_c} \left( \frac{{T_s}}{2} + {T_{\text{comp}}} + {T_{\text{bus}}} \right), \qquad {\text{PM}_{\text{net}}} = \text{PM}_{\text{plant}} - {\Delta \phi} > 0
$$

| **Symbol**                | **Definition**                    | **Unit**                    | **Notes**                                                                                                                                               |
|:--------------------------|:----------------------------------|:----------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${\tau_{\text{ZOH}}}$** | **ZOH Transport Delay**           | $\text{s}$                  | Equivalent continuous delay introduced by discrete Zero-Order Hold reconstruction (${T_s} / 2$).                                                        |
| **${T_s}, {f_s}$**        | **Sampling Period and Rate**      | $\text{s}, \text{Hz}$       | Discrete controller execution period and frequency (${f_s} = 1/{T_s}$).                                                                                 |
| **${T_{\text{comp}}}$**   | **Enforcer Compute Time**         | $\text{s}$                  | Time the enforcer takes to evaluate its constraints within one control period; with ${T_{\text{bus}}}$ it adds to the ZOH delay.                        |
| **${T_d}$**               | **Pure Transport Delay**          | $\text{s}$                  | A fixed delay in a feedback or communication channel, contributing phase lag ${\omega} {T_d}$ at frequency ${\omega}$.                                  |
| **${\omega_c}$**          | **Open-Loop Crossover Frequency** | $\text{rad/s}$              | Frequency at which open-loop gain $\lvert {L}({j}{\omega_c}) \rvert = 1$ ($0\text{ dB}$).                                                               |
| **${\Delta \phi}$**       | **Phase Lag Degradation**         | $\text{rad}$ / $\text{deg}$ | Phase erosion at crossover (${\Delta \phi} \approx 28.65^\circ \cdot ({\omega_c} / {f_s})$ from the hold alone) depleting closed-loop stability margin. |

### Actuator Electrical and Thermal Limits

^sec-notation-actuator-laws

Actuators are constrained by copper winding heating, DC bus rail droop, and magnetic saturation. Sustainable torque output is governed by the coupled electromechanical balance, written per joint with scalar torque:

$$
{V_{\text{bus}}} = {L_{\text{phase}}} \frac{d{i_q}}{d{t}} + {R}({T_{\text{winding}}})\, {i_q} + {K_e} {\omega_m}, \qquad {C_{\text{th}}} \frac{d{T_{\text{winding}}}}{d{t}} = {i_q}^2 {R}({T_{\text{winding}}}) - \frac{{T_{\text{winding}}} - {T_{\text{amb}}}}{{R_{\text{th}}}}, \qquad {\tau} = {\eta_{\text{gear}}} {N_{\text{gear}}} {K_t} {i_q}
$$

| **Symbol**                                   | **Definition**                       | **Unit**                                             | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|:---------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${K_t}, {K_e}$**                           | **Torque and Back-EMF Constants**    | $\text{N}\cdot\text{m/A}, \text{V}\cdot\text{s/rad}$ | Motor electromechanical coupling constants linking winding current to rotor shaft torque and rotor speed to back-EMF voltage (${K_t} \approx {K_e}$ in SI units).                                                                          |
| **${i_q}$**                                  | **Torque-Producing Current**         | $\text{A}$                                           | Quadrature-axis current of a field-oriented drive, ${\tau_{\text{motor}}} = {K_t} {i_q}$. Bare ${I}$ is never a current, because the series core reserves it for arithmetic intensity.                                                     |
| **${I_{\text{phase}}}$**                     | **Motor Phase RMS Current**          | $\text{A}$                                           | RMS phase current driving the stator windings of a brushless PM motor; an uppercase ${I}$ with a subscript names an RMS value or rating.                                                                                                   |
| **${i_{\text{phase}}}$**                     | **Instantaneous Phase Current**      | $\text{A}$                                           | Signed instantaneous electrical phase current, positive from inverter node into winding; distinct from RMS phase current and torque-producing dq current.                                                                                  |
| **${I_{\text{cont}}}, {I_{\text{peak}}}$**   | **Continuous / Peak Current**        | $\text{A}$                                           | RMS current ratings bounded by winding insulation class and inverter MOSFET dissipation.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **${L_{\text{phase}}}, {R_{\text{phase}}}$** | **Phase Inductance and Resistance**  | $\text{H}, {\Omega}$                                 | Stator winding inductance and DC resistance; they set ${\tau_e} = {L_{\text{phase}}}/{R_{\text{phase}}}$.                                                                                                                                  |
| **${R}({T_{\text{winding}}})$**              | **Temperature-Dependent Resistance** | ${\Omega}$                                           | Stator copper resistance at winding temperature ${T_{\text{winding}}}$: ${R}({T_{\text{winding}}}) = {R_0} [1 + {\alpha_{\text{Cu}}}({T_{\text{winding}}} - {T_0})]$ with ${\alpha_{\text{Cu}}} = 0.00393\text{ K}^{-1}$.                  |

| **${\tau_e}$**                               | **Electrical Time Constant**         | $\text{s}$                                           | Winding current rise time constant ${\tau_e} = {L_{\text{phase}}}/{R_{\text{phase}}}$; current reaches $1 - e^{-1}$ of its target in ${\tau_e}$.                                                                                           |

### Shielded Hazards and the Exposure Bound

^sec-notation-shield-laws

Catastrophic failure rates in embodied systems cannot be certified through stochastic testing alone. For a defined hazard class ${h}$, verified interception coverage and independently bounded residual faults constrain the system’s hazard rate. Under a constant-rate Poisson exposure model, zero-failure exposure follows the Rule of Three:

$$
{\lambda_{\text{system}, h}} \le {\lambda_{\text{proposal}, h}} (1 - {c_h}) + {\lambda_{\text{other}}} \le {\lambda_{\text{target}}}, \qquad {T} \ge \frac{-\ln {\alpha_{\text{sig}}}}{{\lambda_{\text{target}}}}
$$

Here ${T}$ is the zero-incident operating exposure, in hours. For independent trials with a common success probability, per-trial campaigns use the same bound in probability form, ${n} \ge \ln{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}/\ln {p_{\text{target}}} \approx 3/(1 - {p_{\text{target}}})$.

| **Symbol**                                         | **Definition**                      | **Unit**        | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|:---------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------|:----------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${\lambda_{\text{system}, h}}$**                 | **System Hazard Rate**              | $\text{h}^{-1}$ | Certified dangerous failure rate of the integrated cyber-physical machine for hazard class ${h}$.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **${\lambda_{\text{proposal}, h}}$**               | **Hazardous Proposal Rate**         | $\text{h}^{-1}$ | Arrival rate of unshielded hazardous action proposals the policy emits before the permission layer, measured against a counterfactual oracle.                                                                                                                                               |
| **${c_h}$**                                        | **Shield Interception Coverage**    | Dimensionless   | Verified conditional probability that the shield intercepts a hazardous proposal of class ${h}$, evaluated over the states in which the policy errs.                                                                                                                                        |
| **${\lambda_{\text{other}}}$**                     | **Hardware and Common-Cause Rate**  | $\text{h}^{-1}$ | Independently bounded hardware-fault and common-cause hazard rate the shield cannot intercept; the floor ${\lambda_{\text{system}, h}}$ reaches when ${c_h} = 1$.                                                                                                                           |
| **${\lambda_{\text{target}}}$**                    | **Target Hazard Rate**              | $\text{h}^{-1}$ | Tolerable dangerous failure rate assigned to the safety function by risk assessment, in the band its governing standard defines.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **${C_{\text{diag}}}$**                            | **Diagnostic Coverage**             | Dimensionless   | Rate-weighted fraction of hardware faults whose hazardous effect is mitigated, ${\lambda_{\text{unmitigated}}} = {\lambda_{\text{raw}}}(1 - {C_{\text{diag}}})$; the hardware counterpart of ${c_h}$, which covers policy proposals.                                                        |
| **${p_{\text{fail}}}, {p_{\text{target}}}$**       | **Per-Trial Probabilities**         | Dimensionless   | Per-trial failure probability, and the per-trial success probability a campaign must demonstrate; trial counts follow ${n} \ge \ln{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}/\ln {p_{\text{target}}}$.                                                                                                           |
| **${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$**                        | **Statistical Significance Level**  | Dimensionless   | Type I error threshold (${\alpha_{\text{sig}}} = 1 - \text{CL}$, e.g., ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}=0.05$ for 95% confidence where $-\ln(0.05) \approx 3.0$).                                                                                                                                     |
| **${\beta}$**                                      | **Type II Error Allowance**         | Dimensionless   | Probability of rejecting an acceptable candidate ($1-{\beta}$ is the power); with ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$ it sets the SPRT boundaries ${\Lambda_{\text{up}}}$ and ${\Lambda_{\text{lo}}}$. Diffusion schedules use indexed ${\beta_s}$; a KL weight ${\beta}$ stays local to its objective. |
| **${n}$**                                          | **Zero-Failure Trial Count**        | Trials          | Required number of incident-free trials to substantiate ${p_{\text{target}}}$; for exposure measured in hours, the bound is on ${T}$ instead.                                                                                                                                               |
| **${\Lambda_m}$**                                  | **Sequential Log-Likelihood Ratio** | Dimensionless   | After ${k}$ failures in ${m}$ trials, ${\Lambda_m} = {k}\ln({p}_1/{p}_0) + ({m}-{k})\ln\frac{1-{p}_1}{1-{p}_0}$ for per-trial failure probabilities ${p}_0$ (unacceptable) and ${p}_1$ (acceptable); SPRT stops when it leaves $({\Lambda_{\text{lo}}}, {\Lambda_{\text{up}}})$.            |
| **${\Lambda_{\text{up}}}, {\Lambda_{\text{lo}}}$** | **SPRT Boundaries**                 | Dimensionless   | Upper and lower stopping thresholds on ${\Lambda_m}$, ${\Lambda_{\text{up}}} \approx \ln((1-{\beta})/{\alpha_{\text{sig}}})$ and ${\Lambda_{\text{lo}}} \approx \ln({\beta}/(1-{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}))$; never bare ${A}$ and ${B}$.                                                        |
| **${C}$**                                          | **Assurance Claim**                 | Proposition     | Top-level safety claim of an assurance argument. Under an independent-premise probability model, its confidence $\Pr({C})$ is the product of the premise confidences for a conjunctive argument, and one minus the product of the legs’ failure probabilities for a multi-legged one.       |

### The Embodied Energy Corollary

^sec-notation-embodied-energy-corollary

Untethered mobile manipulators and autonomous robots operate under strict battery energy reserves and peak thermal dissipation ceilings. Total mission energy (distinct from the ML workload’s ${E_{\text{total}}}$) splits across mechanical actuation work, edge neural computation, and auxiliary sensor payloads. The compute energy expended by edge inference system-on-chip (SoC) devices decomposes into data movement and arithmetic operations according to the core energy corollary:

$$
{E_{\text{mission}}} = {E_{\text{act}}} + {E_{\text{total}}} + {E_{\text{aux}}}, \qquad {E_{\text{total}}} \approx {D_{\text{vol}}} \times {E_{\text{move}}} + {O} \times {E_{\text{compute}}}
$$

For constant loss power in the single-node thermal model, resistive heating in stator windings ${P_{\text{loss}}} \approx \sum {i_q}^2 {R}({T_{\text{winding}}})$ sets the steady-state rise ${\Delta T} = {P_{\text{loss}}} {R_{\text{th}}}$. Transient heating during shorter acceleration or contact bursts follows the coupled first-order thermal balance above.

| **${\tau_{\max}}$**                                  | **Joint Torque Limit**                 | $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$                                                                        | Largest torque the actuator of one joint can deliver, indexed by joint; it bounds ${\tau}$ at the same joint and is not the transient burst rating.                                                                                                            |

| **${m}$**                                            | **Plant Parameters**                   | Record                                                                                         | Physical and hardware properties of the machine in one trial or rollout, such as tolerances, wear, payload mass, state of charge, and calibration; not the mass ${m}$ of a body.                                                                               |
| **${\dot{\mathbf{x}}}$**                             | **Plant State Rate**                   | Dimensionless per unit time                                                                    | Time derivative of the mechanical plant state; the decorated form of the plant state, and not the Cartesian velocity of the tool, which shares its glyph.                                                                                                      |
| **${\mathcal{X}}$**                                  | **Plant State Space**                  | Set                                                                                            | Set of all states the mechanical plant can occupy, positions together with velocities.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **${\omega_m}$**                                     | **Rotor Angular Speed**                | $\text{rad/s}$                                                                                 | Angular speed of a motor rotor about its axis; it sets the back-EMF, and on a direct-drive hub motor it equals the wheel’s angular speed.                                                                                                                      |
| **${N_{\text{poles}}}$**                             | **Rotor Pole-Pair Count**              | Dimensionless                                                                                  | Number of magnetic pole pairs on a motor rotor; multiplying the mechanical rotor speed by it gives the electrical frequency of the drive.                                                                                                                      |
| **${v_q}$**                                          | **Quadrature-Axis Voltage**            | $\text{V}$                                                                                     | Voltage the inverter of a field-oriented drive applies along the quadrature axis, the axis that produces torque.                                                                                                                                               |
| **${\theta_{\text{tilt}}}$**                         | **Tilt Angle**                         | $\text{rad}$                                                                                   | Angular deflection of a body from vertical; zero when the body is upright.                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **${\dot{\tau}_{\max}}$**                            | **Torque Rate Limit**                  | $\text{N}\cdot\text{m/s}$                                                                      | Largest rate of change of joint output torque a drive accepts. | **${K_{\text{joint}}}$**                             | **Transmission Torsional Stiffness**   | $\text{N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$                                                                    | Torsional stiffness of the transmission between rotor and output link, the compliance that couples motor to load.                                                                                                                                              |
| **${\omega_z}$**                                     | **Yaw Rate**                           | $\text{rad/s}$                                                                                 | Angular velocity of a body about its vertical axis, one component of the body angular velocity ${\boldsymbol{\omega}}$.                                                                                                                                        |

## Contact Mechanics and Centroidal Dynamics

^sec-notation-contact

Contact turns a free-moving body into a constrained one. The symbols here describe the forces a contact can transmit, the friction cone that bounds them, and the centroidal quantities that sum them over the whole robot.

| **Symbol**                                             | **Definition**                             | **Unit**                            | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
|:-------------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${\mathbf{S}} \in \mathbb{R}^{{n} \times ({n}+6)}$** | **Actuation Selection Matrix**             | Matrix                              | Matrix $[{\mathbf{0}}, {\mathbf{I}}]$ projecting generalized forces onto actuated joints in floating-base systems.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **${\mathbf{f}}_{k} \in \mathbb{R}^3$**                | **Contact Force Vector**                   | $\text{N}$                          | Physical contact force exerted by environment on contact patch ${k}$ (${\mathbf{f}}_{k} = [{\mathbf{f}_{k, \parallel}}^\top, {f_{k, \perp}}]^\top$); always indexed by the contact.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **${f_n}$**                                            | **Finger normal force**                    | N                                   | Normal force one finger of a grasp presses against the object at its contact.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **${\mathbf{f}_t}$**                                   | **Finger tangential force**                | N                                   | Tangential friction force one finger of a grasp carries at its contact, bounded by the friction cone times ${f_n}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **${{\mathbf{J}_{\text{c}}}}_k({\mathbf{q}})$**        | **Contact Point Jacobian**                 | Mixed                               | Mapping joint velocities to contact patch Cartesian linear velocity: ${\dot{\mathbf{p}}}_k = {{\mathbf{J}_{\text{c}}}}_k({\mathbf{q}}) {\dot{\mathbf{q}}}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **${f_{k, \perp}}$**                                   | **Normal Contact Force**                   | $\text{N}$                          | Unilateral compressive force (${f_{k, \perp}} = {\mathbf{f}}_{k}^\top {\hat{\mathbf{n}}}_k \ge 0$) perpendicular to contact plane.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **${\mathbf{f}_{k, \parallel}}$**                      | **Tangential Contact Shear**               | $\text{N}$                          | Friction traction force tangential to contact plane (${\mathbf{f}_{k, \parallel}} = {\mathbf{f}}_{k} - {f_{k, \perp}} {\hat{\mathbf{n}}}_k$).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **${\mu}$**                                            | **Coulomb Friction Coefficient**           | Dimensionless                       | Static/kinetic coefficient bounding friction cone: $\lVert {\mathbf{f}_{k, \parallel}} \rVert \le {\mu} {f_{k, \perp}}$.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **${\mathbf{c}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$**                    | **Center of Mass (CoM)**                   | $\text{m}$                          | Spatial coordinates of the total articulated body mass centroid (${\mathbf{c}} = \frac{1}{{m}} \sum {m}_i {\mathbf{p}}_i$).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

| **${F_{\text{unbraked}}}$**                            | **Unbraked Impact Force**                  | $\text{N}$                          | Transient collision force peak (${F_{\text{unbraked}}} = {v_{\max}} \sqrt{{k_H} {m_{\text{red}}}}$) governed by ISO/TS 15066.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **${a_{\text{arm}}}$**                                 | **Arm Stopping Deceleration**              | $\text{m/s}^2$                      | Deceleration the arm must achieve after braking onset to stop within the latch force limit.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **${m_H}$**                                            | **Body Region Mass**                       | $\text{kg}$                         | Effective mass of a human body region, here the hand, in the ISO/TS 15066 body model.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **${F_{\text{cmd}}}$**                                 | **Brake Command Contact Force**            | $\text{N}$                          | Contact force against the latch at the instant the brake command issues, after the contact loop responds; distinct from a commanded traction force.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

| **${\dot{\mathbf{h}}_{\text{ang}}}$**                  | **Centroidal Angular Momentum Derivative** | $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$             | Time derivative of the centroidal angular momentum, the net external moment about the center of mass: contact-force moments plus the contact torques.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

| **${\hat{\mathbf{n}}}$**                               | **Contact Normal**                         | Dimensionless                       | Unit vector normal to the contact plane at one contact, pointing into the robot; indexed by contact.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

Privileged in simulation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## Geometry, Transformations, and Perception

^sec-notation-geometry

Every measurement a robot uses arrives in some frame, and every command leaves in another. These symbols name the frames, the rigid transforms between them, and the camera models that project the world onto a sensor.

## Control, Observers, and Safety Filters

^sec-notation-control

The control symbols follow state-space convention. A plant has a state, an input, and a measured output; an observer estimates the state from that output; and a safety filter, such as a control barrier function solved as a quadratic program, edits the input before it reaches the actuators.

| **Symbol**                                                      | **Definition**                               | **Unit**                               | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## Learned Policies for Robotics

^sec-notation-learned-policies

Learned policies propose actions rather than issue them. The symbols below cover how those policies are trained and sampled, including diffusion denoising, action chunking, and vision-language-action models, and how their imitation and reinforcement-learning objectives are written.

| **Symbol**                                                                    | **Definition**                          | **Unit**      | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------|:--------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${\pi_\theta}$**                                                            | **Policy**                              | Distribution  | Deep policy network parameterized by weights ${\theta}$ (italic), mapping observation tensors to action distributions.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **${\mathbf{o}}_{t}$**                                                        | **Multimodal Observation**              | Mixed         | Synchronized tokenized sensor packet (RGB images, depth latents, joint encoders, tactile arrays).                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **${\mathbf{a}}_{t} \in {\mathcal{A}}$**                                      | **Cognitive Policy Action**             | Normalized    | Unprivileged action emitted by neural policy ${\pi_\theta}$ (normalized joint delta, target pose, or gripper command).                                                                                                                                                          |
| **${K}$**                                                                     | **Ensembling Window Length**            | Time steps    | Number of overlapping past chunks blended in receding horizon execution (${K} \le {H}$).                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **${N_{\text{eval}}}$**                                                       | **Decoder Evaluation Count**            | Count         | Dependent forward passes of a generative action head per chunk (denoising or ODE-solver steps); distinct from the ensembling window ${K}$ and the step index ${k}$.                                                                                                             |
| **${\mathbf{z}} \in \mathbb{R}^{{d_z}}$**                                     | **Latent Style / State Embedding**      | Dimensionless | Compressed semantic representation or CVAE latent style variable in ACT policies.                                                                                                                                                                                               |

| **${t_{\text{div}}}$**                                                        | **Divergence Instant**                  | $\text{s}$    | Time at which the autonomous policy’s rollout begins to leave its supported states before a takeover; the start of the interval excluded from imitation targets.                                                                                                                |

## Embedded Real-Time Systems, Power, and Hardware Safety

^sec-notation-embedded

Response-time analysis keeps the Liu–Layland letters of its literature, but only inside that model and only with a task index ${i}$, ${j}$, or ${k}$. The letters are ${C}_i$ (worst-case execution time), ${B}_i$ (blocking), ${I}_i$ (interference), ${J}_i$ (release jitter), ${T}_i$ (period), ${R}_i$ (worst-case response time), and ${D}_i$ (relative deadline, which is ${T_{\text{deadline}}}$ of task ${i}$ in the series’ terms). They combine as in ${R}_i = {C}_i + {B}_i + {I}_i \le {D}_i$. Outside that model these letters keep their table meanings.

The generic parameter symbol ${\theta}$ is the one conventional exception and stays italic. Local matrix algebra follows the same bold rule: matrix operands are bold (${\mathbf{A}}$, ${\mathbf{B}}$, ${\mathbf{C}}$), as in general matrix multiply (GEMM) ${\mathbf{C}}={\alpha} {\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{B}}+{\beta} {\mathbf{C}}$. The bold form marks a matrix operand and is distinct from the italic scalar batch size ${B}$, which remains batch size in scalar contexts. A GEMM’s scalar dimensions are ${M}$, ${N}$, and ${K}$ (${\mathbf{A}}$ is ${M} \times {K}$, ${\mathbf{B}}$ is ${K} \times {N}$). Asymptotic cost is written $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$, which keeps it distinct from the operation count ${O}$.

A physical trial is expensive, so the evaluation chapters count carefully. These symbols name the trials themselves, the samples a metric is computed over, and the threshold exceedances that tail estimates are built from.

| **Symbol**             | **Definition**                 | **Unit**      | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                            |
|:-----------------------|:-------------------------------|:--------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **${n_{\text{cal}}}$** | **Calibration Example Count**  | Count         | Number of held-out calibration examples, and so of nonconformity scores, used to set a conformal prediction threshold. Not the quantization calibration set.                                         |
| **${N}$**              | **Evaluation Sample Count**    | Count         | Number of examples in the evaluation set over which an accuracy or calibration metric is computed; it sets the sampling error. Not the training set size ${D}$.                                      |
| **${X}$**              | **Discrete Random Variable**   | Record        | Discrete random variable, the uncertain quantity whose outcomes a distribution describes; uppercase for the variable, lowercase for one of its outcomes.                                             |
| **${c}$**              | **Trial Condition**            | Record        | Condition of one physical trial: initial plant state, environment parameters, plant parameters, computational configuration, and termination protocol; drawn from the operational test distribution. |
| **${n}$**              | **Trial Index**                | Index         | Position of one physical trial within a learning campaign, counted from one.                                                                                                                         |
| **${p}$**              | **Trial Success Probability**  | Dimensionless | True probability that one trial succeeds under the declared evaluation protocol and test distribution.                                                                                               |
| **${N_u}$**            | **Threshold Exceedance Count** | Count         | Number of trials whose performance metric exceeds the threshold.                                                                                                                                     |
| **${y}$**              | **Threshold Excess**           | Varies        | Amount by which the monitored quantity exceeds the threshold, one recorded exceedance; it carries the unit of the monitored quantity. Its random-variable form is ${Y}$.                             |
| **${Y}$**              | **Threshold Excess Variable**  | Varies        | Excess of the monitored quantity over the threshold, conditional on exceeding it, treated as a random variable.                                                                                      |

## Units and Precision

^sec-notation-conventions-units-precision-fdaf

- Physical units: This book uses SI (metric) units throughout, including meters, kilograms, seconds, watts, and °C, consistent with standard engineering and scientific practice. Where source data was originally reported in imperial units, the book converts to SI and notes the original values parenthetically. A space always separates the number from the unit (for example, 100 ms, 2 TB/s).
- Data and memory: This book uses **decimal SI prefixes only**: KB = $10^3$ bytes, MB = $10^6$, GB = $10^9$, TB = $10^{12}$. Binary-prefixed units do not appear in prose; model sizes, activation footprints, throughputs, and transfer volumes are reported in decimal units (for example, 2 TB/s, 102 MB). Hardware memory capacities are the one exception: they keep the vendor’s nameplate figure and label. A device modeled here with a binary 80 GB capacity holds $80 \times 2^{30}$ bytes; usable capacity and allocation headroom remain separate.
- Compute: The notation distinguishes operation counts from rates. Total work uses FLOPs (for example, GFLOPs, TFLOPs), while throughput uses FLOP/s with decimal prefixes (for example, GFLOP/s, TFLOP/s).
  - 1 TFLOP = $10^{12}$ FLOPs
  - 1 TFLOP/s = $10^{12}$ FLOPs per second
  - Arithmetic intensity conventionally uses FLOP/byte as a unit ratio (floating-point operations per byte moved). This is a ratio unit, not a total-work symbol or throughput symbol.
- Power: Power carries a subscript (${P_{\text{avg}}}$, ${P_{\text{device}}}$, ${P_{\text{cluster}}}$); bare ${P}$ remains parameter count.
- Latency percentiles: Written p50, p95, and p99 in upright type, inside math as well as in prose.
- Currency: Dollar amounts use the dollar sign (`$`); unless otherwise noted, dollar-denominated costs are U.S. dollars (USD).
- Precision:
  - **FP64**: Double precision (8 bytes)
  - **FP32**: Single precision (4 bytes)
  - **TF32**: TensorFloat-32 (19-bit Tensor Core compute mode; inputs and storage remain FP32)
  - **FP16**: Half precision (2 bytes, standard range)
  - **BF16**: Brain float (2 bytes, wide dynamic range)
  - **FP8**: Quarter precision (1 byte, E4M3 or E5M2 format)
  - **FP4**: 4-bit floating-point format
  - **INT8**: 8-bit integer (1 byte)
  - **INT4**: 4-bit integer; lower integer precisions follow the same uppercase `INTn` pattern (for example, INT3, INT2)

## Quick Reference: Resolving Collisions

^sec-notation-conventions-quick-reference-resolving-collisions-a9cf

Throughout this book, two single-letter symbols recur in both machine learning equations and hardware performance budgets. The most frequent symbol collisions across the machine learning, control, and embedded systems literatures are resolved as follows:

| **Symbol** | **ML Meaning** | **Systems Meaning** | **Book Convention**                                    |
|:-----------|:---------------|:--------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------|
| **${B}$**  | **Batch Size** | Bandwidth           | **Batch Size**. Use ${\text{BW}}$ for bandwidth.       |
| **${P}$**  | **Parameters** | Peak FLOP/s         | **Parameters**. Use ${R_{\text{peak}}}$ for peak rate. |

As a general principle, ML conventions take precedence for single letters, while systems concepts get subscripts or multi-letter symbols. This preserves compatibility with the ML literature while distinguishing the robotics and embedded systems meanings used in this book.
