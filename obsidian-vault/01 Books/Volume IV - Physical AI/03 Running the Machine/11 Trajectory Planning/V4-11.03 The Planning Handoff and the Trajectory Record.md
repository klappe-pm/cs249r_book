# V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11 Trajectory Planning|V4-11 Trajectory Planning]]

^sec-planning-handoff

If the planner hands over raw joint setpoints for the reach toward the mug without binding them to the obstacle geometry, the active intent lease, and a pre-checked stop, the arm can chase an expired target or coast past its clearance the moment the next inference cycle stalls. To prevent that failure, the planner consumes the belief record, which supplies obstacle geometry and clearance. It also consumes the intent lease, which supplies the grounded goal, task tolerances, effort ceilings, and the expiry time that bounds the lease (${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$). Throughout, the planner must respect the actuator and transmission limits of the physical body. The handoff connects those inputs to the checks that permit execution, and two things must be fixed before any replacement timing can be budgeted. The first is the map between the tool coordinates in which clearance is measured and the joint coordinates whose limits the drives enforce. The second is the record that carries a candidate across the proposal boundary, with the deadlines and the stop that bound its authority.

↩︎ The belief record and the state invariants it carries are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.04 The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants#^sec-memory-handoff|The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants]]**.

### Mapping Tool Motion to Joint Limits

^sec-planning-handoff-kinematics

A path that keeps the tool inside its speed limit and clear of obstacles in workspace can still command a joint near a straightened elbow to spin faster than its drive allows, tripping a velocity fault mid-reach. Catching that mismatch requires translating workspace motion into the manipulator’s joint coordinates. A configuration $({q}_1, \dots, {q}_n)$ collects the joint positions into a single point in the **configuration space**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.14 Footnotes#^fn3|³]] ${\mathcal{C}}$, and removing configurations occupied by obstacles leaves the collision-free subset ${\mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}} = {\mathcal{C}} \setminus {\mathcal{C}_{\text{obs}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.13 Source References#^ref-lozanoperez1983spatial|Lozano-Pérez 1983]]). Because a geometric path through ${\mathcal{C}_{\text{free}}}$ still lacks timing, an **executable trajectory** assigns physical timestamps to the path, generating continuous state references ${\mathbf{x}}({t}) = [{\mathbf{q}}({t})^\top, {\dot{\mathbf{q}}}({t})^\top]^\top$ and acceleration profiles ${\ddot{\mathbf{q}}}({t})$ alongside feedforward torque profiles ${\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ff}}}({t})$. Whereas the kinematic references state the intended position, velocity, and acceleration, the feedforward terms supply the torque predicted by the rigid-body model. The complete candidate crosses the proposal boundary for independent checking, and only admitted actuator commands cross the causal boundary to the drives.

The drives track joint angles, but clearance is measured in the task space where the tool, the mug, and the conveyor guard sit. In a planar two-link abstraction of the arm’s shoulder, elbow, and wrist with upper-arm length ${l}_1$ and forearm length ${l}_2$, **forward kinematics** gives the tool position directly as a function of the joint angles:

$$
{x} = {l}_1\cos {q}_1 + {l}_2\cos({q}_1+{q}_2), \qquad {y} = {l}_1\sin {q}_1 + {l}_2\sin({q}_1+{q}_2) \tag{1}
$$

^eq-plan-two-link-kinematics

Differentiating [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^eq-plan-two-link-kinematics|equation 1]] gives tool velocity ${\mathbf{v}_e} = {\mathbf{J}}({\mathbf{q}})\,{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}$, where the manipulator Jacobian[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.14 Footnotes#^fn4|⁴]] ${\mathbf{J}} = \partial{\mathbf{p}}/\partial{\mathbf{q}}$ changes with configuration. It is the same map that showed, in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/05 Physical Data/V4-05.10 Cross-Embodiment Transfer#^sec-data-kinematic-topology|Kinematic topology mismatches and null-space projection]]**, why a Cartesian action label can force a different arm through a singularity; here it prices the arm’s own reach. For the two-link arm its determinant is ${l}_1 {l}_2 \sin {q}_2$, which vanishes when the elbow straightens, the singular pose at which no joint motion moves the tool along the arm.

Consider the link lengths from the arm’s published kinematic chain: 0.316 m and 0.384 m, which yield a 0.70 m planar reach ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.13 Source References#^ref-franka2023robot|Franka Robotics GmbH 2023]]). The arm’s weakest direction is the tool direction that a unit of joint rate moves least, set by the smallest singular value ${\sigma_{\min}}$ of ${\mathbf{J}}$, so that $\|{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}\| = {v_{\text{TCP}}}/{\sigma_{\min}}$. Moving the tool in that direction at the 1 m/s tool center point (TCP) limit requires a joint-rate vector of magnitude 4.9 rad/s at a 90° elbow. With two joints, the larger component of a vector is at least its norm divided by $\sqrt{2}$, so at least one joint must then turn at 3.5 rad/s, above the published 2.175 rad/s limit of the shoulder and elbow joints. Straightening the arm makes the demand far worse. The image of a unit joint-rate circle under ${\mathbf{J}}$, the manipulability ellipse of tool velocities, is broad at the 90° elbow and collapses into a narrow sliver across the links at 10° ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^fig-11-two-link-singularity|figure 1]]). Sustaining 1 m/s along the arm, the ellipse’s minor axis, then needs a joint-rate magnitude of 37.8 rad/s. A tool-speed limit bounds no joint speed near a singularity. Every tool-space quantity this chapter checks (the TCP limit, the stopping distance, and the swept clearance) must therefore be mapped back through ${\mathbf{J}}$ to the joint limits the drives enforce. This holds even on a seven-joint arm, whose extra degrees of freedom help the planner steer around singular poses that this planar abstraction omits.

↪ Forward kinematics, the manipulator Jacobian, singularities, and the damped least-squares inverse are developed for ${n}$ joints in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-kinematics|Forward kinematics and singularities]]**.

^fig-11-two-link-singularity

The same map must also run in reverse. A proposal stated in tool space, whether a grasp pose from the intent lease or a Cartesian velocity from a policy or a teleoperation device, leaves the planner to find the joint motion that produces it, the **inverse kinematics** problem. The two-link arm has a closed-form inverse. A seven-joint arm replanning chunk by chunk is solved by differential steps instead: linearize at the current configuration and solve ${\mathbf{J}}\,{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}} = {\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$ for the joint step that moves the tool by ${\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$, then repeat from the new configuration. Where ${\mathbf{J}}$ is invertible the exact step is ${\Delta}{\mathbf{q}} = {\mathbf{J}}^{-1}{\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$. Written along the singular directions of ${\mathbf{J}}$, this step divides the part of ${\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$ along each tool direction by that direction’s singular value ${\sigma}$. The weakest direction therefore receives the largest joint step, $1/{\sigma_{\min}}$ per unit of tool motion, which is exactly the joint-rate demand that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^fig-11-two-link-singularity|figure 1]] charts diverging along the minor axis as the elbow straightens. An exact inverse passes the singularity’s arithmetic straight to the drives.

**Damped least squares** gives up exactness to bound the step ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.13 Source References#^ref-wampler1986manipulator|Wampler 1986]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.13 Source References#^ref-nakamura1986inverse|Nakamura and Hanafusa 1986]]). Instead of demanding ${\mathbf{J}}\,{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}} = {\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$, it chooses the joint step that best trades tool error against joint motion, with a damping length ${\lambda}$ that sets the exchange rate:

$$
{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}_\lambda} = \arg\min_{{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}}} \left(\|{\mathbf{J}}\,{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}}-{\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}\|^2 + {\lambda}^2\|{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}}\|^2\right) = {\mathbf{J}}^\top\!\left({\mathbf{J}}{\mathbf{J}}^\top+{\lambda}^2{\mathbf{I}}\right)^{-1}{\Delta}{\mathbf{p}} \tag{2}
$$

^eq-plan-dls-ik

Setting the gradient of the objective to zero gives $({\mathbf{J}}^\top{\mathbf{J}}+{\lambda}^2{\mathbf{I}})\,{\Delta}{\mathbf{q}} = {\mathbf{J}}^\top{\Delta}{\mathbf{p}}$, and moving ${\mathbf{J}}^\top$ through the inverse yields the form in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^eq-plan-dls-ik|equation 2]], which needs only a $2 \times 2$ solve for the planar arm. A reader who has trained a model will recognize ridge regression, with ${\lambda}^2$ as the weight-decay coefficient. Along each singular direction the gain changes from $1/{\sigma}$ to ${\sigma}/({\sigma}^2+{\lambda}^2)$. Far from the singularity, where ${\sigma} \gg {\lambda}$, the two agree. As ${\sigma}$ falls toward zero the damped gain falls with it instead of diverging, and it never exceeds $1/(2{\lambda})$, reached where ${\sigma} = {\lambda}$. The damping thus places a configuration-independent ceiling on the joint step, which no choice of pose can breach.

With an illustrative ${\lambda}$ of 50 mm, the weakest-direction request at the 1 m/s TCP limit needs 4.6 rad/s of joint rate at the 90° elbow, against 4.9 rad/s undamped, and the tool still realizes 94.3 percent of the requested motion. At the 10° elbow the damped command is 8.3 rad/s instead of 37.8 rad/s, and no pose can demand more than 10 rad/s. The price is paid where the damping acts: the tool realizes only 21.9 percent of the requested motion along the arm, so the step lags its target in exactly the direction the arm cannot move well. That residual is a tracking error the planner must carry into the trajectory’s timing and clearance checks rather than discard. A larger ${\lambda}$ lowers the ceiling and enlarges the error everywhere, which is why practical solvers raise the damping only as ${\sigma_{\min}}$ approaches it.

Damping bounds the inverse-kinematics step; it does not make the step feasible. Even the damped 8.3 rad/s exceeds the 2.175 rad/s joint limit, so the planner must still slow the tool through time parameterization ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.05 Kinodynamic Feasibility#^sec-planning-time-parameterization|section 11.4.5]]), and the permission path still checks every joint rate against the limits the drives enforce. Damped least squares is a better proposal, not a permission.

### The Trajectory Record and Its Deadlines

^sec-planning-handoff-record

If a candidate trajectory crosses from the planner to the controller without an immutable expiration time and a pre-checked stop, a hung host process leaves the drives tracking the last command into an obstacle. What the planner hands over is therefore a **trajectory record**, the ninth record in the record chain and the immutable proposal that the tracking controller follows and the permission path admits. The trajectory record consumes the geometric obstacle bounds from the belief record and the task target and lease window from the intent lease. It binds an untrusted sequence proposal to checked physical limits across the formal boundary between the unprivileged, asynchronous Brain and the deterministic, hard real-time Nervous System. Its header names its coordinate frame ${\mathcal{F}}$, evidence time ${t_0}$, initial reference state, and active intent lease expiry ${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$. The header also carries four deadlines defined in the **MCU monotonic clock domain** (into which host and sensor timestamps are converted with a bounded synchronization error). Nominal tracking begins at ${t_{\text{start}}}$, the nominal setpoints end at ${t_{\text{plan\_exp}}}$, the matched stop must physically begin at ${t_{\text{commit}}}$, and the admitted stop finishes at ${t_{\text{stop\_end}}}$. Beside these header fields, an assumption sidecar declares the payload, friction, obstacle-map epoch, and other conditions under which the candidate was checked ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-trajectory-contract|section 11.8]]). Within the planner itself, each replacement is scheduled against an earlier internal milestone, the blend deadline ${t_{\text{blend}}}$, by which a preferred successor should clear validation. The latest replacement-admission decision on the MCU precedes physical stop onset at ${t_{\text{commit}}}$ by the reserved decision, transport, and actuator delay.

The permission path permits nominal **TRACK** only while the intent lease and trajectory record remain valid, so an expired intent lease terminates nominal tracking immediately, regardless of what the trajectory’s own timestamps allow. To manage asynchronous inference latency tails and hardware faults without compromising safety, the architecture enforces a strict **two-tier deadline hierarchy**:

1.  **Fast Nervous System periodic chunk lease (${T_{\text{lease}}}$, 60 ms):** This high-rate heartbeat governs the real-time execution contract between Brain and Nervous System. The chunk policy proposes base velocity and arm joint targets together, so one chunk lease governs both bodies, and only an admitted replacement chunk renews it, with a desired admission interval of 50 ms on this machine. Qualification must bound successful-admission interarrival, not merely inference or request cadence. If an upstream inference pass hangs or communication drops, the lease lapses 10 ms after its expected renewal, immediately requesting a controlled stop whose onset follows the reserved response delay.
2.  **Trajectory commitment deadline:** The record names the latest physical onset of its matched stop. Replacement admission must close early enough to dispatch that stop and absorb actuator response delay. This geometric limit does not replace the running machine’s shorter chunk lease; the later worked timeline examines an explicitly different, longer-lease configuration.

↩︎ The chunk lease and its sizing from the stopping budget are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-lease-sizing|Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget]]**.

Two stopping responses back these deadlines. A matched stop suffix, latched at admission, begins at ${t_{\text{commit}}}$ if no successor has been admitted; a state-matched fallback, built from the measured state, answers any earlier invalidation, such as a lapsed lease ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-two-tier-deadlines|section 11.5.1]]). Either stop remains authorized after nominal tracking expires, provided its own state, clearance, sensing, and drive-energy premises still hold.

**Definition 11.1: Stopping suffix**

***Stopping suffix*** is the bounded continuation, checked and latched with a trajectory at admission, that brings the machine from a declared entry state to rest along the already-checked path, within the clearance that remains and without further input from the proposer.

1.  **Significance**: It is the term that makes ${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$ checkable before motion begins. From the arm’s 1 m/s TCP limit, the worked suffix sized in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-c2-stopping-suffix|section 11.5.2]] travels 137.5 mm of an illustrative 300 mm of clearance, the free distance along the checked path between the tool and the nearest obstacle. The stop alone therefore claims nearly half the room before any response delay is charged.
2.  **Distinction**: The matched suffix is anchored at the nominal state expected at ${t_{\text{commit}}}$; the state-matched fallback is built from the measured state when authority ends earlier. Both are controlled stops along the checked path, unlike a drive inhibit or brake, whose motion is not planned.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Treating the last nominal setpoint, or a hold at the final position, as the stop. A hold is not a deceleration profile, and a suffix sized with constant deceleration understates the distance any drive that ramps its deceleration will travel.

The two execution consumers read distinct subsets of the trajectory record. The downstream tracking controller interpolates admitted setpoints, timestamps, and feedforward torque terms to drive the motor inverters along a continuous profile fetched from the real-time execution jitter buffer. Simultaneously, the independent permission path inspects the record’s lineage, timing bounds, dynamic assumptions, validity envelopes, and verified stopping continuation (${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$). The permission path monitors current limits and physical tracking error on every millisecond tick without inheriting the planner’s feasibility claim as proof. An unmodeled obstacle, payload slip, or contact wrench immediately revokes TRACK authority and activates the state-matched fallback. Because every contract field maps to an explicit hardware or software gate, the Nervous System safely executes fast physical motion without presuming that subsequent neural inference cycles will complete on schedule. A record of this kind is short-lived by design, so the next constraint is how often a successor must arrive and how long producing one takes.
