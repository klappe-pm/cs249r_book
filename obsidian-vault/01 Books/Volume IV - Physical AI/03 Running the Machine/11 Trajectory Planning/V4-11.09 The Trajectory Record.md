# V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11 Trajectory Planning|V4-11 Trajectory Planning]]

^sec-planning-trajectory-contract

Suppose the planner hands the low-level controller a bare array of 16 joint setpoints for the reach toward the mug, without the obstacle epoch, payload assumption, and pre-checked stopping suffix under which those setpoints were cleared. The MCU then has no way to tell whether the plan is still safe to run when the host stalls or the load changes. The trajectory record, handed over immutably at every replanning cycle, lets the low-level permission path decide whether to admit those setpoints without consulting the high-level planner again. The record carries the header, four deadlines, two deadline tiers, and assumption sidecar defined with the planning handoff in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-handoff-record|section 11.2.2]]. It works as a cyber-physical contract rather than a passive message format. Every field encodes a physical invariant that the downstream runtime can verify before a command crosses the causal boundary.

By pairing a staged admission check with the latched stopping profile of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-inevitable-collision-states|section 11.5.3]], the record lets the MCU evaluate safety independently of the host planner. Even if the host operating system crashes mid-motion, the resident emergency stop profile remains intact on the real-time controller. Bringing the physical machine to rest still demands operational sensors, DC bus energy, and mechanical braking authority. The belief record and intent lease consumed at the planning handoff remain embedded in the record’s lineage. The completed record binds these physical assumptions directly to the proposed motion before forwarding them to the runtime enforcer, where live sensor feedback governs per-tick tracking permission.

Every field in the contract captures an explicit condition of admission. Kinematic boundary derivatives define how the trajectory must mesh with ongoing motion at the seam. Real-time deadlines dictate exactly when nominal tracking must yield to a safe stop. The accompanying sidecar documents the precise geometric and inertial assumptions under which the validator certified the plan. Instead of taking the planner’s assertion of feasibility on faith, the execution runtime uses the record to hold the motion accountable to physical reality.

Hierarchically, the trajectory record answers to the intent lease. The cryptographic digest binds the record to the exact parent lease whose target and effort ceilings the permission path must check against the proposed motion. The motion itself is stored as discrete setpoint arrays or continuous spline coefficients, parameterized by an explicit sample cadence (a 25 ms timestep ${\Delta t_{\text{step}}}$ on the running machine, a 40 Hz stream). The record also carries the nominal trajectory expiration ${t_{\text{plan\_exp}}}$ (400 ms for a single chunk) and canonical SI units. Pairing this explicit cadence and unit specification with the reference coordinate frame and evidence timestamp established in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-handoff|section 11.2]] allows the joint controller to interpolate setpoints and evaluate higher-order derivatives cleanly, eliminating coordinate-frame ambiguities and any reliance on unsynchronized host clocks.

Physical feasibility is never unconditional; it holds only under the environmental geometry and dynamic loads assumed during planning. The immutable sidecar captures these conditions, recording the obstacle-map epoch, payload mass, surface friction coefficients, kinematic limits, validator software version, certified clearance margins, and the source observation identifiers. A cryptographic digest in the fixed header immutably binds this sidecar to the setpoint payload. Because safety proofs cannot extrapolate beyond validated boundaries, the payload and friction values declared in the sidecar must fall strictly within the target operational design domain (ODD) declared in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|The Evaluation Record]]**. If live sensor feedback indicates that the robot has drifted outside these operating assumptions, the permission path immediately revokes the TRACK state, recognizing that clearance margins computed for one world state do not hold in another. Diagnostic metadata, such as random solver seeds and neural model weight hashes, belongs in this audit sidecar for offline postmortem analysis, keeping the per-tick servo loop lean and deterministic.

↪ Conditional permission, the per-tick safe-set test that holds a clearance margin valid only for the live state, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^sec-enforcement-safe-sets-conditional-permission|Safe Sets as Conditional Permission]]**.

On this machine, the chunk policy generates only the nominal motion profile, which the planning runtime then packages into a self-contained record by appending the seam state, the four operational deadlines, and a matched stopping suffix. Inside the fixed header, lineage fields record a monotonic sequence number, the evidence timestamp ${t_0}$, and the digest of the parent intent lease, while timing fields encode the four deadlines from [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-handoff-record|section 11.2.2]]. Seam fields then define the reference frame, each joint’s initial state $({q},{\dot{q}},{\ddot{q}})$ at handoff, and the representation of an admission-checked stopping profile satisfying ${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$. Content fields specify the payload format, byte length, and an integrity digest spanning both the setpoint payload and the sidecar.

Both the variable-length setpoint sequence and the immutable assumption sidecar sit immediately outside the fixed header. The MCU verifies their composite digest before running the admission checks described in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-behavior-seam|section 11.5]]. This dual-digest design decouples integrity from provenance: the payload digest validates contents, while the parent digest anchors lineage. Because the permission path admits a motion proposal based entirely on whether its physical invariants check out rather than on the identity of the process that generated it (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-supervisory-timers|Supervisory timers and proposal wire contracts]]**), the record deliberately omits cryptographic signatures or origin tags.

↪ **Byte layout:** **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-trajectory|Trajectory payload]]** illustrates a record carrying a single polynomial stopping suffix; it does not encode the worked piecewise S-curve.

Instantiated for the warehouse reach toward the mug, the record carries its own deadlines, which are those of the alternate timeline, while the running chunk lease decides how long tracking actually continues. Its parent digest points directly to the mug-acquisition intent lease, its coordinates are expressed in the robot base frame, and every timestamp references the MCU’s monotonic clock. Measured relative to ${t_{\text{start}}}$, the timing fields record the commitment deadline at 300 ms, the nominal trajectory end at 400 ms (derived from 16 setpoints spaced at 25 ms), and the final safe-stop horizon at 575 ms. The 240 ms blend deadline remains a planner scheduling target outside the fixed header. Four candidates can end tracking authority: the admission cutoff at 278 ms, the lease timeout 60 ms after the last admitted chunk, the intent lease expiry, and evidence staleness. On the running machine the lease timeout binds first. The commitment fields then serve as a backstop that acts only under a lease long enough to outlast the cutoff.

The worked seam specifies a 275 ms, jerk-limited ${C}^2$ stopping suffix bounded by peak acceleration 5 m/s² and jerk 66.7 m/s³. It is anchored at the commitment state and brings the arm safely to rest from its 1 m/s TCP velocity limit within 137.5 mm. Serializing and admitting this analytical S-curve requires a specified bounded profile encoding and decoder, bound to the admitted path and stop-entry state, with decoding and validation included in the admission budget (a representation not supplied by the illustrative single-polynomial wire layout). Under the running lease, a lease timeout triggers the state-matched fallback built from the measured state. From zero-acceleration cruise that fallback follows the same S-curve and travels the same distance ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-c2-stopping-suffix|section 11.5.2]]), so the lease path and stop together consume 219.5 mm of the available 300 mm clearance. The 278 ms commitment cutoff never extends the lease. In the sidecar, the planner asserts a payload mass bounded by 3 kg, unobstructed free space leading up to the grasp contact, and operating conditions inside the target ODD. The real-time permission path evaluates all of these upon admission and audits them on every control cycle.

In this concrete trajectory, the temporal ordering strictly satisfies ${t_{\text{start}}}\le {t_{\text{commit}}}<{t_{\text{plan\_exp}}}<{t_{\text{stop\_end}}}$, and the MCU enforces these timing boundaries according to the TRACK and STOP transition rules developed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-handoff|section 11.2]]. Because safety requires both temporal validity and physical realizability, a well-formed timestamp in the header cannot compensate for an unviable stopping suffix. To make latency and timing jitter auditable, the system logs the timestamps for evidence capture, plan request, validation completion, admission, activation, control transfer, and stop completion in a synchronized time domain, exposing the end-to-end response delay of the entire perception-planning-control pipeline.

Every artifact in this runtime chain, from the observation contract and the belief record through the intent lease to the trajectory record itself, declares its physical dependencies and its expiration deadline. The trajectory record proves only what the planner could verify at plan time: that a collision-free stop remains physically feasible from any state the robot might reach before commitment, given the obstacle scene and dynamic payload asserted in its sidecar. Common design pitfalls often mistake this bounded, conditional guarantee for unconditional safety, overlooking what happens when physical reality deviates from the planner’s model.
