*Why must every motion a robot follows already contain the stop it will probably never use?*

A moving robot cannot pause its momentum while a learned policy computes the next piece of its motion. Each new plan must take over from motion already under way. A plan that matches only the arm’s position leaves a jump in velocity or acceleration, which the motors can meet only with a sudden step in torque. A real drive refuses such a step or converts it into vibration, wear, and a spilled load. A path that clears every obstacle can still ask for more joint speed, torque, or current than the body can supply, and the fastest timing along that path demands exactly the abrupt changes a drive cannot make. The next plan can also arrive late, because inference time has a long tail, and a machine that keeps moving while it waits spends the distance it needs to stop. Without trajectory planning, a correct decision about where to go becomes a command the body cannot follow, or motion that cannot be halted in the room that remains. Planning attaches timing, a smooth join to the present state, and a stop that runs without the proposer. Those three properties decide whether a command that crosses the causal boundary can still be brought to rest before its energy reaches something that cannot be undone.

**Learning Objectives**

- Distinguish prediction horizon, executed prefix, renewal cadence, and evidence age when budgeting trajectory replacement
- Map tool-space speed limits to joint rates through the manipulator Jacobian, explain why the mapping fails near a singularity, and bound an inverse-kinematics step there with damped least squares
- Evaluate a timed trajectory and its time parameterization against actuator torque, current-slew, and voltage limits, including reflected rotor inertia (${N_{\text{gear}}}^2 {J_{\text{rotor}}}$)
- Synthesize closed-form quintic spline bridges, anchored at the projected seam state rather than a stale sampled pose, that eliminate acceleration steps and bound commanded jerk across replanning seams
- Size a jerk-limited ${C}^2$ stopping suffix from deceleration and jerk bounds and verify stopping clearance (${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$)
- Compare sampling-based search, trajectory optimization, and learned action chunking by the work each performs and the checks its output still requires
- Construct trajectory records whose two-tier deadlines (high-rate chunk lease and hardware commitment cutoff) keep motion out of inevitable collision states (ICS) and support independent per-tick permission decisions
