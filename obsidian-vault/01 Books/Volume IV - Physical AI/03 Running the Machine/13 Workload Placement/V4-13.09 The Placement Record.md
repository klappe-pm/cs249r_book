# V4-13.09 The Placement Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement|V4-13 Workload Placement]]

^sec-placement-hardware-allocation

Selecting the discrete safety microcontroller on paper does not clear the mobile manipulator for release while its loaded execution fields remain unpopulated on the target hardware. The machine now has an assigned placement for its permission path, an explicit deadline, an unloaded execution estimate, and a factorial stress campaign designed to falsify hardware isolation, but none of those timing properties can support a release sign-off until they are recorded alongside the exact environmental and operational conditions under which they hold.

The **placement record**, the eleventh record in the record chain, formalizes where the safety permission path executes and supplies the physical evidence that its timing bounds remain invariant. It consumes the enforcement record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.12 The Enforcement Record#^sec-enforcement-telemetry|The Enforcement Record]]**, inheriting the declared worst-case execution time and hard deadline of the permission path. To these timing constraints it binds the evidence that would show the bound holding under peak proposal load on production silicon. That evidence consists of the assigned execution domain, privilege ring, nominal loop period, unloaded and loaded observed maxima, AXI interconnect QoS arbitration settings, PDN inductive droop limits, thermal trip thresholds, enabled hardware mitigations, and a cryptographic digest of the external test report ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.09 The Placement Record#^tbl-13-deployment-envelope-schema|table 4]]). An observed loaded maximum that exceeds the declared worst-case execution time immediately refutes the placement claim. Conversely, observed maxima that fall below the bound validate only the specific conditions exercised during testing; for any empirical latency distribution, sample maxima provide supporting evidence for an upper bound rather than an analytical substitute for it.

↩︎ That measured maxima support but cannot replace an analytical worst-case bound is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^sec-nervous-deterministic-fieldbuses|Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism]]**.

The placement record bridges architectural configuration registers with direct physical measurements. Register readbacks of `execution_domain_id` and `privilege_and_ring` document the compute domain topology and execution privilege against architectural status registers, while `shared_axi_channel_qos` records interconnect arbitration configurations and verifies whether unprivileged software can reconfigure bus priorities before stress tests validate the resulting worst-case latency bounds. Alongside those digital settings, `max_pdn_droop_thermal_trip` ties voltage supply margins and junction temperature limits to high-bandwidth oscilloscope probe traces and on-die thermal telemetry gathered under full compute load. Together, `enforcement_record_digest` and `test_report_digest` establish cryptographic provenance by binding the placement record directly to its upstream timing contract and empirical test report.

The record architecture separates concerns into two distinct structures. A compact, fixed-size header stores critical platform configurations and observed execution maxima for low-overhead verification at boot time. This header anchors an external test report by its cryptographic hash. The report holds all non-fixed evidentiary records: complete hardware inventories, empirical latency distributions, total sample counts, environmental conditions, active hardware mitigations, and unresolved operational boundaries.

↪ **Byte layout:** The placement record’s field offsets and sizes are given in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-placement|Placement record]]**.

For the mobile manipulator, the header records the nominal 1 ms tick period and 400 μs hard deadline, while the test report archives the preliminary 135 μs unloaded execution estimate. Both observed-maximum fields remain initialized to all ones (`0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`), and the dedicated power rail cannot yet be documented as an approved mitigation because full empirical qualification remains pending. The loaded execution field remains unpopulated because no stress campaign has yet been executed on this machine. Populating this value requires running the factorial contention campaign of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.08 Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation#^sec-placement-testing-separation|section 13.7]] directly on physical hardware. In that campaign, the discrete microcontroller executes its safety loop while the host application processor saturates neural accelerator clusters with trajectory proposals and writes continuous trace logs at maximum junction temperature, triggering the refusal branch across stress trials. Every cycle time recorded during this contention benchmark must remain at or below the declared worst-case execution time, ${T_{\text{wcet}}} =$ 250 μs. In addition, the onset of powered deceleration, timed from the arrival of the last valid trajectory renewal, must occur within 82 ms. Until physical instrumentation confirms these bounds, the permission-independence claims of the safety case rest on unverified assertions, preventing any deployment safety release.

The external test report inventories all shared silicon and platform resources: memory channels, cache partitions, DMA engines, and power rails, alongside every competing bus master. For each resource, the report documents the hardware arbitration configuration and the resulting contention latency. For the safety permission rail, it records the selected 2 s hold-up capacitance and its pending qualification by a supply-loss fault-injection campaign. The report also defines the electrical isolation boundary by enumerating allowable rail claimants, restricting access strictly to the microcontroller, the gate-drive logic, and the spring-applied brake release coils, while entirely excluding unverified proposal-side compute loads.

Any contention measurement is meaningful only in the context of its exact operating state. The test report therefore qualifies each latency distribution with cryptographic hashes of the active neural model and system firmware, DVFS policies, enclosure thermal interface materials, ambient and junction temperatures, power rail voltages, interconnect arbitration weights, synthetic traffic generation profiles, sensor sampling rates, measurement instrumentation calibration, sample counts, and total test duration. The report records these parameters alongside full latency histograms and explicit enumerations of unexercised operational states, while the fixed header exposes only the scalar maxima for fast verification. For example, the $10^7$-tick trace evaluated in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.08 Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation#^sec-placement-testing-separation|section 13.7]] spans only 2 h 46 min 40 s of operation on a single physical machine. Such exposure demonstrates that the system survived specific benchmark conditions, but it does not exclude rare multi-contender collisions or provide a statistical bound across an entire production fleet.

Every placement record entry requires a predeclared falsification criterion formulated before executing hardware tests. This criterion defines the physical boundary that constitutes architectural failure, such as any safety enforcement cycle exceeding the declared 250 μs worst case during concurrent neural accelerator bursts and DMA transfers. The record documents the empirical execution distribution against this criterion and registers whether the isolation hypothesis survived or failed. When contention violates the latency budget and forces an architectural revision, the record details the specific mitigation deployed, such as configuring bandwidth throttling on the neural accelerator memory bus. The test entry must also explicitly report boundary conditions left unexercised during the test run, such as operating temperatures above the 45 $^{\circ}\text{C}$ bay ambient, multi-bit error injection in tightly coupled memory, or cascading sensor transceiver failures. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15 Adversarial Verification#^sec-verification|Adversarial Verification]]** then consumes the documented arbitration points and shared power rails as a target map for hardware and software fault injection. It deliberately corrupts shared structures to test whether fault containment matches predicted isolation domains. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16 Deployment Release#^sec-release|Deployment Release]]** incorporates the verified execution bounds and unexercised operational limits into the formal safety case, turning raw physical measurements into auditable evidence for real-world deployment.

With the placement record in place, the stopping budget is specified row by row for a wheeled mobile manipulator whose resident fallback is a brake-to-stop suffix on stable ground. A budget developed for one embodiment earns trust as a method only if it survives a machine with different dynamics. A legged humanoid, whose fallback trajectory must dynamically sustain balance while traversing unstructured terrain, provides the most demanding stress test of these principles (perspective [13.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/13_placement/13_placement.html#capstone-placement-humanoid-crucible)).

**Systems Perspective 13.1: When the fallback is a step**

**Question**: Which rows of the stopping budget survive when the fallback is a step rather than a stop?

When hardware placement supports the deterministic execution contribution to the aggregate pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$, the stopping-budget framework applies directly to the mobile manipulator’s powered braking maneuver, with dispatch-to-admission delay allocated and drive-energy qualification still open. However, a bipedal humanoid walking mid-stride possesses no stationary equilibrium into which it can instantaneously brake. Its fallback maneuver is dynamic, a recovery step. Every row of the stopping budget must consequently be re-evaluated for dynamic stepping:

- **Observation age survives.** Temporal latency continues to translate directly into spatial displacement, the conversion stated in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-age-budget|Bounding Observation Age]]**. On an articulated sensor head undergoing angular rotation, this error scales with viewing distance: a feature at range ${r}$ traverses space at tangential speed ${v_{\text{tangential}}} = {\omega} {r}$ under an angular rate ${\omega}$. For instance, an uncalibrated 10 ms camera–IMU temporal offset during a 1.5 rad/s head pan misidentifies the position of an obstacle located 5 m ahead by 7.5 cm.
- **Commitment semantics change.** The latched zero-velocity trajectory suffix established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-behavior-seam|Behavior at the Seam]]** is replaced by a certified recovery stepping trajectory. Rather than terminating in a stationary rest configuration, this contingency plan must be admitted alongside the nominal motion command to guarantee safe placement of the swing foot onto an admissible support polygon.
- **The safety inset changes.** The geometric obstacle clearance margins derived in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^sec-enforcement-safe-sets-conditional-permission|Safe Sets as Conditional Permission]]** no longer suffice to guarantee dynamic balance. A recovery step demands full closed-loop hybrid dynamics, relative-degree constraint analysis, identification of reachable capture regions, and actuation limits under torque saturation. Kinematic tracking uncertainty and ground reaction force disturbances must propagate directly into this forward invariance set before a valid safety inset can be calculated.
- **The placement record expands.** Because a zero-joint-velocity command mid-stride does not establish a balanced terminal state, the hardware isolation boundary must encompass all computational, interconnect, and power resources required to execute the active recovery step. Just as the wheeled manipulator’s placement record isolates the energy storage and gate drivers needed for powered braking, the biped’s placement record must certify dedicated bus bandwidth, joint controller execution enclaves, and surviving drive-energy paths for active balance recovery. Rail hold-up preserves supervisory logic; it does not by itself supply the recovery step.
