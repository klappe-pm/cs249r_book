# V4-13.11 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement|V4-13 Workload Placement]]

^sec-placement-summary

Part I built the mobile manipulator on a hypothesis: its learned proposer runs on application processors and accelerators and its permission path on a bare-metal microcontroller. This chapter turned that hypothesis into a placement decision that evidence can refute. The allocation alone does not separate the two paths, because privilege boundaries govern none of the physical resources the two paths still share. Partitioning therefore requires a justified timing bound and fault evidence, with loaded tests able to refute the claim.

The evidence must cover the physical resources that the placement still shares. A loaded test supports a scoped claim about its workload, arbitration policy, supply, clock governance, and thermal conditions. Separate processor packages remove some coupling paths, but the board’s common supply and communication links remain part of the argument. Across the link, the boundary contract keeps a stalled proposer from holding the permission deadline. The reader makes one bounded attempt per tick and discards a torn record, and a windowed watchdog trips on feeds that arrive too early as well as too late.

The placement record carries that evidence forward. It takes the permission path’s declared worst-case execution time and deadline from the enforcement record and adds the loaded observed maxima for the declared test conditions, the shared-resource queue and power headroom, the common power, clock, reset, and memory failure paths, and the conditions left untested. The machine’s own record holds a 135 μs illustrative unloaded estimate against the declared 250 μs worst case inside the 400 μs deadline. It still awaits loaded test results, which must name the arbitration policy, clocks, sample count, and physical endpoint before any release claim can rest on it.

The observation contract, belief record, intent lease, trajectory record, enforcement record, and placement record now describe one machine. Its timing must hold across the chain, including the memory, power, and communication paths between stages. Placement makes the execution premise of principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]] falsifiable through a threshold, a stress campaign, and a record of what remains untested. Part IV inherits the conditional stopping margin, the unverified premise that aisle crossings are procedurally restricted (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-composite-budget|Sizing the composite stopping budget]]**), and the placement record’s empty loaded field. It also inherits a dispatch-to-admission interval ${\Delta_{\text{da}}}$ that this chapter has allocated at 63.3 ms, inside the 100.8 ms the spare can pay for, but not yet measured. Seven relations carry the chapter’s quantitative argument, and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.11 Summary#^tbl-13-key-equations|table 5]] records each with the decision it settles and the place it is derived.

**Key Takeaways: Silicon placement and memory contention**

- **Logical separation is a hypothesis about hardware:** Privilege rings govern which addresses a task may touch, not where its requests wait, which rail feeds it, or which clock and reset tree it depends on. Loaded tests can refute the claim; justified bounds and fault evidence must support it.
- **Averages cannot bound a deadline, and bandwidth is not the main threat:** On a shared die at full bandwidth, more than 82 FIFO backlogs must precede the snapshot read before it crosses the declared worst-case execution time. Shared-cache miss handling is far stronger: measured slowdowns of 21× and more than 300× cross the declared bound once 4.3 percent or 0.28 percent of the tick is miss stall. Only private memory or regulated co-runners bound that multiplier.
- **Couplings act on four timescales around a millisecond tick:** Rail droop acts in nanoseconds and interconnect queueing in microseconds, below the millisecond tick they threaten. Silicon thermal throttling, by contrast, persists for seconds after the load stops, and chassis structural and battery pack self-heating accumulates over multi-hour shifts. In the rejected shared-die placement, a halved clock stretches the enforcer’s 250 μs cycle to 500 μs while inference runs and for at least 2,175 ticks after it stops.
- **The declared bound is the falsification threshold:** The declared worst-case execution time bounds the tick in isolation, and the selected placement claims that proposal-side load adds no interference term, so any loaded cycle above it refutes the placement’s timing premise even when the deadline still holds. A passing trace supports only the states it tested.
- **A refuted claim forces a priced choice:** Regulation and reservation cost idle capacity, coloring and partitioning cost working set, workload reduction costs policy authority, and relocation adds a board link. On the machine that link costs 1.31 ms of a 63.3 ms dispatch-to-admission allocation dominated by inference and validation.
- **Platform exposure is a placement constraint:** An on-die safety island with its own crystal, rail, and memory removes every die-level coupling except temperature, the reset chain, and the fabric its proposals cross. As of 2026, however, the commercial modules that carry the machine’s application processor do not enable it, so the permission path moves to a discrete controller.
- **The placement record bounds the deployment envelope:** It takes its declared worst-case execution time and deadline from the enforcement record and adds loaded observed maxima, thermal headroom, bus arbitration, mitigations, and untested conditions. On the mobile manipulator the measurement fields are still empty, so the independence claim reaches release review without loaded qualification evidence.

^callout-takeaways--13-2-0f3d8f12

Placement is where the third law becomes a property of hardware. Proposal is not permission only while the proposer cannot reach the permission decision through anything the two share, and on a real machine that includes memory, supply, clock, reset, and heat. The useful habit is to read every architecture diagram as a list of claims, one per shared resource, and to ask of each one what loaded measurement would refute it. The running machine leaves Part III with that placement decision and an explicit evidence gap: the record names the resources that must preserve the permission deadline, the measurements that could refute their bounds, and the fallback that must remain available if the proposer fails. Governing the machine means judging such conditional claims, and the next proposer to claim authority will be a person.
