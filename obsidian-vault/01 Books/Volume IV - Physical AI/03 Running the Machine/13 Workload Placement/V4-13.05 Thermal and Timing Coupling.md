# V4-13.05 Thermal and Timing Coupling

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement|V4-13 Workload Placement]]

^sec-placement-thermal-timing-coupling

A permission core can suffer execution delays long after a heavy proposal workload has terminated, because thermal capacitance traps heat inside the package. While electrical droop and interconnect contention manifest over nanoseconds and microseconds, thermal coupling operates over seconds. When an accelerator and a real-time permission core share a silicon die, a cooling path, and an on-chip power management governor, sustained neural network inference can lower the clock frequency available to safety enforcement, and keep it lowered after the inference stops.

**Thermal clock throttling** is the automatic, hardware-enforced reduction of processor clock frequency triggered when sustained power dissipation pushes the silicon junction temperature (${T_{\text{j}}}$) beyond a critical trip threshold (${T_{\text{trip}}}$). On-die thermal sensing diodes alert the power management controller, which scales down clock frequency through hardware dividers or phase-locked loop (PLL) reconfiguration to prevent gate dielectric breakdown and thermal runaway. Because the physical die, package substrate, and heat spreader require time to dissipate this accumulated thermal energy, the reduced clock frequency persists long after the compute burst ends. For a co-located real-time task, this persistent slowdown can turn a nominal execution margin into a deadline overrun that lasts as long as the package takes to cool.

In this illustrative warehouse scenario, the robot’s electronics bay operates at an ambient temperature of 45$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$. The thermal dissipation path follows the package row of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.03 Two Paths and the Resources They Share#^fig-13-placement-resource-map|figure 1]], shared by every computational component on the application SoC die. The selected warehouse enclosure seals out airborne dust and conductive debris and uses conduction cooling rather than open fan vents. For a shared-die architecture in this enclosure, the conduction path must transfer all heat generated by sustained proposal inference to the chassis without driving the real-time permission core into a throttled clock state.

For a conduction-cooled enclosure, the total thermal resistance from junction to ambient (${R_{\text{th,JA}}}$) sums three distinct mechanical layers:

$$
{R_{\text{th,JA}}} = {R_{\text{th,JC}}} + {R_{\text{th,TIM}}} + {R_{\text{th,chassis}}}.
$$

 Here, ${R_{\text{th,JC}}}$ is the junction-to-case resistance of the silicon package, ${R_{\text{th,TIM}}}$ is the resistance through the thermal interface material, and ${R_{\text{th,chassis}}}$ represents heat conduction through the metal enclosure to the ambient air. Over extended operation, mechanical shock and thermal cycling can degrade the interface material, increasing ${R_{\text{th,TIM}}}$. Simultaneously, battery discharge losses and motor drive electronics inject parasitic heat into the bay; battery Joule heating scales with the square of the discharge current (${I}^2 {R}$). A robust thermal envelope must therefore account for both the mechanical cooling path and the peak concurrent actuator workload. A quiescent bench test on a cold board cannot replicate the thermal reality of a warm, multi-hour operating shift.

Consider an application SoC inside this bay modeled with a lumped **junction-to-ambient thermal resistance** ${R_{\text{th,JA}}}$ and a **thermal time constant**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.14 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]] ${\tau_{\text{th}}}$. Under a continuous power dissipation ${P_{\text{device}}}$, the silicon junction reaches a steady-state temperature of ${T_{\text{amb}}} + {P_{\text{device}}} {R_{\text{th,JA}}}$. When power steps to a new level, the junction temperature converges toward this new equilibrium along a first-order exponential trajectory governed by ${\tau_{\text{th}}}$. This time constant represents the fast pole of the die and package thermal network. While heatsink and structural chassis poles operate on timescales of minutes to hours, holding the chassis at a constant baseline temperature provides an optimistic lower bound on junction heating.

Under the clock and thermal assumptions below, these dynamics eliminate the modeled configuration of candidate (b) in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.03 Two Paths and the Resources They Share#^tbl-13-placement-candidates|table 1]]. In that candidate design, the permission path runs on a real-time CPU cluster located on the main application SoC. Suppose this cluster shares the SoC’s primary clock and power management domain, sized so that the permission tick’s worst-case cycle count, run at the nominal clock, exactly meets its declared worst-case execution time. The sizing is not chosen to make the case fail. The declared bound is the worst execution the placement may assume, so judging the placement at that bound is the honest test, and a tick that executes at its bound has no slack against any slowing of the clock. When sustained neural network inference drives the junction temperature beyond the trip threshold ${T_{\text{trip}}}$, the hardware governor halves the cluster’s clock frequency. As a consequence, every instruction cycle in the tick takes twice as long. The safety software executes the exact same code, but the shared governor injects an unmodeled cross-domain delay that isolated execution time profiling cannot detect. A discrete microcontroller, in contrast, runs on an independent oscillator and dedicated power rail beyond the reach of the SoC’s thermal governor. An on-die safety island fed by its own clock tree and voltage rail would likewise isolate clock generation from the governor, although it would still share the silicon substrate and require rigorous thermal qualification.

A throttled tick outlasts the workload burst because hardware governors enforce **state-dependent hysteresis**. To prevent the clock frequency from chattering rapidly around the trip line, the controller restores the nominal clock only after junction temperature drops below a lower clearance threshold ${T_{\text{clear}}}$. Once proposal inference halts and chip power falls back to baseline, the junction relaxes toward its baseline equilibrium:

$$
{T_{\text{j}}}({t}) = {T_{\text{j},\text{target}}} + ({T_{\text{j},\text{start}}} - {T_{\text{j},\text{target}}}) e^{-{t}/{\tau_{\text{th}}}} \tag{4}
$$

 where ${T_{\text{j},\text{start}}}$ is the junction temperature when throttling begins, ${T_{\text{j},\text{target}}}$ is the baseline equilibrium under quiescent load, and ${\tau_{\text{th}}}$ is the package thermal time constant. Solving [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.05 Thermal and Timing Coupling#^eq-placement-cooling-recovery|equation 4]] for the time required to cool from ${T_{\text{trip}}}$ to ${T_{\text{clear}}}$ gives ${t_{\text{recov}}} = {\tau_{\text{th}}} \ln[({T_{\text{j},\text{start}}} - {T_{\text{j},\text{target}}})/({T_{\text{clear}}} - {T_{\text{j},\text{target}}})]$, which fixes how long the hangover lasts in this scenario.

^eq-placement-cooling-recovery

**Napkin Math 13.2: The thermal hangover**

**Problem**: Candidate (b)’s permission cluster shares the application die’s package and clock governor. Under continuous proposal inference, does the junction reach the trip threshold, what does the throttled clock do to the tick, and for how many ticks does the overrun persist after the load stops?

**Variables**:

- ${T_{\text{amb}}} =$ 45$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, ${R_{\text{th,JA}}} =$ 2 K/W, and ${\tau_{\text{th}}} =$ 8 s (Illustrative; conduction-cooled bay).
- ${P_{\text{base}}} =$ 4 W for the permission loop with idle and memory power, and ${P_{\text{load}}} =$ 26 W with continuous proposal inference (Illustrative). The scenario holds this loaded power constant after throttling; a real device whose governor reduces power requires a coupled power-and-temperature model.
- ${T_{\text{trip}}} =$ 95$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and ${T_{\text{clear}}} =$ 85$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ (Illustrative governor thresholds).
- ${f_{\text{nom}}} =$ 1.2 GHz, halved to ${f_{\text{throt}}} =$ 600 MHz (Illustrative). The tick is sized to ${T_{\text{wcet}}} =$ 250 μs at ${f_{\text{nom}}}$ inside the 400 μs deadline and the 1 ms tick (Chosen), with 90 μs of blocking and interference charged as in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.06 Real-Time Command Transport and Bus Determinism#^eq-nervous-response-budget|equation 4.3]]** (Illustrative).

**Math**: The steady states are ${T_{\text{j},\text{target}}} =$ 45 $+$ 4 $\times$ 2 $=$ 53$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ and ${T_{\text{load}}} =$ 45 $+$ 26 $\times$ 2 $=$ 97$\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, above the trip. Rising from the baseline, the junction trips after ${\tau_{\text{th}}} \ln[({T_{\text{load}}} - {T_{\text{j},\text{target}}})/({T_{\text{load}}} - {T_{\text{trip}}})] =$ 8 $\ln[($ 97 $-$ 53 $)/($ 97 $-$ 95 $)] =$ 24.7 s. The tick needs ${N} = {T_{\text{wcet}}} {f_{\text{nom}}} =$ 300,000 cycles, which at 600 MHz take 500 μs. With blocking and interference the response grows from 340 μs to 590 μs, 190 μs past the deadline. If the load stops just as the junction trips, [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.05 Thermal and Timing Coupling#^eq-placement-cooling-recovery|equation 4]] returns the clock after ${t_{\text{recov}}} =$ 8 $\ln[($ 95 $-$ 53 $)/($ 85 $-$ 53 $)] =$ 2.18 s, which spans 2,175 ticks of 1 ms. If inference has run long enough for the junction to settle at ${T_{\text{load}}}$, cooling starts there instead and takes 8 $\ln[($ 97 $-$ 53 $)/($ 85 $-$ 53 $)] =$ 2.55 s, or 2,547 ticks.

**Result**: After about 24.7 s of continuous inference, every tick overruns its deadline by 47.5 percent for as long as inference continues, and for another 2.18 s to 2.55 s after it stops, depending on how close the junction had climbed to its loaded steady state.

**Systems insight**: While inference continues, the junction stays near ${T_{\text{load}}}$, above the trip, so the cluster remains throttled for as long as the proposer runs, which is the worse case on a machine whose chunk policy runs continuously while it moves. The hangover is the subtler one. Pausing the proposer (at a stop, or when the policy idles) does not restore the clock for at least another 2.18 s, so a remedy that sheds proposal load cannot rescue the tick in time. Throughout, a refusal misses the drive frame timed to the deadline and waits for a later one. Because the throttled response still ends inside the period, each late refusal slips by about one tick, a distance the stopping budget can absorb ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.08 Empirical Verification of Silicon Isolation#^sec-placement-testing-separation|section 13.7]]). The case still disqualifies this modeled configuration, because the loaded tick has crossed the declared bound, every margin downstream was computed on the premise it breaks, and nothing in the premise limits a real governor to halving the clock. The lever is the heat path, not the tick’s code. Any ${R_{\text{th,JA}}}$ that keeps ${P_{\text{load}}} {R_{\text{th,JA}}}$ below ${T_{\text{trip}}} - {T_{\text{amb}}}$ never trips, and an independently clocked placement is unaffected by that host governor. Its own thermal and clock limits remain separate qualification obligations.

The two timescales meet in one picture: a cooling curve measured in seconds holds the clock at 600 MHz, and every tick inside it, measured in microseconds, ends 190 μs past the deadline ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.05 Thermal and Timing Coupling#^fig-13-thermal-hysteresis|figure 3]]). The scenario is also optimistic. Over a full shift, heat accumulating in the chassis raises the baseline ${T_{\text{j},\text{target}}}$, which flattens the cooling gradient and lengthens recovery, and higher-order thermal poles in the enclosure can stretch the hysteresis by minutes. A shared-die placement would need a separately timed hardware fallback to stay safe across every exposed tick.

Heat completes the set of couplings, and placing it beside the other two shows how far apart they sit in time. The rail droop of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.04 Contention for Shared Resources#^sec-placement-resource-contention|section 13.3]] acts within nanoseconds ($10^{-9}\,\text{s}$) of an accelerator current step, while thermal coupling develops over seconds ($10^1\,\text{s}$) as heat diffuses through silicon, package, and chassis. The separation is also a diagnostic: a latency spike that correlates with rail voltage points to power delivery network (PDN) impedance, and a slowdown that correlates with temperature points to the governor. Together with the tick they threaten, the couplings span about thirteen orders of magnitude on one machine, from nanoseconds to hours:

1.  **$10^{-9}\,\text{s}$ (Nanoseconds)**: Simultaneous tensor core switching generates ${L_{\text{pkg}}} \cdot d{I}/d{t}$ inductive rail collapse, stretching transistor gate delays under the ${\alpha}$-power law within individual clock cycles.
2.  **$10^{-6}\,\text{s}$ (Microseconds)**: AXI4 crossbar arbitration and head-of-line FIFO queueing stall memory reads, as row-buffer precharge and activation penalties (${t_{\text{RP}}} + {t_{\text{RCD}}}$) of tens of nanoseconds each accumulate across a queue.
3.  **$10^{-4}\text{--}10^{-3}\,\text{s}$ (Sub-milliseconds to milliseconds)**: The protected work itself. Sensor ingestion and the control barrier filter’s quadratic program run inside the permission tick, and fieldbus frames (such as EtherCAT) repeat on deterministic 1 ms cycles.
4.  **$10^0\text{--}10^1\,\text{s}$ (Seconds)**: Macroscopic silicon die and package heatsink thermal capacitance stores dissipated energy, enforcing governor throttling hysteresis (at least 2.18 s in this scenario) that degrades thousands of control ticks.
5.  **$10^2\text{--}10^4\,\text{s}$ (Minutes to hours)**: Structural chassis thermal mass and battery Joule heating accumulate heat, shifting bay ambient temperature ${T_{\text{amb}}}$ upward and eroding cooling headroom across a multi-hour shift.

*Coupling on one chassis spans nanoseconds to seconds.*

Because thermal mass and heat dissipation operate across seconds and hours, dynamic thermal management (DTM) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.13 Source References#^ref-brooks2001dynamic|Brooks and Martonosi 2001]]) and reactive thread migration cannot restore a co-located permission loop before its next deadline expires. A safety-critical permission path requires guaranteed lower bounds on clock frequency and power supply integrity that survive sustained accelerator workloads. When a shared die cannot guarantee them, moving the permission engine onto an independently clocked and independently powered microcontroller removes the host-governor coupling, though the microcontroller must still stay within its own qualified supply, clock, and thermal limits under the common chassis conditions.
