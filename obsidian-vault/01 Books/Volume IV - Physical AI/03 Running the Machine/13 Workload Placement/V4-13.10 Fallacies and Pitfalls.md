# V4-13.10 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement|V4-13 Workload Placement]]

^sec-placement-fallacies-pitfalls

Placement determines the physical resources shared by complex proposal algorithms and critical enforcement routines. Traces of normal operation cannot prove these systems are truly independent when rare physical events, such as power voltage transients, clock faults, memory contention, or a stalled producer, stress those shared hardware pathways.

**Fallacy**: *Logical privilege rings protect real-time enforcers from shared-rail electrical droop.*

Privilege checks govern digital access, while the rail supplies the gates that perform those checks. The illustrative droop calculation predicts a 39 percent gate-delay increase under its stated model; only device characterization can determine the resulting setup margin or reset behavior. Lockstep cores sharing a rail can share a failure, so comparator coverage does not establish supply independence. The selected isolated rail removes one coupling path and still requires common-supply fault tests.

**Pitfall**: *Sharing phase-locked loops or reset controllers across proposal and permission domains.*

A separate core can still stop when a host-controlled reset asserts or slow when a shared governor changes its clock. The topology must trace each clock and reset to the controlling hardware, then test the relevant faults under load. Naming a controller a safety island does not establish that these dependencies are independent.

**Fallacy**: *Low average memory bus utilization guarantees bounded read latency for real-time safety tasks.*

Average utilization leaves the arrival order and longest queue unspecified. Plain FIFO queueing at the SoC’s full bandwidth is not the danger, as notebook [13.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/13_placement/13_placement.html#nbk-placement-loaded-read) showed. The read tail comes from an FR-FCFS controller that keeps passing the safety read in favor of a streaming accelerator’s row hits, or from the miss-handling contention of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.04 Contention for Shared Resources#^sec-placement-interference-mechanisms|section 13.3.2]], and neither shows in an average. In an illustrative counterexample, such a tail stretches the snapshot read to 320 μs, which leaves 80 μs before the 400 μs deadline, less than the 95 μs needed for evaluation and staging even before blocking and interference are charged. A service bound, qualified bypass, or independent local memory addresses that failure; a low average does not.

**Pitfall**: *Using blocking software mutexes across inter-processor communication boundaries with untrusted neural policies.*

A mutex held by a stalled proposer transfers control of the enforcement deadline to that proposer. An unbounded seqlock retry loop has the same defect when publication remains odd. The selected boundary protocol makes one read attempt per tick and discards a collision. That discard leaves an active valid command in force only while its own evidence, state, and lease remain admissible; it neither resets the lease nor forces premature revocation. Memory ordering and bounded copy time remain part of the placement evidence.
