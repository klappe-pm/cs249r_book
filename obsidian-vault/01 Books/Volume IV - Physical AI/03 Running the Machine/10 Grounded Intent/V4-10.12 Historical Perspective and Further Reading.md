# V4-10.12 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-history

How a word comes to name a thing was a question long before robots acted on the answer. Gibson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-gibson1979ecological|1979]]) gave the thing its modern name, the affordance, what an object offers an agent able to act on it, and Harnad ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-harnad1990symbol|1990]]) posed the grounding problem as a question about symbols that point only to other symbols. Robotics made the affordance quantitative long before learned models proposed one. Nguyen ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-nguyen1988constructing|1988]]) reduced a stable two-finger grasp to a geometric test on friction cones, and Ferrari and Canny ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ferrari1992planning|1992]]) turned force closure into a quality measure in wrench space. Learned grasp generators now propose such grasps directly from point clouds ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-sundermeyer2021contactgraspnet|Sundermeyer et al. 2021]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-fang2023anygrasp|Fang et al. 2023]]), but their scores estimate success on a training distribution and are not the analytic measures they replaced, which is why this chapter rechecks every survivor.

Symbolic planning began on a machine that moved. Fikes and Nilsson ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-fikes1971strips|1971]]) wrote the STRIPS operator language to plan for Shakey, a mobile robot whose actions did not always have the effects their operators listed ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-nilsson1984shakey|Nilsson 1984]]), and PDDL later standardized that language so that planners could be compared on common problems ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-mcdermott1998pddl|McDermott et al. 1998]]). The geometric preconditions that STRIPS left to the robot returned as task and motion planning, which interleaves symbolic search with samplers that certify continuous facts ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-kaelbling2011hierarchical|Kaelbling and Lozano-Pérez 2011]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-garrett2021integrated|Garrett et al. 2021]]). Language models now write the symbolic half, by ranking skills ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ahn2022saycan|Ahn et al. 2022]]) or by translating a request into a planning problem ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liu2023llmp|B. Liu et al. 2023]]), and neither step checks the geometry.

The lease comes from distributed systems. Gray and Cheriton ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-gray1989leases|1989]]) bounded a client’s authority over a cached file by time, so that a crashed holder could not block the others, and chose the term by weighing renewal traffic against the delay a conflicting write must wait. The calibrated refusal comes from statistics. Vovk et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-vovk2005algorithmic|2005]]) built conformal prediction on exchangeability alone, and Ren et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ren2023robots|2023]]) carried it to a robot deciding when to ask for help. The motion lease adds a physical term: it is sized by how fast the evidence behind its target decays, not by clock skew or message cost, and its expiry is enforced on a processor that the proposer cannot stall. For further reading, six works trace the ideas this chapter composes:

- Harnad ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-harnad1990symbol|1990]]) states the symbol grounding problem and argues that symbols acquire meaning only through their connection to sensorimotor categories, the premise behind carrying evidence with every proposal.
- Garrett et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-garrett2021integrated|2021]]) surveys task and motion planning, from operator languages and samplers to the planners that couple them, and shows where symbolic plans need geometric certificates.
- Gray and Cheriton ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-gray1989leases|1989]]) introduces leases for file-cache consistency and analyzes how the lease term trades renewal traffic against availability, the analysis this chapter reworks with evidence drift as the term.
- Angelopoulos and Bates ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-angelopoulos2023gentle|2023]]) is a practical introduction to conformal prediction, with the coverage argument and its exchangeability assumption stated plainly, as [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^eq-intent-conformal-set|equation 3]] uses them.
- Ren et al. ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ren2023robots|2023]]) applies conformal prediction sets to a language-model planner, turning calibrated ambiguity into a request for help, the producer-side refusal of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^sec-intent-four-stage-pipeline|section 10.3.1]].
- Trautman and Krause ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-trautman2010unfreezing|2010]]) names the freezing robot problem and shows why independent forecasts that grow with time block every path in a crowd, the trade-off behind the forward occupancy screen of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^sec-intent-dynamic-forecasting|section 10.6.1]].

**What’s Next: From intent leases to trajectory planning**

When the next inference pass is late, an intent lease fixes the admissible target region, the effort ceilings, and the evidence deadline, but it specifies no timed velocity or acceleration profile. It cannot tell an arm already moving toward the mug how to continue while the proposer stalls. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11 Trajectory Planning#^sec-planning|Trajectory Planning]]** begins from that moment. It turns the lease into a timed trajectory that joins the motion already under way without a step in acceleration, sizes its replacement deadline against the inference tail, and attaches a precomputed stop that runs even if no later pass returns. The trajectory record carries all three, bound to the lease’s digest, and the permission path admits the complete bundle before execution, because a valid goal does not prove that a moving arm can still come to rest.
