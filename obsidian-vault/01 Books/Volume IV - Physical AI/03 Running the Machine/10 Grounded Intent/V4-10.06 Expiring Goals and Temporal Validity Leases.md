# V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-goals-expire

Suppose the host application processor hangs while the robotic arm reaches for the red mug on the moving belt. The fault that makes canceling the reach necessary is the very fault that prevents a cancellation command from being sent. An explicit abort requires a working sender, scheduler, and network link, while the belt carries the mug beyond the grasp tolerance regardless of when the model last published a coordinate. An intent lease must therefore expire autonomously from the physics of environmental drift. Autonomous expiry applies the third law, proposal is not permission (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]]), to physical goals, so that the permission side and not the proposer decides when authority ends. In distributed systems, Gray and Cheriton ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-gray1989leases|1989]]) introduced leases to maintain cache consistency: a coordinator grants time-bounded authority that lapses unless explicitly renewed, preventing a crashed client from blocking others indefinitely. That correctness proof assumes bounded clock drift between client and coordinator. In an embodied system, the Safety MCU enforces this assumption by transforming every incoming timestamped epoch into its own local monotonic timebase with a strictly bounded synchronization error. The physical domain raises the stakes, because an expired cache lease merely delays a read, whereas an unexpired motion lease lets moving mass keep consuming physical clearance unchecked. The privileged Safety MCU acts as coordinator, issuing a transient tracking lease to the unprivileged deliberative host. If the host stalls during inference, pauses for garbage collection, or experiences an operating system kernel panic, the Safety MCU’s local hardware countdown expires and automatically engages a validated fallback response. This requires no network packet or cancellation command from the failed host.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn8|⁸]]

Lease duration is measured exclusively against the Safety MCU’s monotonic hardware clock.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn9|⁹]] Network time-synchronization daemons or operator adjustments can step wall-clock time abruptly backward or forward, while host-level software watchdogs can freeze alongside the user-space process they are designated to monitor. Furthermore, no inference pipeline, regardless of its mean throughput, completely eliminates long-tail p99 scheduling stalls. Placing the lease countdown on an isolated, hardware-timed MCU counter ensures that target authority is revoked locally the instant the lease expires. This hardware isolation decouples expiry detection from host operating system jitter, although total physical stopping distance must still account for fieldbus transmission, drive response, and kinematic deceleration.

The deadline the lease enforces is the evidence horizon ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$ of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** put to a new use. There it bounded how long the evidence behind a chunk keeps the tracked mug within tolerance (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^eq-brain-evidence-horizon|equation 3.5]]**). Here the intent lease starts from the same quantity, and memory supplies its value. The mug-handle belief record carries an invalidation horizon ${T_{\text{inv}}}$ (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-invalidation-horizon|equation 9.14]]**), the elapsed time before its error growth law (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion|equation 9.12]]**) reaches the task tolerance. The lease reads that horizon with the grasp tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}}$ as the error ceiling and the initial sensor error ${e_0}$ as the starting bound.

For a conveyor pick, the growth law charges two bounded effects against that ceiling:

$$
{E}({\Delta} {t}) = {e_0} + {v_{\text{drift}}}\,{\Delta} {t} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,{a_{\text{slip}}}\,{\Delta} {t}^2 \tag{4}
$$

 Here ${v_{\text{drift}}}$ bounds the relative velocity between the gripper frame and the target, including both conveyor motion and unmodeled mobile-base odometry drift, and ${a_{\text{slip}}}$ bounds the residual slip acceleration of the mug on the belt. The evidence-only horizon is the elapsed time at which ${E}({\Delta} {t})$ reaches ${r_{\text{tol}}}$. The numerical examples in this chapter evaluate this upper ceiling with zero additional controller tracking reserve. An admission gate, however, must first subtract a separately qualified nonnegative scalar tracking reserve from ${r_{\text{tol}}}$. It then solves [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^eq-intent-evidence-growth|equation 4]] against the reduced budget. It refuses immediately if the initial target error plus that reserve already reaches the tolerance (alongside the full 3D error-set containment check of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]]). The two limiting regimes are horizons derived earlier in the volume. With no tracker, the horizon is the linear horizon of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-linear-horizon|equation 9.15]]**, with belt speed as its growth rate. With a tracker that cancels belt velocity, the horizon is the slip-only root of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^eq-brain-evidence-horizon|equation 3.5]]**. In this chapter’s symbols they read

$$
{\tau_{\text{ev,untracked}}} = \frac{{r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0}}{{v_{\text{drift}}}} \quad ({a_{\text{slip}}} = 0), \qquad {\tau_{\text{ev,tracked}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2({r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0})}{{a_{\text{slip}}}}} \quad ({v_{\text{drift}}} = 0) \tag{5}
$$

 These evidence-only roots are upper ceilings on target lifetime. When drift velocity and slip acceleration act together, ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$ is the positive root of ${E}({\Delta} {t}) = {r_{\text{tol}}}$ in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^eq-intent-evidence-growth|equation 4]], which is strictly shorter than either single-effect root.

If ${r_{\text{tol}}} \le {e_0}$, the target is inadmissible at the moment of perception. Initial measurement uncertainty alone consumes the entire task tolerance, which obligates the safety controller to reject dependent motion commands immediately. Even when the evidence horizon is strictly positive, granting motion permission requires a separate geometric verification. The system must verify that current physical clearance accommodates total sensing latency, permission evaluation time, fieldbus transmission delay, mechanical brake engagement, and physical braking distance.

For a red mug carried on a takeaway conveyor, the untracked linear root dictates the evidence deadline. Sizing the control pipeline requires determining whether deliberative intent models can refresh targets rapidly enough to renew authority before this window elapses. While the evidence-horizon calculation in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** sized the low-level chunk execution policy against this horizon, deliberative intent models evaluate on a substantially slower cycle.

**Napkin Math 10.2: The cadence gap**

**Problem**: The conveyor carries the mug at 0.20 m/s. Can the 2 Hz intent model keep the target inside its tolerance by re-proposing it, and how slowly would the belt have to run for a model-only renewal to suffice?

**Variables**:

- Grasp tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}} =$ 15 mm and initial handle error ${e_0} =$ 3 mm (Illustrative)
- Belt speed ${v_{\text{drift}}} =$ 200 mm/s (Illustrative)
- Intent period ${T_{\text{period}}} =$ 500 ms (Chosen, 2 Hz) and inference ${t_{\text{infer}}} =$ 336 ms (Derived, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.05 The Cost of a Fresh Evaluation#^sec-brain-memory-wall|The memory wall]]**)

**Math**:

1.  **Horizon** ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^eq-intent-evidence-horizon-roots|equation 5]]): with no tracker, ${\tau_{\text{ev}}} = {\tau_{\text{ev,untracked}}} =$ (15 mm $-$ 3 mm) $/$ 200 mm/s $=$ 60 ms.
2.  **Error at one period**: 3 mm $+$ 200 mm/s $\times$ 500 ms $=$ 103 mm, 88 mm past the tolerance. Inference alone overruns the horizon by 336 ms $-$ 60 ms $=$ 276 ms.
3.  **Renewal ceiling**: Each model-only renewal installs a target computed from a frame captured one inference earlier, and that target stays in force until the next renewal replaces it one period later. Just before the replacement, the evidence behind it is ${T_{\text{period}}} + {t_{\text{infer}}} =$ 836 ms old (the reaction latency of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-action-chunking|Action chunking]]**, here for the intent model), so the drift it can keep up with is (15 mm $-$ 3 mm) $/$ 836 ms $=$ 14.4 mm/s.

**Result**: The model alone could renew the target only on a belt running at 7.2 percent of its speed. Even a slow settling drift of 20 mm/s, such as a mug still sliding to rest after the belt stops, whose 600 ms horizon is charted against inference latency in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^fig-10-lease-dynamics-tradeoff|figure 6]], is too fast for it.

**Systems insight**: Physical drift and allowable tolerance dictate ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$, independent of computing performance. An intent renewal can extend operational authority only if the sum of its dispatch period and inference latency fits entirely within this window. Artificially inflating the lease duration to cover inference delay leaves the pose estimate outside its declared tolerance envelope for 276 ms. Without a qualified, high-frequency local tracker, the system must either slow or stop the conveyor, or refuse the pick task entirely.

The 60 ms limit is a physical evidence deadline that only fresh sensor observations, validated against current motion, can extend. Faster deliberative inference does not lengthen this physical evidence horizon, although a sufficiently fast capture-to-admission pipeline and renewal cadence can fit inside it.

Deploying a qualified local tracker changes these dynamics. By re-measuring the mug in every camera frame and matching conveyor velocity, the local tracking loop prevents conveyor speed from driving error growth. While the initial perception uncertainty of 3 mm still counts against the grasp tolerance, it leaves a target-only error margin of 12 mm for unmodeled physical perturbations before reserving controller tracking error. Sizing against an illustrative worst-case residual slip acceleration of 0.50 m/s², this remaining margin yields the same 219 ms tracked horizon that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** computed for the chunk policy. Because the local tracking pipeline runs at high frame rates, only the tracker’s brief period plus sensing latency must fit within this expanded horizon. The tracker also changes who renews the lease. Each tracked frame yields a newer belief record of the same handle, and the producer re-proposes the same referent against it. The Safety MCU therefore receives renewals with a newer capture epoch on the tracker’s cadence rather than the model’s. Relieved of geometric tracking, the deliberative intent model only re-confirms semantic identity (which mug to grasp) at its own rate, and no renewal waits for a fresh inference. The lease attaches its target to the handle’s affordance frame, whose base-frame pose under a qualified tracker follows the belt. The goal set therefore moves with the mug. The tolerance bounds only how far the handle departs from the tracked estimate under residual slip, rather than how far it departs from a fixed point in the base frame. Whichever horizon governs the active operating mode caps the duration of the intent lease granted for that target ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]]).

Examining a host processor stall illustrates the protection each horizon provides. Suppose the robotic arm reaches toward the red mug under a target admitted without local tracking, and the application processor stalls during neural network inference. If the system held that unvalidated target across a 200 ms stall, the actual mug position would drift 43 mm away from the commanded setpoint, causing the gripper to close on empty air or strike the rim. The Safety MCU does not rely on host health to catch this fault. In the ideal zero-additional-reserve case, at 60 ms the lease expires automatically, commanding braking to begin within 22 ms (accounting for one permission evaluation tick, one fieldbus cycle, and actuator brake onset), even if the current chunk’s own lease has time left. In contrast, under a qualified local tracker, an identical 200 ms stall leaves the target estimate displaced by only 13 mm, inside the target-only tolerance budget, because the ideal tracked horizon extends to 219 ms. Delegating fast geometric updates to local tracking is what allows a 2 Hz deliberative intent model to operate safely in a dynamic physical environment.

On one clock, the proposer’s timing loses to both horizons ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^fig-10-mug-lease-timeline|figure 5]]). Both idealized zero-additional-reserve countdowns end before the 336 ms inference returns and well before the next proposal at 500 ms, so the model can renew neither lease. Absent other renewals, each expiry is followed by the 22 ms stop onset. Under a qualified tracker the tracked countdown restarts with every tracked frame, long before it reaches 219 ms, so the tracked bar in the figure shows what happens only when those renewals stop, as they do when the host stalls. What follows that expiry is still motion, as panel (b) of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-intent-lease-envelope|figure 4]] sketches in speed against time. The drive begins braking 22 ms after expiry, and the stopping distance under the falling curve must already fit the clearance that remains. This is why the lease carries the stopping deadline as a term of its own ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]]).

Sustaining continuous tracking safely requires *fresh physical evidence*, not merely retransmitting a new packet computed from stale sensor observations. Every renewal must derive from an independent physical capture and reach the Safety MCU before the active lease expires. The end-to-end timing budget must account for image capture, inference processing, fieldbus transmission, clock synchronization uncertainty, and permission verification. The timing condition ${T_{\text{period}}} + {L_{\text{lat}}} < {\tau_{\text{ev}}}$ serves only as a necessary scheduling check. When a renewed proposal arrives, the trajectory planner computes a jerk-limited splice originating from the measured physical state. The safety controller admits this spliced trajectory only if the new swept volume, refreshed evidence validity, and emergency stopping reserves remain fully verified. An abrupt target displacement that violates kinematic or clearance bounds triggers immediate refusal or a controlled stop.

Safely executing a bounded lease presupposes that the commanded geometric target is physically achievable by the robot. If a proposed coordinate falls outside kinematic reach or demands joint velocities that exceed actuator limits across the lease window, granting the lease squanders control authority. It also risks tripping drive overcurrent protection as motor controllers saturate attempting to reach unattainable velocities. Determining whether an incoming goal is kinematically and dynamically admissible must precede trajectory generation, establishing a physical admissibility filter between high-level deliberative intent and real-time execution.
