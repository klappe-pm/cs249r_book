# V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-request-to-proposal

When an operator asks for the red mug and the vision backbone instead binds the word “mug” to an adjacent table fixture, the coordinate that crosses the proposal boundary arrives in the trajectory pipeline indistinguishable from a valid handle target. Low-level motor drives monitor encoder counts, winding temperatures, and phase currents, so they have no way to tell whether the setpoint they track belongs to a mug or a bolted bracket. Any unmodeled arm compliance or thermal expansion also adds metric bias that a high semantic confidence score never reveals. Before any motion planner accepts a target, the grounding pipeline must therefore turn the model’s selection into a geometric proposal that carries bounded spatial uncertainty, dated selection evidence, and an explicit expiration time.

### The Four-Stage Open-Vocabulary Grounding Pipeline

^sec-intent-four-stage-pipeline

Passing a detector’s 2D bounding box or an uncalibrated pixel coordinate straight to a motion planner hides two failures at once. Two identical mugs can both score high without triggering an ambiguity warning, and a small depth error along the camera ray can stretch into centimeters of pose error in the robot base frame. To expose each failure where it can be checked, the **four-stage open-vocabulary grounding pipeline** separates semantic referent selection from metric localization and physical actuation:

1.  **Stage 1: Visual encoding**: RGB-D frames from static or eye-in-hand cameras enter a vision backbone running at batch size one on the machine’s accelerator, transforming raw image patches into dense feature embeddings.
2.  **Stage 2: Referent selection**: Given a prompt such as “pick the red ceramic mug,” an open-vocabulary detector such as Grounding DINO or OWL-ViT proposes candidate bounding boxes and scores each against the text prompt ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liu2023grounding|S. Liu et al. 2023]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-minderer2022simple|Minderer et al. 2022]]). The model evaluates each candidate independently without global normalization across boxes (OWL-ViT trains with a sigmoid focal loss ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-minderer2022simple|Minderer et al. 2022]]), while Grounding DINO filters tokens against an absolute threshold). Two identical mugs can therefore both yield high confidence, or a lone glass jar can score above threshold even when no mug is present. A promptable segmentation model such as SAM 2 can refine the chosen box into an object mask and track it across video frames ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ravi2024sam2|Ravi et al. 2024]]). However, a 2D bounding box or segmentation mask provides only image-space localization without metric depth, surface normals, or wrench limits. It indicates where the object projects onto the camera sensor, leaving contact dynamics and grasp mechanics completely unspecified.
3.  **Stage 3: Metric lifting**: Calibrated camera intrinsics, depth measurements, and the hand-eye extrinsic transformation lift the selected pixels into 3D points in the robot base frame, while propagating sensor noise into a base-frame covariance matrix. For stereo and structured-light systems, depth error grows quadratically with range, ${\sigma_z} \approx {Z_c}^2{\sigma_d}/({b} {f})$ for baseline ${b}$ (the stereo term of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^eq-percep-unified-uncertainty-envelope|equation 8.6]]**), focal length ${f}$, and disparity error ${\sigma_d}$, whereas lateral error grows only linearly, ${\sigma_u} {Z_c}/{f}$. Their ratio, approximately $({Z_c}/{b})({\sigma_d}/{\sigma_u})$, scales with range and baseline rather than remaining constant, while time-of-flight sensors have their own range-dependent error profiles. The resulting uncertainty ellipsoid elongates dramatically along the optical line of sight. Any unmodeled extrinsic error from wrist compliance or mounting deflection tilts this elongated ellipsoid directly into the robot’s clearance margin.
4.  **Stage 4: Affordance parameterization**: The lifted 3D geometry transforms into candidate actions. Depending on the architecture, these candidates may take the form of scored 6-DoF grasp sets from a learned grasp generator, 3D attraction and repulsion cost fields ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-huang2023voxposer|Huang et al. 2023]]), parameterized motion primitives ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liang2023code|Liang et al. 2023]]), or decoded action tokens ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-brohan2023rt1|Brohan, Brown, Carbajal, Chebotar, Dabis, et al. 2023]]). Stage 4 filters these candidates against the kinematic reachability, gripper aperture, and collision clearance criteria detailed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.02 Grounded Affordances#^sec-intent-grounded-affordances|section 10.1]]. It then constructs a goal tolerance set ${\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}$ around each surviving affordance frame and assigns the required motion predicates. Finally, it serializes these hypotheses into a fixed-size, statically verified proposal structure ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^tbl-10-intent-proposal-fields|table 2]]) and carries them across the physical silicon boundary between the non-real-time Linux Application Processor (AP) and the hard real-time Safety MCU.

To prevent a learned output from masquerading as a deterministic setpoint, the producer must link the proposed referent to the dated sensor evidence that justified its selection. The upstream observation contract often carries bounding boxes, attention weights, class probabilities, and a full oriented covariance matrix, which are too bulky and variable to send across a real-time link. Instead, the intent proposal references its parent belief record by a SHA-256 digest (which traces data lineage rather than authenticating the sender) and an evidence epoch. Because the digest, a monotonically increasing sequence number, and a trailing CRC-32 checksum belong to the common wire header (**[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-header|Proposal header and chunk payload]]**), [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^tbl-10-intent-proposal-fields|table 2]] lists only the intent payload. The payload holds the candidate pose, conservative base-frame error bounds, task tolerances, and a requested administrative lease ${T_{\text{admin}}}$ that caps how long the host may hold the target before the MCU presumes a stall. That duration is only a request, because the MCU clamps it to the evidence horizon and the stopping deadline derived in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^sec-intent-goals-expire|section 10.5]] and [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]]. Since an object on the belt can shift, roll, or become occluded moments after camera exposure, the permission path immediately rejects any target coordinate that arrives without an active evidence link and a bounded deadline.

When a vision-language model evaluates multiple candidate objects, retaining only the highest-scoring bounding box conceals an unresolved selection. In purely digital applications, a misranked recommendation misleads a user but moves no mass. In robotics, an admitted spatial proposal immediately commands physical momentum. For instance, if a red glass jar sits beside the requested red mug on a conveyor, an uncalibrated tie-break may inadvertently target the jar. This case, the jar beside the mug, returns later in the chapter. The architecture must preserve candidate identity and calibrated uncertainty, rejecting proposals whenever ambiguity exceeds task-specific safety thresholds. The host system can then solicit operator clarification or acquire additional sensor viewpoints, while any previously admitted motion executes under strict lease and fallback guarantees.

Formally, **open-vocabulary grounding ambiguity** occurs when perceptual evidence supports multiple candidate referents, making the selection of any single target an arbitrary tie-break. Because open-vocabulary detectors score candidate bounding boxes independently rather than generating a normalized categorical distribution across objects, they do not natively report this condition. The host application must therefore construct a distribution explicitly. By collecting all candidate boxes exceeding an initial detection threshold and normalizing their raw scores into a vector ${\mathbf{c}} = ({c}_1, \dots, {c}_n)$ where $\sum_i {c}_i = 1$, the system can evaluate candidate spread. Although the Shannon entropy ${H}({\mathbf{c}}) = -\sum_i {c}_i \log {c}_i$ quantifies distributional dispersion, an arbitrary entropy threshold provides no rigorous statistical error bound.

**Conformal prediction** removes this limitation by replacing the single best answer with a set of candidate answers whose coverage holds in finite samples ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-vovk2005algorithmic|Vovk et al. 2005]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-angelopoulos2023gentle|Angelopoulos and Bates 2023]]). A threshold calibrated on held-out labeled cases keeps every candidate it cannot exclude, so the set contains the true answer at a chosen rate across cases without assuming any model of how the scores are distributed. For example, the KnowNo framework applies conformal prediction to identify when a robot should halt and request human clarification ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ren2023robots|Ren et al. 2023]]). On a calibration dataset of ${n_{\text{cal}}}$ labeled scenes, the nonconformity score for scene ${j}$ is ${s}_j = 1 - {c_{y_j}}$, representing one minus the normalized score assigned to the ground-truth referent ${y}_j$. This construction assumes that the true label belongs to the defined candidate option space in calibration and deployment. An absent or filtered-out true object violates that premise unless the option space explicitly includes a no-valid-referent option calibrated in the same way. The critical threshold ${\widehat{q}_{1-\epsilon}}$ corresponds to the $\lceil ({n_{\text{cal}}}+1)(1-{\epsilon})\rceil / {n_{\text{cal}}}$ empirical quantile of these nonconformity scores. At runtime, the prediction set ${\mathcal{C}}$ retains every candidate whose normalized score cannot be excluded at significance level ${\epsilon}$:

$$
{\mathcal{C}} = \{\, {i} : {c}_i \ge 1 - {\widehat{q}_{1-\epsilon}} \,\} \tag{3}
$$

 Provided that calibration and deployment scenes satisfy exchangeability,[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn6|⁶]] the set ${\mathcal{C}}$ is mathematically guaranteed to cover the true referent with probability at least $1-{\epsilon}$. Under exchangeability, the nonconformity score of a new test scene is equally likely to occupy any of the ${n_{\text{cal}}}+1$ ranks relative to the calibration samples. It therefore exceeds the $\lceil ({n_{\text{cal}}}+1)(1-{\epsilon})\rceil$-th smallest calibration score with probability at most ${\epsilon}$. This is marginal true-label coverage across scenes, not the same error bound conditional on singleton acceptance or a guarantee of task success. If the resulting prediction set is a singleton ($|{\mathcal{C}}| = 1$), the pipeline passes its candidate to downstream metric lifting and the remaining admission checks. If $|{\mathcal{C}}| > 1$, the producer refuses with `ERR_GROUNDING_AMBIGUITY`, prompting the system to request clarification from an operator or maneuver for an unoccluded viewpoint before transmitting any motion proposal across the boundary. An empty set ($|{\mathcal{C}}| = 0$) means that no retained option meets the threshold, and the producer refuses it as well. It does not reliably detect an absent mug, because a lone retained wrong jar normalizes to one and survives this score test.

^eq-intent-conformal-set

When both the mug and the glass jar appear simultaneously within the pick window, an open-vocabulary detector assigns raw box scores of 0.62 and 0.48 respectively. Normalizing these values yields normalized candidate scores of 0.56 and 0.44, producing a Shannon entropy of 0.685 nats relative to the two-candidate maximum of 0.693 nats. The entropy shows high dispersion across the two candidates. It does not measure the probability that either is the correct object. Conformal calibration instead supplies marginal true-label coverage under the declared option-space and exchangeability premises.

Across a calibration set of 200 labeled scenes with an allowable error rate ${\epsilon} =$ 0.10, the rank formula selects which calibration score becomes the threshold ${\widehat{q}_{1-\epsilon}}$, the nonconformity score at rank 181 in ascending order. Its value depends on the measured scenes. Assuming that score is ${\widehat{q}_{1-\epsilon}} =$ 0.60, any candidate must achieve a normalized score of at least 0.40 to remain in the prediction set. Because both objects exceed this threshold, the resulting prediction set retains two elements ($|{\mathcal{C}}| = 2$). The producer correctly flags an ambiguity error and refuses to emit a trajectory. Conversely, if the jar scores only 0.10, its normalized value drops to 0.14, falling below the admission threshold. In that contrast case, the prediction set collapses to a singleton ($|{\mathcal{C}}| = 1$), permitting the mug proposal to proceed.

Because conformal coverage guarantees hold marginally across scenes and degrade under distribution shift, production workcells enforce defense-in-depth through deterministic physical invariants. The station therefore adds its own singulation check, a candidate count on fresh depth evidence that runs apart from the grounding model and must find a unique candidate within the pick envelope ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.08 Intent Grounding Failure Modes and Their Checks#^sec-intent-failure-modes|section 10.7]]). This operational gate prevents arbitrary tie-breaking at the station. When multiple objects populate the workspace, downstream motion planners proceed only when perceptual evidence unambiguously discriminates the requested target.

The pipeline therefore spans two processors that share no memory ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-vla-cross-attention-intent-lease|figure 1]]). Everything that can be wrong about meaning runs on the application processor, and everything that must hold regardless of meaning runs on the Safety MCU. Its 1 kHz permission loop arms a lease timer that drops authority without any message from the host. That separation buys independence only when memory access, bus timing, and fault containment are qualified in each of three domains:

- *Application Processor (AP Domain):* A general-purpose, non-real-time operating system executing on host CPUs and hardware accelerators runs the vision-language backbones and, in architectures that use them, 3D cost fields. If the conformal prediction set over candidate referents contains multiple entries ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^eq-intent-conformal-set|equation 3]]), an upstream filter halts execution with an ambiguity alert. When the prediction set yields a unique referent ($|{\mathcal{C}}| = 1$), the AP serializes the candidate goal pose, requested wrench limits, and parent belief digest into a fixed-size, statically structured intent proposal.
- *Board-Level Transport Boundary:* The proposal packet crosses from the AP to the discrete safety MCU over an isolated board-level interconnect, such as a Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI) bus backed by Direct Memory Access (DMA). The MCU receiver copies the transmission into a dedicated staging mailbox within local SRAM. The communication channel is treated as untrusted. Embedded CRC-32 checksums, monotonically increasing sequence numbers, and strict hardware timeout limits detect frame corruption, packet loss, and transmission stalls. The protocol design must also account for worst-case bus latency, as the bus interface alone guarantees no real-time delivery bounds.
- *Safety Microcontroller Unit (MCU Domain):* Operating on a dedicated real-time core, a deterministic control loop evaluates the incoming proposal as an unverified request. The MCU checks the evidence timestamp against its internal monotonic clock, verifies workspace envelopes and joint reachability limits, and clamps requested forces and velocities to certified plant limits. Furthermore, the MCU verifies that a deceleration trajectory can execute safely within existing clearance margins. The MCU arms a hardware countdown timer to the lease expiry ${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$ only after the proposal passes all admission gates. The expiry is the earliest of evidence decay, host silence, and the stopping deadline ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]] formalizes it). The MCU then grants bounded tracking authority to the joint drives.

### What Each Proposal Family Leaves to Admission

^sec-intent-proposal-families

This three-domain boundary stays fixed even when the upstream grounding model is swapped for a different proposal family. What changes from one model family to the next is which physical checks remain for admission to supply. An upstream model may emit a 2D bounding box, a 3D cost field, a scored 6-DoF grasp, or a chunk of normalized action tokens. In every case, treating that output directly as a motion command skips the physical checks that prevent an unseen obstacle collision, a joint-limit hit, or an unbraked contact impact. The pipeline’s stages draw on multimodal feature extraction, cross-attention alignment, and open-vocabulary phrase grounding from computer vision and natural language processing ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-kamath2021mdetr|Kamath et al. 2021]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-gu2022open|Gu et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-radford2021learning|Radford et al. 2021]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liu2023grounding|S. Liu et al. 2023]]), yet an embodied machine cannot rely on a model’s internal activations for physical safety. For example, a semantic skill ranker like SayCan outputs a discrete skill identifier scored by language affordance and value functions ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ahn2022saycan|Ahn et al. 2022]]). A programmatic generator like Code as Policies produces a control script that calls perception and motion APIs ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liang2023code|Liang et al. 2023]]). VoxPoser synthesizes 3D spatial value maps ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-value-map-composition|figure 2]]). OK-Robot emits a distribution of sampled grasp poses ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-real-open-vocab-grounding|figure 3]]). Because none of these outputs natively carries a bounded wrench envelope, a certified clearance margin, or a hardware lease expiration, choosing one model family over another only shifts where conversion happens and leaves the admission checks in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^tbl-10-grounding-paradigms|table 3]] unchanged.

Predicting a syntactically valid coordinate or control script never guarantees physical feasibility. Vision-language and action models are trained to maximize sequence likelihood over offline demonstration datasets. Their loss functions encode neither metric distances, rigid-body collision avoidance, manipulator Jacobian conditioning, nor frictional contact dynamics. Validating a candidate action requires deterministic physical checks evaluated within explicit coordinate frames. These checks include signed-distance queries over robot and environment meshes to verify clearance, numerical inverse-kinematics solvers with joint-limit and singularity screening, and Coulomb friction models to confirm that the planned grasp wrench resists expected gravitational and inertial loads. For contact-rich tasks such as sliding an object, turning a latch, or executing a handover, the system can simulate candidate trajectories in a rigid-body physics engine before committing actuator current. However, simulation fidelity remains bounded by the accuracy of estimated surface friction and inertial parameters. Downstream controllers must still enforce real-time admission checks regardless of simulation outcomes.

OK-Robot shows the same division in a modular pipeline ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-real-open-vocab-grounding|figure 3]]) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liu2024okrobot|Liu et al. 2024]]). Open-vocabulary detectors (OWL-ViT and SAM) isolate the queried object, a class-agnostic generator (AnyGrasp) samples dense 6-DoF candidates over the point cloud, and geometric heuristics prune them to a single pose. That pose is still an uncertified Stage 4 proposal. Before it can command joint motion, it must be bound to dated sensor evidence, given a wrench envelope, and admitted under an expiring lease on the Safety MCU. Every proposal family in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^tbl-10-grounding-paradigms|table 3]] leaves a comparable residue, which the table’s last column itemizes as the evidence admission must still supply.

Regardless of the specific output architecture, raw actions generated by VLA models carry no certified metric bounds. These heads normalize action spaces relative to empirical training distributions rather than physical workspace obstacles. For example, RT-1 and OpenVLA discretize each continuous action dimension into 256 uniform bins across bounded ranges ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-brohan2023rt1|Brohan, Brown, Carbajal, Chebotar, Dabis, et al. 2023]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-kim2024openvla|Kim et al. 2024]]). FAST tokenizes the discrete cosine transform coefficients of a multi-step trajectory chunk ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-pertsch2025fast|Pertsch et al. 2025]]). In contrast, $\pi_0$ generates continuous action chunks through a flow-matching diffusion expert, predicting 50 steps per inference and streaming them at up to 50 Hz ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-black2024pi0|Black et al. 2024]]). Although each approach outputs kinematically coherent trajectories within its training distribution, none verifies dynamic feasibility or collision clearance against surrounding obstacles. Similarly, spatial location tokens in vision-language models like PaliGemma quantize normalized pixel coordinates on the camera sensor. These image tokens transform into metric poses only after incorporating measured depth maps, camera intrinsics, and hand-eye calibration matrices. The geometric uncertainty propagated through this calibration chain, particularly along the camera’s optical axis, often dwarfs the discretization error of the underlying token grid.

Every row leaves admission the same two questions, whether the metric estimate is tight enough for the task and whether it belongs to the right object. The mug’s handle shows how each can fail while the other holds.

### Imprecise Estimates and Wrong Objects

^sec-intent-estimate-identity

When the wrist camera views the red mug from across the workstation before the arm begins its reach, depth uncertainty along the optical ray stretches the 95 percent error region to more than twice the handle’s grasp tolerance. Closing the gripper on that single-frame estimate can therefore drive the fingers into the ceramic rim. Under an illustrative calibrated Gaussian sensor model for that pre-approach wrist-camera frame, the principal standard deviations in the camera frame are 4 mm and 5 mm laterally, and 12 mm along the optical depth axis. Because the optical axis aligns with the gripper’s approach vector, range uncertainty directly challenges position control. A **95 percent statistical region** surrounding the estimated handle coordinate ${\mathbf{p}}_0$ is defined by the Mahalanobis squared distance $({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{p}}_0)^\top{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{depth}}}^{-1}({\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{p}}_0)\le{\chi_{3,0.95}}^2$. With the three-degree-of-freedom chi-square quantile ${\chi_{3,0.95}}^2 =$ 7.815, the distribution expands into principal semi-axes of 11.2 mm, 14.0 mm, and 33.5 mm.

Against the mug’s 15 mm physical grasp tolerance ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^fig-10-intent-lease-envelope|figure 4]], panel a), the ellipsoid fails on one axis only. While both lateral semi-axes fall comfortably within the mechanical tolerance window, the depth semi-axis of 33.5 mm substantially exceeds the allowed threshold. Consequently, the admission gate rejects the proposal, because the controller cannot guarantee that closing the gripper fingers at that estimated setpoint will capture the handle rather than collide with the rim.

The remedy, worked through in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^sec-intent-admissible-goal-sets|section 10.6]], decomposes the reach into distinct phases. The system first admits an intermediate, collision-checked inspection waypoint, allowing the arm to travel closer to the workspace. Once positioned nearby, a second wrist-camera observation constructs a refined belief record, reducing the initial position uncertainty to 3 mm. Because a Gaussian probability region retains an unbounded asymptotic tail, statistical distributions alone cannot certify absolute spatial clearance. The downstream intent proposal therefore needs independently justified conservative error envelopes in the robot base frame before requesting trajectory tracking. Covariance alone supplies only a statistical region.

While a configured statistical gate cleanly rejects detections with large variance envelopes, it cannot catch an entirely incorrect object identified with high semantic confidence. This is a different case from the jar beside the mug, where both candidates entered the prediction set and the producer refused. Consider instead the jar in place of the mug, where a fragile, thin-walled glass jar sits on the workstation where the ceramic mug was expected and no mug is present. When prompted to pick the mug, the vision-language model misidentifies the jar with strong semantic certainty, generating a sharp, low-variance geometric proposal centered on the glass container. The downstream trajectory planner accepts the coordinate, solves inverse kinematics in ${SE(3)}$, verifies joint position and velocity limits, and confirms that the approach path clears all static obstacles in the occupancy map. Because the requested motion satisfies all kinematic and wrench envelopes, the real-time permission path authorizes execution. However, if the gripper applies the clamping force required to hold a heavy ceramic mug, the jaws crush the thin glass container upon contact despite clearing every geometric check.

This scenario marks the boundary between physical verification and semantic premises. The deterministic permission path enforces coordinate envelopes, kinematic limits, actuator torque thresholds, and obstacle clearance against known geometric occupancy grids. These physical invariants cannot confirm whether a given coordinate actually corresponds to the requested object. The premise that the targeted item is indeed the requested ceramic mug remains an unverified upstream assumption outside the hardware controller’s reach. Establishing that premise requires distinct task-level perceptual evidence, which must be tracked through the handover release case (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/16 Deployment Release/V4-16.07 Adjudicating the Safety Case#^sec-release-case-worked-full|Adjudicating the Safety Case]]**) and recorded in the residual-claims register (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18.04 The Epistemic Frontier#^sec-frontier-residual-claims-register|A residual-claims register]]**). A learned task filter upstream cannot close this gap either. The AutoRT deployment showed that a language-model task filter improved the fraction of sensible task proposals while unsafe commands still passed it. Human teleoperators therefore had to intervene and halt execution (war story [10.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/10_intent/10_intent.html#ws-intent-autort-hallucination-constitution)) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ahn2023autort|Ahn et al. 2024]]).

**War Story 10.1: AutoRT task filtering and supervision (2024)**

**Context**: AutoRT deployed foundation models to propose and filter physical manipulation tasks for fleets of mobile manipulators operating in unstructured human office environments ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-ahn2023autort|Ahn et al. 2024]]).

**Mechanism**: In a sample of 259 proposed tasks, human annotators judged 228 (88 percent) acceptable. A second LLM pass then filtered the tasks against a robot “constitution” of safety and embodiment rules. Of the 214 tasks it kept, 200 (93 percent) were acceptable. The filter rejected 17 of the 31 unsuitable tasks, a recall of 55 percent, and passed the other 14.

**Impact**: All 14 misses arose during teleoperated collection. Although the filter raised overall proposal quality, human supervisors had to intervene to intercept every unsafe proposal before robot execution.

**Response**: The system architecture placed physical safety outside the model’s domain. Deployments supplemented learned proposals with hardware joint-force limits, physical emergency stop buttons, and direct human line-of-sight supervision or physical perimeter barricades.

**Systems lesson**: A learned task filter can improve proposal quality, but it cannot serve as a safety referee. An independent, deterministic permission and fallback path must validate every physical action against real-time plant limits before execution.

The distant view and the glass jar mark two distinct verification boundaries. The distant view fails on metric uncertainty along the depth axis, whereas the jar in place of the mug fails on an unverified semantic premise, because metric precision does not establish object identity. A safe geometric proposal must therefore carry both dated physical evidence and task-specific selection evidence across the silicon boundary. By this point the mug proposal holds most of what the lease will carry. The grounded affordance has fixed the target pose and its goal region, and the squeeze bounds of the grasp analysis have set the wrench ceiling. The operator table has named the predicates the grasp must discharge, and the handle’s error envelope has been compared with the task tolerance. Carrying these values is not yet holding authority, however. The proposal still lacks a grant, a deadline, and a stop that works without the proposer, and turning it into bounded authority over the arm is the work of the handoff.
