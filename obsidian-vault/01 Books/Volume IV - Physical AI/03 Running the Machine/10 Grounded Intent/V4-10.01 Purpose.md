*Why is recognizing the right object insufficient to authorize a robot to reach for it?*

A spoken request names an outcome, not a motion. Between the word and the grasp lie questions no label settles: which feature of the object to touch, where that feature sits in metric space, how hard to squeeze it, and how long the observation behind the answer stays true. A model can name the right object while misplacing its handle by more than the gripper tolerates, can locate it precisely a moment before a conveyor carries it away, or can report high confidence about the wrong object altogether. Once any of these answers becomes a coordinate it looks exactly like a correct one, and the drive that tracks it cannot tell the difference, so a machine that lets such a proposal command motion turns an error in meaning or timing into force against ceramic, glass, or a person. A learned target is safe to pursue only when it arrives bounded in space, effort, and time, with refusal available whenever the evidence cannot support it. Those bounds keep learned intent on the proposal side of the causal boundary, where a mistake can still be refused rather than delivered as motion that cannot be undone.

**Learning Objectives**

- Ground natural-language directives and symbolic task operators into metric affordance frames, continuous motion predicates, and verified geometric goal sets
- Emit the intent lease from parent belief records with explicit error envelopes, task tolerances, and effort ceilings
- Calculate intent lease expiry as the earliest of the evidence horizon, the administrative lease, and the stopping deadline
- Decide when grounding ambiguity or a wrong-object premise requires refusal rather than admission
- Evaluate terminal reachability within one evidence window, screen approach corridors against the forward occupancy of moving people, and select guarded, renewed stages for a longer approach
- Design hardware lease expiry that revokes motion authority even when the issuing process hangs or crashes
