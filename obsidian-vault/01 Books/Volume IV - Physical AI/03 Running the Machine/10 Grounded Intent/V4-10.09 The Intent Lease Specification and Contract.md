# V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-lease

If a target region, an actuator effort ceiling, and an expiration deadline travel as separate messages across the inter-core bus, a dropped or delayed packet can leave the trajectory planner tracking a new pose under an old force limit or an expired evidence timestamp. For the red mug, upstream perception and belief filtering distill raw sensor telemetry into those three quantities: a target region bounded by empirical evidence, an effort ceiling on actuator output, and an explicit deadline after which tracking must cease. The intent lease binds them into a single, immutable record for downstream control. Its lineage anchors to the belief record through a SHA-256 digest, while its evidence timestamp maps that record’s sensor transduction epoch directly into the Safety MCU’s monotonic clock domain. This admitted target establishes a verified envelope for downstream planning, leaving trajectory generation and execution permission to subsequent safety stages.

This stage emits the **intent lease**, the eighth record in the book’s sixteen-record chain. Authority divides cleanly between the two processors. The high-performance Application Processor generates an untrusted intent proposal ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^tbl-10-intent-proposal-fields|table 2]]), while the independent, real-time Safety MCU validates and latches the admitted lease. By binding the admitted target to a verified goal set, an affordance frame, and a collection of active safety predicates, this latched lease strictly constrains the downstream trajectory planner before the hardware admits any joint motion.

To guarantee deterministic processing on the safety controller, the proposal payload uses a statically bounded schema. Fixed-size fields eliminate dynamic heap allocation and pointer dereferencing, enabling the validation pipeline to reject malformed records in bounded execution cycles. However, static memory layout alone does not guarantee real-time deadlines. Inter-core memory copies, cache maintenance, lock contention, and bus arbitration must all remain within measured worst-case execution time bounds. The inbound proposal remains an advisory request from untrusted software; the Safety MCU retains exclusive authority over parameter bounds, expiration deadlines, and fallback activation.

↪ Mailboxes, memory barriers, and copy costs on the inter-core seam are developed in **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-ipc|Heterogeneous SoC Mailboxes, Memory Barriers, and Zero-Copy IPC]]**.

The covariance ellipsoid derived for the mug handle in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^sec-intent-estimate-identity|section 10.3.3]] represents an upstream producer estimate. Rather than serializing a dense, oriented $3\times3$ covariance matrix across the real-time wire, the protocol projects these bounds into three axis-aligned **position-error envelopes** $({e_x}, {e_y}, {e_z})$ alongside three requested **task tolerances** $({r_x}, {r_y}, {r_z})$ expressed in the robot base frame ${\mathcal{F}_{\text{base}}}$. The componentwise test $0 \le {e}_i \le {r}_i$ is a necessary preliminary screen. Admission must also establish that the full conservative error set, together with reserved controller tracking error, fits inside the ellipsoidal task region defined in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.02 Grounded Affordances#^sec-intent-grounded-affordances|section 10.1]]. Passing each axis independently does not establish that containment. Three axis-aligned bounds define a rectangular box rather than an ellipsoid, so a corner where errors peak along all three axes simultaneously can protrude outside the curved task boundary even when each individual axis stays within its limit, requiring the Safety MCU to evaluate the ellipsoidal norm at the worst-case corner of that error box plus the reserved tracking margin. The upstream producer must mathematically justify its projection from raw state covariance to these conservative error envelopes, accounting for camera calibration drift, latency jitter, and declared tail-risk percentiles. Angular tolerances and contact-mode transition rules derive from an independently certified task profile, allowing the wire proposal to transmit only the nominal target orientation quaternion.

Temporal validation hinges on the **belief record’s evidence epoch** (the physical instant of sensor exposure, not the time processing completed), mapped into the Safety MCU’s local clock domain. The Safety MCU verifies the parent digest and evidence epoch against retained belief records in local memory, adding the bounded synchronization error to the calculated evidence age. Requested expiration times reference this same monotonic hardware clock. Here, the parent digest establishes **data lineage rather than cryptographic authentication**, enabling forensic analysis to trace an actuator command directly back to the originating camera frame. Because the safety controller gates proposals exclusively on physical invariant checks and kinematic feasibility, the wire format omits cryptographic signatures, prioritizing physical truth over sender identity.

↩︎ Converting a producer’s evidence epoch into the Safety MCU’s monotonic clock, with a bounded synchronization error, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-supervisory-timers|Supervisory timers and proposal wire contracts]]**.

An intent lease progresses through five discrete lifecycle states on the Safety MCU: `Pending`, `Active`, `Renewed`, `Expired`, and `Aborted`. The controller transitions a proposal from `Pending` to `Active` only after verifying all geometric and dynamic ceilings and confirming that a collision-free stopping path remains executable from the current state. Transitioning to `Renewed` requires a strictly monotonic sequence counter, a newer parent belief record with an updated sensor capture epoch, and a fully passed admission re-check. A complex approach trajectory can span multiple consecutive lease renewals as long as fresh perception evidence continues to justify the motion. Dropping an isolated camera frame does not immediately abort tracking if the current coasting window remains valid. However, absent renewal, the hardware timer continues counting toward expiration. When an anomaly, force limit breach, or evidence timeout revokes tracking authority, the controller immediately engages a pre-calculated deceleration or hold trajectory to manage the kinetic energy of the moving manipulator safely.

[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^fig-10-intent-lease-lifecycle|Figure 8]] draws the same machine. Its one subtle transition is the self-loop on a stale renewal. A packet with an old sequence number or an old capture is discarded without resetting the timer, so a host that keeps talking without new evidence still runs out of authority. Expiry and abort end in the same place, the validated fallback that the MCU selects for the measured state.

**Definition 10.3: Expiring intent lease**

***Expiring intent lease*** (${\mathcal{I}_{\text{intent}}}$) is a time-bounded target authorization issued by the independent permission path that binds an admitted target pose and effort ceilings to a monotonic hardware deadline ${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$:

$$
{t_{\text{intent\_exp}}} = \min({t}_0 + {\tau_{\text{ev}}}, \; {t_{\text{adm}}} + {T_{\text{admin}}}, \; {t_{\text{deadline,stop}}}) \tag{9}
$$

 upon whose expiration dependent target-tracking authority is automatically revoked without requiring an explicit cancellation packet. The lease establishes geometric targets and effort bounds for downstream trajectory generation, reserving path execution authority for independent trajectory-level validation.

^eq-intent-lease-expiry-bound

1.  **Significance**: Revokes tracking authority autonomously on a hardware deadline whenever upstream deliberation stalls, engaging a validated fallback response without depending on cooperative shutdown signals from the host processor.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike a standard network keepalive or software heartbeat ping, an intent lease physically couples the temporal validity of sensor evidence (${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$) with real-time braking feasibility, capping lease duration so that a validated stopping maneuver always fits within the verified spatial clearance.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Artificially inflating lease durations to mask tail latency (p99) in vision models or neural planners. Generous timeouts create dangerous open-loop blind spots where the robot sweeps through physical space after environmental conditions have already evolved.

The three terms in the minimization enforce distinct safety bounds that prevent different classes of failure across the cyber-physical stack:

- The **physical target validity horizon** (${t}_0 + {\tau_{\text{ev}}}$) marks the instant on the monotonic clock when unobserved environmental motion or state drift can exhaust the target-error budget remaining within task tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}}$. This term dates strictly from physical sensor capture rather than packet arrival, where ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$ is the admitted target horizon after reserving controller tracking error, bounded above by the upstream belief record’s evidence-only horizon ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^eq-intent-evidence-horizon-roots|equation 5]]). Once this horizon elapses without fresh perception updates, the robot would be tracking an ungrounded hallucination, regardless of whether host software runs without error.
- The **software administrative lease** (${t_{\text{adm}}} + {T_{\text{admin}}}$) establishes an upper bound, measured from the admission tick ${t_{\text{adm}}}$, on how long the Application Processor may retain active tracking authority before the Safety MCU presumes host failure. Even in static environments where objects do not move (${\tau_{\text{ev}}} \to \infty$), operating system scheduling jitter, garbage-collection stalls, memory bus starvation, or kernel panics can freeze the host pipeline. The administrative lease ${T_{\text{admin}}}$ forces the system to decelerate smoothly rather than fly blind. This administrative horizon differs from the trajectory chunk lease ${T_{\text{lease}}}$ of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-lease-sizing|Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget]]**: while a chunk lease governs the execution duration of an individual trajectory polynomial, ${T_{\text{admin}}}$ bounds the persistence of the macro-level target goal itself.
- The **physical stopping boundary** (${t_{\text{deadline,stop}}}$) dictates the absolute latest revocation instant from which a worst-case braking profile can still arrest motion within remaining swept clearance. Given a verified swept-volume clearance ${D_{\text{clear}}}$ and a dynamic stopping distance ${d_{\text{stop}}} = {v}\,{\tau_{\text{rev}}} + {v}^2 / (2{a_{\text{brake}}})$, the deadline evaluates to ${t_{\text{deadline,stop}}} = {t_{\text{adm}}} + ({D_{\text{clear}}} - {d_{\text{stop}}}) / {v}$. Here ${\tau_{\text{rev}}}$ is the post-revocation delay, the tick, bus, and brake-onset terms that make up the 22 ms stop onset. It is the tail of the full pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.05 Defining the Causal Boundary#^sec-boundary-kinematic-roofline|Delay consumes stopping clearance]]**, not a replacement for it. The other terms of ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$, observation age and the lease, are exactly what the deadline itself measures, so charging them again here would count them twice. This kinematic bound assumes positive velocity (${v} > 0$) and nominal braking deceleration. When the manipulator is stationary (${v} = 0$), a static hold assessment replaces the distance formulation. Any dynamic change in tool speed, detected clearance, or actuator thermal limits requires immediate recomputation.

By taking the minimum across all three terms on the Safety MCU’s local hardware timer, motion authority terminates at whichever safety margin exhausts first: sensor evidence staleness, host processor silence, or kinematic clearance depletion. Upon expiration, whatever the state of the host processor, a hardware timer interrupt revokes tracking authority and triggers the fallback response the MCU selected for the current payload, kinematics, and contact mode. The choice depends on physical context. A powered position hold requires continuous motor torque but stabilizes a suspended payload. A controlled path deceleration bleeds kinetic energy within the clearance boundaries. Safe torque disable may protect the windings of compliant backdrivable joints but can drop a suspended load or leave momentum uncontrolled. Any terminal mode requested in the original proposal remains purely advisory. For instance, an upstream policy requesting torque disable while suspending a heavy payload cannot override the powered hold mandated by the safety controller. If no fallback can guarantee safety from the current state, admission rejects the lease before motion begins. Neither a 1 kHz discrete tick nor a hardware interrupt can arrest physical mass instantaneously. Mechanical deceleration is governed by inertia and actuator torque limits, so the clearance it needs must be reserved before any motion is admitted.

The admission pipeline enforces the four handoff invariants of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.05 The Intent Handoff and Bounded Authority#^sec-intent-handoff|section 10.4]] as a staged series of validation gates, ordered from lowest computational cost to highest so that execution time stays bounded:

1.  **Evidence and sequence validation:** Ingest the proposal record from the IPC ring buffer. Instantly drop packets with obsolete sequence numbers or mismatched schema versions. Compare the `parent_belief_digest` and sensor epoch against locally retained spatial memory records. Transform the evidence timestamp into the MCU monotonic clock domain, incorporate the worst-case clock synchronization uncertainty, and reject the request if the resulting evidence age already exceeds the evidence horizon ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$.
2.  **Kinematic and tolerance bounds:** Verify that all pose coordinates are finite numbers and that the orientation quaternion is strictly normalized ($\lVert {\mathbf{q}_{\text{quat}}} \rVert = 1.0 \pm 10^{-4}$). Confirm that error envelopes and requested task tolerances satisfy $0 \le {e}_i \le {r}_i$ along all coordinate axes in ${\mathcal{F}_{\text{base}}}$. Then verify that the full conservative error set, together with reserved controller tracking error, fits inside the ellipsoidal task region. Validate requested wrench thresholds against static actuator ratings and workplace occupancy constraints.
3.  **Dynamic feasibility and fallback clearance:** Evaluate current joint velocities, payload inertia, and sensor-verified spatial clearance to confirm that a valid stopping trajectory exists. Reject any proposal where the sum of reaction delay travel and braking distance exceeds available swept clearance (${d_{\text{stop}}} > {D_{\text{clear}}}$). The MCU does not count candidates in the pick window, because it holds no scene representation. The station’s singulation check runs before a proposal is forwarded ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.08 Intent Grounding Failure Modes and Their Checks#^sec-intent-failure-modes|section 10.7]]), and a window it has not cleared never reaches this stage. This stage admits target geometry and effort bounds rather than a path. The trajectory planner proposes the full continuous trajectory, which the permission path must separately admit.
4.  **Authority latch:** Commit the verified target pose, admitted wrench ceilings, validated expiration deadline (${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$), and pre-selected fallback policy into protected MCU memory. Arm the hardware countdown timer for the admitted interval. Advisory values from the untrusted proposal cannot write to drive registers.
5.  **Runtime watchdog monitoring:** On every high-frequency control cycle and hardware interrupt, compare measured joint states, motor currents, and contact forces against the admitted lease bounds. If the hardware timer expires or force limits are breached, immediately revoke tracking authority and engage the latched fallback routine. A missed renewal does not revoke authority by itself. It leaves the timer running toward expiry.

Stage 1 checks temporal freshness, stage 2 checks spatial containment and the effort ceilings, stage 3 checks the certified fallback, and stages 4 and 5 latch what passed and keep checking it while the plant moves. Together, these five gates emit the admitted target record consumed by the trajectory planner ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^tbl-10-intent-specification-contract|table 6]]), establishing the formal contract boundary between spatial perception and trajectory planning.

By linking `parent_belief_digest` directly to the upstream belief record and emitting its own `intent_digest` to the downstream trajectory record, the intent lease schema ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^tbl-10-intent-specification-contract|table 6]]) creates an unbroken, auditable custody chain extending from camera photons to actuator torque commands. Downstream trajectory planners can neither expand the admitted geometric goal set ${\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.02 Grounded Affordances#^eq-intent-goal-set-se3|equation 1]]) nor prolong tracking authority beyond `expiration_deadline_ns`.

↪ The trajectory record that embeds the lease’s digest and answers to its bounds is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-trajectory-contract|The Trajectory Record]]**.

Filled in for the red mug, the admitted lease links directly to the mug-handle belief record by its digest and sensor capture epoch. The target pose designates the 6-DoF mug-handle affordance frame, where localization uncertainty is bounded by the belief record’s 3 mm initial error envelope, well within the 15 mm task tolerance. The evidence horizon marks the temporal threshold where unobserved conveyor belt motion causes error growth to intersect this tolerance: 60 ms under open-loop conveyor drift, extending to 219 ms when tracking filters provide qualified updates under residual slip. Kinematically, the lease constrains the manipulator to a 1 m/s tool-center-point speed ceiling. Because the facility catalog registers no fragile payload profile for this mug, the wrench ceiling is the default profile’s, and the controller enforces default joint impedance limits, relying on plant-level sorting rather than online compliance adaptation. A cataloged fragile payload would instead carry an item-specific ceiling, bounded below by the squeeze force that holds the payload through transit (**[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-grasp|Grasp mechanics: Friction cones and force closure]]**) and above by its crush limit. Grasping and subsequent handover constitute the target objective, while the MCU retains exclusive authority over the fallback trajectory.

The lease expires at the earliest of the three terms of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^eq-intent-lease-expiry-bound|equation 9]], and the mug record supplies a value for each. The scenario chooses an administrative lease of 100 ms, long enough that one dropped wrist-camera frame does not revoke authority, and a verified clearance of 300 mm ahead of the gripper. The stopping term needs a deceleration the arm can be relied on to deliver, which is not the controller’s command limit. The arm’s handbook bounds its worst-case stop at 552 ms with the full payload at maximum velocity, measured under the stopping test of EN ISO 10218-1 ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-franka2021handbook|Franka Emika GmbH 2021]]). The handbook states only “maximum velocity” and reports the stop in joint angles, so assuming that stop began at the 1.7 m/s Cartesian limit gives a derived credible deceleration of 3.1 m/s². At the 1 m/s ceiling, that deceleration and the 22 ms stop onset give a stopping distance of 184 mm, so the stopping deadline falls 116 ms after admission. With all three countdowns started together, as in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^fig-10-mug-lease-timeline|figure 5]], the 60 ms evidence horizon binds first when the mug is untracked. A tracker stretches the evidence horizon to 219 ms, and the 100 ms administrative lease then binds before the 116 ms stopping deadline. Subtracting the controller tracking reserve, which a deployment must still do, can only shorten the admitted target horizon and bring expiry earlier.

This explicit record structure establishes a concrete boundary for automated fault-injection suites. Systems engineers validate the safety controller by injecting unnormalized quaternions, stale capture timestamps, excessive wrench thresholds, and conflicting fallback requests into the IPC stream, verifying that the Safety MCU deterministically rejects invalid proposals across all operational states and timing margins.

↪ Negating each record’s guarantees, intent lifetime among them, at its handoff to derive injected faults is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/15 Adversarial Verification/V4-15.07 Deriving the Fault List#^sec-verification-inverting-records|Inverting design records and the five-element chain]]**.

**Checkpoint 10.2: Expiring intent and fallback authority**

Verify your understanding of how a proposal is refused or admitted, and how the lease granted from it is sized and revoked:

**Ambiguity refusal**: Why does this station’s singulation rule refuse a pick window holding two candidates, and why must that test run on a confident proposal as well as an uncertain one?

**Wrong-object premise**: Which premise about the grasped object remains outside the permission path even when every coordinate, reachability, and force check passes, and where does the machine carry that premise instead?

**Expiry bound**: Can you name which term of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^eq-intent-lease-expiry-bound|equation 9]] only fresh evidence can extend, which one a silent host triggers, and which one a change in speed or clearance forces the MCU to recompute?

**Clock domain**: Why must the MCU convert the belief record’s evidence epoch into its own monotonic clock, with a bounded conversion error, before checking age and the requested expiry?

**Fallback selection**: Can you explain why the MCU must select a feasible approved hold, deceleration, or torque-disable response from the measured load state rather than obey the proposal’s requested terminal mode?
