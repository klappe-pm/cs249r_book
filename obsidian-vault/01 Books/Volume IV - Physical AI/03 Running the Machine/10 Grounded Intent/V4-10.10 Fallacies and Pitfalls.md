# V4-10.10 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-fallacies-pitfalls

Every failure pattern cataloged here stems from trusting an upstream message to mean more than it physically certifies. Real-time safety interlocks and local execution checks must refuse a proposed target, or revoke tracking authority over an active one, whenever its underlying sensory evidence expires or its spatial justification dissolves. Downstream execution monitors must evaluate evidence that the upstream proposer cannot falsify, enforcing safety invariants even while incoming inference streams remain syntactically valid and numerically confident.

**Fallacy**: *A renewal computed from the same stale frame is a valid renewal.*

In distributed computing and cloud infrastructure, lease renewals often function as software heartbeats. A client process demonstrates liveness and retains exclusive access by transmitting an uncorrupted keepalive packet before a timeout expires. In physical AI, however, software liveness implies nothing about physical evidence validity. If an upstream planner loops cleanly while the physical scene changes, keepalives succeed while the robot drifts blind.

Consider an intent model that repeatedly processes its last wrist-camera frame of a red mug, dispatching renewal requests with incremented sequence numbers, fresh issue timestamps, and identical target poses. Across the network, every field that a keepalive inspects appears healthy and up to date. No new photons, however, have reached the camera sensor, while the physical mug continues moving down the conveyor at 0.20 m/s. Because a sound intent lease dates its authority from the parent belief record’s sensor capture epoch ${t}_0$ rather than packet arrival, the Safety MCU observes that the evidence epoch has stalled. As a result, it expires the target 60 ms after the original camera capture. A naive controller that evaluated renewals based on packet arrival time would permit a spinning software loop to retain tracking authority over an obsolete coordinate indefinitely. **Advancing software timestamps on stale sensory data creates an illusion of liveness while unobserved physical drift violates clearance.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Anchor lease validity strictly to the sensor evidence epoch ${t}_0$ of the parent belief record; reject renewals that fail to supply a qualified newer capture record, including its error bound and bounded clock conversion.

**Pitfall**: *Allowing an unexpired temporal lease to continue commanding motion when target spatial bounds are violated.*

In distributed locking protocols, a lease grants exclusive, unconditional authority over a resource for the entire duration of its temporal grant. In physical AI, however, an intent lease is a coupled spatio-temporal contract:

$$
{\mathcal{I}_{\text{intent}}} = \left( {\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}, \; [{t_{\text{adm}}}, {t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}], \; {\mathbf{w}_{\max}} \right)
$$

 Temporal freshness (${t} < {t_{\text{intent\_exp}}}$) is a necessary condition for motion execution, but never a sufficient one.

Suppose a manipulator arm lowers toward the red mug under an admitted lease with a grasp tolerance of 15 mm. If an unexpected belt slip shifts the mug beyond that tolerance while time remains on the countdown, the lease is still temporally active, but the target has left the spatial goal region ${\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}}$ that justified the approach. Continuing until the countdown timer expires drives the arm toward an empty coordinate or provokes a collision. The runtime permission loop must continually compare the latest qualified target evidence and its age-dependent uncertainty bound against admitted limits. It revokes tracking authority the moment an update shows that the handle has left the goal set, $\|{\mathbf{p}_{\text{target}}}({t}) - {\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{\text{target}}}\|_2 > {r_{\text{tol}}}$, where ${\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{\text{target}}}$ is the admitted target. Under a qualified tracker that target follows the tracked affordance frame along the belt, so the test catches slip beyond the tracker’s prediction rather than the belt motion the tracker already cancels. Running the control loop faster cannot reveal an unobserved slip; sensing delay and bounded error growth must both be accounted for in the response budget. **A temporal lease confers authority only within declared spatial bounds; temporal freshness never excuses spatial divergence.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Embed runtime spatial containment checks (${E}({\Delta} {t}) \le {r_{\text{tol}}}$) in high-rate control loops, revoking dependent authority when qualified evidence or its propagated bound no longer supports the tolerance mid-lease.

**Fallacy**: *A confident grounding needs no refusal path.*

In machine learning pipelines, a high detector score is often treated as definitive proof of identity. Engineers transitioning to physical robotics are frequently tempted to save latency with shortcut logic: `if confidence > threshold: skip_ambiguity_checks()`. That shortcut treats a model’s score as an audit of the physical scene, and it fails across three distinct systems dimensions:

1.  **Scores are not calibrated probabilities:** Neural networks trained with cross-entropy loss are often overconfident ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-guo2017calibration|Guo et al. 2017]]), and calibration fitted on one training distribution degrades under distribution shift (**[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Machine Learning Background#^sec-appendix-ml-calibration|Softmax overconfidence and calibration error]]**). An open-world detector can assign a high score to a transparent glass jar or a printed decal because it represents the closest feature match to the prompt.
2.  **Per-box scores do not measure uniqueness:** Open-vocabulary detectors score each bounding box independently against the text prompt without normalizing across candidate proposals ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-minderer2022simple|Minderer et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-liu2023grounding|S. Liu et al. 2023]]). If two identical red mugs sit in the pick window, both score high. Downstream logic that naively keeps only the top-scoring box turns two valid candidates into an arbitrary choice, concealing real spatial ambiguity from all subsequent planning layers.
3.  **The consequence is physical:** A score describes prompt alignment in representation space rather than confirming that the physical workspace contains exactly one manipulable entity. The two jar cases of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.04 Semantic Request Translation to Geometric Proposals#^sec-intent-request-to-proposal|section 10.3]] mark the line. When the jar sits beside the mug, both enter the prediction set and refusal is mandatory however high either score is. When the jar stands in place of the mug, a confident singleton passes every check, which no score threshold can catch.

Workstation safety requires an independent singulation check on every intent proposal, drawing on evidence that the vision-language model cannot manipulate. An independent candidate-counting stage operating directly on calibrated spatial point clouds or voxel occupancy grids evaluates the physical workspace. If the pick window contains more than one candidate cluster, the station refuses the proposal with `ERR_GROUNDING_AMBIGUITY` before it reaches the Safety MCU, regardless of model confidence. Any earlier active record remains governed by its own evidence epoch, lease duration, and fallback strategy. Transitioning to a safe hold or operator handoff requires an admitted, physically feasible trajectory. **A detector score reflects the model’s match to the prompt, not real-world uniqueness; keeping only the top box can select the wrong physical object.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Enforce the selected station’s singulation rule using qualified independent candidate evidence; treat `ERR_GROUNDING_AMBIGUITY` as a first-class diagnostic that can request a feasible hold or operator handoff without automatically revoking a still-valid active record.

**Fallacy**: *Neural networks can resolve bimodal spatial ambiguity by averaging candidate target locations.*

A continuous regressor trained with squared error converges toward the conditional mean of its targets (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.03 Policy Training Regimes#^sec-training-multimodality-breakdown|Mode averaging when demonstrations split]]**). When demonstrations pick mug $\text{A}$ half the time and mug $\text{B}$ the other half, that mean settles on the midpoint $({\mathbf{y}_A} + {\mathbf{y}_B})/2$, which lies on the empty conveyor belt between two valid targets or directly on whatever obstacle separates them. Because the robot’s collision-free operational space is not convex, the linear average of two feasible goals need not be feasible, and the gripper closes on empty air or collides with the inner edges of both mugs.

Suppose the perception system observes two identical mugs a few centimeters apart and submits their spatial mean as a grasp target. The fused estimate can appear deceptively sharp. Both individual detections may pass association and innovation gates (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-perception-tracking-association|Multi-object tracking and data association]]**), while an averaging estimator reports an artificially low variance that evades covariance expansion gates. However, the independent pick-window geometric check from [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.08 Intent Grounding Failure Modes and Their Checks#^sec-intent-failure-modes|section 10.7]] still detects the presence of two distinct candidates, prompting the station’s admission gate to reject the proposal regardless of what the fused estimate reports. That refusal does not revoke an active, unexpired lease; transitioning to a safe hold must itself remain physically feasible. Generative policies that explicitly represent multiple modes, such as diffusion policies, flow-matching models, or Gaussian mixture models (**[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Machine Learning Background#^sec-appendix-ml-multimodal|Mode averaging and the decoders that avoid it]]**), avoid arithmetic averaging, but downstream execution contracts cannot rely on policy internals. Before crossing the intent boundary, the proposer must commit to a single goal set anchored to a single tracked physical entity. **Averaging bimodal physical targets can replace two valid manipulation candidates with a location that satisfies neither task.**

**Architect’s countermeasure:** Preserve explicit multi-hypothesis distributions at the reasoning boundary (via diffusion policies or Gaussian mixture models), and enforce discrete candidate selection backed by the independent pick-window ambiguity gate.
