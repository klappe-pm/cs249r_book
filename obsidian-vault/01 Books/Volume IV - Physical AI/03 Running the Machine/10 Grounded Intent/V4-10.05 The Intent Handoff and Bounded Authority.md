# V4-10.05 The Intent Handoff and Bounded Authority

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-handoff

If a deliberative host could stream raw joint trajectories or actuator currents directly to the drives, a two-millimeter depth error into a rigid latch plate or a stalled inference thread mid-reach would drive motor currents to saturation before any host process could intervene. When the arm reaches for the red mug, the deliberative host instead submits a candidate intent specifying a bounded target region, an expiration deadline, and explicit physical effort ceilings. The region and the ceilings are the grounding results packaged for transit, the affordance pose with its goal set and the grasp’s wrench and speed limits, and the requested deadline is computed from the parent belief record’s evidence epoch. An independent real-time supervisor intercepts this proposal, vetting each requested parameter against retained sensor evidence and configured plant dynamics. Granting a target lease merely authorizes downstream trajectory planning within certified safety bounds. The deliberative model remains isolated from low-level actuator registers.

This permission path validates every requested target constraint before any motion planner can generate trajectory setpoints. Rather than forcing memory-constrained microcontrollers to maintain full scene representations, host-side spatial engines export compact geometric evidence across a strictly typed interface. The supervisor verifies four mandatory invariants against this evidence:

1.  *Spatial containment invariant:* The proposed geometric goal set ${\mathcal{G}_{\text{goal}}} \subset {SE(3)}$ must lie strictly within the robot’s reachable workspace manifold ${\mathcal{W}}$. The bounded spatial uncertainty envelope $({e_x}, {e_y}, {e_z})$ must fit inside the task tolerance $({r_x}, {r_y}, {r_z})$ without intersecting persistent obstacle geometry in spatial memory ${\mathcal{M}}$.
2.  *Temporal freshness invariant:* The sensor evidence epoch ${t}_0$ must match a retained parent belief record. The unobserved elapsed time on the Safety MCU’s monotonic hardware clock must not exceed the evidence horizon ${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$ that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^sec-intent-goals-expire|section 10.5]] takes from the parent belief record.
3.  *Dynamic effort invariant:* Requested tool-center-point velocity ${v_{\max}}$, acceleration ${a_{\max}}$, and contact wrench ${\mathbf{w}_{\max}} = [{F_{\max}}, {\tau_{\max}}]^\top$ must remain strictly within actuator thermal ratings, link inertia limits, and workpiece structural yield thresholds.
4.  *Certified deterministic fallback invariant:* The robot must possess an independently verified, pre-computed stopping or hold trajectory that brings all moving links to rest within current physical obstacle clearance (${d_{\text{stop}}} \le {D_{\text{clear}}}$) if authority is abruptly revoked.

The first three invariants turn the requirements that opened the chapter into checks: metric physicality and bounded error become spatial containment and the effort ceiling, while temporal expiration becomes freshness. The fourth adds the obligation those requirements left implicit, a stop that works without the proposer. Each invariant guards against a distinct failure mode in physical interaction. Loose target bounds can exceed kinematic grasp tolerances, and stale sensor evidence risks collision with a displaced object. Excessive commanded effort can shatter a workpiece, and an unvetted handoff leaves moving mass without a safe recovery trajectory. The resulting intent lease records the validated envelope that survives these four checks and constrains the downstream trajectory planner, which must synthesize path timing and deceleration profiles that satisfy supervisory admission criteria ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.09 The Intent Lease Specification and Contract#^sec-intent-lease|section 10.8]]). Pairing the evidence epoch with a finite lease expiration keeps the temporal bound deterministic even if the host crashes, because the safety controller compares its own clock against the admitted deadline rather than waiting for a cancellation packet ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^sec-intent-goals-expire|section 10.5]]).

Spatial and temporal bounds alone cannot guarantee safety if the downstream controller commands unbounded effort. Every intent proposal must therefore declare explicit effort ceilings covering tool-center-point velocity, acceleration, and contact wrench. Consider a depth perception error of two millimeters that places a target goal slightly inside a rigid workpiece. An unrestricted position controller will drive actuator currents to saturation trying to eliminate tracking error, imposing severe mechanical stress on the gear train. During a guarded approach to a spring-latched cage door, for example, the host proposes a 15 N tripwire ceiling, well below the 100 N structural latch threshold, paired with an approach velocity capped at 0.03 m/s. The permission path evaluates these values against hardware torque-speed curves and environmental thresholds, admitting only parameters that guarantee structural integrity.

Downstream of the permission path, an impedance controller shapes interaction dynamics, answering a position error with the restoring force of the virtual spring-damper established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.05 Actuator Transmission Limits#^sec-body-transmission-compliance|Transmission compliance, contact impedance, and tooth fracture]]**. Under quasi-static contact, scaling the virtual stiffness along the approach vector directly limits steady-state contact force for a given positional error. Transient impacts, however, demand more than static stiffness modulation; containing peak collision forces requires coordinating approach velocity, passive mechanical compliance, sensor latency, and actuator closed-loop bandwidth.

Omitting spatial, temporal, or dynamic boundaries leaves physical hardware vulnerable during communication dropouts or perception stalls. Stopping distance scales linearly with the reaction delay preceding brake engagement and quadratically with operating velocity (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^eq-body-stopping-distance|equation 2.2]]**). For a mobile base navigating within physical obstacle clearances, a silent or stalled proposer inflates this reaction window by the entire lifespan of the active command lease. As established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-lease-sizing|Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget]]**, this chunk lease duration directly extends the unbraked latency parameter ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$. The verified safety envelope must therefore maintain obstacle clearance greater than the total stopping distance, factoring in sensor pipeline latency, transport jitter, and state estimation uncertainty. Furthermore, powered deceleration requires electrical bus energy and closed-loop control authority to survive an upstream fault. Logic-level power alone cannot generate mechanical braking torque.

The intent lease defines the temporal horizon over which a deliberative goal remains valid. It operates alongside the chunk lease of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.05 Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget#^sec-nervous-lease-sizing|Chunk Lease Sizing from the Stopping Budget]]** rather than replacing it, because the two hold different kinds of authority even though the Safety MCU’s permission path checks both. The chunk lease authorizes motion. It allows an admitted chunk to drive the actuators only for its short lease unless the chunk policy renews it at its own rate. By contrast, the intent lease authorizes a goal, fixing which target that motion may pursue, how hard it may press, and until when, and only newer evidence can renew it. Consequently, each lease ends authority from its own side. When the chunk lease lapses, motion stops even though the goal remains valid. When the intent lease lapses, nominal tracking toward the goal ends at once, even if the current chunk still has lease time left, and the MCU engages the validated fallback (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.03 The Planning Handoff and the Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-handoff-record|The trajectory record and its deadlines]]**). A rejected proposal is the gentler case, because it revokes nothing and leaves already admitted motion running until its own leases lapse. The physical stopping budget must accommodate the cumulative delay across perception, communication, actuator response, and mechanical braking in every case.

**Napkin Math 10.1: What the latch ceilings buy**

**Problem**: The intent lease for the cage door requests a 15 N tripwire and a 0.03 m/s approach speed against a 100 N latch limit. With the local contact loop in place, what force does the latch see when the brake command issues, and what would it see in a counterfactual design with no local contact trip, where pressing stops only when the proposer or the intent lease acts?

**Variables**:

- Tripwire 15 N and approach speed ${v_{\text{latch}}} =$ 0.03 m/s, the requested ceilings; latch limit 100 N (Illustrative)
- Plate stiffness ${k_{\text{latch}}} =$ 400 N/mm (Illustrative) and contact-loop response 2 ms
- Unbraked stall ${\Delta} {t} =$ 200 ms (Illustrative), shorter than one 500 ms intent period

**Math**:

1.  **Force at brake command**: The tripwire fires at 15 N, and the plate keeps compressing while the 2 ms contact loop responds, so the force when the brake command issues is ${F_{\text{cmd}}} = {F_{\text{trip}}} + {k_{\text{latch}}}\,{v_{\text{latch}}}\,{T_c} =$ 15 N $+$ 400 N/mm $\times$ 0.03 m/s $\times$ 2 ms $=$ 39 N. The arm stop that follows has only a fraction of a millimeter of compression left to spend; **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|Intentional contact and in-contact energy barriers]]** derives that stop (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-trip-indentation|equation 12.11]]**).
2.  **Stall travel** (counterfactual, no local trip): ${\Delta} {x} = {v_{\text{latch}}}\,{\Delta} {t} =$ 0.03 m/s $\times$ 200 ms $=$ 6 mm past first contact.
3.  **Stall force**: ${F} = {k_{\text{latch}}}\,{\Delta} {x} =$ 400 N/mm $\times$ 6 mm $=$ 2,400 N.

**Result**: The contact loop brakes at 39 N. Without the local trip, a stop that waited on the proposer would let the arm press on, and the spring model predicts 2,400 N.

**Systems insight**: The predicted force lies far past the 100 N limit, beyond the point where the spring model applies at all; the latch has failed. Sizing and arresting transient contact within milliseconds is the duty of the high-rate, low-latency contact loop. The intent lease supplies the operational boundaries (velocity caps and force tripwires) that keep dynamic loads within the contact loop’s braking envelope, ensuring physical safety even when upstream deliberative proposers stall across multiple intent cycles.

Both the mug grasp and the latch engagement depend on a **privilege separation boundary**.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn7|⁷]] While deliberative models propose goal targets and operational bounds, an independent supervisor enforces safety invariants and executes certified fallback maneuvers whenever evidence expires or contact states change unexpectedly. This design decouples deliberative reasoning from real-time physical protection, inverting the classical priority hierarchy found in early reactive control systems ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-brooks1986robust|Brooks 1986]]).

Structured intent handoffs explicitly disentangle task tolerance, sensor uncertainty, actuator effort limits, and fallback trajectories. External dynamic processes, however, dictate how long these static bounds remain valid. On a moving conveyor, for instance, a correctly localized workpiece drifts continuously after perception capture. The mathematical model governing this uncertainty growth establishes the strict temporal deadline that the independent safety controller must enforce.
