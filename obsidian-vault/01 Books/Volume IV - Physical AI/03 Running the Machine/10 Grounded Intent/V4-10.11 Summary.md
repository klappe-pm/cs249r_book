# V4-10.11 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-summary

Grounded intent transforms an unverified proposal from a learned policy into a bounded, expiring physical target. In this architecture, a foundation model or vision-language-action (VLA) policy proposes a target pose in ${SE(3)}$, a spatial error envelope, and effort ceilings. The Safety MCU operates as an independent gatekeeper, evaluating the proposal against kinematic and physical limits before admitting it as an *intent lease*. This lease anchors directly to a parent belief record in spatial memory and remains valid only until the earliest of three deterministic deadlines: evidence decay (${\tau_{\text{ev}}}$, derived from the belief record after reserving controller tracking error), host silence (${T_{\text{admin}}}$, guarding against communication stalls), and stopping clearance (${t_{\text{deadline,stop}}}$, reserving the space needed to brake to rest). A hardware countdown timer enforces this boundary, revoking trajectory tracking authority without waiting for host confirmation. Because deceleration takes time, the worst-case response latency must be budgeted directly into the stopping clearance. Effort ceilings enforce mechanical safety alongside temporal deadlines. For example, when closing a cage-door latch, contact force continues to rise throughout the response delay of the contact-control loop. Stopping criteria must therefore account for the force already accumulating when the brake command issues, rather than assuming an instantaneous tripwire stop.

Tracking a red mug on a moving conveyor illustrates the multi-rate timing constraints that physical AI systems must reconcile. Under open-loop motion, target evidence remains valid for only 60 ms before conveyor drift carries the object outside the grasp tolerance. Meanwhile, the deliberative intent model executes at 2 Hz, updating its inferences every 500 ms. Because this deliberative period exceeds the evidence lifespan, the high-level policy cannot maintain an accurate target on its own. A qualified real-time tracker resolves this discrepancy by assimilating high-rate sensor observations to extend the evidence horizon to 219 ms, and its fresh captures, not the model’s proposals, renew the lease on a goal set that follows the tracked handle. The deliberative model only reconfirms object identity at its slower cadence, so the arm need not hesitate, slow down, or abort between inferences. Guided by verified spatial corridors, continuous observation, and qualified tracking, the arm completes extended approach maneuvers across sequentially admitted segments, culminating in a grasp aligned with real-time state estimates. When a lease expires, the controller executes a stopping trajectory pre-validated for the current kinematic state, safely dissipating the mechanical momentum already present in the moving links.

In safe embodied architectures, refusal is a first-class operational outcome rather than an exceptional failure. When an instruction points to an ambiguous referent, the candidate set fails the calibrated prediction threshold and the station’s singulation rule. When a proposed terminal motion cannot reach the target before evidence decays, the admission controller rejects the transit bound. Similarly, when a planned motion corridor intersects a coworker’s forward occupancy set, the early screening filter denies permission. Screening the admitted prefix and its complete stopping fallback, rather than a speculative multi-second route, can reduce unnecessary refusal in narrow, shared workspaces. The screen must still cover response delay and deceleration after evidence expiry. Each refusal generates a structured diagnostic record that informs the proposer why the lease was denied, enabling informed re-planning. Symbolic task plans generated by language models integrate through this exact mechanism, mapping symbolic operators into physical preconditions and testable motion predicates. Nevertheless, physical admission gates cannot see past the wrong-object premise, because a model that confidently grounds an action to the incorrect object can satisfy every coordinate, reachability, and contact-force check. **Key Takeaways: Grounded intent at the proposal boundary**

- **Leases grant bounded targets, not trajectories**: The host proposes a target pose, evidence epoch, error envelope, task tolerances, and effort ceilings. The lease the Safety MCU latches binds them to a goal set and motion predicates that the trajectory planner may not expand, and it leaves the path to the planner.
- **Expiry runs on the permission side’s clock**: A hardware countdown ends authority at the earliest of the evidence horizon, the administrative lease, and the stopping deadline, so a hung host loses its target without sending anything. A renewal counts only when it carries a newer capture; a repeated frame with a fresh sequence number renews nothing.
- **Target evidence decays with unobserved drift**: At conveyor speed the mug’s evidence lasts 60 ms while the current intent inference takes 336 ms, so this model cannot renew the target without another source of fresh evidence. Only fresh observations or a qualified tracker, which stretches the horizon to 219 ms, extend validity.
- **Evidence deadlines differ from braking deadlines**: Expiry is not rest. After the lease lapses the arm still needs 22 ms before braking begins and then its stopping distance, so the lease charges the stopping deadline as its own term rather than assuming the evidence horizon covers it.
- **Geometric feasibility does not guarantee semantic correctness**: A learned model can ground an action to the wrong physical object with high confidence and still satisfy every coordinate, reachability, and contact-force check. Because physical admission gates cannot evaluate semantic validity, task-level verification requires independent task evidence and operator oversight.
- **Refusal is a primary operational outcome**: Ambiguous object identity, expired evidence, dynamic reachability violations, and stopping distance constraints all trigger deterministic proposal rejections. Structured diagnostics let the proposer gather more evidence, choose a staged approach, or request operator assistance.

^callout-takeaways--10-2-ed7bc212

When a learned target misbehaves at the machine, the first question is which of its bounds was missing, not how accurate the model was. A target without an evidence epoch cannot be expired, one without an error envelope cannot be compared with a tolerance, and one without an effort ceiling cannot be stopped short of damage, so each omission turns a model error into motion. The remedy is rarely a faster or more confident proposer. On the conveyor, no tuning lets a 336 ms inference hold a 60 ms target. The fixes are a tracker that renews evidence, a slower belt, or a refusal.

The intent lease is the third law, proposal is not permission (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]]), written as a record for goals. The lease grants a target, never a path. It names where the gripper may end, how hard it may press, and until when, and it leaves the route to a planner whose motion must be admitted again on its own evidence. Authority a learned model obtains for a goal therefore never extends to the motion that reaches it, and it lapses on the permission side’s clock whether or not the model is still running.
