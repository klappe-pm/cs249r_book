# V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10 Grounded Intent|V4-10 Grounded Intent]]

^sec-intent-admissible-goal-sets

If the red mug rides past on the far side of the belt, beyond the arm’s physical reach from where the base is parked, no sequence of joint torques can bring the gripper to its handle. In an embodied ML system, this physical impossibility must be intercepted at the proposal boundary, before the target enters the trajectory optimization queue. When an upstream vision-language model or diffusion policy emits a candidate Cartesian coordinate, treating that output as an unverified proposal protects the real-time motion pipeline from executing physically impossible trajectories and from wasting compute cycles attempting to solve them.

A reachability screen also protects the planner’s computational budget. When a target cannot be reached at all, the trajectory optimizer faces an empty feasible set. Local nonlinear solvers cannot certify infeasibility quickly. They typically exhaust their entire iteration budget before timing out, transforming an upstream geometric hallucination into compute starvation on the planning core. Similarly, a requested terminal move that cannot complete before its supporting sensory evidence expires must fail before entering detailed planning. Long-horizon tasks should instead request guarded progress under renewable evidence leases rather than attempting an ungrounded terminal grasp on a single sensor capture.

To avoid spending the planning budget on an already impossible request, the admission layer evaluates proposals against two low-cost preliminary checks:

1.  **Static Workspace Manifold Check (${\mathbf{T}_{\text{target}}} \in {\mathcal{W}}$):** Geometric workspace analysis defines the closed spatial manifold ${\mathcal{W}} \subset {SE(3)}$ of reachable end-effector configurations given link dimensions, joint travel limits, and mounting geometry ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-craig2005introduction|Craig 2005]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-lynch2017modern|Lynch and Park 2017]]).[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn10|¹⁰]] An analytical reachability check evaluates whether the target ${SE(3)}$ pose ${\mathbf{T}_{\text{target}}}$ resides inside this workspace manifold ${\mathcal{W}}$, while an occupancy lookup in spatial memory confirms that the target volume does not intersect persistent static obstacles.
2.  **Dynamic Reachability Transit Check:** Even when a target lies inside the static workspace manifold ${\mathcal{W}}$, it may be physically unreachable within the finite window during which its sensor evidence remains valid. This check evaluates whether the end effector can transition from its measured state $({\mathbf{p}}_0, {\mathbf{v}}_0)$ to the target pose within the remaining window ${t_{\text{intent\_exp}}} - {t_{\text{now,mono}}}$ while respecting its velocity, acceleration, jerk, and thermal torque limits.

Whatever continuous trajectory profile the actuators follow, no physical move covers distance ${d} = \|{\mathbf{p}_{\text{target}}} - {\mathbf{p}}_0\|$ faster than its maximum speed ceiling allows. Here both ${\mathbf{p}}$ terms are translation vectors in the same Cartesian frame, not full ${SE(3)}$ poses; orientation feasibility requires a separate check. For terminal completion before the current expiry, a necessary condition is:

$$
\frac{{d}}{{v_{\max}}} \le {t_{\text{intent\_exp}}} - {t_{\text{now,mono}}}. \tag{6}
$$

 Actuator acceleration limits, jerk ceilings, and motor torque-speed curves only lengthen the required transit time beyond this kinematic floor. A proposal that violates this inequality cannot promise terminal completion in that window. A request for an intermediate guarded segment may remain feasible if its executed motion and stopping fallback fit the current authority, deferring continuation to newly admitted evidence. **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-reachability|Dynamic Reachability, Intent Envelopes, and Differential Games]]** gives triangular and trapezoidal transit bounds and explains how jerk limits change the complete-move check.

^eq-intent-dynamic-reachability-bound

The scalar lower bound is an early rejection test, not a trajectory generator. It avoids optimizing a terminal request that is already too late, leaving detailed acceleration profiles, obstacle avoidance, and stopping reserves to the fuller motion check. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^eq-intent-dynamic-reachability-bound|Equation 6]] couples spatial distance to evidence lifetime, so dynamic reachability evaluates the target and its supporting evidence together rather than the spatial coordinate alone.

↪ Resolving the admitted goal region into a timed trajectory is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.04 Replacement Timing and the Planning Horizon#^sec-planning-goal-to-trajectory|Replacement Timing and the Planning Horizon]]**.

**Definition 10.2: Dynamic reachability**

***Dynamic reachability*** is the property that the end effector can move from its measured state to a target pose, within its velocity, acceleration, jerk, and thermal limits, before the evidence supporting that target expires.

1.  **Significance**: It prices a target in time as well as space. At the arm’s 1 m/s TCP limit, [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^eq-intent-dynamic-reachability-bound|equation 6]] allows at most 219 mm of travel inside the mug’s 219 ms tracked evidence window, so a handle 300 mm away is dynamically unreachable on one capture even though it lies well inside the workspace.
2.  **Distinction**: Static reachability evaluates geometric feasibility alone (whether any joint configuration can achieve the pose). Dynamic reachability introduces the clock and actuator dynamics. Unlike the dynamic obstacle forecasting of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^sec-intent-dynamic-forecasting|section 10.6.1]], which bounds where external actors might move, dynamic reachability bounds where the robot’s end effector can safely travel within a bounded lease.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Attempting to resolve an unreachable target by stretching its evidence deadline in software. Only fresh sensor measurements can renew an expiring window; the remedy is a staged approach whose segments each fit their own evidence and fallback.

On the arm, the mug’s evidence under the qualified tracker stays valid for 219 ms after its capture. At the 1 m/s TCP speed limit, the gripper could cover at most 219 mm even if the entire window remained. A terminal proposal issued with the gripper 300 mm from the handle needs at least 300 ms of transit. It therefore receives `ERR_DYNAMIC_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` if it promises completion on that record. Without the tracker, the full window is only 60 ms and the corresponding speed-only reach is 60 mm. Processing and transport consume part of either window. Acceleration further reduces the reachable distance.

This bound and the fuller checks behind it assume that actuators stay within their nominal thermal ratings, that encoders report the initial state $({\mathbf{p}}_0, {\mathbf{v}}_0)$ correctly, and that the payload matches the inertial model. When winding temperatures cross the thresholds of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.06 Thermal Duty Cycles#^sec-body-thermal-capacity|Thermal capacity and insulation breakdown thresholds]]**, or a force measurement reveals a payload mismatch, the permission path lowers ${a_{\max}}$ and ${v_{\max}}$ (**thermal derating**). It then evaluates new proposals against the reduced limits and rechecks any admitted path that the derating may have made infeasible.

A rejection must not simply discard the packet, which would leave the reasoning model waiting for a task timeout. The intent filter returns a structured diagnostic across the proposal boundary: the classification (`ERR_WORKSPACE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`, `ERR_DYNAMIC_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`, or `ERR_OCCUPANCY_BLOCKED`) and the numerical margin of failure. A dynamic failure reports the shortfall between the transit bound and the remaining window; a workspace failure reports the nearest reachable point on the boundary of ${\mathcal{W}}$. The reasoning layer can then seek fresher evidence, choose another approach, slow the process, or propose moving the base. It cannot extend the evidence horizon without new sensor data.

The diagnostic makes a staged approach possible. Consider a setup where the pre-contact corridor and its stopping reserve are qualified, a closer wrist observation supports the 3 mm error bound, and the tracker holds the declared slip bound. The rejected terminal promise becomes a pre-contact observation goal. A separately checked corridor carries the gripper toward a better view while keeping a stop outside contact, and the closer capture supplies the mug-handle belief record whose error fits the 15 mm tolerance.

Better sensing does not shorten the physical transit floor. The 300 mm approach still takes at least 300 ms, longer than the 219 ms horizon of any one capture, so it needs at least 2 fresh captures. The planner executes guarded segments under chunk leases, and each continuation is checked against the current intent, target evidence, corridor, and fallback. The predicted route beyond the admitted segment carries no authority. Once the last segment and the closure pass the full trajectory check, the gripper completes the grasp inside the tolerance and moves to the admitted hold for handover. If the closer observation fails, identity becomes unresolved, or a renewal arrives late, the next segment is refused, and existing motion continues only while its own evidence, authority, and fallback remain valid.

↪ The staged approach, with each trajectory segment re-anchored to a fresh wrist-camera capture, is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.04 Replacement Timing and the Planning Horizon#^sec-planning-tail-latency-budgeting|Tail latency budgeting across a mission]]**.

### Dynamic Obstacle Forecasting and Forward Occupancy

^sec-intent-dynamic-forecasting

Even when the mug handle lies within kinematic and dynamic reach, a coworker may be about to cross the approach corridor, a conflict that the static workspace and transit tests alone cannot reject. **Dynamic obstacle forecasting** carries the perception and memory estimates forward into regions that moving obstacles could occupy so the intent stage can reject blocked corridors early, before the planner and permission path check the complete timed trajectory. Because this forecast predicts only where people might move and says nothing about whether those people can tell where the machine will move, it covers only one half of how coworkers and robots stay clear of one another.

↪ Motion and signals that let the people around a machine infer its goal early enough to yield are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/17 Responsible Physical AI/V4-17.07 Who Bears the Risk#^sec-responsibility-legible-intent|Legible intent]]**.

The intent filter consumes the dynamic track manifest $[(\text{ID}_k, {\hat{\mathbf{p}}}_k, {\hat{\mathbf{v}}}_k, {\mathbf{P}}_k, {t_{\text{last}}}, \text{state})]$ published by the multi-object tracker, alongside tentative detections, raw occupied regions, and unknown-space evidence, because failure to confirm an identity does not make its occupied region clear. For each confirmed or coasting track ${k}$, the admission engine constructs a **forward occupancy set** ${\mathcal{R}}_k({\Delta} {t})$, the region the actor could occupy ${\Delta} {t}$ after its last measurement:

$$
{\mathcal{R}}_k({\Delta} {t}) = \left\{ {\mathbf{p}} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \;\middle|\; \|{\mathbf{p}} - {\hat{\mathbf{p}}}_k\| \le {r}_k + {v_h} {\Delta} {t} + {k_\sigma} {\sigma_{\text{track}}}({\Delta} {t}) \right\} \tag{7}
$$

 Here ${r}_k$ is a declared body envelope that covers torso, limbs, and carried objects. The term ${v_h}$ is a validated bound on the actor’s speed. The term ${k_\sigma}$ is a selected multiplier whose joint spatial coverage must be calibrated rather than read off a scalar Gaussian rule. The term ${\sigma_{\text{track}}}({\Delta} {t})$ is the root-mean-square position deviation of the propagated track covariance. That deviation need not grow monotonically during coasting, so the check needs a conservative bound over the whole interval. ${\mathcal{R}}_k({\Delta} {t})$ is called an occupancy set rather than a reachability set because dynamic reachability bounds where the robot can get in time, whereas forward occupancy bounds where a person might be. A tracker with a validated acceleration bound can instead center the set on the predicted position ${\hat{\mathbf{p}}}_k + {\hat{\mathbf{v}}}_k {\Delta} {t}$ and grow it with $\frac{1}{2} {a_{k,\max}} {\Delta} {t}^2$, which is tighter over short intervals. The speed-bound form is the conservative one that safety standards use.

^eq-intent-forward-occupancy

↩︎ The dynamic track manifest and the tentative, confirmed, and coasting track lifecycle are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-perception-tracking-association|Multi-object tracking and data association]]**.

[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^eq-intent-forward-occupancy|Equation 7]] is the human half of speed and separation monitoring (SSM), a certified safety function that runs beneath the permission path and keeps a protective separation distance between the moving robot and a person. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-certified-functions|Certified safety functions beneath the permission path]]** sizes that distance under ISO 10218-2:2025, which incorporates the collaborative-application requirements formerly published as ISO/TS 15066 (the technical specification itself remains published and is under revision) ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-iso10218part2|International Organization for Standardization 2025]], [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-isots15066|2016]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-marvel2017implementing|Marvel and Norcross 2017]]). The separation calculation assumes the ISO 13855 walking approach speed[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.14 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]] of 1.6 m/s for an approaching person ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-iso13855|International Organization for Standardization 2024]]). The intent stage uses the same speed bound for an earlier and coarser purpose, rejecting a corridor before the planner spends time on it.

↩︎ The mapping of the robot’s protective-separation terms onto its stopping-distance budget is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.04 Kinetic Momentum#^sec-body-stopping-distance-prebrake|Kinematic stopping distance and pre-brake delay]]**.

Screening a corridor means sweeping the robot’s candidate transit volume ${\mathcal{T}_{\text{robot}}}({\Delta} {t})$ forward in time beside every person’s set. On the aisle of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^fig-10-dynamic-reachability-cones|figure 7]], the coworker’s set grows at the ISO 13855 walking speed from 96 mm over the untracked horizon to 351 mm over the tracked one, and the corridor enters the larger disc, so the screen refuses it. Under declared motion bounds, the early screen requires the candidate transit volume to remain disjoint from every active occupancy set across the execution window. Here ${{t_{\text{age}}}}_k$ is the upper age of actor ${k}$’s last measurement at the current admission epoch, including bounded clock-conversion error, and ${\Delta} {t}$ is additional future time:

$$
{\mathcal{T}_{\text{robot}}}({\Delta} {t}) \cap {\mathcal{R}}_k({{t_{\text{age}}}}_k + {\Delta} {t}) = \emptyset \quad \forall {\Delta} {t} \in [0, {t_{\text{intent\_exp}}} - {t_{\text{now,mono}}}], \;\; \forall {k} \tag{8}
$$

 Full trajectory admission must extend the exclusion check through the response delay and the complete stopping fallback. If a candidate corridor violates [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^eq-intent-dynamic-exclusion|equation 8]] by intersecting an expanding set, the early screen can return `ERR_DYNAMIC_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` and request a different corridor or a yield. The refusal diagnostic returns the earliest predicted conflict time ${\Delta} {t_{\text{conflict}}}$ and the conflicting actor identifier $\text{ID}_k$. Passing this screen does not establish a collision-free trajectory. The planner must supply the timed motion, and the permission path must check its swept volume and stopping reserve against evidence that remains valid. The arithmetic of this linear growth is simple; what decides the outcome is the horizon over which it runs.

**Napkin Math 10.3: The freezing radius**

**Problem**: A coworker walks in the 1.30 m aisle while the arm screens its corridor. How far can the growth term of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.07 Admissible Goal Sets#^eq-intent-forward-occupancy|equation 7]] carry that person’s set over the mug’s evidence horizons and over a 2 s planning horizon, and beyond what horizon does one person’s set alone span the aisle?

**Variables**:

- Approach speed ${v_h} =$ 1.6 m/s (Datasheet, ISO 13855 design value)
- Evidence horizons 60 ms untracked and 219 ms tracked (Derived, [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^eq-intent-evidence-horizon-roots|equation 5]]); planning horizon 2 s (Illustrative)
- Aisle width ${w} =$ 1.30 m (Illustrative)

**Math**:

1.  **Evidence horizons**: 1.6 m/s $\times$ 60 ms $=$ 96 mm, and 1.6 m/s $\times$ 219 ms $=$ 351 mm.
2.  **Planning horizon**: 1.6 m/s $\times$ 2 s $=$ 3.2 m.
3.  **Crossover**: The growth term reaches the aisle width when ${v_h}\,{\Delta} {t} = {w}$, at ${\Delta} {t} =$ 1.30 m $/$ 1.6 m/s $=$ 0.81 s.

**Result**: Over the evidence horizons the set grows by centimeters to decimeters; past 0.81 s a single person’s set covers the full aisle before the body envelope and tracking terms are even added.

**Systems insight**: The duration of the screening horizon dictates whether the robot can plan feasible motion. Extending a dynamic forecast beyond the admitted motion and its complete stopping fallback can reject a corridor for possible human movements outside the current commitment. In narrow operating aisles, unnecessarily long forecast horizons can block otherwise admissible progress. The shorter screen must still cover response delay and deceleration; evidence expiry does not end physical motion.

A set that outgrows the aisle is the root of the **freezing robot problem**. Trautman and Krause showed that when a planner predicts each person independently and lets that uncertainty grow, the predicted occupancy of a crowd covers every path, and the robot stops even though people would make room for it ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.13 Source References#^ref-trautman2010unfreezing|Trautman and Krause 2010]]). Three choices reduce unnecessary conservatism, and none permits shrinking a physically justified bound merely to obtain motion:

1.  *Short receding horizons:* Screening over the evidence horizon rather than seconds ahead keeps the growth term near 351 mm instead of meters. The forecast must still cover the entire executed prefix and the physical fallback, including response delay and deceleration; shortening the lease does not shorten an actor’s possible motion while the robot stops.
2.  *Re-centering on fresh evidence:* A qualified tracker re-centers each set on new measurements (${t}_0 \leftarrow {t_{\text{fresh}}}$), resetting the growth term and the propagated covariance, provided its accuracy and latency support the required bound.
3.  *Interaction-aware prediction:* Trautman and Krause’s own remedy predicts the people and the robot jointly, so that each accounts for how the others will respond. Such predictions are learned or modeled proposals, and they may narrow a set only after their error has been bounded by the same kind of evidence the permission path demands of any other input.

The discs are centered on the coworker’s last measured position, so the tracker’s lifecycle sets their age. A confirmed track receives fresh measurements, while a coasting track accumulates propagated uncertainty and widens the exclusion region as its evidence ages. A goal proposal cannot refresh a stale track without incoming sensor data, and a detour proposed after a refusal needs its own timed check. When a person walks out of view, terminating the software track does not make the occluded space free. Memory already erodes certified free space behind an occlusion at the same walking-speed bound (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^sec-memory-belief-through-occlusion|Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay]]**), and that unknown-space policy remains in force until new observations justify a different claim.

**Checkpoint 10.1: Evidence windows and reach**

A target is admissible only while its evidence lasts, and every early screen in this section is sized by that window.

**Evidence-horizon estimate**: Suppose the mug drifts at 50 mm/s instead of riding the belt, with the same 15 mm tolerance and 3 mm initial error. Can you compute the untracked evidence horizon and decide whether the 2 Hz intent model could renew it without a tracker?

**Transit bound**: Can you explain why a terminal move inside the static workspace can still fail admission, and what a staged approach changes about it and what it cannot change?

**Occupancy vs. reachability**: Which question does a forward occupancy set answer that dynamic reachability does not, and why does screening over the evidence horizon rather than seconds ahead keep the robot from freezing?

**Stretched deadlines**: Why can neither faster processing of the same old frame nor a longer requested lease extend a target whose evidence has expired?

Reachability and corridor filters reject provably impossible requests before planning begins. The survivors remain candidates, carrying the target bounds and obstacle evidence needed for the full motion check. The remaining challenge is distinguishing between failures these physical checks can expose and errors in task meaning that geometry and kinematics alone cannot resolve.
