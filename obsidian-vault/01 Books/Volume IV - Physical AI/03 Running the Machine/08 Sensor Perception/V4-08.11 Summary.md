# V4-08.11 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08 Sensor Perception|V4-08 Sensor Perception]]

^sec-perception-summary

A perception measurement is actionable only when its capture epoch, clock synchronization bound, coordinate frame, and spatial uncertainty match the kinematic requirements of the intended motion. Sensor selection begins with the physical quantity demanded by the control decision: photometric appearance, metric range, Doppler radial velocity, or contact deformation. Physical capture constraints then determine the exact temporal and geometric validity of that measurement. For rolling-shutter cameras, row-by-row readout creates geometric distortion that must be compensated before triangulation, while an optical bearing ray provides directional bearing but no distance until stereo disparity or active time-of-flight ranging resolves depth. Furthermore, calibration matrices remain valid only within the physical and environmental envelopes under which they were characterized. Latency between exposure and downstream dispatch directly consumes the vehicle’s stopping budget. At the drive limit, the first five rows of that budget leave 187.1 mm of the 1.10 m clear distance, a remainder that must still absorb the dispatch-to-admission interval, the jerk-limited stop, and the tracking inset. Whether the machine may drive at that speed is therefore a question this chapter hands forward to **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.07 Silicon Isolation Decisions#^sec-placement-dispatch-admission|The board-link budget and the dispatch-to-admission interval]]** and **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-stopping-envelopes|Stopping Envelopes]]**, which close the budget. **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^tbl-enforcement-stopping-budget|The warehouse mobile manipulator's stopping budget]]** lists every term with the chapter that prices it. Because an embodied system continues to travel while sensor data moves through serialization, transport, neural inference, and planner admission, unmeasured pipeline delays erode physical clearance margins just as severely as spatial measurement error.

Spatial error deducts from that same physical clearance budget. Stereo depth uncertainty scales quadratically with distance under fixed disparity noise, while angular extrinsic misalignment introduces lever-arm errors that widen linearly with range. System designers cannot rely on nominal datasheets or raw model outputs to establish safety bounds. Zero-mean covariance matrices frequently ignore systematic calibration biases, and classification confidences or learned depth logits do not represent calibrated tail probabilities. Establishing a rigorous 99th-percentile clearance inset requires empirical residual quantiles evaluated directly against ground-truth measurements across the operational domain. Perception delivers individual dated, localized, and qualified measurements; integrating these discrete observations into a coherent state estimate that remains reliable between sensor arrivals is the responsibility of spatial memory.

Silent failures present the most severe operational hazards in physical systems, precisely because dropped frames or overt hardware faults trigger immediate system alerts, whereas misdated frames, saturated detectors, or hallucinated traversable paths arrive without raising error flags. To guard against corrupted data, the perception pipeline enforces an explicit observation contract. Downstream consumers inspect and validate the capture timestamp, clock synchronization bound, reference frame, calibration identifier, error model, and hardware health flags before reading payload buffers. Even with rigorous contracts, confirming semantic truth requires corroboration across independent, complementary sensing modalities. **Key Takeaways: Spatial perception and age of information**

- **Observation age begins at capture and is paid in travel:** Hardware-latched exposure timestamps, row readout schedules, and bounded clock conversion enable downstream consumers to compute a qualified upper age bound. Every microsecond elapsed from photon arrival to actuator response translates directly into unobserved travel (${d_{\text{blind}}} = {v} \cdot {\tau_{\text{delay}}}$) deducted from the physical clearance margin.
- **Choose the measurement for the decision:** Photometric appearance, metric range, Doppler radial velocity, high-rate edge transitions, and tactile contact deformation address distinct physical questions. Multi-sensor fusion improves system resilience only when sensor spatial coverage and correlated common-cause failure modes are rigorously qualified.
- **Fix the geometry before interpreting it:** Rolling-shutter readout shears geometric edges under vehicle motion (${\Delta} {x} = {v_{\text{base}}} \cdot {y} \cdot {t_{\text{row}}}$), requiring per-row pose compensation prior to spatial triangulation. Mechanical vibration and thermal cycling also induce extrinsic calibration drift. An extrinsic shift of 0.005 rad at 10 m projects to 50 mm laterally, as large as the entire localization allowance ${\delta_{\text{loc}}}$. Consequently, every calibration record must carry an explicit validity envelope that consumers verify prior to inference.
- **A wrong timestamp is worse than a lost frame:** A dropped frame is an overt failure that triggering systems can handle gracefully, whereas a plausible but misdated frame silently displaces an obstacle’s reconstructed position while reporting deceptively narrow uncertainty. Hardware capture timestamps, clock synchronization error bounds, sequence continuity checks, and age validation must always precede payload interpretation.
- **Claimed error must be measured:** Softmax classification confidences and depth-bin logits do not characterize spatial tail risk, and zero-mean covariance matrices fail to capture systematic calibration bias. Bounding collision risk requires empirical residual quantiles evaluated against high-precision ground truth across operational conditions to establish defensible clearance insets.
- **The contract bounds what a consumer can refuse:** The observation contract packages capture epoch, clock conversion bounds, reference frame, calibration identity, error bounds, and hardware health flags so the planning permission path can reject untrustworthy claims before reading payload data. Even a fully operational sensor can report a clear path that does not exist, and structured observation tokens do not guarantee that downstream policies compensate for transport latency. Establishing semantic truth requires independent cross-modal verification, and spatial memory must synthesize these dated snapshots into a coherent state that persists through occlusion.

^callout-takeaways--8-2-83cdbd80

Perception supplies the grounded evidence that physical action needs: detected obstacles, identified affordances, and measured range and velocity across complementary modalities. Its output is still a sequence of isolated measurements, each bound to a capture epoch and a sensor frame, and each qualified for the instant it was captured. A qualified measurement states where something was, how old that knowledge is, and how far to trust it. It never states where that thing is now, and when a worker steps behind a rack upright, direct perception falls silent.

The observation contract is where Part III’s law, proposal is not permission, first takes physical form. A confident detection does not grant permission to move. The permission path admits motion only on evidence whose age, frame, and error it can check against the clearance that remains, and it refuses everything else before reading the payload. Every later stage in this part inherits that discipline, because each one consumes a dated claim that it did not produce and cannot refresh.
