*Why does an admitted plan still need permission on every control tick?*

A trajectory can be feasible when it arrives and become unsafe while the machine follows it. The body lags its reference, a payload or a hot motor erodes braking authority, a person steps into the path, or the learned proposer stalls with its last command still in force. A machine that checks only before motion begins discovers these changes after the energy is already moving, and the proposer cannot be trusted to notice them, because nothing in its output bounds stopping distance, actuator effort, or timing. Learned proposals therefore need an independent permission path that re-checks current conditions on every tick, keeps useful motion inside a stopping envelope, corrects a command when a feasible correction exists, and holds a response it can execute without the proposer before the remaining margin is spent. Each of those guarantees depends on bounded sensing error, available actuator force, and a decision delivered on time. Enforcement joins these conditions at the causal boundary, where a permitted command becomes motion that software cannot undo.

**Learning Objectives**

- Calculate the finished stopping budget and its speed ceiling, treating the tracking bound as a premise the enforcer monitors on every tick
- Derive how momentum and commanded effort change the remaining safety margin, and the contact force at a latch and at a handover that caps approach speed
- Explain how a control barrier function limits motion while its model, sensing, actuator, and timing assumptions hold
- Formulate a quadratic program that minimally adjusts a proposal within feasible actuator and safety constraints
- Select state-matched fallback responses when sensing, computation, or actuator limits invalidate normal enforcement
- Distinguish the permission path’s unrated performance checks from the certified drive, scanner, and arm safety functions that carry the protective claim beneath it
- Construct an enforcement record and formulate the admitted command that binds each check to its detector, its fallback response, and the records it consumes
