# V4-12.17 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Simplex Architecture**: The design came from Carnegie Mellon in the 1990s as a way to upgrade the controller of a running plant without stopping it. An untested new controller drove the plant while a simple verified controller and a switching rule stood ready to take it back ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-seto1998simplex|Seto et al. 1998]]). The learned proposer occupies the untested controller’s seat, and the design’s premise, that the region from which the verified controller can still recover is known in advance, is the stopping envelope this chapter computes.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Control Lyapunov Function**: A Lyapunov function certifies that an unforced system settles; a control Lyapunov function asks only that, at each state, some admissible input make it decrease. Artstein ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-artstein1983stabilization|1983]]) tied the existence of such a function to stabilizability by feedback, and Sontag ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-sontag1989universal|1989]]) gave an explicit formula that builds the stabilizing input from it. The control barrier function, defined later in this chapter, is its mirror image, keeping the state out of a region instead of pulling it toward a point, and when the two conflict the combined optimization relaxes convergence so that the barrier holds.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Integrator Windup**: A proportional-integral controller’s integral term continues summing tracking error while an actuator sits at its saturation limit, as standard feedback loops do not inherently know that command outputs are clipped. When tracking error reverses, this accumulated state must unwind before the actuator command can leave the saturation rail. That unwinding delay directly consumes safety clearance. While modern anti-windup schemes clamp or back-calculate integrator state during saturation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-astrom2021feedback|Åström and Murray 2021]]), the safety enforcer must still budget whatever residual unwinding delay remains in the low-level hardware controller.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Barrier Function**: In constrained optimization, an interior barrier term such as $-\log(-{g}({x}))$ grows without bound near constraint boundaries, preventing numerical iterates from escaping the feasible set. Barrier certificates extended this mathematical principle to dynamical systems, proving that no trajectory starting in a safe set can ever reach an unsafe state ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-prajna2004barrier|Prajna and Jadbabaie 2004]]). Control barrier functions subsequently formulated this certificate as an explicit, affine condition on the instantaneous control input ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-wieland2007constructive|Wieland and Allgöwer 2007]]). While an optimization algorithm’s safety guarantee holds because its numerical iterates are evaluated exactly in software, a physical plant’s safety guarantee holds only as long as actuator limits, dynamics, and real-time execution match the model assumed by the certificate.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Nagumo’s Theorem**: Nagumo ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-nagumo1942lage|1942]]) established that, under conditions ensuring unique solutions, a closed set is forward-invariant if and only if the system vector field points into the set or remains tangent to its boundary at every boundary point; viability theory subsequently formalized and generalized this principle. Because Nagumo’s condition applies to continuous-time flows, a digital controller executing at periodic control ticks requires an inter-sample buffer, sized among the conditions forward invariance rests on ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-invariance-conditions|section 12.6.3]]), to guarantee physical safety between discrete updates.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Lie Derivative** (the derivative along a flow): Named after Sophus Lie, whose continuous transformation groups generate the flows along which the derivative is taken, the Lie derivative in control theory is identical to the Jacobian-vector product in modern scientific computing. Because both describe the same directional derivative, the automatic differentiation frameworks used during neural policy training can directly evaluate drift and control authority for learned or tabulated barrier functions, whereas the embedded safety enforcer executes the analytical closed form.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **Kinetic barrier directional derivative chain rule**: Evaluating ${\dot{h}}({\theta_{\text{clear}}}, {\omega}) = \frac{\partial {h}}{\partial {\theta_{\text{clear}}}}{\dot{\theta}_{\text{clear}}} + \frac{\partial {h}}{\partial {\omega}}{\dot{\omega}}$ with ${\dot{\theta}_{\text{clear}}} = -{\omega}$ and ${\dot{\omega}} = ({\tau} + {\tau_{\text{bias}}})/{J_{\text{eff}}}$ yields ${\dot{h}} = (-{\omega} - \frac{{\omega}{\tau_{\text{bias}}}}{{J_{\text{eff}}}{\alpha_{\text{net}}}}) - \frac{{\omega}}{{J_{\text{eff}}}{\alpha_{\text{net}}}}{\tau} = {L_{\mathbf{f}} h}({\theta_{\text{clear}}},{\omega}) + {L_{\mathbf{g}} h}({\theta_{\text{clear}}},{\omega}){\tau}$. Substituting into ${\dot{h}} + {\gamma_{\text{cbf}}} {h} \ge 0$ and rearranging produces $\frac{{\omega}({\tau} + {\tau_{\text{bias}}})}{{J_{\text{eff}}}{\alpha_{\text{net}}}} \le {\gamma_{\text{cbf}}} {h} - {\omega}$. Multiplying by the positive factor $\frac{{J_{\text{eff}}}{\alpha_{\text{net}}}}{{\omega}}$ and subtracting the bias yields [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^eq-enforce-kinetic-torque|equation 6]].

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Standstill velocity thresholding**: In microcontroller firmware, floating-point evaluation of $\frac{{\gamma_{\text{cbf}}} {h}}{{\omega}}$ is explicitly guarded by a velocity threshold ${\epsilon_{\text{vel}}}$: when $|{\omega}| \le {\epsilon_{\text{vel}}}$, the kinetic barrier is bypassed in favor of a static acceleration or position-hold bound, preventing floating-point division by zero and NaN exceptions in the sub-millisecond loop.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability**: Originating in the zero-sum differential game formulations of Isaacs ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-isaacs1965differential|1965]]), reachability analysis frames safety as a game between a control policy and a worst-case adversarial disturbance. The resulting safe set contains precisely those states from which the controller can guarantee safety against any bounded disturbance trajectory. Consequently, an HJ reachability table is inherently robust but conservative, and its safety guarantee holds only within the assumed disturbance envelope.

^fn9

<sup>10</sup> **Dead-Man Control**: Early electric locomotives incorporated a spring-loaded dead-man handle that the operator had to depress continuously; releasing the handle (if the driver lost consciousness, for example) interrupted tractive power and engaged the mechanical brakes. System safety arose from the cessation of action rather than the transmission of an explicit fault message. The chunk lease applies the same principle in software. The host must actively renew its permission, ensuring that an upstream processor too damaged to command a stop is inherently incapable of renewing execution authority.

^fn10

<sup>11</sup> **Shielding**: In safe reinforcement learning, a shield sits between the learning agent and its environment and overrides only those actions that would violate a temporal-logic safety specification ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-alshiekh2018safe|Alshiekh et al. 2018]]). The CBF-QP is a continuous-control shield. Its override is the smallest torque change rather than a substituted discrete action, and its specification is a stopping margin rather than a logic formula, which lets it keep the learned action’s direction instead of replacing it.

^fn11

<sup>12</sup> **Dissipativity**: Willems ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-willems1972dissipative|1972]]) generalized circuit passivity to any dynamical system with a storage function, an energy-like quantity that can grow no faster than the power supplied to the system. Interconnected passive systems inherit that bound, which is why contact with an unknown but passive environment can be kept stable without a model of the environment. The energy tank is a storage function that the controller keeps explicitly in software, so the bound holds only as far as the tank’s bookkeeping matches the work the actuators actually do.

^fn12

<sup>13</sup> **Zero-Order Hold**: The order counts the derivatives the hold carries forward. A zero-order hold keeps the command’s value constant until the next sample, while a first-order hold would also extrapolate its slope. For one control tick the plant therefore integrates a torque the controller can no longer change, which is the source of the drift buffer ${v} {T_{\text{tick}}} + \frac{1}{2}{a_{\max}} {T_{\text{tick}}}^2$; **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-zoh|Discrete-time sampling and zero-order hold phase lag]]** develops the hold’s delay.

^fn13

<sup>14</sup> **Active-Set QP**: An active-set solver guesses which constraints hold with equality, solves the resulting linear system, and adds or drops one constraint per iteration. Dual variants such as that of Goldfarb and Idnani ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-goldfarb1983numerically|1983]]) start from the unconstrained optimum, here the nominal command itself, and add only violated constraints, so a proposal that already satisfies every barrier costs almost nothing. The iteration count is bounded by the combinations of the constraint set rather than by a clock, so a fixed iteration cap bounds steps while the time per step must still be measured under interference.

^fn14

<sup>15</sup> **Machine Stop Categories**: IEC 60204-1 distinguishes uncontrolled power removal (Category 0), controlled stopping followed by power removal (Category 1), and controlled stopping with power retained (Category 2). A category defines electrical power status rather than stopping distance; every rung therefore requires its own empirically validated stopping measurement.

^fn15
