# V4-12.12 The Enforcement Record

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^sec-enforcement-telemetry

When a warehouse mobile manipulator halts at a rack end because runtime safety logic rejected a motion proposal, forensic analysis requires unambiguous provenance: which threshold failed, which physical detector measured the violation, and which fallback rung took control of the actuators. The **enforcement record** is the tenth record in the record chain, the point where offline characterization meets online execution. It formalizes runtime safety across three cooperating artifacts:

1.  **Enforcement contract**: The signed, immutable specification compiled into microcontroller firmware that fixes qualified tracking bounds ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$, barrier coefficients, timer deadlines, delivered-effort error and response-delay bounds, and fallback routing tables before deployment.
2.  **Admitted command**: The record’s per-tick output. On each millisecond control cycle, the microcontroller gatekeeper generates an admitted command for transmission across the causal boundary. This payload contains the admitted or projected control ${\mathbf{u}^*}$, the verified barrier margin ${h}({\mathbf{x}})$, tracking error validation ${e}({t}) \le {\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$, and the remaining hardware lease. The gatekeeper forwards only the bare setpoint and protective envelope to the motor drives, committing the complete supervisory state to the audit log.
3.  **Enforcement audit log**: The high-rate forensic stream that captures per-tick proposal evaluations, intervention vectors ${\Delta} {\mathbf{u}} = {\mathbf{u}^*} - {\mathbf{u}_{\text{nom}}}$, quadratic programming (QP) solve latencies, and veto events in non-volatile storage.

Together, these artifacts render safety decisions fully auditable without burdening the actuator bus. The contract predefines the admissible operating envelope, the admitted command tags the immediate actuator setpoint, and the audit log preserves the causal chain of evidence. Real-time enforcement depends entirely on the protected command path, deterministic loop timing, and verified physical braking capabilities.

### The Admitted Command Schema

^sec-enforcement-admitted-command-schema

If the gatekeeper emits a bare torque vector without a monotonic sequence counter, a digest of the admitted parent trajectory, or the remaining lease duration, a replayed frame or a stalled microcontroller cannot be distinguished from a live command, and a post-incident audit cannot reconstruct which safety margins were checked on that millisecond tick. At each control cycle, the gatekeeper therefore produces an admitted command that establishes runtime authority at the drive interface ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.12 The Enforcement Record#^tbl-12-admitted-command-schema|table 5]]). Its fields explicitly bind the actuator setpoint to its parent trajectory, verified safety margins, and active fallback state. Because each entry records a decision made under assumed physical conditions, runtime containment still relies on those assumptions matching physical reality.

|:---------------------------|:------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| `timestamp_mcu_ns`         | `uint64_t` (ns)         | Absolute MCU monotonic timestamp of admission                                                                                                            | Latch instant at causal boundary                                                               |
| `parent_trajectory_digest` | `uint8_t[32]` (SHA-256) | Cryptographic link to the admitted trajectory record and chunk lease                                                                                     | SHA-256 of active trajectory record                                                            |

Formed on every 1 ms tick by the microcontroller and written to the audit log, identifying the selected command, checked assumptions, and state-matched response. Constructing the enforcement record synthesizes four upstream records across the offline and runtime evidence strands, where the offline strand establishes physical plant capabilities and empirical policy competence while the runtime strand tracks live execution state. From the runtime strand, the trajectory record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-trajectory-contract|The Trajectory Record]]** supplies the admitted reference and its precomputed, dynamically feasible stop suffix. From the offline strand, the evaluation record of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-logs|The Evaluation Record]]** provides runtime monitor specifications and the empirical coverage-gap catalog that define the active supervisory channels. The policy manifest of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/06 Policy Training/V4-06.09 The Policy Manifest#^sec-training-policy-manifest|The Policy Manifest]]** defines the declared ODD so that the enforcer’s validity region is restricted to well-sampled operating regimes. The limit records of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.08 Measuring a Machine’s Own Limits#^sec-body-measuring-limits|Measuring a Machine's Own Limits]]** establish empirical braking rates, actuator onset delays, and torque envelopes alongside their operating preconditions. To these upstream parameters, the enforcement record contributes the formal guarantees of the gatekeeping pipeline. These are the qualified tracking bound ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$ and the stopping metrics (${T_{\text{stop}}}, {d_{\text{stop}}}$, which bound the duration and displacement required to arrest plant momentum). They also include the control barrier function coefficients, the discrete execution period, the per-check thresholds mapped to fallback-ladder rungs, and the dynamic validity boundary.

The `parent_trajectory_digest` binds each admitted command to its originating trajectory and active chunk lease, providing forensic traceability back to the planner’s proposal. Digests bind content for integrity checks; sequence counters and monotonic freshness checks reject stale or replayed commands within the protected drive interface. Sender authenticity additionally requires an authenticated channel or message authentication. Similarly, the `lease_remaining_ns` field conveys remaining execution authority, but this parameter guarantees safety only when the drive firmware actively enforces lease expiration by triggering a deterministic physical fallback. Streaming these fields to the audit log, alongside nominal proposals, intervention deltas, barrier margins, tracking errors, and solver latencies, gives engineers the causal evidence needed to reconstruct anomalous behaviors within the temporal resolution of the sensor pipeline.

This division of responsibilities dictates an architectural split at the fieldbus boundary. Motor drives have no operational need for trajectory digests or barrier margins; they require immediate setpoints, current limits, and active fallback commands delivered with fail-safe transport guarantees. Industrial systems handle this through established safety bus architectures. For example, Safety over EtherCAT (FSoE) implements the **black-channel principle**, treating the underlying communication bus as inherently untrusted ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-iec61784-3-2021|International Electrotechnical Commission 2021]]). FSoE encapsulates drive commands within a safety container featuring dedicated cyclic redundancy checks (CRCs), unique connection identifiers, strict sequence counters, and independent watchdog timers. These measures detect communication faults; sender authentication against malicious traffic requires a separate security mechanism. When a communication watchdog expires, the drive autonomously engages its safe stop state without waiting for microcontroller intervention. The rich diagnostic context (digests, barrier margins, intervention vectors, and solver runtimes) streams to the audit log instead. An ad hoc safety-communication scheme for drive control must independently justify the fault detection and response properties covered by established functional-safety standards.

↪ The authority record’s per-tick entries, which log the ladder rung of any active fallback, are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.09 The Authority Record#^sec-intervention-evidence-tuples|Per-tick evidence tuples and the fault injection suite]]**.

Filled in for the warehouse mobile manipulator, the enforcement record fixes the base’s stopping parameters at the rack end alongside the arm’s validity region and monitoring channels at the doorway and packing station. The tracking bound ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$ is 40 mm (matching row 7 of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^tbl-enforcement-stopping-budget|table 1]]), and the resident stopping trajectory is the jerk-limited S-curve suffix, which takes 931.25 ms and covers 605.31 mm from the 1.3 m/s aisle speed, or 1,031.25 ms and 773.44 mm from the 1.5 m/s drive limit. These spatial bounds are evaluated on a 1 ms loop period with a declared WCET of 250 ${\text{µ}}\text{s}$ inside a 400 ${\text{µ}}\text{s}$ deadline, supervised by three parallel timers: the 60 ms chunk lease, the 100 $\text{Hz}$ host heartbeat with its 30 ms silence timeout, and the 5 ms hardware watchdog.

For the manipulator, the validity region governs both the doorway and the packing station. The baseline door policy matches the nominal target ODD of the evaluation record (the nominal plate, standard door resistance, a stationary base, and the guarded approach, whereas the recovery update analyzed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/02 Teaching the Machine/07 Closed-Loop Evaluation/V4-07.09 The Evaluation Record#^sec-evaluation-recovery-update|Qualifying a recovery update]]** qualifies the displaced-plate cell under its own distinct record rather than expanding the baseline region). The packing station admits picking a mug from the conveyor under a live intent lease followed by coworker handover. Across these zones, manipulator monitoring channels enforce dynamic velocity ceilings at the TCP. Inside the door zone, the approach-speed channel from the evaluation record’s `runtime_monitor_specs` caps tool velocity at 0.03 m/s, whereas in unconstrained space the conveyor mug pick operates under the nominal 1 m/s free-space TCP limit. While a coworker holds `accept_item` (**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/14 Supervisory Intervention/V4-14.03 Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance#^sec-intervention-human-authority|Human Authority, Reaction Time, and Clearance]]**), a handover channel engages before the separating distance closes to the arm’s biased stop plus its blind-travel displacement and caps the approach toward the coworker’s hand at 0.10 m/s, well below the 0.24 m/s contact-force ceiling for a free hand capable of recoil. Because a hand trapped against a rack upright or structural link permits an impact speed of at most 0.053 m/s, this handover cap is valid only when physical workstation geometry provably precludes clamping ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|section 12.6.2]]), a condition that the release argument must substantiate.

For the mobile base, operating assumptions distinguish between nominal and low-friction surfaces, each carrying its own cryptographic digest in the release manifest. The standard configuration requires a qualified directional braking lower bound encompassing driven-wheel load share, traction, torque, and stability, where a friction coefficient of at least 0.204 serves only as a necessary optimistic all-weight screen rather than sufficient evidence that the design deceleration holds. By contrast, a restricted configuration accommodates degraded traction down to an inspected-floor friction of 0.12. The restricted illustration stipulates that its 1.177 m/s² braking rate is available (since inspection alone does not establish that lower bound), and its deceleration suffix uses that stipulation to stop in 1015.3 ms across 507.6 mm from 1 m/s beneath the 1.17 m/s complete inspected-floor screen, whose braking premise still requires qualification.

Each base entry incorporates the 63.3 ms dispatch-to-admission allocation and its own scenario-specific remaining clearance. The 48.7 mm stopping reserve belongs to the dry static-obstacle aisle candidate; the restricted configuration must recompute its reserve from its own speed and braking rate. Because this allocation represents an analytical budget, neither configuration is certified for admission until physical deceleration measurements under maximum payload confirm the modeled margin.

Each numerical entry in the record carries the evidence category of its limit record: *measured*, *derived*, *datasheet*, or *illustrative*. These categories reflect fundamentally different degrees of operational confidence. The base’s 931.25 ms stopping time, for instance, is a *derived* value. It follows mathematically from the geometry of the S-curve suffix under a stipulated jerk bound and an illustrative deceleration figure, assuming that motor drives faithfully deliver commanded current and tires maintain continuous traction. Only loaded stopping trials on the actual operating surface establish a *measured* value. Physical tests reveal empirical non-idealities that escape closed-form derivations, including electromechanical brake onset scatter, structural compliance, and surface contamination. Even then, empirical data supports a formal bound only when combined with worst-case fault models and defined operating limits. Explicitly recording the evidence category prevents an integrator from mistaking a derived stopping estimate for a verified hardware limit.

The record also carries the routing of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-fallback-ladder|section 12.8]] as part of its contract. An arriving proposal can neither renew an active command nor revoke its authority merely by being transmitted, and each lease or heartbeat expiry, cycle loss, or bus undervoltage selects the rung that matches the machine’s state and the hardware that survives. Every field of the record is either an integrity token or an operational threshold evaluated against live execution state. These checks examine the proposal’s sequence counter and checksum, its stored dispatch age against 51.8 ms, reconstructed perception latency against validity and stopping-margin criteria, active elapsed time against the chunk lease, tracking error against the allowable envelope ${\epsilon_{\text{track}}}$, joint-velocity and torque-slew ceilings, barrier margins, phase-current and bus-voltage limits, and hardware watchdog timeouts. The contract binds each threshold to the independent sensing hardware that measures it and to the specific fallback rung selected upon violation. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.12 The Enforcement Record#^tbl-12-fallback-hierarchy|Table 6]] organizes these rungs by residual system authority and specifies the hardware timer that triggers each transition.

Because runtime safety guarantees require that plant dynamics remain inside the modeled operational envelope, the enforcement record bounds execution within an explicit validity region. The manipulator’s doorway speed ceiling illustrates how empirical evidence defines these bounds. The policy’s proposed 0.10 m/s approach, refused in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.07 Refusing Proposals and Enforcing State Invariants#^sec-enforcement-authority-refuse|section 12.5]] on evidential and physical grounds, passes only the narrowest timing test, since its 2.5 ms unbraked compression time from first contact to the latch force limit exceeds the 2 ms contact-loop cycle. That naive unbraked deadline ignores both the force already accumulated before the tripwire fires and the compression distance the arm still needs to decelerate to rest, so the record keeps both grounds for refusal beside that pass, the post-trip stop that [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|section 12.6.2]] sized and the absence of trials at that speed, ensuring that no reader mistakes the timing pass for admission. The enforcer therefore clamps the command to the 0.03 m/s evidence-based ceiling, evaluates full contact dynamics, and records the active constraint in the audit log. In contrast, the coworker handover ceiling is derived from the structural impact model ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^eq-release-unbraked-force|equation 13]]), which caps approach velocity below the contact-force limit where modeled impact forces meet allowable biomechanical thresholds for an unconstrained hand. Faster proposals are projected onto this boundary and logged as intervention vectors.

When onboard estimators detect a state outside the validity envelope, such as an undeclared payload mass, the supervisory monitor flags the violation, revokes policy authority, and commands a state-matched stop. Environmental conditions that lack direct onboard sensing, however, cannot trigger dynamic runtime vetoes. Surface friction at the wheel-floor interface represents an unobservable state, because mobile bases carry no transducers to measure instantaneous traction coefficients beneath their wheels. Consequently, the standard configuration relies on verified floor friction as an operating precondition, while low-friction operations require manual floor inspection and commissioning of the restricted configuration.

↪ The floor-friction premise is enforced as an entry of the residual-claims register in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/04 Governing the Machine/18 Engineering Physical AI/V4-18.04 The Epistemic Frontier#^sec-frontier-residual-claims-register|A residual-claims register]]**.

An enforcement record connects proposed neural trajectories to validated physical invariants, operational thresholds, and deterministic fallback responses. Algorithmic correctness, however, cannot compensate for an execution platform that violates its timing or braking commitments under load. An enforcer whose control barrier functions are mathematically verified will still fail if the couplings it still shares with the host, the board-link DMA into its memory, a supply rail, or a clock, delay its cycle under load during high-speed transit. In physical artificial intelligence, safety failures manifest in the time domain just as decisively as in algorithmic logic.
