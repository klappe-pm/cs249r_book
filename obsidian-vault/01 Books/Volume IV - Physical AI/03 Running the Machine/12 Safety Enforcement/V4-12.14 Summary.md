# V4-12.14 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^sec-enforcement-summary

Runtime enforcement grants an admitted plan continuing, conditional permission to drive physical hardware. On every control cycle the enforcer evaluates live plant state against the tracking bound, the stopping reservation, and the actuator authority that remains. It passes a safe proposal through, projects a correctable one back into the admissible set, and selects a resident fallback, without waiting for the learned proposer, when no correction exists or the proposer falls silent.

Clearance is a composite margin, not a distance read from a map. At the warehouse mobile manipulator’s 1.3 m/s aisle speed, travel during the pre-brake delay ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ takes 173.68 mm, the ${C}^2$ stop suffix 605.31 mm, localization and protective margin 150 mm, and the tracking inset 40 mm, for 968.99 mm of the 1.10 m available at the rack end. The remaining 131.01 mm pays for dispatch-to-admission travel (${v}{\Delta_{\text{da}}} =$ 82.3 mm) and leaves a 48.7 mm reserve, so the aisle speed remains a candidate until loaded timing measurements confirm that allocation. A budget built on instantaneous, constant-deceleration braking would have admitted the drive limit, where the jerk-limited stop no longer fits. The budget also holds only while the actuators deliver their assumed deceleration; gravity on a descending payload, bus sag, current ceilings, and thermal throttling all shrink braking authority, so the enforcer must contract the speed envelope before that authority is spent rather than after.

The kinetic barrier turns that reservation into a constraint on the next command. Raw clearance has relative degree two with respect to torque, so a barrier on distance alone leaves the motor no authority at the boundary. Folding approach speed and net braking deceleration into [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^eq-enforce-kinetic-barrier|equation 5]] lowers the relative degree to one, and its zero level set is the single-joint stopping budget written in state space. Under the qualified delivered-effort error and response-delay contract, the least-change quadratic program removes only the part of a proposal that violates the tightened half-space, which preserves useful task progress when the corrected motion remains task-admissible. An independent per-axis clamp can leave the coupled constraint broken. When contact is the task, force replaces clearance as the margin. Force keeps building through the response interval, so the latch and handover ceilings are approach speeds derived from the force at brake onset and from the effective mass a free or a clamped hand meets, each valid only under its stiffness, deceleration, and geometry premises.

When a lease lapses or a checked premise fails, the arbiter selects directly among `PROJECT`, `HOLD`, `STOP`, and `INHIBIT` by the plant’s state and the hardware that survives; the rungs are alternatives, not an escalation. Three timer duties detect different failures: the 60 ms chunk lease bounds a stale proposal, the 30 ms host-heartbeat timeout catches a host stall, and the 5 ms MCU self-watchdog catches a firmware deadlock. Beneath these responses sit the certified functions of the drives, the safety scanner, and the arm’s safety controller, which carry the formal protective claim. On the permission path itself, only the safety MCU’s watchdog that revokes authority when the chunk lease expires or the host heartbeat falls silent is a rated safety function. The admission checks are an unrated performance layer whose job is to reduce unnecessary trips of the assumed qualified protective floor ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-certified-functions|section 12.8.3]]). The enforcement record ties each check to its detector, its fallback, and the evidence category of every number it uses, so that what the permission path verified stays distinct from what it assumed. The checks and ceilings the permission path applies reduce to the relations in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.14 Summary#^tbl-12-key-equations|table 7]].

**Key Takeaways: Conditional enforcement and physical stopping**

- **Tracking is a bounded premise:** Steady-state tracking guarantees do not bound transient overshoot. Clearance insets must incorporate worst-case transient tracking errors validated experimentally across the robot’s full operating envelope.
- **The stopping budget dictates allowable speed:** Sensing latency, reaction delays, ${C}^2$ braking trajectories, tracking error insets, and localization margins all compound into a single admission check. True physical clearance, rather than motor drive limits, determines how fast the machine may safely move.
- **Control barriers must account for kinetic energy:** Because spatial distance has relative degree two with respect to joint torque, a position-only barrier cannot guarantee immediate forward invariance. Formulating a kinetic barrier that incorporates velocity yields an affine torque half-space for safe single-step execution.
- **In physical contact, force replaces distance as the margin:** Mechanical force continues to build throughout the latency of a braking response. Latching stops must be dimensioned from the projected peak force at brake onset, and approach velocities during human handover must be capped based on effective inertia and structural compliance.
- **Distinct timer duties expose different failure modes:** Trajectory chunk leases, host-to-controller heartbeats, microcontroller watchdog cycles, and sensor timestamps expose different failure modes. No single software timeout can protect against all failure modes.
- **Fallback execution is state- and hardware-dependent:** Safely executing a least-change projection, powered zero-velocity hold, controlled deceleration ramp, or torque inhibit requires specific surviving hardware and dynamic state. The fallback must be selected while the admissible control set is still nonempty; an empty chosen intersection loses that filter’s claim, while an alternate response needs its own qualified claim.
- **The software permission path complements certified hardware:** Safety-rated laser scanners, certified drives, and emergency stop interlocks provide the formal protective baseline. The running architecture assumes a rated lease-expiry and heartbeat watchdog on the permission path. The algorithmic admission checks are unrated and operate above this floor to keep operation smooth without tripping certified hard stops.
- **Every intervention requires an audit record:** The enforcer must bind every command token to verified sensor inputs and log all constraint modifications or fallback triggers to an audit log. This logging ensures system designers can audit safety margins on physical hardware and validate assumptions before production deployment.

^callout-takeaways--12-2-366cf807

This chapter is where the third law, proposal is not permission, stops being a design stance and becomes a mechanism. A learned proposal reaches the drives only as a candidate. It earns authority one tick at a time, and only while its evidence is fresh, its lease unexpired, its tracking error within bound, and the stop it would need still inside the clearance. Nothing the proposer emits can extend that authority, and the response that replaces it never waits for the proposer.

A complete runtime enforcement architecture joins the flexibility of learned policies to the determinism of physical safety guarantees. It reserves stopping clearance dynamically, projects candidate controls when a feasible correction exists, and selects a state-matched fallback when communication fails or an invariant breaks. Mathematical safety certificates and algorithmic barrier functions, however, are only as dependable as the underlying real-time compute fabric. Interference through whatever the permission path still shares with the proposers, a board link, a supply rail, a clock, or in some placements a die and a memory bus, can silently consume time that the physical budget already assigned to braking. The final engineering obligation is therefore empirical: verifying that the entire permission pipeline strictly meets its real-time deadlines under worst-case system load on the physical hardware.
