# V4-12.11 Runtime Enforcement Failure Modes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^sec-enforcement-failure-modes

Every guarantee in this chapter has rested on premises about the computer that runs the filter and the plant it models: a held-up logic rail, a bounded cycle, a credible deceleration, and a bus that delivers the admitted command on time. On the warehouse mobile manipulator each of those premises already carries a concrete spatial price. A missed tick costs 1.3 mm of travel at the aisle speed. A lease honored by the host instead of the MCU turns a 200 ms planning stall into 182 mm of unbraked travel and a 50.99 mm overrun. An unprotected integrator spends 195 mm against a 131.0 mm spare. Gravity on the arm and mug takes 75 percent of the joint’s peak torque and quadruples the descending stop. Runtime enforcement failures trace back to four classes, one for each premise:

1.  **Common-cause infrastructure failures**: When the learned policy and the safety filter share power rails, clock sources, reset lines, or thermal paths, one fault can disable both. A current surge during hard acceleration can sag a shared supply until it resets both the neural accelerator and the microcontroller that monitors it, and a shared reset or clock fault can silence the monitor at the instant the policy issues an errant command. The running machine therefore holds its permission logic on an isolated, held-up rail ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-four-rungs|section 12.8.1]]).
2.  **Timing overruns and execution jitter**: A quadratic program that stalls on an ill-conditioned active set, or a filter scheduled on a non-deterministic operating system, spends braking clearance while it computes. An overrunning routine cannot be trusted to detect its own stall, which is why the declared 250 ${\text{µ}}\text{s}$ cycle comes with a qualified preempting timer or independent mechanism that selects the resident fallback when a deadline expires ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-loop-budget|section 12.6.4]]).
3.  **Plant mismatch and unobservable state drift**: The filter evaluates safety against a model. A floor film that lowers friction, a payload that shifts the center of mass, or winding heat that lowers peak torque removes control authority the model still credits, so the solver can admit a deceleration the hardware cannot deliver. Where onboard sensors cannot observe the shift, the safe set must carry qualified uncertainty margins or the operating domain must restrict speed, as the complete optimistic inspected-floor screen falls to 1.17 m/s, before driven-wheel load share and available braking are qualified ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-stopping-envelopes|section 12.2]]).
4.  **Interconnect contention and transport delays**: State reads and command writes must cross physical buses. Unprioritized telemetry or logging can queue a joint reading until it is stale, or hold a filtered command in a transmit queue while the drive keeps executing the previous high-speed setpoint. The running machine carries its admitted command in a cyclic EtherCAT frame (stage 5 of the cycle budget) and charges the board link to the dispatch-to-admission budget.

None of the four yields to a better solver or an automotive-grade microcontroller. Each is answered by evidence. Common causes and contention need isolation and bounded interference, which **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13 Workload Placement#^sec-placement|Workload Placement]]** tests on silicon. Overruns need a verified execution bound with an independent fallback. Plant mismatch needs worst-case rather than nominal parameter margins. All four need a record of every enforcement decision, with its solver latency, active constraints, and sensor timestamps, so that an incident can be traced to the policy, the plant, or the infrastructure.
