# V4-12.09 Admission, Cyclic Checking, and Monitor Independence

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^sec-enforcement-where-enforcer-sits

A planned waypoint can clear a warehouse rack upright in the planner’s geometric check while the physical manipulator overshoots into the steel during execution, and an instantaneous motor torque well within electrical limits can accelerate the arm past the point where its remaining stopping reservation can arrest motion. Relying on trajectory admission alone misses in-flight tracking overshoot and saturation, whereas checking only single-step torque commands without a verified stopping continuation lets the arm enter an inevitable collision state before the local filter reacts. Robust safety enforcement therefore demands both layers (the per-tick cycle of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-loop-budget|section 12.6.4]] on a dedicated MCU and full-plan admission before motion begins). That raises three structural questions: why neither layer can replace the other, where the cyclic checker sits relative to the command stream, and how independent of the proposer the MCU must actually be.

Full-plan admission judges the candidate trajectory, its seams, its swept clearance, and its verified stopping continuation before the machine commits to motion. The expensive geometry behind that judgment (queries of global occupancy octrees, signed distance fields, or mesh collision models) cannot run inside a 1 ms control interrupt merely because the MCU owns final actuation privilege. It therefore runs on the host as a separately scheduled, bounded job, and its result reaches the MCU as the trajectory record. The MCU’s own admission check then verifies that record against bounded, qualified local evidence (the resident stop suffix, its entry state, and the commitment deadline) without traversing the global map. The hard real-time cyclic path evaluates current joint state, remaining lease validity, and available actuator effort on every tick before selecting and dispatching the next physical command.

An in-line checker places its worst-case execution time directly inside the control-loop deadline, consuming a portion of the available compute tick. A parallel monitor instead operates asynchronously alongside the proposal pipeline, avoiding added forward latency during nominal execution but contributing detection latency and trip delay to the physical response budget. Either architectural placement must ensure privilege isolation. The untrusted proposer must never be able to bypass the selected output, and the system must preserve the dedicated sensing and actuation channels required by the fallback controller. As **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.03 Two Paths and the Resources They Share#^sec-placement-two-paths-one-die|Two Paths and the Resources They Share]]** will show from the resources each candidate placement shares, isolating these execution domains across memory spaces or heterogeneous processing cores is what allows the safety monitor and its fallback mechanisms to survive the very proposer faults that trigger an intervention. The physical stopping budget supplies the non-negotiable temporal and spatial boundaries that this hardware split must satisfy.

**Systems Perspective 12.3: The monitor independence rule**

Any valid safety claim must explicitly define which proposer failure modes its sensing, compute, and actuation pathways survive. Placing a monitor on a separate physical core or processor does not establish true independence if that monitor shares a power rail, a memory bus, a sensor link, or an unisolated clock domain with the untrusted proposer. Shared hardware resources require provably bounded interference or a qualified, survivable fallback state. Any environmental condition that the monitor cannot observe directly must remain an explicit, documented operating premise rather than an unstated assumption.

The running example assumes qualified drive, scanner, and arm safety functions and a rated lease/heartbeat watchdog; the release case must name the scoped evidence supporting those functions. Independence, however, is not certification. The runtime permission path is not the machine’s certified functional safety layer, and beneath it sit the certified safety functions embedded within motor drives, safety laser scanners, and the dedicated safety controller. These hardware-interlocked mechanisms carry the formal integrity claims that certification bodies audit against industrial standards. The primary operational duty of the software permission path is to regulate the learned proposer proactively so that the robot never trips those certified hardware interlocks during normal operation. [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.10 The Fallback Ladder#^sec-enforcement-certified-functions|Section 12.8.3]] maps each dynamic response developed throughout this chapter onto that underlying certified safety floor once the complete response hierarchy is established, and names the one narrow MCU function that does carry a rating.

### Where the Thesis Bends

^sec-enforcement-thesis-bends

When a learned locomotion or manipulation policy emits raw joint targets or motor torques at the control rate instead of handing the permission path a multi-step trajectory, the enforcer loses the ability to verify a collision-free stopping continuation before motion begins. It must then either run an active fallback model on every tick or watch its projections induce distributional shift and boundary chattering. Admission and the cyclic check in this chapter both assume that the proposer hands the permission path a plan it can judge before motion: kinematic intent lives in the learned policy, and deterministic motor drives close the torque loops. Much of contemporary robot learning practice abandons this classical division, outputting joint targets at the control rate above stiff proportional-derivative loops ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-lee2020learning|Lee et al. 2020]]) or emitting motor torques end-to-end. The third law, proposal is not permission (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]]), does not disappear in such direct control designs. Instead, the range of physical guarantees that the runtime permission path can uphold narrows in precise, quantifiable ways.

Four guarantees survive because each acts directly on raw commands at the causal boundary, without requiring a forward plan. A per-tick limit on rate and amplitude bounds torque slew and joint velocity at the hardware register interface. The kinetic barrier of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^eq-enforce-kinetic-torque|equation 6]] operates directly in effort space, so a CBF-QP can filter the policy’s output without converting it to an intermediate kinematic setpoint. The passivity-based energy tank of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-intentional-contact|section 12.6.2]] monitors power exchange and bounds the physical work the policy can inject into the environment during contact. Beneath all of these, low-level geofencing, safely-limited speed monitoring, and torque removal backed by mechanical spring brakes remain permanently active under the software stack. Communication timing improves along one dimension. If a qualified freshness deadline is selected at one tick and an independent watchdog enforces it, policy silence is detected within that tick plus bus transport delay, which reduces the lease-expiration travel row of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^tbl-enforcement-stopping-budget|table 1]] from 80.6 mm to 2.6 mm at the aisle speed.

What is lost is admission. Without an explicit multi-step trajectory, the system cannot verify the collision-free stopping suffix before the robot commits to motion. The enforcer must instead evaluate the machine’s dynamic stopping reservation on every tick against an active fallback controller that maintains its own internal braking model. On a wheeled mobile base, that fallback is straightforward to formulate. The controller executes a qualified service-braking response along the current heading, followed by wheel restraint after confirmed standstill. On an underactuated balancing machine, such as a bipedal humanoid, the fallback is itself an active balance controller; cutting motor torque produces an immediate uncontrolled fall rather than a safe state.

Runtime projection also alters the closed sensorimotor loop under which the policy was trained. Every time the enforcer projects a proposed action onto the admissible set, it delivers a state to the environment that the policy never intended, precipitating distributional shift in subsequent observations. This penalty surfaces directly in task performance, as quantified by the intervention metrics of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.08 The Minimal Intervention Principle and Optimization Filters#^sec-enforcement-minimal-intervention|section 12.6]]. Frequent overrides cause the machine to chatter erratically along safety boundaries or freeze conservatively short of its goal. To reconcile this tension, modern architectures adopt filter-aware policy training, incorporating the safety filter directly into the training objective so that the neural network learns to avoid the boundary rather than encountering the enforcer for the first time on physical hardware ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-hsu2024safetyfilter|Hsu et al. 2024]]). The core thesis still holds at the causal boundary, where untrusted proposals never bypass runtime enforcement. What bends is how much of the safety argument can be settled mathematically before motion begins vs. how much performance the machine must sacrifice to settle the remainder while moving.
