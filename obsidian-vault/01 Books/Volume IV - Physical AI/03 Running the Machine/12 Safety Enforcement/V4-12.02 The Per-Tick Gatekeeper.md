# V4-12.02 The Per-Tick Gatekeeper

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12 Safety Enforcement|V4-12 Safety Enforcement]]

^sec-enforcement-deterministic-gatekeeper

Every physical AI system ends in the same act. A command leaves software, becomes current in a motor winding, and puts momentum into a world that does not roll back. Safety enforcement is the runtime mechanism that decides, one control tick at a time, whether the next command may cross that causal boundary. Checking a plan once before it runs does not settle the question, because admission judges the plan against the state the planner predicted, and the machine then executes it against the state it actually reaches. Between those two moments the body can lag its reference, an actuator can run short of torque, a person can step into the aisle, and the learned proposer can stall or emit a setpoint the plan never contained. Enforcement exists so that none of these events is discovered only after the energy has been delivered.

The central constraint is that the learned proposer can neither be trusted with that decision nor be discarded. Its competence is what makes the machine useful, yet nothing in its output bounds stopping distance, actuator effort, or timing. Enforcement resolves the tension by separating proposal from permission, the third law (principle [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/V4 Running the Machine#^pri-vol4-proposal-permission|3]]), and by giving the permission side three jobs. It must hold a stopping reservation, the clearance the machine needs to arrest its motion from the current state; it must correct a proposal minimally when a feasible correction exists; and it must select a response it can execute without the proposer when none does. The chapter builds the three jobs in that order.

On the warehouse mobile manipulator, the safety microcontroller (MCU) admits the run down the aisle only after the matched stop suffix named in its trajectory record is resident in the MCU’s memory and feasible from the commitment entry state defined in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.06 Behavior at the Seam#^sec-planning-two-tier-deadlines|Replacement deadlines and commitment cutoffs]]**. Commitment is the instant after which the machine can no longer abandon the plan’s motion without that latched suffix, so the suffix is matched to the state the machine will have at that instant, not to the states it passes through on the way. Before commitment, a different response must cover the motion. Admission requires an independently executable, state-matched fallback from every state reachable up to that instant. It also requires a bounded representation of the S-curve suffix that the MCU can decode, validate, and execute within its declared workload (the polynomial layout of **[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Systems and Hardware#^sec-appendix-systems-wire-trajectory|Trajectory payload]]** is an illustrative encoding rather than that S-curve representation). Because that admission step checks the plan only once before motion starts, the machine still executes the plan one millisecond tick at a time, and enforcement is the check made on every one of those ticks together with the response it selects when even the resident stop no longer fits.

↩︎ The trajectory record and the matched stop suffix it names are established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/11 Trajectory Planning/V4-11.09 The Trajectory Record#^sec-planning-trajectory-contract|The Trajectory Record]]**.

The enforcer, the permission path’s per-tick gatekeeper, sits on the permission side of the proposal boundary. It is a hard real-time component that evaluates candidate proposals, verifies invariant state bounds, and holds the exclusive hardware authority to permit current flow or command a fallback stop, keeping the task competence of learned policies while denying them authority over geometric clearance, thermal limits, and human-safety invariants.

↩︎ The per-tick permission cadence on which the enforcer runs is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.04 Multi-Rate Cadences#^sec-nervous-time-constants-phase-lag|Physical time constants and sampling phase lag]]**.

In the architecture this chapter follows, the enforcer executes on a dedicated microcontroller wired to the sensing and drive interfaces it needs. It checks each candidate command before the drive can apply it. A packet firewall offers a limited software analogy: a sender may request an operation without having authority to perform it. Unlike a packet filter, the enforcer must also account for momentum already present in the plant and preserve an achievable response when it refuses a request.

↪ The silicon placement that keeps the permission path independent of the proposer is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/13 Workload Placement/V4-13.07 Silicon Isolation Decisions#^sec-placement-isolation-decisions|Silicon Isolation Decisions]]**.

The architecture separates a capable proposer, a resident protective controller, and an output selector. This is the **Simplex architecture**[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.17 Footnotes#^fn1|¹]] ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-seto1998simplex|Seto et al. 1998]]; [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.16 Source References#^ref-sha2001using|Sha 2001]]), the runtime-assurance pattern that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/00 Introduction/01 The Causal Boundary/V4-01.08 The Machine in Five Levels#^sec-boundary-machine-model|The Machine in Five Levels]]** first named. The proposer supplies useful candidates, while the permission path retains the exclusive ability to select a physically feasible response without waiting for it.

The rest of the chapter follows the running machine through that per-tick check. The starting point is the deficit planning left open, when the jerk-limited stop carried the base’s stopping budget past the rack-end clearance at the drive limit while the body’s departure from its reference remained unpriced: the first quantity the enforcer needs is how far the body can stray from the reference it is asked to follow, because every clearance the permission path defends must also contain that error. Once the tracking bound supplies that missing spatial term, the finished stopping budget fixes the reservation and a candidate aisle speed that still requires physical qualification. Actuator saturation then reveals how quickly that reservation shrinks once gravity or thermal limits spend braking authority. A control barrier function turns the reservation into a constraint on the next command. A least-change filter can then correct a violating proposal, both in free space and in contact, inside a declared cycle budget on a processor the proposer cannot preempt. When a check fails or a lease expires, the fallback hierarchy supplies the surviving hardware response, and the chapter closes with the infrastructure failures that break these premises and the enforcement record that binds every check to its evidence.
