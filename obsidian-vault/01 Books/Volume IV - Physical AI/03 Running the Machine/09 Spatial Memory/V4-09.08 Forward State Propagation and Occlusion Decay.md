# V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09 Spatial Memory|V4-09 Spatial Memory]]

^sec-memory-belief-through-occlusion

When a sensor loses sight of a moving body, bringing the machine to a safe stop takes time and demands a usable state estimate throughout the deceleration interval. Forward propagation bridges this blind duration by projecting the most recent observation forward in time, whether the machine continues an authorized task or executes a protective fallback. A learned generative model can predict future trajectories autoregressively, and ensemble dispersion or Monte Carlo rollouts can quantify prediction variance. However, statistical spread alone does not constitute a certified upper bound on physical tracking error. In physical AI systems, forward state propagation must be anchored to explicit error-growth dynamics, whether implemented via the open-loop Kalman prediction step of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^eq-percep-kf-predict|equation 1]], learned stochastic state observers, or expanding covariance ellipsoids.

Forward propagation updates the state estimate and expands its uncertainty envelope while preserving the original hardware capture timestamp. Across the unobserved interval, open-loop state estimation remains valid only so long as the growing error envelope remains within the task’s geometric clearance. The rack end in the warehouse aisle shows where that boundary falls.

### Bounded Error Growth and Free-Space Erosion

^sec-memory-free-space-erosion

At an aisle intersection, the gap between propagated prediction and live observation decides what the robot may safely execute. Where a storage rack ends, a worker may step abruptly into the clear distance ${D_{\text{clear}}} =$ 1.10 m defended by the machine’s stopping budget. Once the rack structure occludes the cross aisle, the central question becomes how long remembered free space can certify that the vehicle’s path remains clear.

Every mathematical dynamics model simplifies physical reality, causing the discrepancy between the projected state and the physical world to compound continuously during sensor dropouts. This uncorrected drift is the open-loop prediction step of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^eq-percep-kf-predict|equation 1]] running without measurement updates, the same widening seen over one sample step in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^fig-08-kalman-predict-update|figure 1]], now compounded across the entire occlusion. In state estimation, this dispersion is called **occlusion covariance expansion**, the continuous widening of uncertainty while the sensors have no line of sight. Formalized by continuous Lyapunov or Riccati covariance propagation equations (**[[05 Reference/Volume IV - Physical AI/V4 Control and Dynamics#^sec-appendix-control-covariance|Continuous Covariance Propagation and Observability]]**), this propagation describes modeled dispersion before sensors regain visibility; hard divergence bounds require separately qualified disturbance limits.

In a deterministic bounded-disturbance framework, empirical sensor characterization and mechanical disturbance limits establish an initial position error bound ${e_0}$ at the instant of occlusion (${\Delta} {t} = 0$). Two bounded physical terms subsequently degrade the state estimate. First, unmodeled kinematics introduce a drift velocity bounded by ${v_{\text{drift}}}$. Second, unmodeled forces and structural dynamics introduce a disturbance acceleration bounded by ${a_{\text{dist}}}$. Over an occlusion interval ${\Delta} {t}$, the worst-case position error bound ${E}({\Delta} {t})$ expands quadratically:

$$
{E}({\Delta} {t}) = {e_0} + {v_{\text{drift}}}{\Delta} {t} + \frac{1}{2}{a_{\text{dist}}}({\Delta} {t})^2 \tag{12}
$$

^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion

At the rack end, the stored belief is not the tracked position of an external object, but rather a spatial occupancy assertion: no person stands within the protective clearance margin ${\delta_{\text{margin}}}$ (100 mm) of the robot. The spatial map vouches for this claim out to the free-space depth ${d_{\text{free}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.07 Certified Spatial Assertions in Scene Memory#^sec-memory-volumetric-metric-maps|section 9.6.1]]), defined as the distance from the vehicle hull to the nearest cell lacking active sensory evidence. Any obstacle hidden from the sensors lies across that occlusion boundary.

Two physical uncertainties consume this free-space buffer. First, onboard localization uncertainty introduces a bounded error ${\delta_{\text{loc}}}$ (50 mm), meaning the true physical clearance may already be smaller than the nominal map indicates. Second, an unobserved person can advance outward from behind the rack corner at an approach velocity bounded by ${v_h} =$ 1.6 m/s, the standardized human walking speed mandated in safety standards ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-iso13855|International Organization for Standardization 2024]]).[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.16 Footnotes#^fn10|¹⁰]] Matching these physical parameters to the quadratic expansion in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion|equation 12]] yields an error growth ${E}({\Delta} {t}) = {\delta_{\text{loc}}} + {v_h}\,{\Delta} {t}$ with zero acceleration disturbance (${a_{\text{dist}}} = 0$). The allowable task tolerance is the uncommitted clearance ${d_{\text{free}}} - {\delta_{\text{margin}}}$. As a result, the certified free region erodes inward from the unobserved frontier at walking speed, causing the spatial claim to lapse at:

$$
{T_{\text{inv}}} = \frac{{d_{\text{free}}} - {\delta_{\text{loc}}} - {\delta_{\text{margin}}}}{{v_h}} \tag{13}
$$

^eq-mem-free-space-expiry

This derivation starts from the observed free-space depth at capture. A frozen snapshot constructed with erosion already reserved through its support deadline must not apply that same erosion again; its fixed header still limits use to the validated interval. The safety pipeline evaluates this **free-space erosion** law across every swept volume that the admitted motion and its stopping trajectory will occupy. Any nonpositive result (${T_{\text{inv}}} \le 0$) indicates that memory certifies no safe clearance along that path. This relationship formalizes the worst-case boundary of occlusion-aware reachable sets used in automated vehicle navigation, where the reachable volume of an occluded traffic participant expands outward from the visibility horizon ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-orzechowski2018tackling|Orzechowski et al. 2018]]).

Two positions of the hull bracket the problem. Abreast of the rack end, the unobserved cross aisle begins immediately at the upright beam, so ${d_{\text{free}}}$ equals the lateral clearance between the hull and the rack. Ahead of the vehicle along its current heading, the nearest unobserved boundary lies at the rack corner, ${D_{\text{clear}}}$ away. In the top view of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^fig-09-rack-end-erosion|figure 10]], a person hidden in the cross aisle can be anywhere inside a disc that grows at ${v_h}$ from the rack end. In its time plot, the depth certified ahead of the hull falls to zero at 593.75 ms, while the depth abreast sits at zero from the instant of capture. Notebook [9.2](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/09_memory/09_memory.html#nbk-memory-erosion-deadline) evaluates both cases numerically.

**Napkin Math 9.2: The erosion deadline**

**Problem**: The navigation camera has just seen the aisle empty, and the rack uprights hide the cross aisle. For how long does that frame certify the space abreast of the rack end, and the space ahead of the hull, and how much of the second deadline is left when memory receives the frame?

**Variables**:

- Localization bound ${\delta_{\text{loc}}} =$ 50 mm (illustrative) and protective clearance ${\delta_{\text{margin}}} =$ 100 mm (chosen).
- Approach speed ${v_h} =$ 1.6 m/s (datasheet: the ISO 13855 walking speed).
- Free-space depth abreast, the side clearance, ${d_{\text{free}}} =$ 150 mm; ahead, ${d_{\text{free}}} = {D_{\text{clear}}} =$ 1.10 m (both illustrative).
- Frame age at dispatch ${t_{\text{age}}} =$ 51.6 ms (derived, **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-latency-waterfall|The latency waterfall]]**); navigation-camera frame period 16.7 ms (datasheet).

**Math**: Abreast of the rack end, [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-free-space-expiry|equation 13]] gives

$$
T_{\text{inv}} = \frac{(0.15 - 0.05 - 0.10)\ \text{m}}{1.6\ \text{m/s}} = 0\ \text{ms}
$$

 Ahead of the hull it gives

$$
T_{\text{inv}} = \frac{(1.10 - 0.05 - 0.10)\ \text{m}}{1.6\ \text{m/s}} = 593.75\ \text{ms}
$$

 and the frame’s age at dispatch leaves

$$
593.75 - 51.6 = 542.15\ \text{ms} \approx 33 \times 16.7\ \text{ms}
$$

**Result**: Abreast of the rack end the frame certifies nothing, even at the instant of capture. Ahead of the hull it certifies 593.75 ms, of which the frame’s age spends 82.6 mm of depth before memory receives it, leaving about 33 frame periods.

**Systems insight**: The deadline is set by the distance from the unobserved boundary, not by the frame rate or the speed of the lookup. Each millimeter of free depth beyond ${\delta_{\text{loc}}} + {\delta_{\text{margin}}}$ buys 0.625 ms, so no camera, however fast, gives the position beside the rack end any memory at all; only a sensor that sees the cross aisle can.

In the open aisle, spatial memory reliably bridges temporary camera frame drops. However, the notebook’s deadline is computed for a hull standing at its current pose. A hull that drives toward the rack end at speed ${v}$ closes on the occlusion boundary while the hidden person closes on it from the other side. The depth certified ahead therefore shrinks at ${v} + {v_h}$ rather than at ${v_h}$. Each successive swept volume along the planned path starts closer to the boundary, until the horizon reaches zero abreast of the rack uprights. The stopping budget makes the same point in distance. At the 1.5 m/s drive limit, the stopping-budget terms charged before memory enters already claim 912.9 mm of the 1.10 m clear distance before anyone moves in the cross aisle. The safety enforcement path must therefore evaluate [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-free-space-expiry|equation 13]] along the entire planned trajectory. The robot must bring itself to a controlled stop, or reacquire line-of-sight visibility into the cross aisle, before any point on the swept path breaches its invalidation deadline. The clear distance the sensors can still see caps the speed at which the hull may approach an occluded rack end, a ceiling that **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-phase-portraits|Phase portraits and speed ceilings]]** plots against clearance. Memory certifies clearance only to a depth governed by the physical distance to the occlusion boundary. At the rack corner, that geometric depth vanishes completely.

Nothing in the software data structures explicitly marks the moment a spatial record expires. The occupancy map may continue to report an empty cross aisle, and the state estimator may continue to publish syntactically valid ROS messages, yet the prediction has ceased to function as physical evidence. Cells within the erosion front revert to unobserved space, and the region beside the rack end becomes uncertified the instant the uprights obscure line of sight. The robot’s stopping budget defends against a static obstacle at ${D_{\text{clear}}}$, such as a stationary worker or a dropped tote. Spatial memory adds a temporal deadline to that stopping budget without granting any additional physical braking clearance; the 912.9 mm partial stopping distance established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-latency-waterfall|The latency waterfall]]** is the same with memory as without it.

A pedestrian actively walking toward the robot during a stopping maneuver alters the safety budget completely. In standardized safety calculations, an approaching human introduces an intrusion distance ${v_h}({\tau_{\text{delay}}} + {T_{\text{stop}}})$, where walking speed acts across both the system reaction latency ${\tau_{\text{delay}}}$ and the mechanical braking time ${T_{\text{stop}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-iso13855|International Organization for Standardization 2024]]). At the 1.5 m/s drive limit, where a constant-deceleration stop requires ${T_{\text{stop}}} = {v}/{a_{\text{brake}}}$, this pedestrian displacement term alone consumes 1.41 m. This distance already includes the declared reaction latency and exceeds the entire 1.10 m clearance before adding the separately charged controller dispatch interval or the vehicle’s own stopping travel. The walking-person speed ceiling this term imposes on a jerk-limited stop is derived in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.04 Stopping Envelopes#^sec-enforcement-composite-budget|Sizing the composite stopping budget]]**.

Consequently, static clearance models cannot justify an unrestricted shared pedestrian crossing. Mitigating this risk requires engineered site controls, such as designated pedestrian crosswalks coupled with fixed infrastructure presence sensing at rack corners. Until detection coverage, sensor latency, and traffic protocols are qualified, site operations must retain this premise as an active operational constraint. Under no circumstances can stale spatial memory substitute for missing sensory evidence. Regardless of which traffic rules govern the facility, the robot’s permission pipeline must reject any trajectory whose clearance margin relies on unobserved space past the belief’s invalidation deadline.

### The Invalidation Horizon

^sec-memory-invalidation-horizon

The two rack-end deadlines, for the aisle ahead and for the space abreast of the rack end, are instances of one physical quantity: the elapsed time at which a belief’s growing error bound reaches the tolerance of the task it supports. Beyond that point, an extrapolated belief can no longer prevent a collision or a mechanical jam.

**Definition 9.2: Belief state invalidation horizon**

***Belief state invalidation horizon*** (${T_{\text{inv}}}$) is the elapsed time since the last physical observation at which the belief’s validated error bound ${E}({\Delta} {t})$ first reaches the tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}}$:

$$
{T_{\text{inv}}} = \inf \{ {\Delta} {t} \ge 0 : {E}({\Delta} {t}) \ge {r_{\text{tol}}} \} \tag{14}
$$

 where ${E}({\Delta} {t})$ is a bounded-disturbance growth law such as [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion|equation 12]] or a calibrated error quantile, and a belief whose evidence epoch is ${t_0}$ stops supporting motion at ${t_0} + {T_{\text{inv}}}$.

^eq-mem-invalidation-horizon

1.  **Significance**: Marks the instant at which forward-propagated state estimation ceases to function as valid physical evidence, requiring the permission path to refuse dependent motion proposals before geometric clearance is breached. The same walking-speed bound gives the free space ahead of the hull 593.75 ms and the space abreast of the rack end 0 ms, so the horizon belongs to a belief and its tolerance, never to the machine as a whole.
2.  **Distinction**: Unlike open-loop timeout watchdogs that monitor software process heartbeat intervals, the invalidation horizon is derived from continuous physical kinematics, bounded disturbance dynamics, and mechanical clearance constraints.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Allowing a neural planner or Kalman filter to continuously extrapolate state estimates during extended sensor dropouts without bounding error expansion, mistaking smooth mathematical extrapolation for physical ground truth.

When the error growth law is linear, ${E}({\Delta} {t}) = {e_0} + {r}\,{\Delta} {t}$, the invalidation horizon simplifies to a direct closed form:

$$
{T_{\text{inv}}} = \frac{{r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0}}{{r}} \tag{15}
$$

 where a nonpositive result (${T_{\text{inv}}} \le 0$) indicates that the belief cannot support the task even at the instant of capture. The free-space erosion law of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-free-space-expiry|equation 13]] instantiates this linear form with initial localization uncertainty ${e_0} = {\delta_{\text{loc}}}$, approach speed ${r} = {v_h}$, and task clearance ${r_{\text{tol}}} = {d_{\text{free}}} - {\delta_{\text{margin}}}$. The moving mug-handle belief in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.11 The Belief Record Schema#^sec-memory-spatial-state-schema|section 9.10]] applies this exact formulation using conveyor belt velocity. Computed for the evidence behind one action chunk, the root of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-invalidation-horizon|equation 14]] is the evidence horizon of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]**.

^eq-mem-linear-horizon

### Accelerating Error at the Latch Slot

^sec-memory-latch-slot-growth

In the rack-end scenario, a linear velocity bound set the erosion rate; at the cage door, the narrow opening of the latch-release slot sets the task tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}}$ while unmodeled accelerations compound quadratically over time. When the gripper finger enters the slot, the end-effector blocks the wrist camera’s view, forcing the state estimator to integrate commanded joint torques through a nominal plant model without visual feedback. Because unmodeled forces and cable drag accelerate position error far faster than constant-velocity drift, an open-loop bridge that held across a dropped frame in the aisle can jam the finger against the slot wall in tens of milliseconds.

Throughout that blind insertion, the finger must remain aligned within 1 mm of the slot centerline, defining the mechanical task clearance ${r_{\text{tol}}}$. The other bounds carry over from the missed-frame scenario in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.06 Coordinate Transform Expiry and Localization Drift#^sec-memory-reference-frames|section 9.5]]: an initial position error ${e_0} \le$ 0.1 mm and an unobserved velocity drift ${v_{\text{drift}}} \le$ 12 mm/s. During occlusion, dynamic disturbances add an unmodeled acceleration bounded by ${a_{\text{dist}}} \le$ 50 mm/s². Under these bounds the error grows quadratically from ${e_0}$ and meets ${r_{\text{tol}}}$ at the invalidation horizon ${T_{\text{inv}}} =$ 66 ms ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^fig-uncertainty-growth|figure 11]]). A naive constant-velocity estimate rises more slowly and keeps admitting the belief out to 75 ms, after the bounded error has already breached the clearance.

Because the belief representation transforms the slot relative to the wrist pose at its exact capture epoch, which resolves the coordinate staleness analyzed in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.06 Coordinate Transform Expiry and Localization Drift#^sec-memory-reference-frames|section 9.5]], the growth law in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion|equation 12]] is freed from lever-arm rotational compounding. Position error expands solely through drift velocity and disturbance acceleration. Over a single 16.7 ms frame interval, this formulation limits position error to 0.31 mm, compared to 1.05 mm under an uncoordinated coordinate transform chain. Eliminating lever-arm extrapolation error allows the manipulation system to bridge brief sensor dropouts reliably where an unaligned pipeline would fail immediately.

Evaluating this bounded growth model with the scenario parameters yields ${E}({\Delta} {t}) = 0.1 + 12\,{\Delta} {t} + 25\,({\Delta} {t})^2$, where elapsed time ${\Delta} {t}$ is in seconds and error ${E}({\Delta} {t})$ is in millimeters. Setting ${E}({\Delta} {t}) = {r_{\text{tol}}}$ produces the quadratic characteristic equation $25\,({\Delta} {t})^2 + 12\,{\Delta} {t} - 0.9 = 0$. The positive real root establishes an invalidation horizon of ${T_{\text{inv}}} =$ 66 ms. Motion plans relying on this visual estimate must complete or transition to a qualified fallback before this deadline, with deceleration commands dispatched in advance.

If the mechanical slot is machined to a tighter clearance of 0.30 mm, the invalidation horizon contracts sharply to 16.1 ms ($25\,({\Delta} {t})^2 + 12\,{\Delta} {t} - 0.2 = 0$). Tightening the mechanical clearance by a factor of 3.3× reduces the available prediction window by a factor of 4.1×, because the initial error consumes a larger share of the tighter tolerance. Subtracting the unchanged initial error leaves 0.9 mm in the original slot and 0.2 mm in the tighter one, so the usable-slack ratio exceeds the total-tolerance ratio. Acceleration separately shortens both horizons. In contrast, an estimator that naively assumes constant velocity (${a_{\text{dist}}} = 0$) predicts an overly optimistic horizon of $({r_{\text{tol}}} - {e_0})/{v_{\text{drift}}} =$ 75 ms. By that time, the bounded acceleration model permits error up to 1.141 mm, exceeding the allowable clearance by 14.1 percent. A controller operating on constant-velocity extrapolation would continue unguided insertion for 9 ms beyond safe physical bounds, risking mechanical jam or tool damage.

### Covariance Growth and Probabilistic Bounds

^sec-memory-covariance-growth

During a sensor dropout, double-integrating unmodeled acceleration noise causes position variance to grow with the cube of elapsed time, and feeding a raw variance directly into a geometric clearance check confuses statistical spread with a physical boundary. Both worked examples above bounded position error with deterministic limits. Inside a stochastic state estimator, variance propagation separates three distinct physical mechanisms that drive error growth during an occlusion. An unmeasured velocity error at the onset of occlusion causes position variance to expand quadratically as ${\sigma_{v,0}}^2({\Delta} {t})^2$. Unmodeled stochastic forces such as torque ripple or structural vibration, integrated twice across the dynamics, expand position variance cubically as $\frac{1}{3}{q_c}({\Delta} {t})^3$; these variance terms define the spread that calibrated confidence quantiles must absorb. In contrast, a persistent unmodeled force bounded by $|{a_{\text{dist}}}|$ (cable drag or sliding friction, for instance) deflects the body deterministically by at most $\frac{1}{2}{a_{\text{dist}}}({\Delta} {t})^2$, corresponding to the quadratic term in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-occlusion-expansion|equation 12]].[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.16 Footnotes#^fn11|¹¹]]

These stochastic variance terms populate the estimator’s internal **covariance matrix**, which quantifies the dispersion of a probabilistic error model rather than a hard physical clearance bound. Position variances carry squared length units ($\text{m}^2$), so their growth cannot be substituted directly into geometric clearance checks. To examine how stochastic uncertainty expands during complete sensor dropouts, consider a kinematic body whose continuous translational acceleration is driven by unmodeled disturbance or actuator jitter modeled as zero-mean continuous Gaussian white noise. The acceleration obeys ${\ddot{\mathbf{p}}}({t}) = {\mathbf{w}}({t})$, with ${\mathbf{w}}({t}) \sim {\mathcal{WN}}(0, {\mathbf{Q}_c})$, where ${\mathbf{Q}_c} = {q_c} {\mathbf{I}}$ represents the continuous power spectral density of acceleration noise with physical units $\text{m}^2/\text{s}^3$.

Integrating acceleration once yields velocity ${\mathbf{v}}({t}) = {\mathbf{v}}(0) + \int_0^{t} {\mathbf{w}}({t'})\,d{t'}$. Integrating a second time over elapsed duration ${t}$ gives the physical position:

$$
{\mathbf{p}}({t}) = {\mathbf{p}}(0) + {\mathbf{v}}(0){t} + \int_0^{t} ({t}-{t'}){\mathbf{w}}({t'})\,d{t'}
$$

To isolate the contribution of process noise, assume initial position and velocity are known precisely. During an occlusion interval where external measurement updates cease, the estimator projects state forward using its nominal transition model ${\hat{\mathbf{p}}}({t}) = {\hat{\mathbf{p}}}(0) + {\hat{\mathbf{v}}}(0){t}$. The unobserved position error covariance ${\mathbf{P}_{pp}}({t}) = \mathbb{E}[({\mathbf{p}}({t}) - {\hat{\mathbf{p}}}({t}))({\mathbf{p}}({t}) - {\hat{\mathbf{p}}}({t}))^\top]$ accumulates continuous noise variance through double integration:

$$
{\mathbf{P}_{pp}}({t}) = \int_0^{t} ({t}-{t'})^2 {q_c} {\mathbf{I}}\,d{t'} = {q_c} {\mathbf{I}} \left[ -\frac{({t}-{t'})^3}{3} \right]_0^{t} = \frac{1}{3} {q_c} {t}^3 {\mathbf{I}}
$$

Double integration causes process-noise covariance to expand cubically with elapsed time ($\propto {t}^3$), the same $\sqrt{{t}^3/3}$ growth that set the BMI088 accelerometer’s noise contribution in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-perception-state-estimation|section 9.2]]. If initial state uncertainty is nonzero, the complete error covariance additionally incorporates propagated initial position variance, initial velocity variance, and their cross-covariance terms. Raw covariance represents stochastic dispersion rather than a guaranteed physical clearance boundary. To use this uncertainty in safety enforcement, the system must project covariance into a spatial safety margin through a calibrated coverage factor ${k}$ (for instance, corresponding to a Chebyshev or Gaussian confidence bound), augmented by bounded systemic biases omitted by the noise model. For spherical or polyhedral bounding volumes, the nominal geometric hull can be inflated dynamically:

$$
{r_{\text{inflated}}}({t}) = {r_{\text{hull}}} + {k} \sqrt{{\lambda_{\max}}({\mathbf{P}_{pp}}({t}))}
$$

 where ${\lambda_{\max}}({\mathbf{P}_{pp}}({t}))$ denotes the principal eigenvalue of the position covariance matrix. The belief record must explicitly furnish these growth bounds and underlying assumptions before the permission path can rely on them to keep execution collision free.

↪ The barrier-function permission test that consumes such clearance margins is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/12 Safety Enforcement/V4-12.06 Safe Sets as Conditional Permission#^sec-enforcement-safe-sets-conditional-permission|Safe Sets as Conditional Permission]]**.

A safety argument can rest on one of two distinct paradigms. In a **bounded-disturbance** framework, rigorous physical qualification establishes explicit limits on initial state errors, maximum drift velocities, disturbance accelerations, clock synchronization offsets, and contact mode transitions. Under these conditions, the 593.75 ms aisle-ahead horizon and the 66 ms latch-slot horizon represent deterministic, conditional worst-case deadlines within the qualified operating domain. Alternatively, in an empirical or data-driven regime, controlled sensor dropout trials benchmarked against high-precision ground truth can calibrate residual error quantiles (such as p99 or p99.9) across a representative operational distribution. This yields a probabilistic admission bound with a 1 percent or 0.1 percent tail risk, prior to unmodeled structural shifts. In both architectures, the spatial memory record and the consuming controller must explicitly declare the active validation regime, track operating domain invariants, and trigger a verified safe fallback if observations exceed the validity boundary.

Both frameworks converge on one engineering invariant. The invalidation horizon (definition [9.2](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/09_memory/09_memory.html#dfn-memory-belief-invalidation-horizon)) marks the precise instant at which the propagated error bound intersects the allowable task tolerance, regardless of whether that bound stems from worst-case physics or calibrated statistical quantiles. Belief expiration is physical rather than computational. It is neither a generic network socket timeout nor an arbitrary 100 ms polling heuristic selected for scheduling convenience. Rather, ${T_{\text{inv}}}$ is a deadline dictated by spatial clearance, machine kinematics, and disturbance dynamics. Beyond ${t_0} + {T_{\text{inv}}}$, downstream controllers cannot assume the estimated pose satisfies the task’s safety envelope.

### Expiry Across the Machine’s Beliefs

^sec-memory-expiry-across-beliefs

A single global staleness timeout across the robot either admits a blind latch insertion long after the finger has drifted past the slot wall, or halts the mobile base in an open aisle while half a second of certified clearance remains. Because operating speed, carried payload, and contact state reshape disturbance bounds dynamically, the invalidation horizon in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-invalidation-horizon|equation 14]] cannot be reduced to a single global constant for the entire robot. Instead, the perception and memory interface must compute distinct expiry horizons for each operational regime. Transitions between regimes can alter validity instantaneously; for example, shifting from free flight to mechanical contact introduces impulsive impact dynamics within milliseconds, violating the continuity assumptions of smooth drift models.

Consequently, multiple spatial beliefs coexisting on the same physical platform expire along completely different timelines ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^tbl-09-belief-decay-horizons|table 4]]). A manipulator finger aligning with a latch slot is constrained by tight millimeter tolerances and compounding acceleration disturbances. Simultaneously, free-space claims around the mobile base and tracking estimates of a coworker near the arm share the same standardized human walking speed bound ${v_h}$. Even these human-centric beliefs, however, lapse at markedly different intervals, because each originates from a different initial localization uncertainty and must satisfy a distinct geometric tolerance. For volumetric free space, that tolerance is determined by the distance to the nearest occlusion frontier.

|:-----------------------------------|:----------------------------------------|----------------:|----------:|:--------------------------------------------------------------------|

The coworker’s initial error is illustrative and its tolerance chosen, while its speed is the same walking-speed bound, so all three rows set by a person’s motion grow linearly. The last column names the required disposition by that root; any physical response must begin early enough to remain feasible. The mug-handle belief, whose growth is set by the conveyor rather than a person, is worked in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.11 The Belief Record Schema#^sec-memory-spatial-state-schema|section 9.10]].

^tbl-09-belief-decay-horizons

When an expiry deadline is reached, the motion permission system must refuse dependent actuation proposals until fresh sensory observations restore valid evidence. Even upon reacquisition, sensory updates renew state authority only under strict validation checks. Upon receiving new sensor data, the estimator computes the measurement innovation ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-perception-state-estimation|section 9.2]]), the discrepancy between incoming physical measurements and predicted sensor observations. Incorporating this innovation requires verifying data association, sensor calibration, and physical feasibility constraints. A large innovation residual typically signals an unmodeled disturbance, an outlier measurement, or a tracking failure, which the estimation filter must isolate before granting motion permissions. Conversely, a state update must never trigger discontinuous step commands to low-level motor controllers; trajectory generation must preserve acceleration continuity. Furthermore, as the force-and-bias case of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.09 Observability Ceilings and Estimation Limits#^sec-memory-state-estimation-bounds|section 9.8]] shows, a deceptively small innovation residual in observable dimensions provides no guarantee of accuracy along unobserved state dimensions.

**Checkpoint 9.2: Deadlines, frames, and maps**

Every deadline in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^tbl-09-belief-decay-horizons|table 4]] comes from a growth law, a tolerance, and a map that preserved the evidence epochs behind them. These items test all three.

**Expiry estimate**: If the coworker’s tolerance tightened from 150 mm to 100 mm, with the same initial error and walking speed, what would its invalidation horizon be, and where would the row then fall relative to the latch-slot finger?

**Horizon vs. timeout**: Can you explain why a single staleness timeout for every belief must over-admit some rows of the table and under-admit others?

**Common-epoch composition**: Can you explain why the same finger misses the slot after one stale frame on a hand-composed chain yet stays within it for several frames when the slot is chained against the wrist pose of its own capture epoch?

**Representation choice**: Can you explain why the local snapshot is a Euclidean distance field, and which resource, rather than lookup time, then limits its voxel pitch?

**Map revisions**: Can you explain why a loop closure can misplace a remembered cell whose evidence epoch still passes every age check?

Every invalidation horizon is only as trustworthy as its error growth model. Such a model remains dependable only to the extent that the physical system can detect when its assumptions fail, whether through innovation monitoring upon reacquisition or systematic validation during sensor dropout trials. When unmodeled disturbances act along unobservable degrees of freedom, the system can drift far outside its tolerance without generating any detectable residual, which is why conservative geometric clearance and explicit physical bounds remain indispensable.
