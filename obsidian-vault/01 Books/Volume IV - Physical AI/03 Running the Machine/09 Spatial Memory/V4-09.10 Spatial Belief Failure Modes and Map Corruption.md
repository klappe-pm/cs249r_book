# V4-09.10 Spatial Belief Failure Modes and Map Corruption

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09 Spatial Memory|V4-09 Spatial Memory]]

^sec-memory-failure-modes

The failure modes of spatial memory rarely announce themselves with operating system exceptions or kernel panic logs. When a corrupted pointer or segmentation fault halts a process, the failure is loud and immediate, even if bringing the physical mechanism safely to rest requires dedicated fallback hardware. In contrast, spatial memory failures are quiet and deceptive. The memory subsystem continues publishing well-formed, syntactically valid data structures at high frequency, while the geometric coordinates inside those structures have parted ways with physical reality.

This divergence exposes the **middleware QoS mirage**, the memory-side counterpart of a limit **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/04 The Nervous System/V4-04.04 Multi-Rate Cadences#^sec-nervous-mailbox-exchanges|Lock-free and wait-free mailbox exchanges]]** established for transport, where no middleware setting could stand in for the chunk lease. DDS-based robotics middleware, including ROS 2, provides Quality of Service (QoS) policies such as `Deadline` and `Liveliness`. These policies monitor declared message intervals and publisher liveliness assertions, not the physical truth of the payload. If an upstream camera driver deadlocks or an object tracker loses its target while a republisher continues sending its cached matrix and satisfying those communication contracts, the policies need not report a violation. Physical evidence validation must therefore accompany transport monitoring; otherwise frozen transforms, stale state-store entries, and phantom tracks can reach motor controllers as apparently healthy data.

Consider a camera that abruptly stops delivering frames while an intermediate transport node continuously republishes the last calculated transform under fresh timestamps. A downstream controller with a 5 $\text{ms}$ publish-time freshness check accepts every message without protest. The scenario separates two kinds of failure in an embodied system. In a **loud failure**, a component crashes, halts execution, drops network packets, clears an output buffer, or asserts an explicit hardware error bit. Downstream consumers detect these faults through explicit validity flags or bounded stream timeouts. Avoiding harm then requires a qualified fallback (dynamic braking, position hold, or compliant impedance control) whose detection and response delays fit the remaining physical margin. In a **silent failure**, the spatial memory subsystem continues publishing structurally complete, numerically well-formed state records with valid status bits, low covariance flags, and monotonically increasing timestamps, even as the coordinates within those records drift away from the physical machine. Downstream control loops consume these corrupted records without raising an exception, closing feedback loops around phantom geometry. Physical actuators execute commands derived directly from these plausible yet false trajectories, such as driving an arm toward a target that now intersects a wall. Silent map corruption can therefore cross the causal boundary and inject destructive forces into the environment long before any software monitor registers an anomaly.

When an upstream sensor halts entirely, the timestamp anti-pattern of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.05 State Epochs and Temporal Anchoring#^sec-memory-state-epoch|section 9.4]] manifests as the **frozen transform**. This represents one of the most deceptive silent failures in embodied robotics because republishing a stale spatial snapshot with updated transport timestamps masks a dead sensor. In an illustrative timeline on the mobile manipulator, the wrist camera’s tracking pipeline computes the target object’s transform ${\mathbf{T}_{\text{camera}}^{\text{object}}}$ at timestamp ${t_0} = 0\text{ ms}$. If the optical sensor subsequently disconnects, drops frames, or suffers thread starvation at ${t} =$ 10 $\text{ms}$, the tracking algorithm ceases to produce new spatial evidence. If the message-passing middleware or an intermediate transport node retains that last computed matrix in an unexpired buffer and republishes it at 1,000 $\text{Hz}$, generating a synthetic transmission timestamp ${t}_k = {t_0} + {k}\,{T_{\text{rep}}}$ with period ${T_{\text{rep}}} =$ 1 $\text{ms}$, it erases the temporal provenance of the original measurement. A downstream tracking controller verifies freshness by computing data age as ${\Delta} {t} = {t_{\text{current}}} - {t_{\text{msg}}}$. Because the republisher stamped the message with the current system clock, ${\Delta} {t}$ evaluates to less than 1 $\text{ms}$, satisfying the freshness threshold of ${t_{\text{age},\max}} =$ 5 $\text{ms}$. The controller consumes the transform as current evidence. If the mug rides the pick-station conveyor at 0.20 m/s while the reported transform remains static, the controller accumulates 20 $\text{mm}$ of spatial tracking error in 100 $\text{ms}$. This exceeds the 15 mm grasp tolerance, yet the estimator reports zero estimated tracking divergence.

Phantom-object tracks arise when an estimator decouples the lifetime of spatial coordinates from the lifetime of existence evidence. Target tracking literature has long treated existence as an independent random variable alongside spatial kinematics: integrated probabilistic data association carries an explicit existence probability that updates with every sensor scan ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-musicki1994integrated|Mušicki et al. 1994]]). Modern learned trackers implement this separation in different ways. ByteTrack pairs object detection with a Kalman motion model, maintaining unassociated tracks in a grace buffer across a fixed window of missed frames ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-zhang2022bytetrack|Zhang et al. 2022]]); query-based architectures like MOTR persist learned object queries across successive video frames ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-zeng2022motr|Zeng et al. 2022]]). In either design, when an object is occluded or removed from the scene, the tracker enters the coasting state of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-perception-tracking-association|section 9.2.2]] and continues propagating its state vector ${\mathbf{x}} = [{\mathbf{p}}^\top, {\mathbf{v}}^\top]^\top$ under an open-loop motion model. If retention rules are overly generous, the estimator maintains a persistent phantom entity in the state store. The path planner routes the manipulator around empty space, or an automated grasping routine closes its fingers on vacuum. Conversely, evicting an occluded obstacle prematurely creates a severe collision hazard when the arm sweeps through the physical space. Resolving phantom tracks requires moving beyond passive measurement absence or arbitrary heuristic timeouts to active negative evidence ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.07 Certified Spatial Assertions in Scene Memory#^sec-memory-scene-attention-buffers|section 9.6]]). When a line-of-sight sensor ray sweeps the candidate volume and encounters zero density, raymarching actively evicts the phantom obstacle from the 3D state store as soon as line of sight confirms clearance. In manipulation, unfulfilled contact expectations (such as zero load at a predicted contact boundary) immediately trigger physical hypothesis invalidation.

Coordinate frame misattributions divide along a similar boundary between syntax and semantics. A **schema-detectable frame mismatch** occurs when a publisher supplies a malformed frame identifier or a value outside a declared frame enumeration. Static schema enforcement can reject that packet at the serialization boundary. However, a well-formed identifier can still name an undefined link in the transformation tree graph. Resolving an otherwise well-formed identifier against the current transform tree requires a frame-aware validator or consumer; the transport schema alone does not establish that the named parent link exists. In contrast, a **semantically wrong frame assignment** occurs when an engineer or configuration file assigns a valid, type-correct frame identifier that represents the wrong physical link. For example, commanding the mobile manipulator’s fingertip trajectory using the transform of the arm’s bare mounting flange ${\mathbf{T}_{\text{flange}}}$ instead of the gripper’s tool tip ${\mathbf{T}_{\text{tool}}}$ satisfies every schema validation check, type assertion, and tree traversal test. In an illustrative setup with a tool tip 150 mm beyond the flange along the tool ${z}$-axis, a flange-as-tip error shifts the intended contact point by that offset. Whether contact occurs depends on the fixture geometry and admitted path. Detecting a semantic frame error requires physical consistency checks, such as verifying measured motor torques against an analytical dynamic model of the arm, or cross-referencing multi-camera optical tracker observations against joint encoder kinematics.

When sensor updates drop out, an estimator relies entirely on its predictive forward model to advance state estimates across the blind interval. A well-formulated filter propagates the mean state estimate ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}({t})$ and covariance ${\mathbf{P}}({t})$ under its stated dynamics and process-noise model. The worked drift model grows position uncertainty during the gap; general covariance propagation need not be monotonic in every direction. An **overconfident predictor** understates process variance ${\mathbf{Q}}$, holding ${\mathbf{P}}({t})$ artificially compact over an extended prediction duration, such as beyond an illustrative 200 ms. When fresh sensor measurements eventually arrive, the artificially small covariance causes the filter to discount incoming observations; it weights its internal dead-reckoning so heavily that it classifies valid measurements as statistical outliers and rejects them. Engineers identify overconfidence during system qualification by executing staged synthetic occlusions, dropping sensor frames for a range of intervals and evaluating the innovation ${\boldsymbol{\nu}}$ at reacquisition against the covariance ${\mathbf{S}}$ the filter predicted for it. These are the NIS and NEES tests of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.03 Estimating State from Dated Measurements#^sec-memory-estimator-consistency|section 9.2.1]] applied at the end of a gap rather than in steady state. Averaged over the staged trials, the NIS at reacquisition, and the NEES against ground truth, must fall inside their chi-squared acceptance intervals ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-barshalom2001estimation|Bar-Shalom et al. 2001]]), and an average above the interval indicates an overly optimistic process noise model. Drift along an unobservable degree of freedom, such as workpiece slippage inside compliant gripper pads, produces no reacquisition residual at all ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.09 Observability Ceilings and Estimation Limits#^sec-memory-state-estimation-bounds|section 9.8]]). At vehicle scale, a terrain belief stands until the wheels themselves contradict it.

**War Story 9.1: A rover trapped in sand (2009)**

**Context**: By 2009 the *Spirit* rover had been driving for three years with an inoperative right-front wheel drive, dragging the dead wheel behind it as it drove backward on drives planned by operators on Earth. In the valley west of Home Plate, at a site the team named Troy, it drove onto soil that looked like the ground it had already crossed ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-arvidson2010spirit|Arvidson et al. 2010]]).

**Mechanism**: A thin sulfate-rich crust covered loose sulfate and basaltic sand. The surface’s appearance, which is all that orbital and rover cameras observe, carried no information about the shear strength underneath. Climbing soil-covered slopes on five working wheels already produced high slip, and at Troy the wheels broke through the crust and excavated the sand beneath instead of advancing the chassis ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-arvidson2010spirit|Arvidson et al. 2010]]).

**Impact**: *Spirit* became embedded in late April and early May 2009. Months of extrication attempts, rehearsed on an Earth testbed, failed, and in January 2010 the mission redesignated the rover a stationary research platform.

**Response**: Slip detection already existed. Visual odometry, which compares the rover’s tracked motion with its commanded wheel rotation, had been used on both rovers to measure slip and stop drives when it exceeded a limit ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.15 Source References#^ref-maimone2007two|Maimone et al. 2007]]). That measurement bounded how deep each drive could dig once slip appeared, but it could not warn before the wheels broke through.

**Systems lesson**: Traversability inferred from appearance does not observe the property that matters, the soil’s strength, so a stored traversability belief can be well formed and wrong. Slip is the physical residual that exposes it, and the belief must expire the moment that residual departs from its bound.

Frozen transforms, phantom tracks, misassigned frames, and overconfident predictors each demand distinct validation checks. The belief record of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.04 The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants#^sec-memory-handoff|section 9.3]] makes their provenance, age, frame, and growth assumptions available to consumers; independent physical checks still have to establish whether those claims hold. The filled record below collects those obligations for the latch-slot finger and the moving mug.
