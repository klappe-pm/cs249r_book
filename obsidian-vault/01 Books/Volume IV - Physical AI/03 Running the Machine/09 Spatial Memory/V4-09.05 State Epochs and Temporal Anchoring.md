# V4-09.05 State Epochs and Temporal Anchoring

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09 Spatial Memory|V4-09 Spatial Memory]]

^sec-memory-state-epoch

A bounding box or 3D Gaussian pose emitted by a neural object detector describes where an entity was when photons struck the camera CMOS sensor, not where it is when the inference tensor arrives in memory. Every spatial claim refers to a specific physical instant, its **epoch**, and any state crossing a physical AI pipeline carries three distinct epochs before an actuator can fire. The **acquisition epoch** (${t_{\text{acq}}}$) anchors the observation contract to physical ground truth: the exposure midpoint for a camera frame, or the precise instant a hardware register latches an optical encoder count. Every observation carries its own acquisition epoch (the capture epoch of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.04 Dating and Placing the Measurement#^sec-perception-handoff|Dating and Placing the Measurement]]**). For each state component used by a consumer, the evidence epoch is the latest acquisition epoch of a measurement that constrained that component; for a belief built from one observation, the two coincide. A record combining differently constrained components must preserve those epochs separately, or use a conservative shared epoch and growth bound that supports every component the query needs. The rest of the chapter speaks of the evidence epoch whenever the belief, not a single observation, is meant. Downstream processing then introduces two subsequent epochs. The **estimate epoch** (${t_{\text{est}}}$) marks the physical instant that the estimated state describes. A kinematic predictor or state observer can advance this temporal coordinate beyond the last observation without receiving fresh measurements; the instant computation completes is a separate scheduling detail. The **publication epoch** (${t_{\text{pub}}}$) marks the instant the serialized state record is committed to the transport bus or shared-memory ring buffer for dispatch to downstream consumers.

Collapsing these three timestamps into a single receipt or transmission timestamp destroys the physical anchor. This is the publish-timestamp anti-pattern of **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^sec-body-measurement-freshness|Measurement Freshness]]**, in which a wrapper stamps its output with the system wall clock after exposure, transport, inference, and postprocessing have already elapsed. What memory adds is a second way to commit it. A wrapper that stamps a propagated estimate with its publication epoch, or a filter that renews ${t_0}$ when it advances ${t_{\text{est}}}$, hides the evidence age just as surely as one that stamps a raw frame on arrival. Downstream receivers then ingest a message that appears freshly minted but wraps stale evidence. The age-as-displacement relation established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.05 Bounding Observation Age#^sec-perception-age-budget|Bounding Observation Age]]** governs this entire pipeline. While publication epochs help diagnose pipeline latency and transport jitter, only the acquisition epoch enables a motion controller to compensate for physical movement that occurred while data were in flight.

↩︎ The hardware capture stamp this anti-pattern discards is established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.04 Dating and Placing the Measurement#^sec-perception-handoff|Dating and Placing the Measurement]]**.

Even when a system faithfully records acquisition epochs, physical AI architectures rely on distributed processing nodes whose local oscillator clocks drift independently. Connecting to a shared bus does not synchronize these time bases; the deployed clock-conversion protocol must establish an explicit error bound under realistic network loads and fault conditions.[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.16 Footnotes#^fn4|⁴]] That temporal uncertainty directly converts to spatial uncertainty scaled by the relative speed of the observed entity. The observation contract defined in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.09 The Observation Contract#^sec-perception-observation-contract|The Observation Contract]]** carries this bound as its clock-conversion error, and any spatial belief derived from that observation preserves it. Every consumer adds the synchronization bound to the elapsed evidence age before judging freshness, incorporating the resulting positional uncertainty alongside sensor resolution limits and structural deflection. The permission path therefore treats this clock-synchronization uncertainty ${\epsilon_{\text{sync}}}$ as an irreducible temporal margin, expanding the required clearance by ${d_{\text{sync}}} = {v_{\text{rel}}} {\epsilon_{\text{sync}}}$ before granting motor torque authorization. With the observation contract’s illustrative 0.2 ms bound and the base at its 1.5 m/s drive limit, that term is 0.3 mm, small beside the 50 mm localization bound but never zero. The bound matters most where it is loosest, on a link whose synchronization has not been measured under load.

Physical systems routinely combine asynchronously acquired signals, even when hardware triggers synchronize subsets of sensors. In an illustrative asynchronous cycle, a tactile array might latch normal force readings at ${t}_1 =$ 12 ms, an optical tracking camera might have its exposure midpoint at ${t}_2 =$ 18.5 ms, and joint optical encoders might sample angular positions at ${t}_3 =$ 21 ms. These observations occur at different epochs and cannot be combined through direct tensor concatenation or naive algebraic operations. Multimodal foundation models and vision-language-action (VLA) backbones frequently pack visual patch tokens, proprioceptive vectors, and tactile embeddings into a single sequence under the implicit assumption that all tokens capture a simultaneous physical snapshot. However, nothing in that token serialization records that the camera frame came from ${t}_2$ and the encoder reading from ${t}_3$. As a result, representations inferred from unaligned inputs carry unquantified temporal mismatch errors. Fusing a camera observation at ${t}_2$ with an encoder observation at ${t}_3$ requires the consumer to project one state across the temporal gap ${\Delta} {t} = |{t}_3 - {t}_2| =$ 2.5 ms. The projection uses explicit kinematic models and bounded velocities (for example, propagating the historical camera observation along the joint velocity vector to reconcile it with the instantaneous encoder reading). The gap is small in time but not in space; at the arm’s 1.7 m/s Cartesian speed limit, the tool moves 4.25 mm in 2.5 ms, a millimeter-scale error that a fine insertion cannot absorb. Without the true acquisition epoch for each individual signal, the alignment calculation cannot determine the sign or magnitude of ${\Delta} {t}$, leaving uncorrected temporal offsets to compound geometric errors across every inference cycle. Placed on one shared timeline ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.05 State Epochs and Temporal Anchoring#^fig-09-epoch-alignment|figure 2]]), the three epochs fall at distinct instants, and the camera evidence must be carried across the 2.5 ms gap before it can be fused with the encoder state.

^fig-09-epoch-alignment

Equipped with these explicit epochs and the clock-synchronization bound carried in the record of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.04 The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants#^sec-memory-handoff|section 9.3]], downstream consumers can evaluate the elapsed duration between the evidence epoch and the execution deadline. They can then compute the worst-case expansion of the spatial error bound, and verify whether the state remains within task tolerances. Omitting the acquisition epoch leaves the consumer blind to transport delays and scheduling jitter, degrading deterministic closed-loop control into open-loop guesswork. Because physical geometry continuously evolves as bodies move through space, spatial transforms between reference frames are subject to the same temporal decay, carrying their own distinct evidence epochs and bounded uncertainties.
