# V4-09.11 The Belief Record Schema

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/Volume 4 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09 Spatial Memory|V4-09 Spatial Memory]]

^sec-memory-spatial-state-schema

The belief record introduced in [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.04 The Spatial Memory Handoff and State Invariants#^sec-memory-handoff|section 9.3]], the seventh record in the record chain, binds each state estimate to its capture epoch, coordinate frame, and error-growth law so that downstream controllers can catch a frozen transform, a chimeric frame chain, or a prediction that has outlived its slot tolerance. This schema unifies discrete tracked entities and continuous volumetric representations ([[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.07 Certified Spatial Assertions in Scene Memory#^tbl-09-schema-volumetric-belief|table 2]]) under one set of time and error rules, serving both trajectory planning on the host and bounded local permission checks on real-time microcontrollers. From the observation contracts established in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/08 Sensor Perception/V4-08.09 The Observation Contract#^sec-perception-observation-contract|The Observation Contract]]**, the record inherits the raw sensor capture epoch, clock identifier, and hardware conversion bound. For differently constrained state components, it preserves separate evidence epochs or a conservative shared epoch and growth bound, as required by [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.05 State Epochs and Temporal Anchoring#^sec-memory-state-epoch|section 9.4]]. With these fields, the safety permission controller translates the sensor’s capture timestamp into its own local monotonic clock with bounded synchronization error before evaluating evidence age. Persistent memory contributes five fields of its own. An estimate epoch dates the propagated state without renewing physical evidence. An uncertainty-growth bound projects error expansion across blind intervals. A validity region defines the operating envelope where that bound holds. The record also carries a physical invalidation deadline. Finally, a query-work allocation is set to at most 2 μs per local collision query in the reference design, and it becomes a justified execution bound only after qualification on the chosen hardware. Across its lifetime, each record moves through the four-state lifecycle of [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.11 The Belief Record Schema#^fig-09-spatial-belief-state-machine|figure 12]], whose guards enforce two rules. Only fresh evidence returns a record to `VALID`, and leaving `UNOBSERVABLE` also requires restoring the missing physical constraint.

![[90 Assets/Volume IV/09 Memory/Fig09 Spatial Belief State Machine 01545e6d9c.svg]]

*State diagram with four boxes. VALID, labeled measured, E at most e max, leads by an occlusion arrow to DEGRADED, labeled predicted, E of delta t at most e max, which leads by an arrow labeled E of delta t equals e max or region breach to EXPIRED, labeled supports no motion. Two rails above return from DEGRADED and EXPIRED to VALID under the guard fresh evidence and innovation gate and model revalidated. Figure 12: **Spatial belief state machine and transition invariants**: Each state carries its invariant inside the box and each edge its guard. Occlusion moves a record from VALID to DEGRADED, where the error bound grows under the declared law, and the bound reaching ${r_{\text{tol}}}$ or a breach of the validity region moves it to EXPIRED. Every edge back to VALID passes the same evidence guard, so freshness alone cannot restore observability and extrapolation cannot renew validity; leaving UNOBSERVABLE also needs the missing constraint restored.

^fig-09-spatial-belief-state-machine

The transitions enforce strict physical invariants across the spatial cache:

1.  `VALID` $\to$ `DEGRADED`: Occurs upon sensor occlusion or field-of-view exit when direct measurement ceases, but the declared growth law still supports the task. Downstream consumers expand safety margins according to that law: stochastic models require calibrated coverage percentiles, whereas bounded-disturbance models enforce worst-case physical limits.
2.  `DEGRADED` $\to$ `EXPIRED`: Occurs deterministically when projected uncertainty breaches task tolerance (${E}({\Delta} {t}) \ge {r_{\text{tol}}}$) or operating conditions leave the declared validity region. The entry immediately ceases to support dependent motion commands. Safety controllers must always reserve a feasible fallback trajectory in advance, because invalidating a software record cannot arrest physical momentum.
3.  `ANY` $\to$ `UNOBSERVABLE`: Occurs immediately when physical observability rank collapses, such as unconstrained sensor bias drift under thermal changes or mechanical slippage along unmonitored degrees of freedom.
4.  `ANY` $\to$ `VALID`: Requires fresh physical evidence (${t_0} = {t_{\text{fresh}}}$), innovation residuals passing gating checks, and revalidation of the dynamic model within the operating regime. Returning from `UNOBSERVABLE` additionally requires a restored physical constraint that resolves the rank deficiency; acquiring another ambiguous sample from the unconstrained sensor is insufficient. A belief record can never transition from `DEGRADED` or `EXPIRED` back to `VALID` through predictive extrapolation, autoregressive rollout, or software republishing.

[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.11 The Belief Record Schema#^tbl-09-invalidation-epochs|Table 5]] instantiates this schema for the latch-slot finger as a concrete bounded-disturbance belief record, and the prose that follows pairs it with the linear-drift record for the moving mug handle. Together, the two records show how the same fourteen fields accommodate both accelerating manipulation errors and constant-velocity transport bounds.

The same schema governs the target belief consumed during grasping, the six-degree-of-freedom pose of a mug handle as the container advances along a takeaway conveyor toward the robotic manipulator. This record anchors its pose in the robot base frame, registers its evidence epoch to the wrist camera’s final pre-occlusion capture, and synchronizes clocks under the same 0.2 ms conversion bound. At capture, calibration limits initial pose error to ${e_0} =$ 3 mm. Its error-growth law differs from that of the latch-slot finger. Because the conveyor belt advances at a bounded velocity of at most 0.20 m/s without independent acceleration, unobserved positional drift accumulates linearly over time according to [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-linear-horizon|equation 15]], reaching the 15 mm grasp tolerance at ${t_0} +$ 60 ms. That is the freshness deadline **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/02 The Physical Body/V4-02.03 Measurement Freshness#^sec-body-measurement-freshness|Measurement Freshness]]** derived for this same mug, from the same numbers; what memory adds is that the deadline now travels inside the record, beside the evidence epoch it is measured from. The validity region requires that the belt run forward within this rated speed and that the mug remain upright and seated. Any operational violation (a conveyor stall, a speed surge, or a shock that tips the mug) expires the record immediately regardless of elapsed time. In the worked tracked-evidence model, an active visual tracker compensates the nominal conveyor motion, leaving residual belt slip as the declared growth term. The tighter horizon requires qualification of the tracker’s initial error, capture-to-use age, and residual dynamics; frame rate alone cannot establish it. This is the tracked evidence horizon met in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/01 Machine Anatomy/03 The Cognitive Brain/V4-03.06 Chunk Supply and Evidence Validity#^sec-brain-two-proposers|Two proposers and the evidence horizon]]** and carried into the intent lease in **[[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/10 Grounded Intent/V4-10.06 Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases#^sec-intent-goals-expire|Expiring Goals and Temporal Validity Leases]]**.

Downstream consumers evaluate either record through an invariant admission procedure. The controller converts the sensor capture epoch to its local clock, adds the synchronization uncertainty margin to the elapsed age, verifies the lifecycle status and validity region, and evaluates the growth law against the clearance budget of the proposed trajectory. Continuous-field representations follow an equivalent procedure, evaluating local signed-distance or occupancy snapshots against spatial clearance bounds within their qualified query allocations. Passing this admission check admits the belief to the **separate permission decision**; it does not command actuator motion. When an updated record fails admission or expires, an active motion command may continue only until the expiry of the record on which it was admitted. If that expiry arrives before a refreshed record does, the real-time controller must commit to a verified fallback maneuver before physical margins are exhausted. Notebook [9.3](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol4/09_memory/09_memory.html#nbk-memory-admission-margin) walks through this admission evaluation for the mug-handle belief.

**Napkin Math 9.3: The admission margin**

**Problem**: A trajectory proposal requests closing the gripper on the mug handle 35 ms after the evidence epoch, measured on the consumer’s local clock. Does the spatial belief remain admissible for the grasp at that instant, and how much validity margin remains?

**Variables**:

- Initial error ${e_0} =$ 3 mm and conveyor speed bound ${v_{\text{conv}}} =$ 0.20 m/s (both illustrative); grasp tolerance ${r_{\text{tol}}} =$ 15 mm (chosen); expiry ${T_{\text{inv}}} =$ 60 ms (derived from [[01 Books/Volume IV - Physical AI/03 Running the Machine/09 Spatial Memory/V4-09.08 Forward State Propagation and Occlusion Decay#^eq-mem-linear-horizon|equation 15]]).
- Clock-conversion bound ${\epsilon_{\text{sync}}} =$ 0.2 ms (illustrative) and execution age 35 ms (illustrative).
- Lifecycle `DEGRADED`, with validity region intact: the belt advances forward within nominal limits and the mug remains seated.

**Math**: The consumer charges the clock synchronization bound to the elapsed age, evaluates the growth law at this upper bound, and computes the remaining belief lifetime:

$$
\Delta t_{\text{upper}} = 35 + 0.2 = 35.2\ \text{ms}
$$

$$
E = 3\ \text{mm} + 0.2\ \text{mm/ms} \times 35.2\ \text{ms} = 10.04\ \text{mm} \le 15\ \text{mm}
$$

$$
60 - 35.2 = 24.8\ \text{ms}
$$

**Result**: The belief passes admission at the stated instant, retaining 4.96 mm of clearance margin and 24.8 ms of belief lifetime; the limiting evidence-use age is 59.8 ms after the evidence epoch.

**Systems insight**: Belief admission supplies a time-bounded input to the separate motion-permission decision. No grasp duration has been supplied, so the limiting evidence-use age is not a guaranteed completion time. The motion controller must establish a feasible completion or fallback before physical margins are exhausted within the remaining 24.8 ms. If an external disturbance knocks the mug loose, the record expires immediately despite valid timestamps, because the physical system has breached the validity region for which the growth bound was derived.

**Checkpoint 9.3: Belief expiry and the memory record**

These items close the chapter’s body: the epochs a record carries, the deadline that ends it, the states no estimator can recover, and the lifecycle and admission check that govern all three.

**Epoch classification**: Distinguish the evidence epoch (${t_0}$) from the estimate epoch (${t_{\text{est}}}$) and publication epoch (${t_{\text{pub}}}$). Why does stamping an estimate with its publication time conceal the physical error accumulated since capture?

**Admission margin**: For the mug-handle record, why does the consumer add the clock-conversion bound to the elapsed age before evaluating the growth law, and what must the controller do with the remaining lifetime once admission passes?

**Unobservable manifold hazards**: Explain why vanishing innovation residuals (${y} - {\hat{y}} = 0$) fail to reveal sensor bias drift (such as thermal expansion from nearby actuators) along unobservable degrees of freedom.

**State lifecycle state machine**: Trace the deterministic transitions between `VALID`, `DEGRADED`, `EXPIRED`, and `UNOBSERVABLE`. Why does renewing a record require fresh physical evidence, and why does recovering from `UNOBSERVABLE` require restoring an underlying physical constraint?

**Belief record**: Enumerate the required fields of the belief record (the seventh record in the book’s architecture), including hardware capture epochs, qualified collision-query allocations, and task-space validity regions.
