# V3-03.11 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03 Test-Time Compute|V3-03 Test-Time Compute]]

^sec-vol3-test-time-compute-history

Spending compute at decision time goes back to the earliest game-playing programs. Claude Shannon’s 1950 design for a chess program separated generating candidate moves from scoring the resulting board positions with a heuristic evaluation function ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-shannon1950programming|Shannon 1950]]). Because game trees grow exponentially with depth, searching every branch to the end was impossible, and heuristic search, which generates candidates and prunes weak branches with domain evaluations, became a central theme of artificial intelligence ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-newell1976computer|Newell and Simon 1976]]). Beam search entered through speech recognition in HARPY ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-lowerre1976|Lowerre 1976]]), while the two stopping rules used in this chapter came from statistics and economics: Abraham Wald’s sequential probability ratio test ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-wald1945sequential|Wald 1945]]) and Herbert Simon’s satisficing ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-simon1956rational|Simon 1956]]).

Dependency-directed revision has an equally long history in systems and planning. Early robotics planners such as STRIPS and PLANEX checked plan preconditions against sensor readings and replanned when an observation contradicted an assumed state ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-fikes1972learning|Fikes et al. 1972]]). Truth maintenance systems ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-doyle1979truth|Doyle 1979]]) and `make` ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-feldman1979make|Feldman 1979]]) turned that idea into the explicit dependency graphs that revisable candidate plans use today. As search spaces grew wider, Monte Carlo tree search ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-coulom2006efficient|Coulom 2006]]; [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-kocsis2006bandit|Kocsis and Szepesv’ari 2006]]) introduced bandit-based tree expansion, and AlphaGo combined PUCT with neural policy and value networks to steer search through huge branching factors ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-silver2016mastering|Silver et al. 2016]]).

Language models brought these search mechanisms together over text and code. In program synthesis, pass@${k}$ measured how much parallel sampling improves functional test passage ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-kulal2019spoc|Kulal et al. 2019]]; [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-chen2021evaluating|Chen et al. 2021]]), and Cobbe et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-cobbe2021training|2021]]) showed that training a separate verifier to rank sampled math solutions could beat scaling generator parameters alone. Chain-of-thought prompting extended depth within a single call ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-wei2022cot|Wei et al. 2022]]), and self-consistency combined breadth with majority voting ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-wang2022self|Wang et al. 2023]]). Process supervision moved learned scoring from final answers to intermediate steps ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-uesato2022solving|Uesato et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-lightman2023let|Lightman et al. 2024]]), which enabled Tree of Thoughts ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-yao2023tree|Yao et al. 2023]]) to run beam and tree search over multi-step reasoning.

More recent work treats test-time compute as an explicit budget to allocate across axes. Snell et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-snell2024scaling|2024]]) showed that the best mix of sequential revision, parallel sampling, and verifier-guided search depends on task difficulty. Easy tasks favor revision, moderate tasks favor hybrid search, and tasks where the base model has near-zero success need a stronger model rather than more samples. Reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks then trained models to run long internal scratchpads that test ideas and backtrack inside a single generation ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-deepseekai2025deepseekr1|DeepSeek-AI et al. 2025]]). Finally, pushing search hard against learned reward models exposed Goodhart’s law ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-goodhart1984|Goodhart 1984]]) in the form of reward-model overoptimization ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-gao2023scaling|Gao et al. 2023]]), where the winner’s proxy score keeps climbing while held-out accuracy peaks and falls.

- **Search as problem solving** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-newell1976computer|Newell and Simon 1976]]). Introduces heuristic search as generating and pruning hypotheses under resource limits, the framework that test-time compute policies build on.
- **Monte Carlo tree search** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-browne2012survey|Browne et al. 2012]]). Surveys tree-search selection rules, rollouts, and backup operators adapted for multi-step language-model search.
- **Trained verifiers** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-cobbe2021training|Cobbe et al. 2021]]). Shows that sampling candidates and ranking them with a trained verifier can beat scaling generator parameters alone.
- **Process supervision** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-lightman2023let|Lightman et al. 2024]]). Compares outcome-level and step-level supervision, detailing the labeling cost and pruning benefits of process reward models.
- **Reward-model overoptimization** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-gao2023scaling|Gao et al. 2023]]). Measures how proxy reward scores diverge from held-out accuracy as best-of-${N}$ pool size grows.
- **Compute-optimal test-time allocation** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.13 Source References#^ref-snell2024scaling|Snell et al. 2024]]). Analyzes how the frontier across parallel sampling, revision, and tree search shifts with task difficulty under a fixed compute budget.
