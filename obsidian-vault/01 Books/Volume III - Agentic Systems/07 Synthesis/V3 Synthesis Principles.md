# V3 Synthesis Principles

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]]

^synthesis-principles

Parts I through VI developed the responsibilities needed to carry a task from a model proposal to an assessed outcome. They priced calls and candidate selection, separated information from derived computation, mediated effects and disclosures, and governed continuation, recovery, and evaluation. Learning and delegation introduced optional ways to change the system, each requiring evidence that the change serves the task. Part VII brings these responsibilities together on one trajectory of the remediation agent ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.05 The H·S·A Exposure Space#^sec-vol3-intro-lighthouses|Sec — V3-01.05 The H·S·A Exposure Space]]). That agent carries all three H·S·A exposures at once through a multi-turn diagnosis, durable shared production state, and authority over a live production service. A working component is not enough, because its assumptions must also match the evidence, authority, and state supplied across the next boundary.

Rather than introducing a new principle, Part VII returns to invariant closure (principle ) and the principles that refine it, following closure to its last boundary. Each transition needs an owner, a record, and a stated response when the available evidence is insufficient, and the contract that sets those requirements needs someone authorized to adopt it. A model can help formulate goals, propose designs, draft tests, and critique a task contract, but those outputs remain proposals. They do not adopt the contract, grant permission, change acceptance criteria, or certify the model’s own work, for the same reason that the model’s self-report cannot close any other invariant. Authorized people and organizations own those decisions and the residual duties they leave, which is the specification boundary developed in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/07 Synthesis/20 Conclusion/V3-20.11 Essential Complexity and the Specification Boundary#^sec-vol3-conclusion-brooks|Sec — V3-20.11 Essential Complexity and the Specification Boundary]].

[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/07 Synthesis/20 Conclusion/V3-20 Conclusion#^sec-vol3-conclusion|Sec — V3-20 Conclusion]] first answers the three questions the introduction posed ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.09 Book Organization#^sec-vol3-intro-questions|Sec — V3-01.09 Book Organization]]), then traces one incident through diagnostic reads, a scoped approval, an uncertain external effect, and an acceptance decision. It uses the contract to assign responsibility at subsystem boundaries, compares interventions using evidence, and constructs a scoped safety case. The closing argument concerns accountability rather than a limit on what assistance a model can provide. Useful proposals still need authorized adoption, justified acceptance, and ownership of what remains unresolved.
