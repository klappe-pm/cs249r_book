# V3-19.08 Spending Governance

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19 Agent Economics|V3-19 Agent Economics]]

^sec-vol3-agent-economics-governance

When a coordinator admits concurrent sub-agents or child tasks, simple balance checks collapse under race conditions. If two children inspect the remaining free balance simultaneously before either records a reservation, both admission checks succeed, yet their combined liability immediately breaches the authorized task budget. Task cancellation introduces a second concurrency bug: returning a stopped child’s allocation to the free pool while its remote service call remains billable allows another worker to spend funds that are already committed to third-party infrastructure. The harness already solved this for one trajectory. Its bounded-liability admission reserves an operation’s maximum charge before dispatch and releases it only at settlement (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.10 Budgets and Ceilings#^sec-vol3-agent-harness-reservation-settlement|Reservation and settlement]]**). Trajectory-level encapsulation (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/V3 Agent Runtime Principles#^pri-vol3-trajectory-encapsulation|10]]) requires the same invariant to hold when the allowance is split across children, and to stay conserved across concurrent dispatch, node crashes, and late settlement.

### Liability Bounds and Admission

^sec-vol3-agent-economics-liability-bounds

If an admission gate reserves only the average token cost of a model call, a single request that hits its maximum completion cap or misses the prompt cache will settle above its reservation and breach the task’s spending ceiling. To prevent overruns, let ${E}({\tau})$ denote gross charges settled into a task’s budget account at time ${\tau}$ and ${R}({\tau})$ the funds withheld there for outstanding liabilities, delegated allocations, and protected finalization. A task with authorized budget ${C_{\max}}$ maintains the invariant

$$
{E}({\tau})+{R}({\tau})\le {C_{\max}}.
$$

The harness’s bounded-liability test (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.10 Budgets and Ceilings#^eq-vol3-bounded-liability-admission|equation 10.1]]**) enforces this invariant at admission. Before dispatch, the runtime reserves a strict upper bound ${\bar{c}}$ on the operation’s maximum charge across every billing category (for a model call, including the maximum completion and hidden reasoning tokens) rather than an expected cost. It funds teardown in advance. Under a hard-ceiling policy, it rejects or replaces any operation that lacks an enforceable bound.

Budget enforcement also depends on complete mediation, applied here to money: every billable interaction must pass through the accounting gateway. If worker nodes hold shared credentials that bypass the reservation gate, or if unmonitored retry loops dispatch billable requests off the books, the budget account’s guarantees no longer hold. Prompt caching and tiered volume discounts present a related operational subtlety: while cache hits reduce final settlement charges, they can reduce the upfront reservation ${\bar{c}}$ only if the provider contractually guarantees the discount prior to request dispatch. If a cache miss remains possible, the runtime must withhold the full un-discounted rate.

↩︎ Complete mediation, the requirement that every access pass through one checking point, is established for tool calls in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/03 Tool Use/07 Tool Calling/V3-07.03 Tool Subsystem Architecture#^sec-vol3-tool-calling-complete-mediation|Complete mediation]]**.

The budget account tracks gross liabilities rather than net cash flows. Releasing an unspent reservation restores permission to spend money that was never actually charged. Subsequent provider credits, reconciliations, or post-hoc refunds are recorded as separate external adjustments that leave gross spending and historical authorizations intact. Fresh spending capacity enters the system only through an operator’s explicit budget grant, never through automatic replenishment from error recoveries or the speculative promise of a newly spawned candidate branch.

### Budget Conservation Across Descendants

^sec-vol3-agent-economics-budget-conservation

If a fleet dashboard sums reserved balances across a parent and its children, it double-counts every delegated dollar; if it inspects only the parent’s settled charges, it misses active spending still accumulating in open child accounts. To keep balances conserved across a delegation tree, each node ${k}$ maintains a runtime-owned budget account with the same structure, tracking its allocation ${{C_{\max}}}_k$, settled gross charges ${E}_k$, reservations ${R}_k$, and free balance ${F}_k$:

$$
{{C_{\max}}}_k={E}_k+{R}_k+{F}_k,\qquad {E}_k,{R}_k,{F}_k\ge0.
$$

A budget reservation, the harness’s bounded-liability reservation applied at node ${k}$, removes funds from the free balance ${F}_k$ before an operation can be dispatched. Multi-agent coordination partitions consumable budgets into nested sub-budgets. What this section adds is the settlement side, how a delegated allocation is counted, when it may be released, and what happens when a bill arrives late or exceeds its bound. In a hierarchical agent tree, delegating an allocation ${b}$ to a child node withholds those funds in the parent’s reservation pool ${R_{P}}$ while the child manages them within its own budget account (${C_{\max,C}} = {F_{C}} = {b}$). The parent’s reservation and the child’s entire account thus represent the same backing funds seen from two levels of the tree. Fleet-wide reporting attributes each charge exactly once through its unique operation record, while the tree hierarchy tracks which branch holds the authority to back it.

The state transitions summarized in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^tbl-vol3-budget-transitions|table 4]] are strictly atomic with respect to competing admissions and control-plane changes. Because spending represents an irreversible external commitment, budget updates must durably survive the failure domain specified in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/11 Durable Execution/V3-11.04 Durable Intent Before Dispatch#^sec-vol3-persistence-wal|Durable Intent Before Dispatch]]** before any billable request leaves the runtime. If a worker node crashes, a replacement coordinator reconstructs active reservations directly from this write-ahead log rather than resetting balances because the former worker disappeared. As in multi-agent delegation, budget differs from permission because it is consumed. A child’s read grant leaves the parent’s read access intact, whereas a delegated allocation withholds funds the parent could otherwise spend, which is why the budget account must track it as a reservation.

↩︎ Shared permissions, partitioned consumable budgets, and nested sub-budgets under delegation are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.09 Capability Delegation#^sec-vol3-multiagent-attenuated-grants|Attenuated grants and split allowances]]**.

| Budget event                              | Required condition                                                             | Conserved update                                                                                           |
|:------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Allocate ${b}$ to a child                 | Current authority and eligibility; ${b}\le {F_{P}}$                            | Parent moves ${b}$ from ${F_{P}}$ to ${R_{P}}$; child starts with ${C_{\max,C}}={F_{C}}={b}$               |
| Admit an operation bounded by ${\bar{c}}$ | Current guards pass; ${\bar{c}}\le {F}_k$                                      | Move ${\bar{c}}$ from ${F}_k$ to ${R}_k$ and record the operation and attempt binding                      |
| Final settlement with charge ${c}$        | Authenticated final evidence; $0\le {c}\le {\bar{c}}$; reservation still owned | Remove ${\bar{c}}$ from ${R}_k$, add ${c}$ to ${E}_k$, and return ${\bar{c}}-{c}$ to ${F}_k$               |
| Stop a child with unsettled work          | New admission is closed; outstanding records remain owned                      | Keep backing for its calls and descendants; stopping alone releases no liability                           |
| Close a fully settled child               | No further admission; calls and descendants settled; ${R_{C}}=0$               | Parent removes ${C_{\max,C}}$ from ${R_{P}}$, adds ${E_{C}}$ to ${E_{P}}$, and adds ${F_{C}}$ to ${F_{P}}$ |

Table 4: **Conserved budget transitions**: Each update preserves the allocated total. Closure requires financial settlement, not merely a stopped local process.

^tbl-vol3-budget-transitions

Every budget reservation is tied to a unique compound identifier binding the task, logical operation, transport attempt number, pricing tier, and cost bound ${\bar{c}}$. When downstream work finishes, settlement requires authenticated usage receipts evaluated against a durable **settlement identity**, the idempotency key under which the final charge is recorded in the budget account. It is settlement before retry (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/03 Tool Use/V3 Tool Use Principles#^pri-vol3-exactly-once-settlement|8]]) applied to money. The operation and attempt identifiers that let a tool call be retried without duplicating its effect also let a bill be redelivered without duplicating its charge. If network retries cause the provider to deliver identical billing evidence multiple times, the runtime applies the state transition at most once, verifying that repeated deliveries contain identical payloads. A conflicting payload under an existing settlement identity indicates a reconciliation fault, which the runtime must neither blindly overwrite nor silently ignore. When long-running services report intermediate consumption, the runtime can incrementally move settled amounts into ${E}_k$ while continuing to withhold a bound on remaining liabilities. It releases the reservation entirely only upon definitive finality. Deduplicating a remote side effect at an API endpoint and deduplicating an internal budget record serve two distinct contracts.

↩︎ Operation and attempt identifiers, which let a retried tool call be recognized as the same logical operation, are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/03 Tool Use/07 Tool Calling/V3-07.06 Retrying Uncertain Actions#^sec-vol3-tool-calling-operation-ids|Operation identity and endpoint contracts]]**.

If an external service reports a charge that exceeds its initial reservation (${c} > {\bar{c}}$), the system’s foundational cost invariant has failed. Silently clamping the reported charge to ${\bar{c}}$ or allowing the free balance ${F}_k$ to drop below zero would conceal a critical safety failure. Instead, the runtime immediately flags the discrepancy, halts further admissions within the affected subtree, and escalates the incident for reconciliation. Until an administrator or reconciliation procedure resolves the dispute, the system’s strict financial guarantee is temporarily suspended. Provided cost bounds remain valid, admissions execute atomically, and settlements are strictly deduplicated, every state transition preserves non-negative balances (${E}_k, {R}_k, {F}_k \ge 0$) and guarantees that no child sub-agent can spend outside its allocated backing.

Cancellation is where conservation is most easily broken. The cancellation context tree of **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.08 Bounding In-Flight Work#^sec-vol3-multiagent-cancellation-trees|Cancellation context trees]]** stops a child from admitting new billable work the moment control is revoked, but remote requests, inference batches, and cloud jobs the child already dispatched may keep running and billing until their own timeouts fire. The conservation rule therefore treats a stopped child as still open, and its whole allocation stays in the parent’s reservation until every outstanding operation reaches authenticated final settlement (the stop row of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^tbl-vol3-budget-transitions|table 4]]). A runtime that does not want to keep an inactive child’s account in memory may hand it to a settlement coordinator. This is allowed only if the handover carries every known charge, outstanding reservation, and unencumbered balance with its operation lineage, so that at no point can funds be released twice or a liability be left unbacked. The finalization reserve of the harness’s admission test, applied at each node, funds the status queries and sandbox teardown that settlement requires after ordinary admission has closed, without reopening general spending authority. Accounting reports that cross a cutoff boundary distinguish settled historical charges from active liabilities held in reserve. The notebook below traces this distinction for a canceled child that receives a late bill from an external provider.

**Napkin Math 19.10: A canceled child with a late bill**

**Problem**: A hypothetical repair has a \$5 budget. Its planning charge has settled at \$0.042, and it allocates \$1.20 to trace analysis and \$2.50 to fuzzing. The trace child closes with a final charge of \$0.38. The fuzzing child has settled \$0.85 and holds \$1 for a remote operation when its local executor confirms termination. No further child work is admitted. The remote operation later terminates with a final additional bill of \$0.70. All other charges and required finalization are included in these records, and neither child has descendants. When can the root release the fuzzing allocation?

**Math**: The entire child allocation remains reserved at the root until its account closes. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^tbl-vol3-late-bill-balances|Table 5]] follows the root’s balances under the same conservation rule throughout.

| Root account boundary                   | Settled ${E}$ | Reserved ${R}$ | Free ${F}$ |
|:----------------------------------------|--------------:|---------------:|-----------:|
| Planning settled                        |       \$0.042 |            \$0 |    \$4.958 |
| Both allocations granted                |       \$0.042 |         \$3.70 |    \$1.258 |
| Trace child closed                      |       \$0.422 |         \$2.50 |    \$2.078 |
| Fuzzing locally stopped; bill pending   |       \$0.422 |         \$2.50 |    \$2.078 |
| Late bill settled; fuzzing child closed |       \$1.972 |            \$0 |    \$3.028 |

Table 5: **Root Balances Through a Late Bill**: Local stopping retains the pending child’s allocation until its charges settle and its account closes.

^tbl-vol3-late-bill-balances

At local termination, the fuzzing child’s account has \$0.65 free, but its remote reservation remains outstanding. Final settlement releases the unused part of that reservation, leaving \$0.95 to return when the child closes. Re-delivery of the same final billing record changes none of these balances.

**Systems insight**: A premature release upon local process termination would have returned \$1.65 (the allocation minus already-settled charges), even though only \$0.65 was truly unencumbered. The subsequent \$0.70 bill would then have landed on funds already committed to another child task. The governing invariant upon cancellation is strict: release at most the allocation minus settled charges minus the enforceable upper bound on outstanding work, and return the remainder only as each remote operation reaches final settlement. Financial safety is only as reliable as those upper bounds and the completeness of the budget records.

The conservation rule shows up visually as bars of equal length ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^fig-vol3-hierarchical-budget|figure 5]]). At every boundary of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^tbl-vol3-late-bill-balances|table 5]], funds move among settled, reserved, and free balances without leaving the root’s budget, and the root’s reservation stays fixed from the fuzzing child’s local stop until its late bill settles.

### Spending Alarms and Admission Limits

^sec-vol3-agent-economics-alarms-admission-limits

Even when a cumulative task budget is strictly conserved, an overall ceiling cannot prevent damaging expenditure spikes. If an unconstrained agent launches dozens of model calls concurrently, it can burn through its entire allocation in seconds. A retrospective **spending alarm** monitors the burn rate by dividing the gross charges settled over a trailing window of duration ${T_{\text{win}}}$ by ${T_{\text{win}}}$. However, because external billing is often delayed, a retrospective alarm can stay quiet while expensive requests are in flight and spike only after execution has already halted. While retrospective alarms are useful for triggering administrative alerts or throttling subsequent work, they act after the fact, on money that has already been spent.

To prevent rapid budget exhaustion, the runtime must enforce proactive **admission limits** that evaluate new financial commitments, in the form of reservations, before dispatch. For a spending rate limit permitting at most ${C_{\text{win}}}$ in new liabilities over any trailing window of duration ${T_{\text{win}}}$, a robust admission policy enforces

$$
\sum_{i:\,t-{T_{\text{win}}}<t_i\le t}{\bar{c}}_i\le {C_{\text{win}}},
$$

where ${t}_i$ is the admission timestamp and ${\bar{c}}_i$ is the bounded charge of each billable operation.[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.16 Footnotes#^fn9|⁹]] Every prospective admission atomically validates both this sliding-window limit and the cumulative budget balance. This window constraint applies across all child tasks within the coordinator’s scope, rather than granting a fresh window allowance to every newly spawned worker. Delegating an allocation down the agent tree is an internal balance partition, not a newly admitted billable dispatch. Furthermore, retaining ${\bar{c}}_i$ in the window calculation until its admission timestamp ages out prevents a rapid sequence of fast-completing calls from triggering an unbounded burst. This policy guarantees a strict upper bound on committed work over the interval, regardless of when external providers finish processing requests or transmit final invoices.

Rate-limiting policies must also protect capacity and budget for mandatory finalization routines. If an aggressive rate limiter blocks all outgoing traffic during a spending spike, it can starve the very cancellation and status queries required to terminate running tasks and settle outstanding reservations. Dedicating isolated execution lanes or explicit finalization allowances within the accounting policy prevents this deadlock. Furthermore, completing a diagnostic probe or clearing an operational alarm must never prematurely release funds held for unrelated active reservations.

When an agent enters a non-converging loop, evidenced by repeated execution failures or unvarying candidate artifacts, the runtime triggers a spending hold under its semantic watchdog policies. This intervention blocks new ordinary billable work before runaway iterations exhaust the task budget. Already-dispatched work may continue accruing charges within its reserved bounds. The runtime enforces the designated cancellation or escalation workflow while keeping all outstanding liabilities reserved. Evaluating whether completed work meets task requirements remains the responsibility of the domain acceptance contract, rather than the spending controller.

↩︎ Semantic watchdogs, which detect a non-converging loop from its progress evidence and place the trajectory in an admission hold, are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/12 Failure Recovery/V3-12.07 Semantic Watchdogs#^sec-vol3-sagas-watchdogs|Semantic Watchdogs]]**.

Budget reservations turn spending governance from after-the-fact telemetry into an enforceable contract under declared pricing and failure models. They decide what a design may spend, not whether it is worth spending on. With cost, latency, and residual liabilities bounded, the remaining choice is which design delivers enough accepted work for its expense: a deterministic workflow, a single foundation model invocation, a bounded loop, or a delegated tree of agents.
