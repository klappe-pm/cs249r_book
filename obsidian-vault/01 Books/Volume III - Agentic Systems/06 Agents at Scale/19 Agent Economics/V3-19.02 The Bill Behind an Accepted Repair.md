# V3-19.02 The Bill Behind an Accepted Repair

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19 Agent Economics|V3-19 Agent Economics]]

^sec-vol3-agent-economics-overview

A team running the repair agent (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.05 The H·S·A Exposure Space#^sec-vol3-intro-lighthouses|Four lighthouse workloads]]**) is offered a model whose tokens cost a tenth as much. On the provider’s invoice the switch looks like a large saving. In the team’s own accounts it can raise the price of every repair that ships. The cheaper model may need more turns to reach a passing patch, and each turn holds a sandbox open and invokes tools. More tickets may fail assessment, and each failure still paid for its model calls, its environment, its checks, and the engineer who triages it. The token discount is real, but it applies to only part of the account.

![[90 Assets/Volume III/13 Evaluation/Hsa Locator Hsa 18ba12f601.svg]]

*H·S·A locator with all three axes, Horizon, State, and Authority, highlighted.*

*Horizon, state, and authority each set the closure a task must pay for.*

Offers like this one recur, because the price of a fixed capability falls quickly. Epoch AI ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.15 Source References#^ref-epochai2025inferenceprices|2025]]) track the cheapest model that reaches a fixed score on each of several benchmarks, and [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.02 The Bill Behind an Accepted Repair#^fig-vol3-price-at-fixed-capability|figure 1]] plots three of those price series.

![[90 Assets/Volume III/19 Agent Economics/Cell 3 Output 1 5c9f265d4d.png]]

Figure 1: **The Price of a Fixed Capability Falls Fast**: Lowest price, in dollars per million tokens on a logarithmic axis, of any model that reaches a fixed benchmark score, plotted at each new low. Each line holds the capability constant, so its descent is price change alone, and the legend gives each series’ fitted decline per year. Prices are the source’s 3:1 blend of input and output rates; the series end in early 2025. Data from Epoch AI ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.15 Source References#^ref-epochai2025inferenceprices|2025]]).

^fig-vol3-price-at-fixed-capability

Across all 21 capability thresholds in the source, the fitted decline runs from 9× to roughly 860× per year, with a median of 46×. A falling token price shrinks only the inference terms of the attempt cost. Sandbox lifetime, verification, cleanup, and human review are priced per turn, per check, and per hour of attention, not per token. Each price cut therefore moves a larger share of the bill onto terms the provider’s discount never touches, and onto the acceptance probability in the denominator.

The runtime built so far can already say part of what the team needs. The evaluation discipline of **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13 Agent Evaluation#^sec-vol3-evaluation|Agent Evaluation]]** decides which repairs count as accepted and how two policies are compared on matched tasks, and the harness’s budget ceilings stop any one task from spending without limit. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.10 Benchmarking Against a Single Agent#^sec-vol3-multiagent-evaluation|Benchmarking Against a Single Agent]]** joined the two once, deciding for one stated workload whether splitting a migration across workers repaid its coordination tax against a matched single controller. None of these says in general whether a policy is worth running. An accuracy figure reported without its cost cannot rank two designs, which is why cost-controlled evaluation reports the two jointly ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.15 Source References#^ref-kapoor2024agents|Kapoor et al. 2024]]), and a ceiling that holds says nothing about how much of the spending under it bought accepted work.

↩︎ Per-task budget ceilings and the bounded-liability admission test that enforces them are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.10 Budgets and Ceilings#^sec-vol3-controlplane-accounting|Budgets and Ceilings]]**.

What is missing is an account that joins spending to accepted outcomes. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^sec-vol3-intro-horizon-arithmetic|Reliability and Cost over a Horizon]]** introduced cost per accepted task, the expected cost of an attempt divided by the probability that the attempt is accepted, as one of the book’s two quantitative fundamentals. Here that ratio becomes something a team can measure over a real cohort of tasks. Its numerator holds every charge an attempt incurs, and its denominator counts only outcomes accepted under one declared contract. Each lever the runtime controls (reuse, retries, acceleration, routing, capacity, and delegation) is worth buying only if it lowers the ratio or buys a deadline the objective values.

The account comes first, in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.03 Cost per Accepted Task#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-accounting|section 19.2]], together with the rules that keep failed, canceled, and still-pending work inside it. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.04 Context Reuse and Retry Budgets#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-caching|Section 19.3]] prices what the runtime pays to carry context from call to call and how many attempts it should buy. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.05 Critical-Path Latency#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-criticalpath|Section 19.4]] finds where faster execution actually shortens a task, and [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.06 Model Cascades and Routing#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-cascades|section 19.5]] decides which model should attempt the work at all. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.07 Capacity for Trajectories#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-capacity|Section 19.6]] sizes the services that concurrent trajectories hold and wait on, and [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.08 Spending Governance#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-governance|section 19.7]] makes spending limits hold across delegated children and late bills. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.09 Pricing the Choice of Architecture#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-selection|Section 19.8]] then compares whole architectures under one contract, and [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/19 Agent Economics/V3-19.10 Cost per Accepted Task by Archetype#^sec-vol3-agent-economics-synthesis|section 19.9]] prices all four lighthouse workloads to show that each is bound by a different term of the same account.
