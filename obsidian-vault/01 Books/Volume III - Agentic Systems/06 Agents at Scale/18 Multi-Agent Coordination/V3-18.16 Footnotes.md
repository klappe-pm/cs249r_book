# V3-18.16 Footnotes

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18 Multi-Agent Coordination|V3-18 Multi-Agent Coordination]]

^footnotes

<sup>1</sup> **Amdahl’s Argument**: Amdahl’s 1967 conference paper contains no equation; it argues in prose that the serial housekeeping of a program limits what any number of parallel processors can deliver, and the familiar formula was written down by later authors. The lesson transfers intact to delegation, where planning, integration, and final assessment are the housekeeping that no added worker absorbs.

^fn1

<sup>2</sup> **Contract Net Protocol**: In Smith’s 1980 protocol, a manager node announces a task, idle contractor nodes submit bids, and the manager awards the contract to a chosen bidder, which may subcontract in turn. The award represents the conceptual ancestor of the typed task envelope: it fixes which agent owns the subtask and what structured receipt must return, eliminating the ambiguity inherent in prompt-level delegation.

^fn2

<sup>3</sup> **Blackboard Architecture**: The Hearsay-II speech-understanding system of the 1970s coordinated independent knowledge sources that never communicated directly with one another, interacting solely by posting and reading hypotheses on a shared global data structure. Its central scheduling challenge, deciding which pending contribution to evaluate next, reappears whenever agent workers claim tasks from a shared operational store.

^fn3

<sup>4</sup> **Greedy List Scheduling**: Graham established that any list schedule which never leaves a worker slot idle while a dependency-ready vertex waits completes within a factor of $2-1/{N_{\text{workers}}}$ of the optimal makespan. Graham also demonstrated that such schedules can suffer counterintuitive timing anomalies where adding worker slots or reducing individual task durations actually increases overall completion time ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.15 Source References#^ref-graham1969bounds|Graham 1969]]). A runtime dispatcher that greedily schedules ready work is therefore near-optimal relative to the theoretical bound in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.04 Coordination Topologies#^eq-vol3-multiagent-dag-bound|equation 3]], and an observed latency regression after expanding worker pools does not necessarily indicate a scheduler implementation bug.

^fn4

<sup>5</sup> **Erlang Supervision Trees**: Erlang emerged from a telephone-switching research program initiated in 1981, established on the design principle that systems should allow failing processes to crash cleanly while a designated supervisor restores known-good state, rather than attempting defensive error handling locally ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.15 Source References#^ref-armstrong2003making|Armstrong 2003]]). In agentic systems, this principle is even more vital: allowing a confused agent trajectory to attempt local recovery within a contaminated context window frequently compounds its errors, whereas a supervisor restart provisions a clean execution context from recorded inputs.

^fn5

<sup>6</sup> **Birthday Bound**: The classical birthday problem calculates the number of people required before two share a birthday. The answer, twenty-three people for even odds across 365 days, often surprises newcomers because the number of potential pairs grows quadratically with group size. Systems designers exploit this same relation to size hash tables and cryptographic identifiers. In multi-agent execution, each worker represents an individual and the repository files represent the calendar, with collision probability amplified because each worker samples ${w}$ distinct files simultaneously rather than a single day.

^fn6

<sup>7</sup> **N-Version Programming**: N-version programming builds several distinct implementations of a software specification and votes on their runtime outputs. Knight and Leveson had twenty-seven versions written independently across two universities and evaluated each against one million test inputs; although individual versions achieved high reliability, inputs on which several failed together were far more common than independence allowed ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.15 Source References#^ref-knight1986experimental|Knight and Leveson 1986]]). Language models trained on overlapping web-scale data face this exact vulnerability: benchmark evaluations across hundreds of models show heavily correlated errors, with the strongest correlation appearing among the largest and most capable models ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.15 Source References#^ref-kim2025correlated|Kim et al. 2025]]).

^fn7

<sup>8</sup> **Byzantine Bound**: The replica bound fixes how large a faulty fraction a protocol tolerates, not why replicas fail; the one-third limit applies to unsigned messages, and digital signatures relax it ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.15 Source References#^ref-lamport1982byzantine|Lamport et al. 1982]]). Classical deployments seek to keep simultaneous faults below that bound through physical diversity, running distinct code bases on separate hardware in different administrative domains. Model reviewers drawn from common model families, or trained on overlapping web-scale data, lack that isolation by default.

^fn8

<sup>9</sup> **Hedged Requests**: Dean and Barroso’s recipe defers the second request until the first has been outstanding longer than the 95th-percentile expected latency, which bounds the extra load to roughly the fraction of requests that are that slow. For agents the delay matters more than for storage reads, because each hedge is a full model trajectory with its own reservation, and a hedge issued too early doubles spend on the common case to shorten the rare one.

^fn9
