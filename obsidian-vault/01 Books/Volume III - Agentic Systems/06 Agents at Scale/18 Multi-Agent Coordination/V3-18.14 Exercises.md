# V3-18.14 Exercises

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18 Multi-Agent Coordination|V3-18 Multi-Agent Coordination]]

^sec-vol3-multi-agent-exercises

Every figure given below is a hypothetical problem input, not a measurement of a real system. Each problem ends in a design decision, and the calculation exists to justify it.

1.  **Choosing the lever in a worker-count screen (Basic).** A fixed work partition has divisible fraction ${f_{\text{parallel}}}=0.9$ and normalized overhead ${h}({N_{\text{workers}}})={\gamma}({N_{\text{workers}}}-1)$ with ${\gamma}=0.01$. The resource ceiling permits at most $20$ workers, and each added worker adds roughly the same token spend.
    1.  Using [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.03 The Delegation Trade-Off#^eq-vol3-multiagent-speedup|equation 1]], compute the normalized elapsed time at ${N_{\text{workers}}}=4$, $8$, and $16$, the integer count that minimizes it, and the speedup at that count. Compare the count with $\sqrt{{f_{\text{parallel}}}/{\gamma}}$.
    2.  Two proposals compete for the same engineering effort: halving the per-worker overhead to ${\gamma}=0.005$, or halving the serial work so that ${f_{\text{parallel}}}=0.95$. Compute the best integer count and its speedup under each proposal.
    3.  Decide which proposal to fund once worker spend is counted, and state which proposal alone can ever deliver a speedup above $10\times$.
2.  **Hot files in a shared workspace (Basic).** A repository holds ${n}=5{,}000$ files, and each worker writes ${w}=20$ of them, chosen uniformly and independently.
    1.  Using [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.06 Optimistic Concurrency Control#^eq-vol3-multiagent-write-collision|equation 4]], compute the probability of at least one shared write for ${N_{\text{workers}}}=2$ and ${N_{\text{workers}}}=4$ workers, both exactly and by the exponential approximation. Find the smallest fleet for which a shared write is more likely than not.
    2.  Each worker also edits one shared dependency manifest with probability $0.3$, independently of its other writes. For four workers, compute the probability that at least two edit the manifest, and the probability of any shared write at all.
    3.  A colleague proposes a single shared working directory for four workers because “collisions are under 40 percent.” Decide, and name the two hazards of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.06 Optimistic Concurrency Control#^sec-vol3-multiagent-concurrency|section 18.5]] that remain even when no two workers write the same file.
3.  **Sizing a credit window for staleness (Basic).** A producer emits candidate items at ${\lambda_{\text{arr}}}=2$ items per second. A consumer model writes a $400$-token critique of each item at $50$ output tokens per second, and each returned record occupies at most ${D_{\text{vol}}}=8{,}192$ bytes. An item is useful only if the consumer begins serving it within $60$ s of admission. For initial sizing, screen a saturated credit window by requiring its modeled mean residence to be no greater than this interval; assume negligible transport time and full utilization at the stated service rate.
    1.  Compute the consumer’s service rate ${\mu}$, the backlog of an unbounded queue after five minutes, and the wait of the newest item behind that backlog.
    2.  Using the full-window mean residence ${N_{\text{win}}}/{\mu}$, find the largest integer window ${N_{\text{win}}}$ that passes this sizing screen and its payload bound ${N_{\text{win}}}{D_{\text{vol}}}$. Does passing the screen establish that every item begins service before its freshness deadline?
    3.  Find the minimum number of identical consumers needed to keep pace with the producer and the largest shared window that passes the same mean-residence screen at their aggregate service rate. State how admission timestamps and a freshness check before service should handle an item that has waited too long. Explain why cancellation and settlement traffic must not draw on these data credits.
4.  **A fourth slot or a shorter vertex (Intermediate).** A migration plan has a shared specification ${v}_1$ ($30$ s); four module refactors ${v}_2$ through ${v}_5$ ($60$, $60$, $90$, and $40$ s), each depending only on ${v}_1$; and an integration check ${v}_6$ ($50$ s) depending on all four refactors. Each vertex occupies one interchangeable slot, with no other delays.
    1.  Compute ${W}$ and ${T_{\text{crit}}}$ from [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.04 Coordination Topologies#^eq-vol3-multiagent-dag-work|equation 2]], and the lower bound of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.04 Coordination Topologies#^eq-vol3-multiagent-dag-bound|equation 3]] for ${N_{\text{workers}}}=2$, $3$, and $4$ slots.
    2.  By assigning the four refactors to slots, find the shortest achievable makespan for each ${N_{\text{workers}}}$. Explain why the bound is not attained at ${N_{\text{workers}}}=2$.
    3.  The team currently runs ${N_{\text{workers}}}=3$ and can afford either a fourth slot or engineering that shortens ${v}_4$ to $60$ s. Decide, and state what shortening ${v}_4$ buys once the fourth slot exists.
5.  **Pricing deadline coverage (Intermediate).** Under matched resource ceilings, a single controller spends an expected $\$0.40$ per attempt with ${p_{\text{acc}}}=0.60$, and $40$ of every $100$ attempts are accepted by the deadline. A delegated configuration spends $\$0.90$ per attempt with ${p_{\text{acc}}}=0.80$, and $72$ of every $100$ attempts are accepted by the deadline. Spend includes failed attempts and canceled branches, and cost is proportional to tokens.
    1.  Compute ${C_{\text{eff}}}$ for both configurations from **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-cost-per-accepted-task|equation 1.2]]**, and their ratio.
    2.  Find the token multiple ${r}$, the delegated acceptance that would restore break-even on ${C_{\text{eff}}}$, and the largest single-controller acceptance for which break-even is possible at all.
    3.  Per $100$ attempts, compute the marginal cost of each additional on-time acceptance. One service values an on-time accepted migration at $\$1$, another at $\$5$. Decide which configuration each should run.
6.  **Hedged lookups for the support agent (Intermediate, second-lighthouse transfer).** The support agent answers a refund-eligibility question by dispatching three read-only lookups in parallel: the customer record, the order history, and the governing policy clause. All three are required. Each lookup attempt independently returns an admissible result within $1$ s with probability $0.70$ and within $2$ s with probability $0.95$. The service target is that at least $95$ percent of answers have all three results within $2$ s. Assume $1{,}000$ sessions dispatch their three initial lookups simultaneously. The lookup pool has $4{,}000$ slots, and each launched attempt retains its reservation until the $2$ s observation cutoff, even if its result arrives earlier. An attempt that has not returned an admissible result remains outstanding until that cutoff. Treat the stated completion probabilities as unchanged by load when calculating a policy without admission blocking.
    1.  Compute the probability that all three results arrive within $2$ s without hedging. Explain why the ${k}$-of-${N_{\text{workers}}}$ stopping rule of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.08 Bounding In-Flight Work#^sec-vol3-multiagent-backpressure|section 18.7]] does not rescue this fan-out.
    2.  Compare two hedging policies: duplicating every lookup at dispatch, and starting a replacement only for a lookup still outstanding at $1$ s, which must then itself return within the remaining $1$ s. Assuming every proposed hedge is admitted, compute the per-lookup and all-three probabilities within $2$ s. For each policy, compute both expected slot demand and the largest possible slot demand before the cutoff.
    3.  Decide whether either calculation establishes that the policy meets the deadline target within the hard slot limit. Propose an admission rule for delayed hedges, and explain why deadline acceptance must be evaluated again when the rule can deny replacements. Contrast the binding constraint here with the coherency limit on the repair agent’s migration, and state what must hold before the same hedging could be applied to issuing the refund itself.
7.  **Bounding a remediation delegation tree (Intermediate, second-lighthouse transfer).** The remediation agent is restoring a degraded `checkout` service. Its root grant permits reading metrics and logs for `checkout` and scaling the `checkout` deployment between $4$ and $12$ replicas; it permits no configuration writes. The root’s remaining allowance is $\$50$, of which $\$5$ is a protected finalization reserve and $\$6$ is already reserved for the root’s own outstanding work. The model proposes three children, each with a $\$20$ allowance, “less than the parent’s remaining $\$50$”: a metrics worker that reads `checkout` metrics, a log worker that reads logs for `checkout` and `payments`, and a capacity worker that scales `checkout` between $4$ and $16$ replicas.
    1.  State which proposed grants violate ${\mathcal{A}}({\Gamma_{\text{child}}})\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}({\Gamma_{\text{parent}}})$ and how to narrow them. Compute the worst-case liability if each child’s allowance is copied from the parent’s balance, and which children atomic reservation admits if they are dispatched in the order listed. Find the largest equal allowance that admits all three. The capacity worker then proposes two scale workers at $\$8$ each; state which the runtime admits and repair the split.
    2.  An operator takes over and the runtime cancels the root at ${t}=0$. Both scale workers are active, and each submits at most one scale step every $5$ s to a remote gateway. Under a cached-authorization policy the gateway trusts a lineage record for up to $18$ s, with a $2$ s clock-skew allowance. Under a strict policy it looks up the current status and generation of each of the $3$ nodes in a scale worker’s lineage at $4$ ms per lookup. Compute the stale-policy window, the most scale steps it can admit after cancellation, and the strict policy’s added latency as a fraction of the step interval. Choose a policy for scale steps and one for metric reads, and state what the strict policy costs if the authoritative store is unreachable for $30$ s.
    3.  Under the policy chosen in part (b), the cost record at cancellation shows: the metrics worker settled $\$4$ and stopped; the log worker settled $\$9$, and a log-export job it started before cancellation is still unresolved with a bounded liability of $\$3$; the first scale worker settled $\$2$ and was confirmed terminated; the second scale worker settled nothing, and a node-pool scale-up it started before cancellation has an unknown outcome with a bounded liability of $\$5$; the root’s own work settled $\$4$. Compute the allowance the runtime may release now, the amount still held, and the release a “cancellation refunds all reservations” rule would claim. A follow-up remediation task needs a $\$20$ allowance. Decide whether to admit it now, and state who owns the two open operations and what that owner must retain.
8.  **Buying evidence past a correlated floor (Advanced).** Reviewers each falsely approve a known-invalid candidate with marginal probability ${p}=0.10$, and their errors follow the beta mixture of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.07 Correlated Ensemble Failures#^sec-vol3-multiagent-consensus|section 18.6]] with ${\rho}=0.30$. The release rule requires that an invalid candidate be approved with probability at most $0.01$. A reviewer call costs $\$0.20$. A protected regression test costs $\$0.50$ and passes an invalid candidate with probability $0.05$, assumed independent of the candidate’s reviewer propensity ${Q}$.
    1.  Compute the beta parameters ${a}$ and ${b}$, and the majority false-approval probability for three reviewers under independent errors and under the mixture.
    2.  Compute the mixture’s majority false approval for seven reviewers and the floor $\Pr({Q}>1/2)$, numerically. Decide whether any number of reviewers of this kind can meet the release rule.
    3.  Price two designs per candidate: seven reviewers under majority vote, and three reviewers plus the protected test, with approval requiring both a reviewer majority and a passing test. Compute each design’s false-approval probability and choose one.
    4.  Critique the assumption that the test is independent of ${Q}$. Name a mechanism from this chapter that would correlate the test with the reviewers, and state the measurement that must precede release.
9.  **Fitting the scalability law before choosing a fan-out (Advanced, critique and redesign).** Trials of a repair-migration partition under matched resources measure relative capacity $\text{Speedup}(2)=1.835$ and $\text{Speedup}(4)=2.878$. The serial reference takes $40$ minutes, and the service requires completion within $11$ minutes. The expected cost of an attempt grows with worker count as $\$1.00+\$0.30({N_{\text{workers}}}-1)$, and acceptance does not change with ${N_{\text{workers}}}$.
    1.  Rearrange [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.10 Benchmarking Against a Single Agent#^eq-vol3-multiagent-usl|equation 5]] so that it is linear in ${\sigma_{\text{cont}}}$ and ${\kappa_{\text{coh}}}$, fit both coefficients from the two measurements, and compute ${N_{\text{workers}}}^*$ from [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/06 Agents at Scale/18 Multi-Agent Coordination/V3-18.10 Benchmarking Against a Single Agent#^eq-vol3-multiagent-usl-peak|equation 6]], the best integer count and its capacity, and the smallest count that runs slower than one worker.
    2.  A trial at ${N_{\text{workers}}}=8$ measures $2.90$. Compare it with the fitted prediction, and state what the gap implies about using the fit outside the measured range.
    3.  The team proposes deploying the best integer count from part (a). Critique the proposal against the deadline. For this calculation, suppose two proposed redesigns have the following responses: sharding the integration queue halves ${\sigma_{\text{cont}}}$, and repartitioning the migration so interface changes land before caller work begins halves ${\kappa_{\text{coh}}}$. For each, find the smallest worker count that meets the deadline, if any, and its cost per attempt, and recommend a design under these assumptions. State what trace or matched intervention evidence would establish the assumed coefficient responses in a deployment.

**What’s Next: From coordination to cost**

When is the time saved by delegation worth its additional resource use? This chapter answered for one stated workload, charging failed attempts and canceled branches to the accepted tasks they served. That accounting is a special case of a question every deployment faces, for one agent or many. The next chapter opens with the repair team offered a model whose tokens cost a tenth as much and asks why the switch can still raise the price of every repair that ships. Answering it means counting what each accepted task costs across all its attempts, how context reuse, retries, and model choice change that cost, and how concurrent trajectories compete for capacity. The same account then prices delegation itself, without dropping the reservations and unresolved obligations established here.
