# V3-16.17 Exercises

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning|V3-16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning]]

^sec-vol3-15-fine-tuning-exercises-a4330106

Every given below is a hypothetical problem input chosen for round arithmetic, not a measurement of any system. Each problem asks for a calculation and the training or release decision it supports.

1.  **Per-invocation serialization under a token budget (Basic).** The serving policy of the repair agent omits earlier generated text before each later invocation, so every turn of a trajectory becomes its own training example ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.04 Serializing Trajectories#^sec-vol3-fine-tuning-serialization|section 16.3]]). Assume that the delivered prefix at each turn still grows by approximately ${\Delta S_{\text{turn}}}$ tokens despite the omitted generated text. Trajectories run ${H}=40$ turns, each turn adds about ${\Delta S_{\text{turn}}}=500$ tokens, the corpus holds 2,000 admitted trajectories, and the run may store at most 200 million training tokens.

    1.  Compute the stored tokens per trajectory under per-invocation serialization and under a single transcript, and the expansion factor between them.
    2.  Compute how many complete trajectories fit the budget under each serialization, and what fraction of the corpus the per-invocation run can use.
    3.  Name the serving-side change that would make a single transcript a faithful example, and state what the training pipeline must verify before relying on it.

2.  **Target count and dilution (Basic).** A serialized repair example stores 2,000 prompt and schema tokens, 18,000 observation tokens, and 1,500 policy-response tokens. Of the response tokens, 300 belong to a refused proposal that curation retains as context only. The prompt and schema count includes the initial boundary; no preceding external seed is supplied. The example has no rationale and no padding.

    1.  Compute ${N_{\text{active}}}$ under [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.05 Observation Loss Masking#^eq-vol3-sft-masked-objective|equation 1]], the selected share of the stored input, and the factor by which an unmasked token average would dilute each selected target’s coefficient.
    2.  A loader instead selects every policy-response token. Compute the fraction of the imitation weight that lands on the refused proposal, and state what behavior that weight trains.
    3.  A colleague expects masking to cut this example’s training compute by the dilution factor from part (a). Explain why the saving is far smaller, and identify the only work a loader can skip at masked positions.

3.  **Normalization and segmentation (Basic).** A batch holds one long repair trajectory with 6,000 selected targets and 15 short file-lookup examples with 200 selected targets each.

    1.  Compute the long trajectory’s share of the batch objective under token averaging ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.06 Batching Variable-Length Trajectories#^eq-vol3-loss-token|equation 2]]) and under example averaging ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.06 Batching Variable-Length Trajectories#^eq-vol3-loss-example|equation 3]]).
    2.  Serialization instead splits the long trajectory into 20 per-invocation examples of 300 selected targets each, every example selecting only its own turn’s response. Recompute both shares.
    3.  The short lookups exercise a capability the release must retain. Choose a normalization and a segmentation, and state what the choice costs the long task’s influence on the update.

4.  **Packing and the cross-example work a kernel can skip (Intermediate).** Ten independent examples have stored lengths of 16,384, 8,192, 6,144, 4,096, 4,096, 2,048, 2,048, 1,024, 1,024, and 512 tokens. Each buffer holds 16,384 positions.

    1.  Compute the occupancy of a padded batch that allocates the maximum length to every row.
    2.  Pack the examples by first-fit decreasing, placing each example, longest first, in the first buffer with room. Report the buffers, the packed occupancy, and the factor by which packing reduces allocated positions.
    3.  For the buffer holding the 8,192-, 6,144-, and 2,048-token examples, compute the fraction of causal score entries (the lower triangle, diagonal included) that block-diagonal attention ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.06 Batching Variable-Length Trajectories#^eq-vol3-block-diagonal-attention|equation 4]]) sets to $-\infty$. Explain what that fraction means for a variable-length attention kernel, and why the mask, not the skipped work, is the correctness requirement.

5.  **When on-policy labeling pays (Intermediate).** Offline imitation of the repair agent reaches a disagreement rate of ${\epsilon_E}=0.01$ on reference states over a horizon of ${H}=50$ decision steps. Three DAgger rounds would each collect 400 trajectories that run the full horizon and label all 50 visited states of each, and each label costs \$0.05 to obtain and \$0.25 to verify by a sandbox continuation. After aggregation, the retrained policy’s disagreement on its own states is ${\epsilon_\pi}=0.02$, and one deviation followed by reference recovery costs at most ${u}=2$.

    1.  Compute both bounds of theorem [16.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/16_fine_tuning/16_fine_tuning.html#thrm-15-imitation-regret), their ratio, and the reference-state error offline imitation would need to match the learner-state bound.
    2.  Compute the labeling cost of the three rounds, and the horizon at which the reference-state bound saturates at its cap.
    3.  A remediation step for the remediation agent changes live configuration with no adequate reversal, so one deviation there can spoil the rest of the task. Recompute the learner-state bound with ${u}={H}$ at the same ${\epsilon_\pi}$, and state whether further labeling or a runtime mechanism should carry that step’s reliability.

6.  **Reading preference margins (Intermediate).** Two checkpoints trained with [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.09 Trajectory Preference Optimization#^eq-vol3-dpo-trajectory|equation 7]] at ${\beta}=0.1$ are compared on one admitted repair pair. On the selected spans, the reference log-probability sums are $-22$ for the preferred record and $-24$ for the dispreferred one. Checkpoint A assigns $-20$ and $-25$; checkpoint B assigns $-23$ and $-30$. The sums exclude a shared 1,000-token prefix with identical conditioning, whose inclusion would add $+0.5$ to each record’s score under either checkpoint.

    1.  Compute each checkpoint’s scores ${\Delta_w}$ and ${\Delta_l}$, margin ${z}$, pair loss, saturation factor ${\sigma}(-{z})$, and full gradient coefficient ${\beta}{\sigma}(-{z})$.
    2.  Show that including the shared prefix leaves every quantity in part (a) unchanged.
    3.  A team proposes promoting checkpoint B because its pair loss is lower. Critique the proposal from the scores, and name the measurement that decides between the checkpoints.

7.  **Sizing an adapter run on a fixed memory budget (Intermediate).** A hypothetical base model has 20 billion parameters. The run attaches rank-32 adapters to four matrices in each of 48 layers, and each adapted matrix has 6,144 input and 6,144 output coordinates. Use the chapter’s convention of 16 bytes of state per trainable parameter and 2 bytes per frozen parameter, with decimal GB. The accelerator offers 48 GB, of which 16 GB is reserved for activations and workspace.

    1.  Compute the adapter parameter count, its fraction of the base, and the parameter state of full fine-tuning and of the adapter run. Decide whether either fits the remaining budget.
    2.  Store the frozen base at 4 bits per parameter plus 0.5 bits per parameter of quantization metadata. Recompute the adapter run’s parameter state, decide whether it fits, and compute what doubling the rank would add.
    3.  Five agent roles each need their own adaptation. With every weight stored at 2 bytes per parameter, compare five full-weight copies with one shared base plus five adapters, and state what the shared base does not provide between roles.

8.  **Context distillation for the support agent (Advanced).** The support agent runs short sessions under concurrency, so its binding constraint is per-turn input and response latency rather than the long observations that dominate the repair corpus. Each turn’s input carries a 3,000-token procedural checklist, a 1,500-token refund policy that changes weekly, and about 1,500 tokens of conversation and customer record. Sessions average four turns, and the deployment serves 2 million sessions per month. Input tokens cost \$2 per million, and a cached prefix is billed at one tenth of that rate. Each redistillation, a training run plus the release evaluation, costs \$15,000, and the checklist is revised once a month.

    1.  Using [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.03 Scope of Supervised Fine-Tuning#^tbl-vol3-fine-tuning-scope|table 1]], decide which of the three input segments may be distilled into the weights and which must stay in context.
    2.  Compute the monthly input tokens and spend that distilling the checklist removes, first with no prefix caching and then with the stable prefix always billed at the cached rate. Compute the net monthly saving after redistillation in each case, and the number of checklist revisions per month at which the uncached saving reaches zero.
    3.  Decide whether to distill under each serving configuration. State what must be measured, rather than inferred from the token reduction, before claiming a latency gain, and contrast the constraint that decides this case with the one that governs masking for the repair agent.

9.  **Critique a release proposal (Advanced).** A team proposes promoting a fine-tuned repair policy on the following evidence. Its all-token training loss fell. On 500 closed-loop trials each, the baseline was accepted in 60 percent of trials at a mean attempt cost of \$1.20, and the candidate in 66 percent at \$1.50. On familiar interfaces, schema-following accuracy rose from 0.92 to 0.95. On a renamed-argument test set, the baseline scored an SFA of 0.90 with a PRE of 0.02, and the candidate an SFA of 0.81 with a PRE of 0.12.

    1.  Compute ${C_{\text{eff}}}$ for each policy in the per-attempt form of **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-cost-per-accepted-task|equation 1.2]]**, and the largest mean attempt cost at which the candidate would match the baseline.
    2.  Using a normal approximation for independent trials, compute the standard error of the difference in acceptance rates and the difference measured in standard errors.
    3.  Interpret the pattern in SFA and PRE, and redesign the release gate. Name what the decision should be now, which measurements must be added or repeated, and which training change to test first.
    4.  Suppose the two arms in fact ran on the same 500 tasks. State what record the paired comparison of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.12 Evaluating a Fine-Tuned Agent#^sec-vol3-fine-tuning-benchmarking|section 16.11]] needs beyond the two acceptance rates, and explain how the count of discordant tasks could make the difference in part (b) more or less convincing than the independent-trials calculation suggests.

**What’s Next: From admitted responses to reward-scored interaction**

When a task offers an outcome check but no demonstrated response for each decision, the adaptation framework must evolve beyond supervised imitation and static comparisons. Every update in this chapter needed a demonstrated or compared response, so the version conflict that `trained_policy_v1` never saw could be taught only by labeling that state or injecting it. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards#^sec-vol3-rlvr|Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards]]** starts from that checkpoint and lets the repair agent’s acceptance check score fresh attempts on the states the policy reaches by itself, turning each verdict into a weight update. The check then stops being only a release gate and becomes the objective. That shift raises questions this chapter’s objectives never posed: whether the reward resists the shortcuts optimization will find, how one terminal verdict assigns credit across many turns, how rollout records stay faithful to the policy that sampled them, and what the rollout and verifier fleet costs. Containment and closed-loop release evidence apply as before.
