# V3-16.15 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning|V3-16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning]]

^sec-vol3-fine-tuning-summary

Trajectory fine-tuning changes the policy itself, and every decision in this chapter serves one requirement, that the update teach the lesson curation admitted and nothing else. A trajectory mixes the policy’s responses with the environment’s replies, so a loss computed over the wrong positions, under the wrong prefixes, or with the wrong batch weighting can fall while the behavior it teaches drifts. Even a faithful update is fitted on recorded prefixes. Its value is settled only by closed-loop evidence on the states the policy reaches on its own, under the same runtime that bounds every proposal it makes.

The quantitative decisions of the chapter rest on the relations collected in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.15 Summary#^tbl-vol3-15-fine-tuning-key-equations-dc7dab9e|table 13]]. **Key Takeaways: Preserve the lesson from record to release**

- **Weights hold procedure, never contracts**: Training changes what the policy proposes, not what the runtime permits. Stable procedure can move into the weights, while schemas, permissions, and any guidance that changes faster than a training and evaluation cycle stay in context, and enforcement stays in the runtime.
- **A trajectory has two authors; train one**: Observations stay in the input as context but receive no direct loss. An unmasked average dilutes each selected target by the inverse of its selected share of predicted positions, 20.5× in the worked inventory, and teaches the policy to invent tool results.
- **A loader can change the lesson without changing the loss**: Later evidence in a prefix, a shifted label, or a causal mask across packed examples each redefines the prediction problem. Normalization is a choice too, since token averaging gives a long trajectory 32× the weight of a short one in the worked batch.
- **Where error is measured sets the horizon penalty**: The same 2 percent disagreement over 30 steps bounds excess cost at 18 units on reference states and 0.6 on learner states when the deviation disadvantage is bounded by one. Long-horizon agents therefore need labels on the states they actually visit.
- **A preference score measures one comparison**: A pair’s logistic score moves with ${\beta}$ and can rise by suppressing the dispreferred record, so it measures fit to that comparison, never the probability that a repair is accepted.
- **Adapters cut state by bytes, not parameter fraction**: Training 0.10 percent of the parameters still keeps 12.6 percent of the parameter state, because the frozen base stays resident. The lever is the frozen base’s representation, not the adapter rank.
- **Release on closed-loop acceptance, not loss**: A candidate is promoted on task acceptance, ${C_{\text{eff}}}$, schema behavior, and retained capability under its full serving configuration. Rollback selects an earlier policy for future work and leaves effects already dispatched to settlement and compensation.

^callout-takeaways--16-2-504e6bbe

Verified trajectory post-training (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/V3 Trajectory Learning Principles#^pri-vol3-trajectory-post-training|13]]) now has a concrete implementation obligation at the token level: preserve useful conditioning while aligning the objective with the selected predictions, rather than equating every recorded policy token with a target. At the record level, it connects the admitted lesson to a versioned update whose usefulness remains an evaluation question. Invariant closure (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.06 Invariant Closure#^pri-invariant-closure|1]]) stays outside that update, which is why the proposal boundary holds from the first training example to the release decision.
