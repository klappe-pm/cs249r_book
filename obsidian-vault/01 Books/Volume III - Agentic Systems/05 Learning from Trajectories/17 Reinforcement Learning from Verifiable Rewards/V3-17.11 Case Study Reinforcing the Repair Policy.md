# V3-17.11 Case Study Reinforcing the Repair Policy

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards|V3-17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards]]

^sec-vol3-rlvr-synthesis

Consider the connection-pool repair group with rewards $[0,1,0]$ from [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^sec-vol3-rlvr-grpo|section 17.5]]. Three fresh trials, `rl_trial_01`, `rl_trial_02`, and `rl_trial_03`, execute task `pool_repair_01` against the fixture `pool_fixture_v1`, sampled from the supervised checkpoint `trained_policy_v1`, and each ends in a verdict under `repair_accept_v1`. The recovery run that produced workspace `ws_r20` and the demonstrations built from it remain historical artifacts of curation and fine-tuning; they supply comparisons, not targets, for this group. Following the group through each stage of the chapter shows which question every stage answers before the middle rollout is allowed to move the weights.

The first question is whether the lone pass is a pass. A $[0,1,0]$ group on a hard bug is exactly the shape that tampering produces, so the verdict for `rl_trial_02` counts only because it came from the assessment side of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.08 Verifier Isolation#^tbl-vol3-dual-sandbox-boundaries|table 7]]. The patch was admitted as a source-only diff, applied to a fresh copy of the fixture, and graded by a controller whose tests and result store the candidate could not reach. The defect is a race on connection return, so the fixture is timing-sensitive, and the all-pass rule of notebook [17.5](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/17_rlvr/17_rlvr.html#nbk-vol3-rlvr-repeated-checks) applies. If an incorrect patch passes a single trial with probability 0.20, 3 independent trials cut its acceptance to 0.8 percent at a 5.88 percent risk of rejecting a correct one. A trial whose verifier crashed, or whose container was preempted, is marked unresolved under the stop-state rules of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.07 Multi-Turn Agentic RL#^sec-vol3-rlvr-stopping-censored|section 17.6.3]] rather than coerced to zero. Converting an infrastructure fault into a task failure penalizes the policy for the cluster and shifts the group baseline.

The second question is whether the update can be computed on what the policy actually saw. Each rollout record holds the delivered prefix and sampled token IDs of every invocation, the behavior log probabilities under the recorded sampling configuration, and the checkpoint version that produced them ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.07 Multi-Turn Agentic RL#^tbl-vol3-rlvr-rollout-record|table 6]]). Before admission, the trainer checks each record’s version lag against its bound ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.10 Environments and Rollouts#^eq-vol3-rlvr-staleness|equation 8]]); a trial that ran long enough to fall behind is excluded with its reason logged rather than silently relabeled under the newest weights. In this worked example, all three rewards are resolved and all three members satisfy the lag bound, so the original complete group is eligible. Under its declared complete-group rule, an unresolved or stale member prevents the group from entering the update; excluding members and recomputing its coefficients would require a separately declared objective and selection analysis.

Only then does GRPO compute coefficients. The pass receives 1.414214 and each failure -0.707107 (notebook [17.3](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/17_rlvr/17_rlvr.html#nbk-vol3-grpo-worked-group)), and under per-rollout averaging each token of rollout ${i}$ carries ${\hat{A}}_i/{N}_i$ ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^eq-vol3-grpo-length-coefficient|equation 5]]), so the length of each failure sets how hard its tokens are pushed down. Because the reward scores only the outcome, every token in `rl_trial_02` receives the same coefficient. The edit that adds the missing notification and any redundant status checks along the way are reinforced alike, and only many such groups separate them statistically. The frozen reference policy stays distinct from the behavior policy, contributing only the divergence penalty. Because the GRPO name covers several variants, the training log records the clipping thresholds, the length and dispersion normalizations, and the penalty weight ${\beta_{\text{KL}}}$ with the update.

When an update over eligible groups yields a candidate checkpoint, such as `rl_policy_v1`, lineage binds its weights and inference configuration to the rollout records, fixtures, and verifier versions that produced it. The controller may promote `rl_policy_v1` to the rollout fleet for further collection, since that is an internal training decision. Releasing it is a different decision. Higher training reward on a known fixture can coexist with regression on unobserved failure modes. The benchmark study discussed in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.10 Environments and Rollouts#^sec-vol3-rlvr-release-gating|section 17.9.6]] illustrates how higher pass@1 can coexist with unchanged broad-sampling performance. The release case therefore rests on held-out recovery fixtures evaluated by pass@1, pass@${k}$ at large ${k}$, and pass$^{k}$ under the protocol of **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13 Agent Evaluation#^sec-vol3-evaluation|Agent Evaluation]]**. Each stage therefore answers one question that no other stage can. The isolated verifier says whether the reward is evidence. The record says whether the update is faithful. The coefficients say how hard the evidence pushes. The independent evaluation says whether the result belongs in production.
