# V3-17.09 Training Pathologies

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards|V3-17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards]]

^sec-vol3-rlvr-pathologies

Even when an execution sandbox isolates the grader and every gradient step updates cleanly, reinforcement learning can steer an agentic policy into unhelpful or degenerate behaviors. Training curves alone rarely reveal why: for a complex repair task, rising reward accompanied by longer rollouts might indicate thorough automated testing, repetitive failed edits, or merely a shift in which tasks completed grading before timing out. Systematically diagnosing these pathologies requires connecting shifts in sampled token distributions, rollout lengths, reward sub-components, and gradient statistics to concrete hypotheses, because each failure mode demands a distinct systems intervention.

### Diversity and Token Entropy

^sec-vol3-rlvr-diversity-entropy

When a policy commits prematurely to a single failing strategy on unsolved tasks, its sampled rollouts repeat the same tool calls and stop exploring the alternatives needed to discover a passing repair. **Token entropy** tracks this narrowing at the token level by measuring uncertainty in a specified next-token distribution. For a delivered prefix ${x_{<t}}$ and vocabulary ${\mathcal{V}}$,

$$
{\mathcal{H}}\bigl({\pi_\theta}(\cdot\mid {x_{<t}})\bigr) =-\sum_{{v}\in{\mathcal{V}}}{\pi_\theta}({v}\mid {x_{<t}}) \log{\pi_\theta}({v}\mid {x_{<t}}),
$$

with natural logarithms giving entropy in nats and $0\log0$ interpreted as zero. When logging this metric, we must explicitly distinguish the raw base-model distribution from the post-sampling distribution shaped by temperature scaling, top-${k}$ filtering, or grammar constraints. Adjusting inference parameters alters measured entropy without changing network weights, just as changes in benchmark difficulty or prompt length skew aggregate averages, so any reported entropy number is incomplete without specifying the prefix population and decoding pipeline that generated it. Even with those held fixed, low entropy is appropriate at deterministic syntax positions, such as emitting closing brackets or fixed tool schemas. Rather than setting an arbitrary global entropy threshold, engineers should inspect action divergence and tool-call variety on unsolved fixtures alongside token-level uncertainty.

Uniform rewards within a sample group introduce a similar diagnostic ambiguity. When all ${G}$ trajectories in a group receive identical scores, the zero-contrast update of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^sec-vol3-rlvr-grpo-zero-variance|section 17.5.4]] zeroes out the reward-surrogate gradient. This zero-contrast condition is an algebraic property of group normalization, but its root cause varies: diverse attempts might all fail, diverse valid solutions might all pass, or an uncalibrated test harness might fail to distinguish partial progress. Disentangling these mechanisms requires inspecting the actual trajectory logs rather than relying on summary statistics. [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.09 Training Pathologies#^tbl-vol3-rlvr-pathologies|Table 8]] maps common training observations to their underlying ambiguities and practical diagnostic checks. To ensure that an apparent change in policy behavior is not merely an artifact of shifting measurement conditions, all diagnostic comparisons must hold the task fixtures, sampling parameters, reward functions, and grader versions strictly constant.

| Observation                                                  | What remains ambiguous                                                              | Useful next check                                                                                                      |
|:-------------------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Lower entropy and repeated actions on unsolved tasks         | Narrower exploration, changed decoding constraints, or a changed task mix           | Compare action and candidate diversity on fixed diagnostic fixtures under the same sampling configuration              |
| More equal-reward groups                                     | Mastered tasks, persistently unsuccessful tasks, or missing assessment distinctions | Separate all-pass, all-fail, and unresolved cases; inspect coverage and reward components                              |
| More generated tokens without higher acceptance              | Necessary checking, unproductive repetition, or a population/termination change     | Compare lengths by outcome and task family, including budget stops and unavailable verdicts                            |
| Higher training reward without better independent assessment | Useful adaptation, reward-specific shortcuts, or changed grading                    | Audit candidate artifacts and reward components, then test the identified configuration under the independent protocol |

Table 8: **Training observations and diagnostic checks**: A metric narrows an investigation without proving an internal mechanism or prescribing a guaranteed remedy.

### Output Length

^sec-vol3-rlvr-output-length

An unchanged average reward often conceals an expensive failure migration, where cheap syntax failures early in training turn into prolonged, compute-intensive execution loops that still fail verification. Conversely, a verbose trajectory may reflect thorough exploratory testing that culminates in a working patch, whereas a terse rollout may indicate early abandonment. Because generated token counts, tool invocation counts, and wall-clock execution time capture distinct execution costs across those cases, tracking mean trajectory length alone is rarely sufficient; engineers should inspect full length distributions segmented by outcome (accepted, failed, pending, and unknown).

The length bias of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^sec-vol3-rlvr-grpo-length-normalization|section 17.5.3]] is the first mechanism to audit when failing trajectories lengthen unexpectedly, because per-rollout averaging charges each token of a long failure less than each token of a short one. Training the same fixed diagnostic tasks under the per-rollout and the constant-denominator objectives separates growth caused by that incentive from growth that reflects useful work, such as additional diagnostic checks that the environment reward favors.

When system requirements favor concise solutions, any length penalty introduced into the reward function must respect the preference hierarchy established in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.04 Verifiable Rewards#^sec-vol3-rlvr-rewards|section 17.3]]. Under bounded reward structures, enforcing ${\lambda_{\text{fmt}}}+{\lambda_{\text{len}}}<1$ guarantees that any passing trajectory scores higher than any failing trajectory, regardless of length. A poorly calibrated length penalty that causes a verbose successful repair to score lower than an immediate failure corrupts task incentives. Reward-based length penalties influence policy preferences only during learning; hard execution budgets (such as maximum token limits or turn timeouts) must remain strictly enforced by the execution runtime.

### Entropy Collapse and Regularization

^sec-vol3-rlvr-entropy-collapse

Standard symmetric ratio clipping provides a direct algorithmic route to **entropy collapse**, the premature loss of action diversity on tasks the agent has not yet solved. When an agent discovers a sequence that partially succeeds early in training, the positive advantage boosts the probabilities of those tokens, rapidly sharpening the distribution around that single path. The positive-advantage surrogate in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^sec-vol3-rlvr-grpo-clipped-objective|section 17.5.2]] stops increasing at a policy ratio of $1+{\epsilon_{\text{clip}}}$, but this incentive threshold acts with extreme asymmetry in absolute probability space.

With ${\epsilon_{\text{clip}}}$ set to 0.2, a dominant token already at probability 0.9 has an incentive threshold of 1.08. Because this threshold exceeds 1.0, the surrogate continues rewarding increases throughout the remaining probability mass. In contrast, for an alternative candidate token at probability 0.01, the positive surrogate incentive flattens after an absolute probability increase of 0.002. Gradients from other tokens and loss terms can still move a token’s probability ratio beyond the clipping interval. Rarely sampled actions therefore face a steep climb to regain probability, while dominant actions consolidate their lead. As action diversity drops across unsolved tasks, the ${G}$ rollouts in each group collapse into near-identical trajectories. Their rewards tie, precipitating the zero-contrast state described in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^sec-vol3-rlvr-grpo-zero-variance|section 17.5.4]] where the reward-surrogate gradient vanishes.

To counteract this dynamic, the **clip-higher** formulation decouples the clipping boundaries into an asymmetric interval $[1-{\epsilon_{\text{low}}}, 1+{\epsilon_{\text{high}}}]$ with ${\epsilon_{\text{high}}} > {\epsilon_{\text{low}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.16 Source References#^ref-yu2025dapo|Yu et al. 2025]]). This wider upper bound allows low-probability tokens backed by positive advantages to climb more rapidly, while the lower bound still curbs policy drift for penalized tokens. As always, diagnostic context matters: low entropy at deterministic syntax positions is normal and desirable; the operational danger is collapsing diversity among tool invocations and decision branches on unsolved tasks.

Alternative regularizers attack this problem through different mechanisms. An explicit entropy bonus directly penalizes sharp token distributions across targeted positions. A reference-policy divergence penalty, such as the ${\beta_{\text{KL}}}$-weighted term of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.06 Group Relative Policy Optimization#^eq-vol3-grpo-objective|equation 4]], instead penalizes drift from the initial base model, though it enforces no minimum entropy floor of its own. Both approaches modify the loss surface and require controlled experimentation: hold task distributions and sampling parameters fixed, evaluate on downstream task success and token efficiency, and track policy-ratio distributions. A smoother training loss is weak evidence of healthy exploration; only verified task completion counts.

When exploration collapses, an agent can enter repetitive loops (emitting duplicate shell commands or re-reading unchanged files) where runtime watchdog mechanisms must intervene. However, repetitive behavior in multi-turn environments is inherently ambiguous: waiting for a slow build, polling an asynchronous API, or retrying a failed test after adjusting parameters can mimic an infinite loop. Systems should log the exact termination trigger and apply the stop-state rules of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.07 Multi-Turn Agentic RL#^sec-vol3-rlvr-stopping-censored|section 17.6.3]], which score budget exhaustion as a task failure and keep infrastructure interruptions out of the reward. Terminating a runaway rollout caps resource consumption immediately, but it settles nothing outside the slot; environment state and external side effects still pass to their cleanup handlers.

↩︎ Runtime watchdog mechanisms for detecting and terminating repetitive agent loops are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/12 Failure Recovery/V3-12.07 Semantic Watchdogs#^sec-vol3-sagas-watchdogs|Semantic Watchdogs]]**.

### Recovery Behavior

^sec-vol3-rlvr-deliberation

Reports such as the 2025 DeepSeek-R1 study document spontaneous increases in response length during pure reinforcement learning (R1-Zero), accompanied by natural-language tokens where the model appears to reconsider prior reasoning steps ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/17 Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards/V3-17.16 Source References#^ref-deepseekai2025deepseekr1|DeepSeek-AI et al. 2025]]). From a systems perspective, however, introspective tokens such as “Wait, let me double-check” are merely generated text, not verified cognitive state. In an agentic system interacting with real infrastructure, the sole criterion for genuine recovery behavior is whether subsequent tool invocations successfully correct an execution failure or adapt to unexpected environmental feedback.

Consider testing recovery on the connection-pool repair task. A diagnostic evaluation fixture can intentionally inject an environmental disturbance, for example a dependency version conflict introduced immediately after the agent completes its initial inspection. The evaluation then measures whether the policy gathers fresh diagnostic data, revises its patch to satisfy the conflicting constraints, and successfully passes the test suite within the original resource allowance. An evaluation must track failed and unhandled executions alongside successful runs, so that apparent recoveries do not reflect hidden timeout artifacts. Designing experiments around controlled perturbations provides empirical evidence of recovery under the tested perturbation, just as targeted recovery demonstrations mitigate compounding errors during imitation learning.

↩︎ Recovery trajectories as a remedy for compounding errors in imitation learning are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.07 Exposure Bias#^sec-vol3-fine-tuning-exposure-bias|Exposure Bias]]**.

Matched-budget evaluations, in which candidate policies and baselines operate under identical token and turn limits, reliably separate productive deliberation from empty verbosity. Generating this diagnostic evidence at scale demands robust infrastructure: a versioned catalog of task fixtures, structured rollout traces with explicit execution states, and sufficient isolated grader capacity. These diagnostic demands directly link algorithmic health to the systems architecture of the rollout pipeline.
