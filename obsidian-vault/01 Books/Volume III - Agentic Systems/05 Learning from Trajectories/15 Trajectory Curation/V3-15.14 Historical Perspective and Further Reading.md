# V3-15.14 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15 Trajectory Curation|V3-15 Trajectory Curation]]

^sec-vol3-trajectory-curation-history

The principles governing trajectory curation adapt lessons from data mining, information retrieval, and relational databases. Data mining first identified data leakage as a systemic failure mode where information about an evaluation target inadvertently reaches the learner through correlated records ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-kaufman2012leakage|Kaufman et al. 2012]]). In software modeling, duplicated source files were shown to inflate benchmark accuracy artificially until evaluation splits underwent thorough deduplication ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-allamanis2019adverse|Allamanis 2019]]). Modern screening pipelines borrow directly from web search, where minwise hashing estimated document resemblance from compact signatures ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-broder1997resemblance|Broder 1997]]) and locality-sensitive hashing reduced high-dimensional similarity search to sublinear time ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-indyk1998approximate|Indyk and Motwani 1998]]). Database query processing supplied the twin mechanisms of admission control and data provenance. Optimizers established how to order expensive filter predicates by their cost-to-selectivity rank to minimize wasted computation ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-hellerstein1993predicate|Hellerstein and Stonebraker 1993]]), while provenance frameworks formalized which source records produced a derived value and why ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-buneman2001why|Buneman et al. 2001]]).

Learning from self-generated execution traces shares an equally deep lineage in reinforcement learning and symbolic reasoning. Classical imitation learning demonstrated that a policy trained exclusively on expert demonstrations compounds its errors when deployed, because small mistakes push the agent into unfamiliar states where it cannot recover ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|Ross et al. 2011]]). Ross, Gordon, and Bagnell showed that querying an expert on the learner’s own visited states restores linear error bounds; recovery demonstrations are the systems realization of that theoretical remedy. Expert iteration formalized this dynamic into an alternating loop of generation, search-based selection, and retraining, using tree search to discover improved decisions for a neural network to imitate ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-anthony2017thinking|Anthony et al. 2017]]). Bootstrapped reasoning extended this loop to language models by sampling reasoning paths and filtering for traces that reached correct answers ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-zelikman2022star|Zelikman et al. 2022]]). Process supervision then tightened verification from coarse terminal outcomes down to individual reasoning steps ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-uesato2022solving|Uesato et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-lightman2023let|Lightman et al. 2024]]).

Every self-training method eventually meets the same problem, because the filter that selects training data is itself an imperfect part of the system it is meant to improve. Production machine learning pipelines first exposed the systemic risks of hidden feedback loops, where an operational model’s outputs silently shape its own subsequent training distribution ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-sculley2015hidden|Sculley et al. 2015]]). Reward modeling demonstrated Goodhart’s law directly: excessive optimization against a learned proxy drives reward scores up while true task quality deteriorates ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-gao2023scaling|Gao et al. 2023]]). Training repeatedly on a model’s own unchecked outputs speeds model collapse, in which diversity shrinks with each round and the rare cases in the tail disappear first ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-shumailov2024|Shumailov et al. 2024]]). Agentic execution sharpens each of these failure modes because trajectories do not exist in isolation. They execute stateful tool calls, operate under specific environment permissions, and can inadvertently manipulate or game their own evaluation harnesses. The safeguards of this chapter, from verifier cascades and disjoint task-group splits to lineage envelopes and dataset manifests ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-gebru2021|Gebru et al. 2021]]), apply classical systems isolation and provenance guarantees directly to the trajectory as the atomic unit of machine learning data.

For further reading, start with these:

- **End-to-end arguments** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-saltzer1984endtoend|Saltzer et al. 1984]]): The classic statement of why a function belongs where it can be implemented completely and correctly. In trajectory curation, this principle argues for repairing tool interfaces and runtime environments directly rather than training model weights to work around platform flaws.
- **Predicate migration** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-hellerstein1993predicate|Hellerstein and Stonebraker 1993]]): The foundational database paper on ordering expensive filter checks by cost per rejection, formalizing the exchange argument used to structure multi-stage verifier cascades.
- **Imitation learning as no-regret online learning** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-ross2011reduction|Ross et al. 2011]]): The analysis demonstrating why training exclusively on clean expert prefixes leaves an agent unprepared for its own mistakes, establishing the theoretical rationale for recovery demonstrations.
- **Hidden technical debt in machine learning systems** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-sculley2015hidden|Sculley et al. 2015]]): A comprehensive systems catalog of data dependencies and feedback loops that any self-improving corpus pipeline inherits.
- **Datasheets for datasets** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-gebru2021|Gebru et al. 2021]]): The documentation discipline that a corpus manifest adapts into versioned, machine-auditable metadata fields.
- **Reward-model overoptimization** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-gao2023scaling|Gao et al. 2023]]): A controlled empirical study showing how aggressive optimization against a learned proxy reward drives proxy scores up while true task quality degrades.
