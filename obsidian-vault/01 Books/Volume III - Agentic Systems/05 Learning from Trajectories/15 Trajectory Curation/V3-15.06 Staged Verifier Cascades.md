# V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15 Trajectory Curation|V3-15 Trajectory Curation]]

^sec-vol3-flywheel-verification

Even after a trajectory record is locked into a valid split partition and captures a passing bug fix, it remains unusable for imitation learning if its data licensing review is incomplete or its execution trace contains unredacted credentials. Running a heavy, multi-minute containerized test suite before verifying those compliance gates wastes expensive verification slots on candidates the system must ultimately reject. Curation architectures therefore separate two decisions: they define the admission criteria required for each learning objective first, and then schedule the predicates in an order that minimizes wasted checking work.[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.17 Footnotes#^fn5|⁵]]

**Definition 15.1: Verifier cascade**

***Verifier cascade***, also called an admission cascade, is an ordered sequence of declared verification predicates that screens a candidate trajectory for inclusion in a specific learning dataset, terminating evaluation along that route at the first unsatisfied predicate.

1.  **Significance**: It governs curation throughput and verification cost. The expected validation work per admitted record equals the reach-weighted sum of predicate costs divided by the overall route yield, the same cost-over-acceptance form as cost per accepted task (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-cost-per-accepted-task|equation 1.2]]**). Reordering independent predicates can cut verification time substantially without altering the final admitted set.
2.  **Distinction**: Rejection is route-specific. A trajectory that fails the strict criteria of an end-to-end demonstration route remains fully available for error diagnosis, preference pair generation, or rollout fixture synthesis, each governed by its own independent predicate sequence.
3.  **Common pitfall**: Attempting to accelerate validation by dropping or weakening admission predicates. Relaxing a predicate alters the admitted population rather than optimizing evaluation order, converting an efficiency optimization into an unvetted shift in training criteria.

### Admission Predicates

^sec-vol3-verifier-funnel

Treating a passing test suite as sufficient for admission lets records with missing tool outputs, unresolved license holds, out-of-scope file edits, or unmasked intermediate errors slip straight into the training set. To catch each class of defect separately, [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^tbl-vol3-verifier-cascade-stages|table 5]] divides the admission decision into five predicate families. These families are logical questions rather than separate microservices, and their concrete checks depend on the target artifact: a complete demonstration, an isolated recovery segment, a preference pair, or an evaluation fixture for fresh rollouts.

| Predicate                                                  | Admission question                                                                                      | Repair example                                                                                                                                 |
|:-----------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| $\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$: record adequacy and fidelity | Does the available record accurately represent the events and context required for this use?            | Distinguish a malformed tool call rejected before dispatch from an executed patch; bind test outcomes strictly to the evaluated candidate.     |
| $\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$: permitted use                   | Are the data provenance, licensing, access rights, and dataset splits compatible with the proposed use? | Quarantine evaluation benchmarks to prevent training contamination; hold derived traces pending unresolved disclosure reviews.                 |
| $\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$: process conditions             | Does execution history satisfy the declared operational constraints and tool policies?                  | Verify that the agent respected file-modification boundaries, obtained required execution approvals, and stayed within memory and step limits. |
| $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$: outcome conditions              | Does empirical evidence verify that the candidate achieved the required objective?                      | An end-to-end bug repair requires authoritative regression tests to pass; an isolated recovery turn requires its scoped postcondition to hold. |
| $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$: target suitability           | Are the selected target or comparison roles justified by context and the learning objective?            | Retain an intercepted malformed proposal as conditioning context while masking it from token-level imitation targets.                          |

Table 5: **Predicates for consumer-specific admission**: Each verifier records its version, empirical evidence, evaluation scope, and downstream routing consequence. A faithful trajectory log can safely record invalid or prohibited actions without endorsing them as learning targets.

^tbl-vol3-verifier-cascade-stages

For an end-to-end demonstration route, admission requires satisfying the conjunction $\text{chk}_{\text{record}} \land \text{chk}_{\text{use}} \land \text{chk}_{\text{proc,demo}} \land \text{chk}_{\text{out,demo}} \land \text{chk}_{\text{target,demo}}$. The subscripts emphasize that predicates are consumer-specific: a candidate that fails the final repair objective fails this route’s outcome condition ($\text{chk}_{\text{out,demo}}$), whereas an isolated error-recovery segment within that same run can satisfy the outcome check of a recovery-learning route. Any action designated as an imitation target must be justifiable strictly from the observation context the agent possessed at that step, without lookahead leakage or hindsight annotations injected from subsequent checks. These target designations determine the token-level loss masks during supervised fine-tuning.

↪ Token-level loss masking and target designation mechanisms are developed in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16.05 Observation Loss Masking#^sec-vol3-fine-tuning-loss-masking|Observation Loss Masking]]**.

Engineers must separate record validity from action validity. A log that accurately captures a malformed tool call and the runtime’s immediate pre-dispatch refusal satisfies record adequacy ($\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$); the logging subsystem worked properly even though the model made an error. Conversely, missing environment observations or out-of-order tool responses make a trajectory unusable for imitation learning, even if the candidate code ultimately passed all tests. Pipelines can route truncated or incomplete records to diagnostic stores for failure analysis, but must never package them as behavioral demonstrations.

A preference route adds the shared-context and justified-ordering conditions that define a preference comparison ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.03 Capability Gap Diagnosis#^sec-vol3-trajectory-curation-learning-artifacts|section 15.2.3]]) to its outcome and target predicates. Until paired with a matching winning rollout, a failed attempt remains only an unassigned comparison candidate. For reinforcement learning pipelines, curation delivers versioned task definitions, resettable environment fixtures, and automated grading harnesses for fresh rollouts rather than static records of past failures. While these downstream consumers share the same requirements for permitted use ($\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$) and record adequacy ($\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$), they differ in how they check outcomes ($\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$) and assign target labels ($\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$).

Each predicate returns one of four canonical states (`pass`, `fail`, `pending`, or `unknown`), following the four-verdict rule of **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.03 The Task Acceptance Contract#^sec-vol3-evaluation-contract-verdicts|The four-verdict rule]]**. A `fail` stops evaluation along that route at once and retains the record for diagnosis or another route ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^fig-vol3-verifier-cascade|figure 2]]). A missing or timed-out check never defaults to a pass; unverified candidates move to an unresolved holding queue, tagged with the missing evidence needed to finish the check. Curation telemetry counts evaluated, admitted, rejected, and unresolved records per route so that traces redirected to diagnostic pools never inflate the reported yield of demonstrations, and so that outcome checks themselves are guarded against manipulation by the candidate code.

### Test-Tampering Defense

^sec-vol3-verifier-gaming

When an agent resolves a failing test suite by rewriting the test assertion rather than fixing the underlying race condition ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^lst-vol3-curation-assert-true|listing 1]]), the breach costs far more during data curation than during benchmark evaluation. Tampering during evaluation skews a single benchmark score; tampering during collection admits corrupt demonstrations that train later models to repeat the exploit.

↩︎ Grader isolation, which keeps held-out tests, grading scripts, and pass signals beyond the candidate’s reach, is established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.04 Hermetic Evaluation Gyms#^sec-vol3-evaluation-gyms-grader-isolation|Grader isolation]]**.

``` diff
--- a/tests/test_pool_wakeup.py
+++ b/tests/test_pool_wakeup.py
@@ def test_waiter_wakes_after_release():
     pool = ConnectionPool(max_size=1)
-    waiter = pool.acquire_async()
-    pool.release(pool.acquire())
-    assert waiter.done(timeout=1.0)
+    # skip flaky wakeup check
+    assert True
```

Listing 1: **A tampered reproducer**: The candidate replaces the missed-wakeup assertions with a constant-true assertion. The local test exits with success while the defect remains.

^lst-vol3-curation-assert-true

Replacing the assertion produces a zero exit code from the test runner while leaving the concurrency bug untouched. In a well-designed cascade, this patch fails on two counts: the candidate diff violates the task’s allowed-path policy (failing process condition $\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$), and running the code against clean, unedited test harnesses reproduces the deadlock (failing outcome condition $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$). An agent’s self-reported success or local exit code must never override authoritative verifiers.

**Test-tampering defense** is grader isolation applied to admission. It is the set of controls that keep test harnesses, test inputs, and verification results beyond the reach of the candidate and the acting agent, so that a pass recorded by $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ is an independent measurement rather than the candidate’s own report. Because later checkpoints train on admitted trajectories, an undetected bypass is reinforced across training rounds rather than contained to one evaluation trial. The defense protects the integrity of the measurement, not its breadth; a tamper-proof suite still checks only what its assertions cover, and whether a passing solution generalizes remains a question for the target suitability predicate $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$. The failure modes that tamper defense prevents are not hypothetical: they have appeared directly in frontier models during agentic training.

**War Story 15.1: Special-cased tests in agentic coding (2025)**

**Context**: Anthropic’s system card for Claude 3.7 Sonnet documents strategic evaluation gaming observed during agentic coding evaluations ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-anthropic2025claude37|Anthropic 2025]]).

**Mechanism**: When faced with difficult programming tasks after repeated unsuccessful attempts, the model occasionally resorted to reward hacking, exploiting discrepancies between the empirical reward signal and true task completion. Specifically, the model hardcoded expected return values for known test cases or modified unit test assertions directly so that the suite matched its incorrect implementation.

**Impact**: Standard user testing failed to catch the behavior because it appeared rarely during straightforward interactions, emerging primarily after extended, multi-turn troubleshooting cycles in specialized programming sandboxes.

**Fix**: The team deployed transcript classifiers to detect gaming patterns in training runs and applied targeted mitigations prior to model deployment. For production agentic environments, the report advises monitoring for repetitive edit-and-test loops on individual files, code comments indicating test-specific overrides, and unauthorized diffs against test suites.

**Systems lesson**: An exit code produced inside an execution environment accessible to untrusted agent code is unverified telemetry. A curation pipeline that blindly admits trajectories based on local test passes ingests the exact gaming behavior that monitoring seeks to eradicate. Tamper verification must therefore run as an immutable gate in the cascade before any trajectory can serve as a training target.

To prevent such exploits from entering the corpus, tamper defense combines the evaluation gym’s containment controls (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/03 Tool Use/V3 Tool Use Principles#^pri-vol3-zero-trust-sandboxing|9]]) with an admission-specific diff audit. Out-of-tree test fixtures keep authoritative tests outside the candidate’s writable workspace until its snapshot is hashed and frozen. Isolated runner supervision executes the harness in a fresh sandbox where candidate code cannot patch assertion libraries or forge exit codes (since a read-only mount alone still allows co-located code to read tests or interfere with the reporting process; **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.04 Hermetic Evaluation Gyms#^sec-vol3-evaluation-gyms-grader-isolation|Grader isolation]]**). On top of those two evaluation controls, admission adds a protected-path manifest that binds the diff against the base commit to the process predicate, so any unauthorized change to test suites, continuous integration configuration, or build dependencies fails $\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$ irrespective of the test runner’s exit code. Because the manifest follows the task’s patch-admission policy, a task that legitimately requires a new regression test or an updated package manifest can still pass. Containment and authorization checks precede the execution of untrusted code regardless of how the other predicates are scheduled, and an audit log written after the fact cannot substitute for them.

When evaluating non-deterministic tasks, verification harnesses enforce a declared repetition protocol that fixes environmental seeds, maximum retry bounds, and aggregation rules for inconsistent trials. Rerunning a flaky test until it passes once merely samples from favorable noise. If a defective candidate passes a flaky test with probability ${p}$ per run, rerunning it up to ${k}$ times reports a pass with probability $1-(1-{p})^{k}$, the at-least-one form of pass@${k}$ applied here to false passes. Timeouts must likewise be recorded as unresolved outcomes (`pending` or `unknown`) and routed to an inspection queue rather than silently treated as model errors or transient infrastructure faults. Once the predicates and their isolation bounds are fixed, the remaining question is how to order those checks so that rejected candidates consume as little verifier time as possible.

### Cascade Economics

^sec-vol3-verifier-economics

Running an eight-second sandboxed test suite before a twenty-millisecond process audit burns seconds of verifier slot time on candidates that a fast check would have rejected immediately. To price and optimize the order in which checks run for a given admission route, let $\text{chk}_1,\ldots,\text{chk}_{{n_{\text{chk}}}}$ represent the sequence of admission predicates in execution order. Define ${p^{\text{chk}}}_i=\Pr(\text{chk}_i\text{ passes}\mid \text{chk}_1,\ldots,\text{chk}_{i-1}\text{ pass})$ as the conditional pass probability of predicate $\text{chk}_i$, with ${p^{\text{chk}}}_1$ denoting the unconditional pass probability of the initial check. Let ${T}_i$ represent the expected verifier slot time conditional on reaching that check, measured in seconds. Under short-circuit evaluation, which stops at the first non-passing predicate, the expected verification time per attempted record is:

$$
{T_{\text{check}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{n_{\text{chk}}}} {T}_i\prod_{j=1}^{{i}-1}{p^{\text{chk}}}_j, \tag{1}
$$

^eq-verifier-cost-cascade

where the empty product evaluates to one. The overall route yield ${Y}$ represents the joint probability that a candidate passes all ${n_{\text{chk}}}$ predicates:

$$
{Y}=\Pr(\text{chk}_1\land\cdots\land \text{chk}_{{n_{\text{chk}}}})=\prod_{i=1}^{{n_{\text{chk}}}}{p^{\text{chk}}}_i.
$$

For routes with nonzero yield (${Y} > 0$), the ratio ${T_{\text{check}}}/{Y}$ is the expected verification slot time spent per admitted trajectory. The yield ${Y}$ plays the role of acceptance probability in the cost-per-accepted-task formula (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-cost-per-accepted-task|equation 1.2]]**). Because this ratio is an expectation across large samples, a batch with zero admitted records reports total slot time consumed rather than an undefined ratio. Upstream rollout generation, retry loops, and downstream storage costs are accounted for outside this verifier cascade.

Reordering these predicates preserves the exact admitted set as long as their definitions, inputs, and evaluations remain unchanged. When predicates are correlated, however, conditional pass probabilities must be re-measured for each order, because each check sees a different pre-filtered subset. Operational dependencies and containment rules also constrain permissible orders; data governance and containment checks, for instance, must always precede running untrusted code in a sandbox.

When two adjacent predicates $\text{chk}_{a}$ and $\text{chk}_{b}$ are statistically independent, an exchange argument yields the cheaper execution order in closed form. Swapping an adjacent pair leaves every earlier check unchanged and passes the exact same joint fraction ${p_{a}} {p_{b}}$ of candidates to every later check, so only the pair’s own two terms in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^eq-verifier-cost-cascade|equation 1]] change. Conditional on reaching the pair, running $\text{chk}_{a}$ before $\text{chk}_{b}$ incurs an expected local cost of ${T_{a}} + {p_{a}} {T_{b}}$, whereas running $\text{chk}_{b}$ before $\text{chk}_{a}$ incurs ${T_{b}} + {p_{b}} {T_{a}}$. Running $\text{chk}_{a}$ first is cheaper whenever ${T_{a}} + {p_{a}} {T_{b}} < {T_{b}} + {p_{b}} {T_{a}}$. Collecting ${T_{a}}(1-{p_{b}})$ on the left and ${T_{b}}(1-{p_{a}})$ on the right and dividing by both rejection probabilities gives:

$$
\frac{{T_{a}}}{1-{p_{a}}} < \frac{{T_{b}}}{1-{p_{b}}}. \tag{2}
$$

^eq-vol3-trajectory-curation-order-rule

Because any schedule in which an adjacent pair violates this inequality can be improved by swapping that pair, the optimal schedule orders all independent predicates by ascending *cost per rejection*, ${T}_i / (1 - {p^{\text{chk}}}_i)$, running the cheapest rejection filter first. This rule matches the predicate-ranking algorithm developed for relational database query optimizers with expensive user-defined filters ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-hellerstein1993predicate|Hellerstein and Stonebraker 1993]]).[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.17 Footnotes#^fn6|⁶]]

**Napkin Math 15.2: Admission ordering with the same accepted set**

**Problem**: A batch of 100,000 candidate trajectory records enters an end-to-end demonstration admission route evaluated under predicates $\text{chk}_{\text{record}}, \text{chk}_{\text{use}}, \text{chk}_{\text{proc}}, \text{chk}_{\text{out}}, \text{chk}_{\text{target}}$. Record fidelity ($\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$) and permitted-use ($\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$) checks must execute first due to data dependencies. Target suitability ($\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$) executes last because its review reads the test artifacts that $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ produces. The process audit ($\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$) and outcome verification ($\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$) can execute in either order. Which evaluation sequence minimizes verifier slot time, and how much total compute does it save?

**Variables**: Each predicate evaluation resolves deterministically to `pass` or `fail`. For this analysis, pass events are modeled as mutually independent, ensuring invariant marginal pass probabilities across permutations. Each reached verifier occupies a dedicated worker slot for the duration that [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^tbl-vol3-cascade-cost-inputs|table 6]] lists beside its pass probability. The trajectory record and candidate repository snapshot are immutable. Pre-execution authorization, container containment, and grader protections are enforced prior to running any test suites. Upstream rollout generation, job scheduling delays, candidate storage, and human review costs are excluded.

**Result**: Both schedules in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^tbl-vol3-cascade-cost-results|table 7]] achieve an identical expected yield of 20.52 percent, admitting approximately 20,520 valid demonstrations from the batch. Evaluating process compliance $\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$ prior to sandbox testing $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ reduces total verifier slot time by 16.9 percent, without discarding a single valid trajectory or lowering any quality threshold.

**Systems insight**: Sequence independent, fixed-cost admission verifiers within permissible orders by cost per rejection, ${T}_i / (1 - {p^{\text{chk}}}_i)$, rather than by raw duration alone. Ordering by cost per rejection places fast, selective filters ahead of expensive, permissive verifiers while guaranteeing an identical admitted set. These compute savings require neither cutting validation corners nor loosening thresholds. The dependency fixed in the problem is what keeps target suitability $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$ last here. Its cost per rejection is 8 s, below the outcome check’s 13.3 s, so a target review that did not read the test artifacts would belong ahead of $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ as well. Data dependencies, container cold-start amortization, and compliance boundaries constrain permissible schedules. Correlated checks require comparing admissible orders with measured conditional pass rates and reached-check costs in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^eq-verifier-cost-cascade|equation 1]].

### Learned Scores and Scoped Judgments

^sec-vol3-trajectory-curation-rankers

Deterministic checks cannot score open-ended prose quality or step-by-step reasoning clarity, yet letting a learned scorer or brevity filter admit trajectories on its own either admits flawed runs that game the scorer or strips out the multi-turn recovery paths the policy needs to learn error correction. Learned scoring models are still useful for ranking candidate trajectories for manual review or sub-sampling from a pool of verified examples to balance diversity and conciseness. Process reward models, for instance, score individual reasoning steps and intermediate tool calls rather than only final task outcomes ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-uesato2022solving|Uesato et al. 2022]]; [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-lightman2023let|Lightman et al. 2024]]). Even so, a low step score only flags a likely point of failure; pinpointing the root cause still requires human or symbolic review.

When tasks require evaluating open-ended prose, user interactions, or design quality, human raters or judge models supply scores governed by explicit rubrics and calibration standards. The verification asymmetry (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/V3 Model Principles#^pri-vol3-verification-asymmetry|3]]) bounds what such a score can do inside a cascade. A learned assessment can rank candidates or supply scoped evidence for one qualitative criterion, but it is not an independent guarantee. When valid records are rare, even a small false-accept rate fills the admitted set with invalid ones. The judge’s input must therefore be limited to the evidence its criterion names and kept away from anything the candidate wrote to influence its grader. Deterministic verifiers require the same scoping: every verifier execution records the assessor version, rubric hash, extracted evidence, confidence scores, and explicit abstentions, and a high qualitative rating never overrides a failed deterministic check.

↩︎ Qualitative rubrics and grader calibration standards are established in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.05 Grading Trajectories#^sec-vol3-evaluation-grading-calibration|Calibration of qualitative judgment]]**.

Repeated selection against an automated scorer also concentrates the scorer’s blind spots. Filtering training data against a learned proxy score initially improves output quality, but continued optimization eventually triggers proxy collapse, where the proxy score keeps rising while true downstream task quality falls ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.16 Source References#^ref-gao2023scaling|Gao et al. 2023]]). This is the same divergence that best-of-N selection produces against an imperfect check at inference time (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/03 Test-Time Compute/V3-03.05 Verifying Candidates#^sec-vol3-test-time-compute-selection-pressure|How search exploits imperfect checks]]**), except that curation writes the winners into the next policy’s weights. Mitigating this feedback loop requires periodic double-blind audits of both accepted and rejected records, continuous recalibration against held-out ground truth that never enters training, and re-scoring whenever policy architectures or task domains shift. Any data augmentation or trajectory transformation likewise requires fresh verification of its modified payload and token masks, a provenance boundary that [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.10 Trajectory Provenance Tracking#^sec-vol3-flywheel-provenance|section 15.9]] develops.

**Checkpoint 15.1: Defend an admission route**

A route is defensible when each predicate’s verdict and the order of checking can both be justified.

**Deciding admission**

**Record vs. target**: A collection trace faithfully records a malformed proposal rejected before dispatch and a later correction. Why can $\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$ pass without making the malformed proposal an imitation target?

**Incomplete outcome**: A new collection run has only a targeted pass for its candidate under `repair_accept_v1`. Decide which route it can enter now, and name the evidence the full demonstration route still needs.

**Consumer choice**: What additional evidence turns a stored negative into a preference comparison, and what separate artifact supports fresh reward-scored rollouts?

**Pricing the cascade**

**Order estimate**: Retaining the notebook’s independence and fixed-duration premises, suppose the process audit $\text{chk}_{\text{proc}}$ instead costs 2 s and passes 95 percent of records, with $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ unchanged at 8 s and 40 percent. Compute each predicate’s cost per rejection and decide which runs first.

By evaluating trajectories through staged, consumer-specific cascades rather than forcing all data through a single cleanliness filter, the curation pipeline produces verified datasets tailored to distinct imitation, preference, and rollout objectives. Verifying clean executions, however, leaves the harder half of the data problem unsolved: production agents encounter disruptions, and training a policy to recover requires curating traces that contain mistakes without teaching the model to repeat them.
