# V3-15.15 Exercises

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15 Trajectory Curation|V3-15 Trajectory Curation]]

^sec-vol3-14-trajectory-curation-exercises-d5aa1f5a

Every given below is a hypothetical problem input chosen for round arithmetic, not a measurement of any system. Each problem asks for a calculation and the curation or collection decision it supports.

1.  **Sizing a protected split (Basic).** A collection job makes ${k}=5$ attempts on each of 2,000 repair tasks. A colleague proposes assigning individual traces to training with probability ${f_{\text{train}}}=0.9$ and to the final test otherwise.

    1.  Compute the fraction and number of tasks whose attempts straddle the partition, and the smallest ${k}$ at which a majority of tasks straddle.
    2.  Compute the probability that a given test attempt has at least one sibling attempt of the same task in training, and state what the resulting test score measures.
    3.  The team switches to a group split ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.05 Split Design#^sec-vol3-flywheel-evaluation|section 15.4]]). Treating tasks as independent trials with an acceptance rate near one half, compute how many task groups the final test must hold for the standard error of its task-level acceptance rate to be at most 0.025, and what fraction of the 2,000 tasks that reserves.

2.  **Ordering an admission route (Basic).** A demonstration route has three independent predicates beyond record adequacy: a permitted-use lookup $\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$ that takes 0.05 s and passes 70 percent of records, a sandboxed test run $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$ that takes 30 s and passes 50 percent, and a model-judged target review $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$ that takes 4 s and passes 80 percent. Target review does not need the test result, and the current order is $\text{chk}_{\text{use}}\to \text{chk}_{\text{out}}\to \text{chk}_{\text{target}}$.

    1.  Rank the predicates by cost per rejection ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^eq-vol3-trajectory-curation-order-rule|equation 2]]) and choose the order.
    2.  Compute ${T_{\text{check}}}$ per attempted record and per admitted record for the current and chosen orders ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^eq-verifier-cost-cascade|equation 1]]), and the fraction of verifier-slot work the change saves.
    3.  A colleague proposes dropping $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$ instead, because it lowers checking time per admitted record further. Compute that figure and the new yield, and explain why the comparison with part (b) is not a cost comparison at all.

3.  **Three denominators for one mixture (Basic).** A retained demonstration pool holds 20,000 direct-success records averaging 6,000 stored tokens and 1,000 selected target tokens, and 5,000 recovery records averaging 30,000 stored tokens and 2,500 selected target tokens.

    1.  Compute the recovery class’s share of records, stored tokens, and selected targets, and the context per target token for each class.
    2.  The team wants recovery to supply half of the selected targets. Holding total selected targets at the current value, compute the presentations each class needs, how many recovery records must repeat, and the change in processed tokens.
    3.  Holding processed tokens at the current value instead, compute the total selected targets at the same half-and-half mixture. State which budget this team should fix when training time is the scarce resource, and what the arithmetic cannot decide.

4.  **Spending a sampling budget on contrast (Intermediate).** A collector has 8,000 attempts for 1,000 repair fixtures in three difficulty bands: 400 fixtures with per-attempt pass rate ${p}=0.1$, 400 with ${p}=0.5$, and 200 with ${p}=0.9$. Attempts are independent, and each fixture receives ${k}=8$.

    1.  For each band and in total, compute the expected number of fixtures that yield at least one success and the expected number of mixed groups ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.08 The Self-Improvement Loop#^eq-vol3-trajectory-curation-informative|equation 3]]). Compute the attempts spent per mixed group.
    2.  A colleague proposes dropping the easy band and spending its 1,600 attempts on the hard band at ${k}=12$. Compute the new expected number of mixed groups and state what the imitation corpus loses.
    3.  Decide whether to adopt the proposal for a group-relative objective and for an imitation artifact, and explain why the easy band’s all-success groups are not wasted attempts.

5.  **Reviewing after selection (Intermediate).** A cohort makes ${k}=10$ independent attempts on each of 200 tasks. Each attempt succeeds with probability 0.2 and costs \$0.50 for generation, verification, and storage. Every successful attempt goes to human target review at \$6, which passes 75 percent of the successes it sees, independently. The selector emits at most one artifact per task, the first passing success in attempt order.

    1.  Compute the expected number of emitted artifacts, the cohort cost, and ${C_{\text{emit}}}$ ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.11 Case Study Curating the Repair Record#^eq-cost-usable|equation 5]]).
    2.  The team instead reviews a task’s successes in attempt order and stops at its first pass. Show that the set of emitting tasks is unchanged, and compute the expected reviews per task, the new cohort cost, and ${C_{\text{emit}}}$.
    3.  A second proposal halves ${k}$ to 5 with the stopping review in place. Compute ${C_{\text{emit}}}$ and the emitted count, and decide between the two proposals, naming what the cheaper unit price costs the corpus.

6.  **The research agent’s collector (Intermediate).** The research agent reads untrusted sources, so each collection record keeps the retrieved pages that its claims cite, and the permitted-use review of that retained content is expensive. A collector runs 32 accelerators at 40 accelerator-seconds per attempt, checks attempts on 16 verifier slots, and writes records of at most 1 GB to a store that sustains 500 MB/s (decimal units). The independent predicates take and pass as follows: record adequacy $\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$, 0.02 s and 98 percent; permitted use $\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$, 6 s and 60 percent; citation grounding $\text{chk}_{\text{out}}$, 3 s and 70 percent; target review $\text{chk}_{\text{target}}$, 2 s and 90 percent. No predicate depends on another’s result. Use the saturated planning model with sufficient end-to-end credits, no additional shared service bottleneck, and each write charged at its record ceiling; the calculated rate is not a measurement of actual collector throughput.

    1.  Order the predicates by [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.06 Staged Verifier Cascades#^eq-vol3-trajectory-curation-order-rule|equation 2]] and compute ${T_{\text{check}}}$ and the yield ${Y}$.
    2.  Evaluate [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.09 Collection Pipeline Architecture#^eq-vol3-trajectory-curation-throughput-bound|equation 4]], name the binding stage in this model, and compute its planned admitted records per hour. Compute the write bandwidth that would let generation bind, and the admitted rate then.
    3.  The binding stage differs from the repair collector of notebook [15.6](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/15_trajectory_curation/15_trajectory_curation.html#nbk-14-collection-bottleneck). Explain why, and evaluate a proposal to cut the record ceiling to 100 MB by truncating retrieved pages. Propose a design that lowers stored bytes without weakening $\text{chk}_{\text{record}}$ or $\text{chk}_{\text{use}}$.

7.  **Staging production feedback for the support agent (Advanced).** The support agent serves customers who are waiting, so its collector captures production sessions it cannot delay or pause. Sessions finish at ${\lambda_{\text{arr}}}=50$ per second and last 120 s, with capture running throughout. A policy-grounding judge assesses each captured record on ${N_{\text{workers}}}=40$ slots in a mean of 0.6 s. Records have a ceiling of 250 KB, and staging holds ${M_{\text{stage}}}=4$ GB and at most 10,000 outstanding records (decimal units). During an incident the judge’s endpoint degrades and every assessment runs to its 10 s timeout. The controller needs 20 s to act, and staging starts nearly empty.

    1.  Compute the nominal utilization, the degraded capacity, and the staging growth rate. Compute the time until the byte limit and until the record-count limit is reached, and state which binds.
    2.  Size the high watermark on the binding limit, counting records from sessions already capturing and growth during the controller’s delay.
    3.  Backpressure on the source is unavailable here, unlike in the repair collector. Compute the capture fraction that holds staging steady during the incident, and critique a proposal to keep capturing only the shortest sessions. State what the corpus manifest must record about the uncaptured sessions.

8.  **Designing a leakage screen within a review budget (Advanced).** A corpus of 100,000 records is screened with 128-component MinHash signatures before split assignment ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.05 Split Design#^sec-vol3-trajectory-dedup-minhash|section 15.4.3]]). Assume that 2,000 record pairs are true relatives with Jaccard similarity 0.8 and that every other pair has similarity 0.1. Reviewers can inspect 20,000 candidate pairs per screening pass.

    1.  For banding designs of 32 bands of 4 rows, 16 of 8, and 8 of 16, compute the expected false candidates among unrelated pairs and the recall of true relatives. Decide which designs fit the review budget.
    2.  Redesign the screen so that it keeps the recall of the 32-by-4 design and fits the budget, using the full signature. Justify the chosen threshold with the estimator’s standard error, and estimate the machine work it adds.
    3.  Name a relationship that no choice of bands will catch, and the mechanism that must catch it instead.

**What’s Next: From corpus membership to learning targets**

Translating an admitted recovery record into a gradient update requires reinforcing the correction rather than the refused proposal it conditions on. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning/V3-16 Trajectory Fine-Tuning#^sec-vol3-fine-tuning|Trajectory Fine-Tuning]]** opens with the record this chapter published to `repair_demonstrations_v1`, whose targets `recovery_targets_v1` already fixed. It first asks which parts of the repair behavior belong in weights at all, then turns the curation contract into training examples. Serialization must reconstruct each prefix exactly as the agent received it, loss masking must assign direct supervised loss terms to the selected targets alone, and batching must keep independent examples from reading one another. Preference learning applies a different objective to justified comparisons. Curation decides which material is eligible and why, and the next chapter decides how that material shapes an update and whether the changed policy earns a release under the same runtime controls.
