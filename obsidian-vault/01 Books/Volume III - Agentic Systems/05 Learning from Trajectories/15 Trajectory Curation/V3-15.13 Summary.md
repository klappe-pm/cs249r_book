# V3-15.13 Summary

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15 Trajectory Curation|V3-15 Trajectory Curation]]

^sec-vol3-trajectory-curation-summary

A trajectory record and a training example answer different questions. The record says what happened under stated conditions. The example asserts that some part of what happened is worth reproducing, for a named learner, and will be judged against evidence that learner never saw. Every mechanism in the chapter keeps those two apart. Probes keep system defects out of the weights. Task-group splits keep evaluation evidence out of selection. Target selection keeps a faithfully recorded mistake in context and out of the loss. Lineage keeps a transformed record from inheriting a decision made about its source.

Collection credits, lineage envelopes, and the corpus manifest make those decisions operational. The price of the result follows the book’s cost-per-accepted-task form twice over. Checking work per admitted record is cascade cost divided by yield, and cost per emitted artifact is attempt cost divided by emission yield, so yield is the lever in both. Self-improvement changes the source distribution with every round, which is why the manifest keeps demonstrations, comparisons, and rollout fixtures distinct and why external assessment stays outside the loop. The quantitative decisions of the chapter rest on the relations collected in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15.13 Summary#^tbl-vol3-14-trajectory-curation-key-equations-211c0e9e|table 16]].

**Key Takeaways: Give every learning artifact a declared use**

- **Weights are the last remedy, not the first**: Counterfactual probes place many failures in context, schemas, or the runtime, where a reversible repair removes them for every model version. Training earns only the residual that survives, and demonstrations, preference comparisons, and rollout fixtures each carry their own admission conditions.
- **A split protects a claim, not a file**: With several attempts per task, a random split over traces puts most tasks on both sides, so the test score rewards recall of solved siblings. Assign whole task groups, carry the assignment into every derived record, and fix it before collection begins.
- **Cheaper checking must admit the same corpus**: Ordering independent, fixed-cost predicates within permissible orders by cost per rejection, ${T}_i/(1-{p^{\text{chk}}}_i)$, cuts verification work at an unchanged accepted set. Dropping or loosening a predicate buys a different corpus, so its lower unit price is not a saving.
- **A recorded mistake is context, not a target**: Recovery records keep the error and its refusal as conditioning history and select only the assessed correction as supervision. Mixtures are specified in selected targets and budgeted in stored tokens, because context per target differs by class.
- **Self-selected data narrows what it rewards**: When the policy supplies its own records, rising acceptance can hide the recoveries the selector rarely keeps. Distinct action sequences per fixture, pass@1 against pass@${k}$, and an external anchor show whether the loop is still learning or only repeating itself.
- **Staging is sized for the burst, not the average**: A verifier timeout burst, not average utilization, exhausts reserved staging, so each run holds a credit at the record ceiling until durable disposition. The cheapest stage can bind the illustrated planning rate while generation divided by yield sets its active-resource price.
- **The manifest is the contract the trainer reads**: Records and derived artifacts bind to source versions, checks, transformations, permissions, split membership, and intended consumers. Integrity proves unmodified bytes, not truth or permission, so every derived record earns its own admission decision.

^callout-takeaways--15-2-ad5fa39a

Verified trajectory post-training (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/V3 Trajectory Learning Principles#^pri-vol3-trajectory-post-training|13]]) now has a concrete decision boundary: evidence supports a specified learning use, and the corpus manifest carries that decision through selection and transformation so the trainer can tell context from targets. The trained policy still faces evaluation and still runs under the runtime’s authority and resource controls.
