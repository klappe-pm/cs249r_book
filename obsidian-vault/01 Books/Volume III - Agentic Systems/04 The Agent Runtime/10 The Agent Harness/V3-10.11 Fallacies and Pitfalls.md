# V3-10.11 Fallacies and Pitfalls

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10 The Agent Harness|V3-10 The Agent Harness]]

^sec-vol3-agent-harness-fallacies

Architectural failures in agent systems rarely stem from exotic distributed bugs; they occur when engineers mistake a convenient implementation mechanism or an agreeable language model for an authoritative supervisory guarantee. The harness must enforce safety, resource bounds, and lifecycle invariants externally, regardless of how cooperative the model appears.

**Fallacy**: *Using asynchronous coroutines is sufficient to build a harness.*

Asynchronous coroutines and non-blocking I/O primitives yield worker threads during network calls, but cooperative concurrency is not a supervisory control plane. A coroutine lacks runtime-owned control state and guarded admission control. When a harness relies solely on naive coroutine scheduling and polls for operator signals only after a complete model turn returns, it surrenders control during inference. For an allowance of 4,096 tokens, an operator issuing a pause command is left waiting roughly 86.3 s, even at the queue-free reference generation rates analyzed in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.06 Control Actions#^sec-vol3-controlplane-signals|section 10.5]]. A harness keeps control state in an authoritative record that a pause updates at once, and it applies streaming cancellation: it stops waiting on the reply immediately and asks the serving engine to stop generating. The stop takes effect locally without waiting for the allowance to drain, while the call’s reservation stays outstanding until the service reports how many tokens it actually generated.

**Pitfall**: *Checking only measured spend before dispatch.*

Evaluating admission against settled accounting balances ignores both in-flight liabilities and terminal cleanup obligations. In notebook [10.2](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/10_agent_harness/10_agent_harness.html#nbk-10-bounded-liability-admission), the settled bill shows \$0.92 of remaining headroom, making a new \$0.21 call appear affordable. However, once the harness factors in the unsettled test reservation alongside the mandatory finalization reserve, the true available headroom shrinks to \$0.12. Admitting that call overcommits the budget and leaves downstream finalization checks \$0.09 short of their required funds. To prevent budget overruns, the runtime must enforce bounded-liability admission before dispatch under [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.10 Budgets and Ceilings#^eq-vol3-bounded-liability-admission|equation 1]], reserving worst-case consumption upfront, settling against authoritative usage records upon completion, and ring-fencing funds for clean termination. Presenting the remaining balance to the model can assist its internal planning, but runtime admission control alone must provide the hard guarantee.

**Fallacy**: *A parseable reply is dispatchable after cancellation.*

When an invocation is canceled mid-stream due to a timeout or an operator signal, the incoming buffer may still contain syntactically valid fragments, such as a complete tool call formatted in JSON. Syntactic validity is merely a serialization property that confers neither execution authority nor semantic completeness. Under the quarantining rule, which applies invariant closure (principle [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.06 Invariant Closure#^pri-invariant-closure|1]]) to the reply status, incomplete or aborted outputs must be isolated as diagnostic telemetry rather than forwarded to tool executors. Any subsequent action must originate from a fresh turn that passes the harness’s standard status and admission checks.

**Pitfall**: *Treating a pause, timeout, or terminal state as settlement.*

Stopping a wait after a timeout is not equivalent to settling the underlying resource obligation. Releasing a test job’s reservation upon a local timeout returns \$0.50, which is 14.3 percent of the total trajectory allowance in notebook [10.2](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/10_agent_harness/10_agent_harness.html#nbk-10-bounded-liability-admission), to the uncommitted pool. If subsequent turns claim that capacity before the remote job finishes and reports its actual bill, the trajectory overshoots its budget ceiling by up to the reallocated amount. A correct settlement rule separates observation eligibility from settlement. When an operation times out, the harness revokes the agent’s eligibility to observe the late output, keeps its charges open in the operation table, and routes authenticated late evidence to the settlement owner rather than injecting it into an obsolete model turn.

**Fallacy**: *An instruction in the prompt is a runtime control.*

Natural-language prompt instructions communicate semantic intent to the model, but they cannot enforce runtime security boundaries. A model that honors a freeze or budget constraint with probability 99 percent on any single turn sustains that compliance across a 30-turn trajectory only 74 percent of the time (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-horizon-compounding|equation 1.1]]**). Consequently, 26 percent of executed trajectories violate the constraint at least once. The production outage in war story [10.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/10_agent_harness/10_agent_harness.html#ws-10-replit-code-freeze-2025) shows the missing piece: the freeze existed only as prompt text, and nothing at the dispatch boundary enforced it. The war story does not report a turn count or a compliance rate, so it cannot show the compounding itself; the arithmetic shows why a freeze held that way fails in a predictable share of long runs. An authoritative policy guard must sit at the dispatch boundary, evaluating the proposed action deterministically on every turn regardless of what the prompt requested.
