# V3-10.14 Exercises

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10 The Agent Harness|V3-10 The Agent Harness]]

^sec-vol3-agent-harness-exercises

Every given below is a hypothetical problem input, not a measurement of a particular model, provider, or service. Prices are in dollars per million tokens unless stated otherwise. Each problem asks for a number and then for the decision the number supports.

1.  **Where a pause lands (Basic).** A repair harness checks `pending_control` only when a model reply ends. Generation takes 20 ms per output token with no queueing, and each call’s output allowance is ${K_{\max}} = 8{,}000$ tokens. The operator requirement is that a pause stop new admission within 15 s of its request.

    Compute the worst-case wait before the pause takes effect, and the largest ${K_{\max}}$ that meets the requirement without streaming cancellation. For this hypothetical problem, assume a 3,000-token patch can be partitioned into independently complete, validated edit proposals, each no larger than the reduced output cap. Their complete serialization totals 3,000 output tokens with no added proposal overhead, and each call carries the same 40,000-token context without a cache discount. If ${K_{\max}}$ is lowered to meet the requirement, what is the minimum number of complete calls, and how many extra input tokens, at \$3 per million, does resending the context cost compared with one call? Explain why an indivisible proposal cannot instead be completed by concatenating truncated replies. The serving service acknowledges a streaming cancellation within 2 s. Decide between the lower cap and streaming cancellation, and state what the pause must still leave in the operation table for a test job already running.

2.  **Admission after settlement (Basic).** A repair trajectory has a \$5.00 allowance, \$4.05 of settled spend, a \$0.25 reservation on an unsettled test job, and a \$0.40 finalization reserve. The next model call carries 80,000 input tokens and ${K_{\max}} = 2{,}000$, at \$2 per million input tokens and \$10 per million output tokens.

    Use [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/10 The Agent Harness/V3-10.10 Budgets and Ceilings#^eq-vol3-bounded-liability-admission|equation 1]] to decide whether the call is admitted, and compare the spend-check headroom. The call settles with 20,000 uncached input tokens, 60,000 cached input tokens at \$0.20 per million, and 900 output tokens. How much reservation does settlement release? The reply proposes paid tool calls bounded at \$0.10 each; how many can the harness admit? A defective harness had instead released the test job’s reservation when its wait timed out. How many tool calls would it admit, and by how much would the finalization reserve fall short once the test’s full \$0.25 charge arrives?

3.  **Workspaces held in escrow (Basic).** Review requests for repair submissions arrive at 12 per hour, and reviewers take 45 minutes on average to decide. Each held trajectory keeps a workspace lease of 4 GB, and the pool reserves 24 GB for held workspaces.

    Use Little’s law to compute the mean number of held trajectories and the memory they retain. What mean decision time would fit the reserved pool? An alternative snapshots each workspace to storage on entering `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, keeping 0.25 GB resident, and restores it in 40 s at dispatch. Compute its resident memory and the restore delay as a fraction of the mean decision time. Recommend one design, and explain why a pool sized from the mean alone would still fail after an incident that produces a burst of review requests.

4.  **Three classes under deficit round-robin (Intermediate).** A harness schedules three classes with quantum 10,000 charge tokens per unit weight per round: support calls (weight 4; 3,000 input tokens and a 500-token output allowance), repair calls (weight 2; 30,000 and 4,000), and maintenance calls (weight 1; 60,000 and 4,000). Assume all classes remain continuously backlogged at the stated call sizes, no provider or local-capacity gate binds, and carried credit has a ceiling large enough for the largest permitted call. One round takes 2 s.

    Compute each class’s share of the scheduling charge, its long-run calls per round, and the rounds of credit one call needs. What is the mean interval between maintenance-class admissions in seconds? An engineer caps every class’s carried credit at 50,000 tokens to limit bursts. Determine which class stops running and why. To increase maintenance-class admission frequency, one proposal raises the maintenance weight to 3; another compacts maintenance inputs to 20,000 tokens. Compute the rounds per call and the support class’s charge share under each proposal, and choose the one that leaves support service unchanged.

5.  **Sizing a dollar ceiling for cache misses (Intermediate).** A repair trajectory runs 25 turns with a 10,000-token base prompt, 2,000 tokens appended per turn, and 300 output tokens per turn; each call’s output allowance is 4,000 tokens. Prices are \$3 per million uncached input tokens, \$0.30 per million cached input tokens, and \$15 per million output tokens, and each turn’s earlier prefix is billed as cached input.

    Use **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/01 The Model/02 The Foundation Model/V3-02.09 The Cost of a Model Call#^eq-vol3-trajectory-resend|equation 2.9]]** to compute the total input tokens, and the settled cost with and without prefix reuse. The team set ${C_{\max}} = \$1.00$ from the cached bill with a \$0.15 finalization reserve, and the harness reserves each call at the undiscounted input rate and full output allowance. After 24 cached turns, is the 25th call admitted, and with what headroom? A long approval wait evicts the cache before one turn, so that turn’s whole input is billed uncached and later turns hit the cache again. Compute the extra charge for an eviction before the 6th turn and before the 24th turn, and state how many of each the trajectory can absorb before the 25th call is refused. What ${C_{\max}}$ guarantees admission of all 25 calls if every turn misses? Recommend how to size the ceiling.

6.  **Which allowance binds the research agent (Intermediate).** The research agent starts from a 6,000-token base prompt, appends 12,000 tokens of bounded page observations per turn, and generates 400 output tokens per turn with a 2,000-token output allowance. Each turn takes 45 s. Its budget is ${\mathbf{B}} = \langle 40 \text{ turns},\ 1.5 \text{ million tokens},\ 1{,}800 \text{ s},\ \$3.00 \rangle$, where the token meter counts cached input in full, and prices match problem 5.

    Admitting a turn requires its input plus its output allowance to fit the remaining token allowance. Find the last admitted turn, and compute the used fraction of every dimension at that point with prefix reuse. Which allowance binds, and how does that differ from the repair trajectory of notebook [10.3](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/10_agent_harness/10_agent_harness.html#nbk-10-multi-dimensional-accounting-and-prefix-cache)? A compaction policy runs after every fifth turn: a compaction call reads the full context and writes 2,000 tokens, and the context then restarts from the base prompt plus an 8,000-token summary. Find how many turns the agent now completes before a turn or compaction is refused. Compare this with the token allowance needed to reach 30 turns without compaction. Decide between compaction and a larger ${N_{\max}}$, naming the evidence a summary of untrusted pages must keep by reference.

7.  **Choosing a ceiling from a trial (Advanced).** A generous-budget run of 100 repair tasks, capped at \$10, gives the following outcomes: 30 tasks accepted at about \$0.50 each, 25 at about \$1.50, 15 at about \$3.00, and 5 at about \$6.00; the remaining 25 never reached acceptance and spent the full \$10. Assume that under a lower cap a task that the generous run accepted at or below the cap is still accepted at the same cost, and every other task spends exactly the cap.

    Compute the accepted count and cost per accepted task (**[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.07 Reliability and Cost Over a Horizon#^eq-vol3-cost-per-accepted-task|equation 1.2]]**) for caps of \$1, \$2, \$4, and \$10. A colleague proposes \$10 because it maximizes acceptance; critique the proposal. Treating the acceptance count as binomial and freezing each policy’s total cost, estimate the marginal relative standard error of ${C_{\text{eff}}}$ at each cap. Use the first-order approximation that the relative standard error of inverse acceptance equals the acceptance-rate standard error divided by the estimated acceptance fraction. Is the gap between the two lowest-cost caps large compared with each policy’s marginal error? State which paired cost and outcome records, and which declared comparison criterion, a formal verdict would require; these marginal errors alone do not establish distinguishability. How many tasks would bring the relative error to 3 percent at the \$2 cap’s acceptance fraction? Finally, explain why a policy that steers or compacts near its cap must be tested as a policy, and why these numbers cannot simply be read from the generous run for it.

8.  **Settling timed-out test jobs (Advanced).** Each repair trajectory dispatches six test jobs to a metered runner that charges \$0.01 per second and enforces a 90 s cap. The harness waits 30 s per job, and 70 percent of jobs finish within the wait at an average of 20 s; the rest run on, averaging 60 s in total. The trajectory allowance is ${C_{\max}} = \$4.00$. The current harness releases a job’s whole reservation when its wait times out and books nothing until the charge arrives.

    Compute each job’s bounded liability, the expected late charge per trajectory that the defective harness leaves uncovered, the worst case, and both as fractions of ${C_{\max}}$. Under the settlement rule, how much allowance does a timed-out job hold beyond its eventual charge, on average, while it runs? The runner also supports confirmed cancellation, which stops a job within 5 s and reports its final usage. Compute the charge and the released reservation for a job canceled at its timeout, and the expected test cost per trajectory under “cancel at timeout” and “let it run.” Redesign the timeout policy for the repair agent, stating which evidence releases each reservation and what a canceled job’s missing result costs the repair.

**What’s Next: From a live record to a durable one**

If the host fails after reserving and dispatching the test job but before recording its response, the live record alone cannot preserve the admitted operation, its reservation, the held approval, or the evidence needed to resume. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/11 Durable Execution/V3-11 Durable Execution#^sec-vol3-durable-execution|Durable Execution]]** develops the history and ordering needed to reconstruct selected control state and distinguish recorded outcomes from unresolved operations after a crash. Rebuilding the record does not itself prove that a remote effect failed, so resumption must preserve the tool’s settlement contract rather than blindly repeat the call.
