# V3-14.07 The Release Decision

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14 Operating Agents|V3-14 Operating Agents]]

^sec-vol3-evaluation-synthesis

In a paired evaluation cohort, `candidate_v2` accepted more tasks than `baseline_v1`, while an isolated diagnostic probe pointed toward an effective observation-interface repair. Taken on their own, these indicators seem encouraging, but neither outcome satisfies a sound engineering release gate. The two results also concern different configurations. The 150-pair cohort compared `candidate_v2` as submitted, and the worked trial of `pool_repair_01` that produced `ws_r19` sits outside that cohort. The interface repair changes `candidate_v2`’s observation adapter, so the configuration it would produce is a revision that needs its own paired evaluation; the probe is evidence about the defect, not about the revision’s cohort performance. Evaluating an agentic system requires an accountable evidence trail. Before routing production traffic to a new candidate, engineers determine which claims have verified empirical support, which operational obligations remain unresolved, and what follow-up experiment the data actually justifies.

### The Decision Record

^sec-vol3-evaluation-synthesis-architecture

When an individual trial verdict, a paired cohort comparison, and a diagnostic probe sit in separate reports without their provenance, scope, and operational boundaries attached, a positive result on the localized probe can easily be mistaken for missing regression checks or fleet-wide superiority evidence. A structured decision record prevents those disparate experimental results from being conflated by bringing the worked individual trial, the paired cohort comparison, and the diagnostic interface experiment together in one place ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.07 The Release Decision#^tbl-vol3-controlplane-layers|table 8]]). Rather than duplicating bulky workspace snapshots, the record links directly to immutable, content-addressed artifacts and explicitly logs when trace payloads, token charges, or downstream verdicts remain unavailable.

### Evidence Readiness

^sec-vol3-evaluation-synthesis-readiness

Promoting `candidate_v2` on the current evidence would ship a configuration whose trial-level regression checks are unfinished, whose interface repair has been tested on only one saved prefix, and whose paired comparison interval still crosses the degradation threshold. The decision record leaves those three engineering obligations open, each at a different layer of the evaluation stack.

1.  **Trial-level verification**: The targeted repair in `ws_r19` passed its immediate assertion, but its full regression suite and final resource accounting remain pending. Engineers cannot mark an individual trial successful while side effects and outstanding token bills remain unquantified.
2.  **Component-level diagnosis**: The observation-interface modification proved effective on continuations from one saved prefix of `pool_repair_01`, but success from a single prefix does not demonstrate generalized capability. Validating the interface repair requires testing across diverse environments and workloads.
3.  **Cohort-level release gate**: The paired comparison across the task cohort yielded a confidence interval that spans the noninferiority threshold. The next comparison needs a preregistered paired sample designed for the anticipated gain, discordance, tolerance, and power.

Inspecting a single execution trace in greater detail resolves none of these three obligations. A granular trace can explain why an agent failed a specific step, but it cannot substitute for missing regression suites, multi-fixture validation, or cohort-wide statistical significance. Collecting that missing evidence under an established protocol is also distinct from altering the evaluation question itself. When a candidate fails to clear a deployment threshold, engineers may feel tempted to prune difficult tasks, drop incomplete trials, expand token budgets mid-flight, or tune task selection criteria. While these adjustments reliably inflate raw acceptance rates, they distort the operational meaning of the benchmark. Such post hoc filtering measures an artificially sanitized workload rather than genuine system capability. When task specifications, resource constraints, or operational boundaries legitimately change, the engineering team must record a revised evaluation protocol and test the configuration against a fresh, independent sample.

### The Declared Decision Rule

^sec-vol3-evaluation-synthesis-math

Even if `candidate_v2` ran faster or cheaper than `baseline_v1`, a release gate cannot let a gain in latency or cost override an inconclusive acceptance comparison where the lower limit of the paired interval drops below the permissible degradation threshold $-{\delta_{\text{tol}}}$. That empirical evidence establishes neither noninferiority nor superiority: the interval does not rule out the specified unacceptable loss, and it does not establish that `candidate_v2` is inferior either. An inconclusive observed interval also does not by itself determine the study’s planned power. The planning count in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.06 Statistical Evaluation Rigor#^sec-vol3-evaluation-paired|Paired outcome comparison]]** is about 217 paired evaluations if the observed advantage persists, and even that count clears the gate with only roughly even odds. A powered next sample therefore uses the paired-variance planning approximation in **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.06 Statistical Evaluation Rigor#^sec-vol3-evaluation-rigor|Statistical Evaluation Rigor]]**, with its gain, discordance, and tolerance fixed in advance.

In production environments, engineering teams guard against wishful thinking by encoding deployment policies into explicit, declared decision rules. A release gate is formulated as a logical conjunction of independent criteria, each anchored to concrete empirical evidence:

$$
{\mathbf{1}_{\text{ship}}}={\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{L_{\Delta}}>-{\delta_{\text{tol}}}\right]\wedge {\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{\mathcal{G}_{c}}\ge(1-{\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}})\,{\mathcal{G}_{b}}\right]\wedge {\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{\widehat{T}_{c,0.99}}\le {T_{\text{deadline}}}\right]\wedge {\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{V_{c}}=0\right]. \tag{5}
$$

^eq-vol3-eval-release-gate

Each predicate in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.07 The Release Decision#^eq-vol3-eval-release-gate|equation 5]] enforces a distinct operational requirement:

- **Paired noninferiority** (${\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{L_{\Delta}}>-{\delta_{\text{tol}}}\right]$): The lower bound of the confidence interval for the paired acceptance difference, ${L_{\Delta}}$, must exceed the allowed degradation margin $-{\delta_{\text{tol}}}$. Clearing it supports, at the interval’s stated nominal confidence, the claim that the candidate does not degrade task completion by more than the tolerance.
- **Goodput preservation** (${\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{\mathcal{G}_{c}}\ge(1-{\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}})\,{\mathcal{G}_{b}}\right]$): Candidate trajectory goodput ${\mathcal{G}_{c}}$ must match baseline goodput ${\mathcal{G}_{b}}$ within a declared relative tolerance ${\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}}$, evaluated across matched traffic profiles, workloads, and compute capacity.
- **Tail-latency constraint** (${\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{\widehat{T}_{c,0.99}}\le {T_{\text{deadline}}}\right]$): The empirical 99th-percentile task completion time ${\widehat{T}_{c,0.99}}$ across the evaluation cohort must not exceed the operational ceiling ${T_{\text{deadline}}}$. This bound serves as a cohort-level service-level objective rather than a per-task watchdog timeout.
- **Zero-violation tripwire** (${\mathbb{I}}\!\left[{V_{c}}=0\right]$): The count of verified safety or contract violations ${V_{c}}$, such as unauthorized data modifications, privilege escalations, or uncontained tool executions, must equal zero. A single verified breach trips the gate immediately. Engineers must note, however, that observing zero violations within an evaluation cohort demonstrates only the absence of detected failures on that specific sample; it does not constitute a mathematical proof of absolute safety.

Certain operational metrics serve as diagnostic triggers rather than binary gates. For example, if a behavioral-drift metric such as the tool-distribution divergence in [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.06 Staged Releases#^eq-vol3-eval-tool-drift|equation 1]] crosses its declared alert threshold, the gate pipeline routes the candidate to manual engineering review rather than failing it outright. A significant change in tool usage frequency is not necessarily an anomaly; it often represents the exact behavioral modification intended by an interface or prompt redesign.

Because the release rule is a strict boolean conjunction, a failure in any single predicate yields ${\mathbf{1}_{\text{ship}}} = 0$. Exceptional tail latency, lower token expenses, or an unblemished safety record cannot compensate for an inconclusive or degraded task acceptance rate. At the same time, the gate enforces only the predicates explicitly written into its formula. Any failure mode the conjunction does not monitor passes through it, as war story [14.1](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol3/14_agent_operations/14_agent_operations.html#ws-vol3-evaluation-gpt4o-sycophancy-2025) showed.

For the worked candidate the first predicate already fails, so the gate returns zero without evaluating the goodput, latency, and violation terms, none of which the worked evidence measures for `candidate_v2`. The decision is to retain `baseline_v1` in production and withhold live traffic from `candidate_v2`. The engineering team’s immediate next steps are to complete the pending regression runs for `ws_r19`, reconcile open token and tool expenditures, and pre-register an appropriately powered sample size before collecting further paired comparison data.

Automated gates evaluate these conditions deterministically, yet the underlying metrics reflect varying degrees of statistical uncertainty. A release evaluation report must therefore label each criterion with its precise operational status: satisfied, failed, or inconclusive under the declared evidence rule. Furthermore, individual 95 percent confidence intervals evaluated across separate operational metrics do not compound into an overall 95 percent system guarantee. When ${k}$ statistical predicates are each allowed probability ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$ of a wrong verdict, the chance that at least one of them is wrong is bounded only by ${k}{\alpha_{\text{sig}}}$, since the probability of any error is at most the sum of the individual error probabilities. It approaches that bound when the individual errors rarely coincide. That combined probability is the family-wise error rate, and the simplest control divides the allowed error among the tests, testing each predicate at ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}/{k}$. Production evaluation protocols must document their family-wise error accounting, specifying how they control for multiple hypothesis tests and for repeated peeking across sequential evaluation checkpoints.

**Checkpoint 14.4: Defend the evaluation decision**

**Evidence scope**: Why does the targeted pass for `ws_r19` not accept its trial while the required regression result is pending? Why is a recovered result for `ws_r18` not a replacement?

**Comparison**: Why can a candidate have more accepted trials in the paired sample without clearing the prespecified noninferiority rule?

**Diagnosis**: What does a successful observation-interface intervention on one fixture support, and what broader claim still needs evaluation?

**Next step**: Using the paired-comparison notebook’s planning formula, how many task pairs would center the expected lower limit on the tolerance if the true gain were hypothesized to be half the observed one? Which discordance and power assumptions would a powered plan also need? How can the evaluation harness prevent repeated testing or selective exclusions from altering the claimed decision rule?

^callout-checkpoint-14-4-6584889a

An agent evaluation pipeline exists to produce an auditable decision, recorded immutably, that identifies the exact configuration under test, establishes what the empirical evidence proves, authorizes the next operational step, and transparently logs every duty that remains open. Skipping any link in that chain turns an operational measurement into a misleading release signal.
