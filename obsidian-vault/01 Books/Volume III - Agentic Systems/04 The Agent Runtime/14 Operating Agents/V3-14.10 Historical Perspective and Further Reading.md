# V3-14.10 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14 Operating Agents|V3-14 Operating Agents]]

^sec-vol3-operations-history

The statistics of staged release are older than electronic computing. During World War II, Abraham Wald developed the sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) to optimize industrial quality inspection, showing that deciding acceptance or rejection dynamically as evidence accumulates minimizes the expected sample size relative to fixed-horizon trials ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-wald1945sequential|Wald 1945]]).

The telemetry required to diagnose multi-turn agent execution draws directly from distributed systems observability. Lamport established that asynchronous events across decoupled components admit only a partial order unless system metadata explicitly captures their causal links ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-lamport1978time|Lamport 1978]]). Fonseca et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-fonseca2007xtrace|2007]]) extended this principle to modern network architectures with X-Trace, propagating deterministic task contexts across diverse protocol layers and administrative domains to reconstruct complete end-to-end execution paths. In the same years, production web services transformed deployment validation into continuous experimentation: Kohavi et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-kohavi2008|2009]]) codified online controlled experiments with staged canary deployments as standard release gates. Sigelman et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-sigelman2010dapper|2010]]) then proved in Dapper that distributed tracing could operate continuously at production scale by decoupling span collection from the critical path and employing aggressive head-based sampling to bound runtime overhead to negligible levels. Google’s site reliability engineering practice established the blameless postmortem, institutionalizing structured incident postmortems as essential operational records for diagnosing systemic failures ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-beyer2016sre|Beyer et al. 2016]]). When engineering teams began monitoring live experimentation dashboards in real time, Johari et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-johari2017peeking|2017]]) proved that repeated significance testing inflates false-positive rates far above the nominal level, establishing why sequential testing bounds, the descendants of Wald’s test, must govern automated canary rollouts. Agent releases add what Wald’s inspection lines did not face: labels that arrive hours after admission, tasks whose effects outlive the decision, and harms that no monitored metric records, the gap that the 2025 sycophancy rollback exposed ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-openai2025sycophancy|OpenAI 2025b]]).

**Further reading.**

- **Wald, “Sequential Tests of Statistical Hypotheses”** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-wald1945sequential|Wald 1945]]): The original derivation of the sequential probability ratio test, its approximate decision boundaries, and its average sample number, providing the mathematical foundation for staged canary deployments and early-stopping test monitors.
- **Sigelman et al., “Dapper”** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-sigelman2010dapper|Sigelman et al. 2010]]): The foundational architecture of production distributed tracing under strict overhead budgets, examining the sampling policies and trace contexts that enable fine-grained post-hoc diagnosis.
- **Kohavi, Tang, and Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-kohavi2020trustworthy|Kohavi et al. 2020]]): A practitioner’s guide to randomized experimentation in production systems, covering assignment units, sample-ratio mismatches, and the statistical pitfalls that invalidate live rollout decisions.
- **Beyer et al., *Site Reliability Engineering*** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.12 Source References#^ref-beyer2016sre|Beyer et al. 2016]]): The practitioner’s account of operating production services, including the blameless postmortem and the release engineering that agent operations extend from people to components.
