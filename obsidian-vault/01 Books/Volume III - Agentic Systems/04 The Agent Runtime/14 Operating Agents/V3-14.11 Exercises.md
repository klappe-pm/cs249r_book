# V3-14.11 Exercises

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14 Operating Agents|V3-14 Operating Agents]]

^sec-vol3-14-agent-operations-exercises-77954586

Each problem states its constraints and ends in a decision about what the evidence supports or what to collect next. Givens are hypothetical unless stated otherwise, and every interval is a nominal 95 percent interval with ${z}=1.96$.

**1. Where the time went (Basic).** A repair-agent timing trial records these spans on one host clock, in seconds after segment start. Retrieval runs 0.0 to 1.2, a model call proposing checks 1.2 to 4.0, targeted tests 4.0 to 13.0, a static lint check 4.0 to 7.5, a dependency audit 4.0 to 10.0, and a model call synthesizing the report 14.0 to 17.0. The three checks are independent of one another, synthesis waits for all three, and the enclosing segment ends at 17.5. (a) Compute the sum of the leaf span durations and the critical-path duration, and find how much of the 17.5 s segment the spans leave unattributed. (b) A cache cuts the targeted tests to 5.0 s with everything else unchanged. Compute the new critical path and the saving, and name the span that now limits the check phase. (c) A routing change instead halves both model calls. Compute its saving, compare it with part (b), and state which assumption about the unattributed intervals both estimates share. (d) Decide which change to fund first if each costs the same to build, and name the controlled measurement that must confirm the saving before it is claimed.

**2. A retention budget for rare failures (Intermediate).** A repair fleet runs 20,000 evaluation trials per day, each with a 2 MB (decimal) permitted trace body. At the selection boundary, 3 percent of trials have failed a criterion, 1 percent remain pending or unknown, and the rest are ordinary accepted trials. One failure mode occurs 8 times per week, and downstream diagnostic storage is capped at 1.5 GB per day. (a) Compute the daily volume of full retention and of uniform head sampling at ${\theta}=2$ percent, and the probability that head sampling keeps no detailed trace of the rare mode in a week. (b) Compute the daily volume of tail selection that keeps every failed and unresolved trial plus a 1 percent random control sample of ordinary trials, and check it against the cap. (c) Design a selection policy within the cap that keeps every failed trial. State the sampling rates for unresolved and ordinary trials, the inclusion probabilities the record must keep, and one change to the trace body that would relax the cap. (d) An analyst reports the fraction of retained traces under the policy of part (b) that failed as the fleet’s failure rate. Compute the reported value, and name the record the failure rate must come from.

**3. Drift in shadow execution (Intermediate).** A shadow run of the revised repair configuration records its tool calls over four categories, read, test, lint, and edit. The baseline distributes its calls as 0.40, 0.30, 0.10, and 0.20, and the candidate as 0.30, 0.45, 0.05, and 0.20. Two independent baseline samples of the same size differ by a Jensen–Shannon divergence of 0.004 nats. (a) Compute ${\mathcal{D}_{\text{JS}}}$ between baseline and candidate with [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.06 Staged Releases#^eq-vol3-eval-tool-drift|equation 1]], in nats, and express it as a fraction of its maximum, $\ln 2$. (b) Release policy routes a candidate to engineering review when its divergence exceeds three times the baseline-versus-baseline value. Decide whether this candidate is routed, and whether the decision would change under a five-times rule. (c) The candidate is the paginated observation interface, which retrieves remaining results instead of guessing. Explain why more test calls and fewer reads may be the intended change, and state which measurement, not the divergence, decides whether the shift is an improvement. (d) The shadow copies also read from a production ticketing service. Name three effects such reads can have and the declarations the shadow design must make before it mirrors any input.

**4. Postmortem of a wrong refund answer (Advanced).** The support agent tells a customer that a purchase is refundable within 60 days, while the current policy allows 30. The retained trace holds three records. The raw retrieval result contains two policy passages, the current version and a superseded one, each with an effective date; the delivered context contains both passages with the effective dates removed by the observation adapter; and the answer quotes the superseded passage. The team restores a compatible saved prefix ending just before the retrieval call and runs 40 fresh continuations in each of three branches, holding the model configuration, sampling settings, prompt, and a policy-grounded reference check fixed; every run reaches a known verdict. Branch A keeps the original adapter and yields 18 wrong answers. Branch B delivers the effective dates and yields 3 wrong answers. Branch C removes the superseded passage from the index, so only the current version is retrieved, and yields none. Release policy requires the Wilson upper limit on the support agent’s per-session wrong-answer rate to be at most 2 percent. (a) For each claim, state whether the retained record alone, a regression replay of the captured result without a model call, or only the intervention can establish it: the adapter removes effective dates; the model received both passages; the missing dates contributed to the wrong answer. (b) Compute each branch’s wrong-answer rate with its Wilson interval. Decide whether the data support the claim that delivering effective dates reduces wrong answers from this prefix, and whether 40 runs per branch distinguish branch B from branch C. (c) Compute how many consecutive correct continuations a fix would need before its Wilson upper limit met the release policy, and the corresponding lower confidence bound on five-session consistency from branch C’s upper limit at 40 runs, assuming independent recurrences from this same saved prefix. (d) A teammate reports a fourth branch that delivers the dates and also lowers the sampling temperature to zero, with 40 of 40 correct, and proposes shipping it as the fix. Critique the proposal, choose a corrective change, and write the postmortem record’s lines for established findings, tested change, and remaining questions.

**5. Size a canary against a deadline (Advanced).** A repair-agent canary monitors acceptance with the SPRT of [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/14 Operating Agents/V3-14.06 Staged Releases#^eq-vol3-release-sprt|equation 2]], comparing ${p}_0=0.80$ with ${p}_1=0.70$ at design targets ${\alpha_{\text{sig}}}=0.05$ and ${\beta_{\text{stat}}}=0.10$. Eligible tasks arrive regularly at 40 per hour, each label arrives 2 hours after admission when the regression suite finishes, and under ${p}_1$ the planning target is an approximate expected decision time of at most 24 hours from the first admission. Separately, a harm the monitor does not measure occurs in 0.5 percent of candidate tasks, and release policy requires the candidate to run for 7 days at its allocation before any wider release. (a) Compute ${u}$, ${b_{\text{low}}}$, ${m}_1$, and the no-overshoot $\mathbb{E}_1[{N_{\text{stop}}}]$. (b) Find the smallest allocation ${\rho}$ that meets that expected-time target. (c) Compute the expected excess failures from the measured regression at that ${\rho}$ and at ${\rho}=25$ percent. (d) If the allocation continues for all seven days, compute the expected count of unmeasured harms over the 7-day period at both allocations. Choose ${\rho}$ and justify it from parts (b) through (d). Explain why this exposure calculation is not an instruction to keep admitting the candidate after a degradation alarm. (e) A teammate proposes replacing the SPRT with a fixed-sample test rerun after every new label, stopping at the first value below 0.05. Critique the proposal.

**6. Critique a release report (Advanced).** A team asks to promote a repair-agent candidate with the following evidence. The candidate accepted 78 percent of tasks and the baseline 72 percent, reported as a 6-point gain. The baseline ran on 100 tasks sampled last month, and the candidate on 100 newly sampled tasks. The team dropped 12 candidate trials whose regression suites had not finished, so the candidate accepted 69 of the remaining 88. Report quality counted as accepted when an LLM judge scored it at least 4 of 5, justified by 90 percent raw agreement with a second judge. The failure rate in the release notes was computed from tail-sampled traces. (a) Under the four-verdict rule, bound the candidate’s accepted fraction of its admitted cohort, and state what the 6-point claim becomes. (b) Using **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/04 The Agent Runtime/13 Agent Evaluation/V3-13.06 Statistical Evaluation Rigor#^eq-vol3-eval-planning-power|equation 13.3]]** with ${\bar{p}}=0.75$, a 5 percent two-sided significance target, and 80 percent power, compute the tasks per independent arm needed to detect a 6-point difference. (c) Redesign the comparison as paired. Assuming a discordant fraction ${\psi}=0.15$, an anticipated gain of 3 points, and ${\delta_{\text{tol}}}=2$ points, find the number of task pairs at which the normal-approximation lower limit ${\hat{\Delta}}-{z}\sqrt{({\psi}-{\hat{\Delta}}^2)/{N}}$ clears $-{\delta_{\text{tol}}}$ if the gain holds. Contrast this expected-limit-centering count with the fixed-variance normal planning count at 80 percent power, using power quantile 0.84. Both calculations assume the stated gain and discordance; the powered count approximates a normal gate rather than calibrating the exact paired-score interval. (d) Identify the remaining defects in the report and write a defensible protocol: which criteria the automated gate enforces, which measurements the judge must carry, and which conditions the team must declare before collecting data.

**What’s Next: From operational evidence to curated trajectories**

Operation leaves a qualified record: verdicts held in the accounting record, traces retained with their inclusion probabilities, and a postmortem that separated an interface defect from a model behavior and a runtime refusal. **[[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/05 Learning from Trajectories/15 Trajectory Curation/V3-15 Trajectory Curation#^sec-vol3-trajectory-curation|Trajectory Curation]]** uses that record to guide diagnosis, admission, and corpus design, and its first decision is triage. The truncated-observation failure that the postmortem traced largely to the observation adapter belongs in an interface fix, not in a corpus. A logged success is not automatically a useful demonstration, and a failure is not automatically a policy defect. Preserve criterion and configuration provenance, unresolved evidence, and the separation between development data and held-out evaluation. Curation turns that qualified record into a learning-data decision.
