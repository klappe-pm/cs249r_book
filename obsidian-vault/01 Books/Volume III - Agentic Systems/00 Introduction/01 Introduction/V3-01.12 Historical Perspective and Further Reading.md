# V3-01.12 Historical Perspective and Further Reading

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/Volume 3 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01 Introduction|V3-01 Introduction]]

^sec-vol3-intro-history

The closed-loop architecture at the core of agentic systems predates modern machine learning by decades. In founding cybernetics, Norbert Wiener established feedback as the organizing principle of control: a system observes its environment, measures the discrepancy between current state and a target goal, and applies corrective actions ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-wiener1948cybernetics|Wiener 1948]]). Writing a decade later, Wiener warned of the operational hazard inherent in rapid feedback: when an automated machine acts faster than a human operator can intervene, it must be constrained to the exact purpose intended ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-wiener1960|Wiener 1960]]). Artificial intelligence adopted this structure through the rational agent framework, defining an agent by its continuous cycle of perception and environment interaction ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-russell2020artificial|Russell and Norvig 2020]]). Reinforcement learning subsequently formalized that cycle, treating the iterative trajectory of states, actions, rewards, and observations as the central object of learning ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-sutton2018reinforcement|Sutton and Barto 2018]]).

What transformed with foundation models was the capability of the component driving the loop. Rather than relying on specialized heuristics or hand-crafted policies, a general-purpose model pretrained across diverse data distributions can now synthesize reasoning traces and emit concrete actions in text and code ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-bommasani2021opportunities|Bommasani et al. 2021]]). The ReAct architecture demonstrated that interleaving model-generated reasoning with tool execution feedback allows language models to operate effectively in open software environments ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-yao2023react|Yao et al. 2023]]). Real-world software engineering benchmarks crystallized this shift, demonstrating that an agent’s capability must be measured not by fluency on static prompts, but by whether its proposed patches pass unseen repository test suites ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-jimenez2024swebench|Jimenez et al. 2024]]).

The scaffolding surrounding the model builds on classical systems research addressing unreliable components. John von Neumann first investigated how to synthesize dependable computational systems from failure-prone parts, demonstrating that modular redundancy, majority voting, and periodic state restoration can bound compound failure rates ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-vonneumann1956probabilistic|Neumann 1956]]). Subsequent distributed systems research formalized the fault assumptions that supervisors must accommodate. These range from fail-stop models, where damaged components halt detectably ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-schlichting1983failstop|Schlichting and Schneider 1983]]), to Byzantine failure models, where components behave arbitrarily or deceptively ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-lamport1982byzantine|Lamport et al. 1982]]). The modern dependability taxonomy codified the causal sequence linking latent faults, runtime errors, and observable system failures, drawing an essential distinction between errors that trigger overt alarms and those that silently corrupt downstream execution ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-avizienis2004basic|Avizienis et al. 2004]]).

Because foundation models generate probabilistic outputs, verification cannot be offloaded to the generator itself. The end-to-end argument in system design established that complete correctness checks cannot be delegated to intermediate subcomponents; they must ultimately reside at the endpoint that understands application-level task requirements ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-saltzer1984endtoend|Saltzer et al. 1984]]). At the same time, Dijkstra’s foundational observation that software testing demonstrates the presence of bugs rather than their absence bounds the certainty that any validation suite can provide ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-dijkstra1970structured|Dijkstra 1970]]).

Managing delegation and authority safely relies on principles developed across operating systems and distributed transaction design. Capability-based addressing, introduced by Dennis and Van Horn, bound access rights directly to unforgeable, fine-grained object references rather than ambient process identities ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-dennis1966|Dennis and Van Horn 1966]]). Saltzer and Schroeder codified these patterns into enduring design principles for computer protection, emphasizing least privilege, complete mediation of access requests, and economy of mechanism ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-saltzer1975|Saltzer and Schroeder 1975]]). Hardy later uncovered the hazard of ambient authority: in a confused deputy attack, an unprivileged client manipulates a privileged utility into misusing permissions granted by its environment rather than by the specific caller ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-hardy1988confused|Hardy 1988]]).

Extended autonomous execution also confronts the reality that multi-step external workflows cannot be rolled back atomically. Garcia-Molina and Salem developed sagas to manage long-running transactions, coordinating forward progress through predefined compensating actions when operations fail midway ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-garciamolina1987sagas|Garcia-Molina and Salem 1987]]). At interface boundaries, Meyer’s design by contract established that modular software reliability depends on explicit, executable preconditions, postconditions, and invariants ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-meyer1992applying|Meyer 1992]]). Autonomous agents reopen each of these classical security and reliability challenges simultaneously. Because language models interleave data interpretation with control flow, external data cannot be assumed benign. Greshake et al. ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-greshake2023not|2023]]) demonstrated that indirect prompt injection turns untrusted external content into instructions the model may follow. The result shows that for an agent guided by natural language, read access is itself an exposure, because what the agent reads can steer every action it proposes, even though only the runtime can grant those actions.

- **The Protection of Information in Computer Systems** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-saltzer1975|Saltzer and Schroeder 1975]]). The classic formulation of protection mechanisms for computer systems, including least privilege, complete mediation, and fail-safe defaults. This paper provides the conceptual foundation for modern tool authorization, sandboxing, and privilege boundaries in agent architectures.
- **End-to-End Arguments in System Design** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-saltzer1984endtoend|Saltzer et al. 1984]]). The foundational rationale for placing validation checks at the application endpoints that maintain task semantics. In agent engineering, it clarifies why intermediate generation fluency cannot certify end-to-end task completion.
- **Basic Concepts and Taxonomy of Dependable and Secure Computing** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-avizienis2004basic|Avizienis et al. 2004]]). The standard taxonomy distinguishing faults, errors, and failures across computing systems. It provides the disciplined vocabulary needed to diagnose agent failure modes, including the fail-plausible errors characteristic of foundation models.
- ***Principles of Computer System Design*** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-saltzer2009|Saltzer and Kaashoek 2009]]). A comprehensive textbook on system decomposition, atomicity, virtualization, and protection. It supplies the essential systems foundation for building resilient agent orchestrators, runtimes, and tool harnesses.
- ***Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-sutton2018reinforcement|Sutton and Barto 2018]]). The definitive treatise on sequential decision-making in dynamic environments. It provides the formal vocabulary of states, actions, rewards, and environment trajectories that underpins the execution model in this chapter.
- **ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models** ([[01 Books/Volume III - Agentic Systems/00 Introduction/01 Introduction/V3-01.14 Source References#^ref-yao2023react|Yao et al. 2023]]). The seminal paper demonstrating that interleaving reasoning traces with tool invocations enables language models to solve multi-step problems. Reading it alongside this chapter highlights the boundary between the model’s proposed actions and the external system responsible for executing and verifying them.
