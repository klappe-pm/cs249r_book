# V1-09.10 Cross-Layer Interactions

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/Volume 1 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/09 Data Selection/V1-09 Data Selection|V1-09 Data Selection]]

^sec-data-selection-crosslayer-interactions-1f39

Halving a training corpus halves the forward and backward passes, yet the resulting model may be no cheaper to compress or serve. Real ML systems combine data selection with model compression and hardware acceleration, and these cross-layer interactions amplify or undermine each other in ways no single layer reveals. Model compression (removing weights after training, storing them at lower precision, or distilling into a smaller network, all developed in **[[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/10 Model Compression/V1-10 Model Compression#^sec-model-compression|Model Compression]]**) acts on the trained network, and its savings reach serving latency only when the hardware and software that run the model can exploit the smaller or lower-precision result ([[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/09 Data Selection/V1-09.14 Source References#^ref-hoefler2021sparsity|Hoefler et al. 2021]]).

Because the training corpus shapes the representations that compression later works on, a model trained on an uncurated, repetitive corpus can spend capacity on redundant features that post-training pruning then discards, whereas a curated corpus of diverse, informative examples can yield more compact representations from the start. That effect is an empirical hypothesis to verify, not a property of every selected dataset. The two levers therefore compose without substituting for each other. Data selection reduces training work and shapes learned representations, model compression shrinks the deployment artifact, and their combined impact is measured on serving latency and task accuracy rather than inferred from the upstream dataset reduction.

↪ Why accelerators turn structured sparsity patterns into wall-clock speedup while unstructured sparsity rarely pays off is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/11 Hardware Acceleration/V1-11.05 Compute Units#^sec-hardware-acceleration-nm-structured-sparsity-mechanics-9a71|N:M structured sparsity mechanics]]**.

↪ Co-designing post-training pruning and quantization under the silicon contract is developed in **[[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/10 Model Compression/V1-10.02 Fitting the Deployment Budget#^sec-model-compression-optimization-framework-9e21|Fitting the Deployment Budget]]**.

Production deployments execute these techniques as one pipeline in two stages: data selection and training turn raw inputs into a trained model, and post-training compression and hardware acceleration then deploy that artifact ([[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/09 Data Selection/V1-09.10 Cross-Layer Interactions#^fig-optimization-stack|figure 13]]). Every stage along this end-to-end path amplifies or attenuates downstream efficiency.

Figure 13: **The Optimization Stack**: Data artifacts flow through selection and training before the resulting model enters compression and deployment. Upstream changes propagate quality and cost effects downstream, but reducing training data does not automatically simplify the model or deployment hardware.

^fig-optimization-stack

Tracing artifacts through the pipeline in [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/03 Optimization/09 Data Selection/V1-09.10 Cross-Layer Interactions#^fig-optimization-stack|figure 13]] shows why data selection sits at the head of the optimization stack. Pruning raw inputs reduces upstream training FLOPs, but it does not automatically shrink parameter footprint, improve quantization headroom, or relax serving latency constraints in the downstream compression and hardware stages. Each downstream tier inherits both the computational savings and any representational deficits created upstream. An aggressive filter that discards rare but critical edge cases can force downstream engineers to train longer or abandon aggressive quantization to preserve task accuracy, turning an apparent upstream saving into a production failure when selection assumptions go unchecked.

1. What total speedup follows from the chapter’s multiplicative D·A·M reasoning?

    1.  An $8\times$ speedup, because halving workload, halving per-sample work, and doubling throughput multiply on the same critical path
    2.  A $6\times$ speedup, because the factors add across layers
    3.  A $2\times$ speedup, because only the hardware improvement matters once arithmetic throughput doubles
    4.  A $4\times$ speedup, because reducing training examples and reducing per-sample work are the same effect

2.  Why are upstream data selection and downstream model compression complementary rather than interchangeable?

    1.  Model compression can only be applied to computer vision models, whereas data selection is restricted to NLP
    2.  Data selection eliminates backward passes entirely, whereas model compression eliminates forward passes
    3.  Data selection optimizes only inference latency on edge devices, whereas model compression only affects training time
    4.  Data selection reduces how many examples are processed, whereas model compression reduces the artifact size and can lower execution cost per example on compatible hardware
