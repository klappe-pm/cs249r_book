*How can a carefully selected subset retain most of the value of a much larger dataset?*

Training pays to acquire, label, score, move, and process examples even though their learning value differs. Large datasets contain redundant, noisy, or target-misaligned samples, while scarce regions may lack the cases a model needs, such as the rare conditions under which a deployed system is most likely to fail. Training on every example treats a near-duplicate and a rare failure case as equally worth paying for, and at scale much of the compute budget can go to relearning what the model already knows. That heterogeneity turns the choice of examples into a systems optimization rather than a data-cleaning chore. The objective is therefore not the smallest dataset but the lowest end-to-end cost of reaching target quality without losing rare cases, underrepresented groups, or deployment-relevant coverage. Every strategy adds its own overhead, because examples must be scored, generated, shuffled, stored, or validated, and selection latency, storage access, or distributed coordination can erase the labeling or training savings that strategy promises. A method is worth applying only when the learning it preserves or creates exceeds the work required to apply it. This distinction matters across repeated experiments, where useful reductions compound, and across adaptation, where the value of an example changes with the model and target distribution. In D·A·M terms, it is data-algorithm co-design: shape the training signal so each unit of machine work yields more useful learning.

**Learning Objectives**

- Explain data selection as data-algorithm co-design that controls sample value, training work, and labeling cost
- Calculate the information-compute ratio and apply the compute-optimal tokens-per-parameter check to diagnose a data-starved run
- Apply the selection inequality to test whether selection overhead beats full-dataset training cost
- Compare deduplication, coreset selection, and quality pruning for reducing pretraining data under coverage floors
- Design curriculum, active learning, synthetic-data, or label-free strategies for changing data value during training
- Evaluate whether a selection pipeline keeps the accelerator fed under storage locality, data echoing, and multi-accelerator consistency constraints
- Select data-selection investments using return on investment, break-even, and amortization analysis
