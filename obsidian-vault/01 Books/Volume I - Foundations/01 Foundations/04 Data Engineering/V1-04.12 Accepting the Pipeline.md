# V1-04.12 Accepting the Pipeline

[[Home|Home]] · [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/Volume 1 Overview|Volume Overview]] · [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/01 Foundations/04 Data Engineering/V1-04 Data Engineering|V1-04 Data Engineering]]

^sec-data-engineering-accepting-the-kws-pipeline

A pipeline whose individual stages all run cleanly can still fail deployment if the trained keyword spotter misses its detection target, wakes too often on ordinary speech, or overruns the always-on island’s memory when feature buffers and runtime state are added to the model weights. Evaluating the candidate design of [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/01 Foundations/04 Data Engineering/V1-04.04 Requirements and Data Budget#^tbl-data-engineering-kws-configuration|table 2]] as a complete pipeline therefore begins with an explicit acceptance contract: the 98 percent keyword detection target on the declared evaluation population, the expected false-wake budget below 1 false wake/month, the 256 KB model limit, and the separate 16 KB preprocessing-state budget, alongside the latency and power requirements of [[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/01 Foundations/04 Data Engineering/V1-04.04 Requirements and Data Budget#^sec-data-engineering-framework-application-keyword-spotting-case-study-c8e9|section 4.3]]. Model bytes alone cannot establish a device fit; peak feature buffers and runtime state must fit the device’s total memory allocation.

The false-wake target is a data requirement in its own right. Under a Poisson arrival model, if the true rate were three false wakes across the test window, the probability of observing zero false wakes by chance would be $e^{-3}$, or about 5 percent. Any higher true rate makes a zero count even less likely, so observing zero false wakes rules out, at 95 percent confidence, every true rate of three or more false wakes across the window. The upper 95 percent confidence bound on the true rate is therefore about three events divided by the hours listened, known as the zero-event rule of three ([[01 Books/Volume I - Foundations/01 Foundations/04 Data Engineering/V1-04.17 Source References#^ref-hanley1983nothing|Hanley and Lippman-Hand 1983]]). Keeping that upper bound at or below one false wake per 720-hour month requires listening for three full months with zero false wakes, which takes about 2,160 hours of representative nonkeyword audio, or about 248 GB of recordings at 16 kHz. That requirement is as concrete as the label count. The allocation in Notebook [4.2](https://mlsysbook.ai/vol1/04_data_engineering/04_data_engineering.html#nbk-data-engineering-optimizing-kws-design-space) never costed it, so the team must add an evaluation-collection line, because no amount of training data can stand in for these hours.

To test the choice of 13 rather than 40 MFCC coefficients, both candidates use the same versioned speaker-separated data splits, label budget, and recorded training procedure. Each candidate packages its own fitted preprocessing state with its model. Validation data selects the detection threshold; a separate, untouched evaluation set then measures keyword recognition and false rejection, while representative nonkeyword streams measure false accepts per hour through the deployed detection path. The team fixes the required statistical confidence and listening duration before evaluation and accepts the false-wake claim only when the upper uncertainty bound, expressed over the same listening duration as the target, meets that target. Insufficient listening time leaves the claim unestablished even if no false wake occurs. Replaying fixed audio through the training and device preprocessing paths checks feature parity, and measurements on the target device establish peak memory, latency, and power.

The budget charges collection and review to the labeling allocation, and generation, retained versions, storage, and processing to their stated allocations. The labeling allocation buys only the 750K real examples; synthetic data must come out of the other allocations. If the 13-coefficient candidate meets every acceptance condition, the larger representation needs a measured quality benefit worth its added device cost. If the smaller candidate fails quality, the 40-coefficient alternative is acceptable only if it closes that gap while passing the same resource and cost checks. If neither passes, the team revises coverage, representation, or the product requirements and repeats validation; it cannot declare success from the design-space gains alone. The rest of the chapter assumes the 13-coefficient candidate passes.

The ResNet-50 scenario has a simpler contract: delivery must not limit the training rate. It passes on a SATA solid-state drive today, provided the images are read as large sequential shards, which lets the drive supply about 3,333 img/s against the 3,200 img/s the accelerator sustains. It fails as soon as faster kernels or a faster accelerator push the rate toward the compute ceiling, which is why the storage tier is sized for the rate the pipeline is heading toward rather than the rate it runs at today.

Acceptance does not end the work of data engineering. In production, input environments continuously drift: sensor hardware shifts, upstream schemas mutate, and annotator pools turn over. Without active operational maintenance, static pipelines quietly degrade over time.

**Self-Check: Question**

1.  During evaluation, a keyword spotter produces zero false wakes over 500 hours of representative non-keyword audio. Does this establish a budget of at most one false wake per month (about 720 hours of listening) at 95 percent confidence?

    1.  No. With zero observed events, the 95 percent upper bound on the rate is about three events divided by 500 hours, roughly one per 167 hours, far above one per 720 hours; about 2,160 hours with no false wakes would be needed.
    2.  Yes. Zero observed false wakes means the true false-wake rate is zero.
    3.  Yes. Five hundred hours is close enough to a month of listening to support a monthly claim.
    4.  No. False wakes can only be measured on devices after launch, never in offline evaluation.

2.  The ResNet-50 pipeline passes acceptance on a SATA solid-state drive today. Explain why the chapter still sizes the storage tier for a higher rate, and what change would make the SATA drive the bottleneck.
